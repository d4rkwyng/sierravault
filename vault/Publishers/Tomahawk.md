# Tomahawk

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Tomahawk was the label under which [[Coktel Vision|Coktel Vision]] published its non-educational games, from simulations and racing games to adventures, a beat 'em up and erotic titles.[^ref-6][^ref-20][^ref-23][^ref-27][^ref-28] It was presented as a subsidiary, but it was not a separate company.[^ref-1][^ref-6][^ref-16] Coktel designer Muriel Tramis described it this way: "Tomahawk was not another company; it was simply a trick, a brand under which Coktel Vision created some 'touchy' games like Emmanuelle or Geisha."[^ref-16] Coktel used the brand to keep those titles apart from its educational software.[^ref-6][^ref-16]

Tomahawk's own titles came out between 1988 and 1991; MobyGames credits it on 14 games from those years.[^ref-1] French magazines were still naming it as publisher in 1992: *Tilt* reviewed *Gobliiins* in January 1992 with "Editeur : Tomahawk",[^ref-24] listed Tomahawk as publisher of the Amiga versions of *A.G.E.* and *Fascination* in May,[^ref-25] and in June credited the *Fascination* CD-ROM to Tomahawk, distributed by Euro-CD.[^ref-26] Re-releases carried the label into 1993, including an Italian release of *Gobliiins*[^ref-2] and Atari ST re-releases of *Emmanuelle*, *Geisha* and *Legend of Djel*.[^ref-5]

## Sierra Connection

[[Publishers/Sierra On-Line|Sierra On-Line]] published *Gobliiins* in the United States in 1992, while Tomahawk published it in France, Germany, Italy and the United Kingdom.[^ref-2] Sierra's purchase of Coktel Vision was finalised in October 1993.[^ref-6] According to MobyGames, Sierra then "used the former Tomahawk distribution structure to build its own European distribution network."[^ref-12]
