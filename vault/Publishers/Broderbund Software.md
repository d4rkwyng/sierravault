# Broderbund Software

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Broderbund Software (originally Brøderbund) was a major American software publisher founded in 1980 by Doug and Gary Carlston.[^ref-1][^ref-9] The company was known for educational software (*Carmen Sandiego* series, *The Print Shop*) and games (*Prince of Persia*, *Myst*).[^ref-2][^ref-5] Broderbund was acquired by The Learning Company in 1998.[^ref-3][^ref-11]

Broderbund published Cyan's *Myst* in 1993; Wikipedia calls it the best-selling PC game for nearly a decade.[^ref-6][^ref-18] Activision never held the rights. According to Robyn Miller, when the Millers had earlier pitched the game to Activision under the title *The Gray Summons*, Activision told them to stick to children's games.[^ref-18]

## Sierra Connection

The Strong museum describes Brøderbund as having risen to prominence "in competition and conversation with other software publishers such as Sierra and SSI."[^ref-4] The two companies were also close: Brøderbund "forged a special bond with On-Line Systems," and on a 1981 whitewater-rafting trip Doug Carlston and Ken Williams seriously discussed merging them before deciding it wouldn't make sense.[^ref-9]

**The 1991 merger that wasn't:** On March 8, 1991, Sierra On-Line and Broderbund announced that they had signed a letter of intent to merge. The combined company was expected to be called Sierra-Broderbund, Inc., with Ken Williams as CEO of the consolidated company, Doug Carlston as Broderbund president, and Broderbund president Ed Auer heading a new manufacturing and sales unit.[^ref-16] *Computer Gaming World* reported that the Wall Street Journal estimated the stock swap at over $88 million, and that Broderbund would in effect have become a wholly owned subsidiary of Sierra.[^ref-16] The talks were terminated on March 27, 1991, after the directors concluded that key understandings on combining the two management teams were unlikely. Ken Williams told *CGW*: "Both companies were doing so well and having so much fun that we looked at the work that it would have taken to put the companies together and decided that it wasn't best."[^ref-17][^ref-1][^ref-11]

## Later Ownership

Broderbund became a wholly owned subsidiary of The Learning Company on August 31, 1998, and The Learning Company, including Broderbund, became a division of Mattel on May 13, 1999.[^ref-3][^ref-6] Doug Carlston later called Mattel "dumb money" that "didn't know anything about software."[^ref-7] Mattel sold Broderbund to Gores Technology Group in October 2000; Gores sold the entertainment division to Ubi Soft in March 2001, and Riverdeep Interactive Learning bought The Learning Company in September 2001 and "all of Brøderbund" on August 26, 2002.[^ref-3][^ref-12]

Riverdeep later licensed the Broderbund product line to Encore. A Navarre Corporation press release dated November 11 announced an expanded licence making Encore "the Exclusive North American Publisher of Broderbund Products"; the year is not visible in the archived copy, and Wikipedia dates the announcement to November 2008.[^ref-19][^ref-20] Today broderbund.com is run by Encore Software LLC.[^ref-14]
