# Coktel Vision

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Coktel Vision was a French video game developer and publisher founded in 1984 by Roland Oskian, a former executive at Matra Espace.[^ref-16][^ref-1] Some databases give 1985 as its founding year.[^ref-2][^ref-15] The company was best known for its educational and adventure games: the Adi educational range and, among adventure players, the Gobliiins series.[^ref-1][^ref-16][^ref-7]

Sierra On-Line acquired Coktel Vision S.A. on 29 October 1993, for an initial purchase price of about $5.3 million.[^ref-17] Under the merger agreement, Sierra entrusted Coktel with distributing all of its entertainment and educational programs in the European market, and Sierra in turn represented Coktel's products in the United States under licence.[^ref-16] In October 2005 VU Games sold Coktel to the French publisher Mindscape, which kept Coktel as a brand until Mindscape closed in 2011.[^ref-19][^ref-1]

For detailed information about the studio's development history, see [[Coktel Vision|Coktel Vision (Developer)]].

## Sierra Connection

As part of the deal, Sierra published Coktel's French titles worldwide, while Coktel localised Sierra's games and published them through its own Tomahawk label.[^ref-1] Sierra On-Line also published the Gobliiins series, and when Sierra became involved with the third game, Goblins 3 was renamed Goblins Quest 3 to align it with Sierra's King's Quest, Police Quest and Space Quest series.[^ref-7][^ref-3]

In February 1996 CUC International agreed to buy Sierra, and Coktel became part of CUC's software branch; that branch was sold to Havas in 1998, and Havas was later acquired by Vivendi.[^ref-18][^ref-1] After Urban Runner became a commercial failure in 1996, Coktel turned from adventure games to educational titles, focusing on the European market.[^ref-1]
