# Activision

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Activision Publishing, Inc.[^ref-2] is an American video game publisher based in Santa Monica, California, and one of the largest third-party video game publishers in the world.[^ref-1] It was founded on October 1, 1979, by former Atari programmers David Crane, Alan Miller and Bob Whitehead with Jim Levy, a record-industry executive who had been working at GRT Records.[^ref-1][^ref-3][^ref-11] Activision was the first independent, third-party console video game developer.[^ref-1]

The company grew from its origins as an Atari 2600 developer into one of the industry's largest publishers.[^ref-3] On July 9, 2008, Activision completed its merger with Vivendi Games (parent of Blizzard Entertainment and Sierra) and was renamed Activision Blizzard.[^ref-16][^ref-4][^ref-10] Microsoft announced a deal to buy Activision Blizzard for $68.7 billion in January 2022 and completed it on October 13, 2023, for a total purchase price of $75.4 billion, bringing the company under Microsoft Gaming.[^ref-18][^ref-17][^ref-5][^ref-12]

## Sierra Connection

Activision's relationship with Sierra properties spans several decades and contexts:

**Vivendi Games Merger (2008):** The 2008 merger brought [[Publishers/Sierra On-Line|Sierra]] and its properties into Activision Blizzard. Sierra did not continue as a working studio: Wikipedia records that Sierra and other Vivendi Games divisions ceased operations after the merger.[^ref-4][^ref-20] Several Sierra properties were then sold off in 2008–09, including the Leisure Suit Larry IP to Codemasters (February 2009), Evil Genius, Ground Control, Empire Earth, Lords of Magic and Lords of the Realm to Rebellion (July 2009), and The Incredible Machine to Jeff Tunnell's PushButton Labs (October 2009).[^ref-20]

**Sierra Brand Revival (2014):** In August 2014 Activision relaunched Sierra as an indie publishing label. Sierra's website described a "focus on indie studios and innovative, graphically unique games that include new IP and reimaginings of beloved Sierra classics," with Geometry Wars³: Dimensions "planned to be the first game set to launch under the new Sierra indie label" and a King's Quest game coming in 2015.[^ref-19][^ref-20]

**King's Quest Reboot (2015):** Under the revived label, Activision published the episodic *King's Quest* reimagining developed by The Odd Gentlemen (see [[2015 - King's Quest|King's Quest]]). Activision still sells it as King's Quest: The Complete Collection.[^ref-19][^ref-6]

**Dynamix Titles (1989):** Activision published two 1989 games developed by Dynamix, *Deathtrack* and *MechWarrior*, before Sierra bought Dynamix in 1990.[^ref-21]

## History

### Founding and Golden Age (1979-1983)

Activision's founding programmers left Atari after their games had accounted for a large share of Atari's sales while they were refused recognition and royalties.[^ref-1] The company's early success came from titles like *Kaboom!* (1981), its first game to sell over a million units, and *Pitfall!* (1982), which sold more than four million copies.[^ref-1]

### Video Game Crash and Recovery (1983-1991)

The video game crash of 1983 hit Activision hard, forcing diversification into home computer games and the acquisition of text adventure pioneer Infocom in 1986.[^ref-8] The company renamed itself Mediagenic in 1988 and quickly fell into debt.[^ref-1]

### Bobby Kotick Era (1991-2023)

In 1991 Bobby Kotick and partners took control of the struggling Mediagenic: according to Forbes, an investment of only $440,000 left Kotick and three partners owning a third of the company, with Kotick as the new chief executive.[^ref-9] He rebuilt the company and restored the Activision name.[^ref-1] Under Kotick's leadership, Activision acquired numerous studios and built major franchises including Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, Guitar Hero, and Call of Duty.[^ref-1]

### Microsoft Acquisition (2023-Present)

Microsoft completed its acquisition of Activision Blizzard on October 13, 2023, for a total purchase price of $75.4 billion.[^ref-17][^ref-12] Wikipedia describes it as the largest video game acquisition by value.[^ref-5]
