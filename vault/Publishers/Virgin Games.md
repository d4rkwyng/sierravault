# Virgin Games

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Virgin Games (later Virgin Interactive Entertainment) began as the video game publishing division of the Virgin Group. Nick Alexander formed it in London in 1983.[^ref-1][^ref-3] It traded as Virgin Mastertronic from 1988 to 1991 and was renamed Virgin Interactive Entertainment in 1993.[^ref-1] Its US arm, Virgin Games, Inc. (later Virgin Interactive Entertainment, Inc.), was based in Irvine, California.[^ref-2][^ref-28] MobyGames dates the Virgin Games, Inc. name to 1984–1993.[^ref-2] Sega Retro gives a different chronology: Mastertronic opened a US office in Irvine in 1986, and Virgin's 1988 acquisition of Mastertronic brought that office into Virgin's operations.[^ref-29] Virgin Games bought Westwood Associates in 1992[^ref-29][^ref-30] (later Westwood Studios).[^ref-30] Wikipedia lists its owners as Virgin Group (1983–1994), Blockbuster (1994), Spelling Entertainment (1994–1998) and Titus Interactive (1999–2005).[^ref-1] By 1998 the company was a division of Spelling Entertainment Group, with global headquarters in Irvine and international headquarters in London.[^ref-28]

The US operations were sold to Electronic Arts in 1998.[^ref-1][^ref-3] Titus Interactive announced in 1999 that it would buy a 50.1% majority stake in the European company[^ref-1][^ref-3] (Wikipedia's Titus article says Titus acquired shares in Interplay in late 1999, including a stake in Virgin Interactive)[^ref-31] and announced full ownership on 16 April 2001.[^ref-1] Titus renamed it Avalon Interactive in 2003, with the change taking effect on 1 July,[^ref-1][^ref-3][^ref-27] and the UK operations were dissolved by November 2005.[^ref-1]

## Sierra Connection

Virgin Games published titles developed by studios that later became part of Sierra:

**Synergistic Software:** Virgin published *Spirit of Excalibur* (1990, through Virgin Mastertronic in Europe and Virgin Games in the US), *Vengeance of Excalibur* (1991) and *Conan: The Cimmerian* (1991).[^ref-16][^ref-17][^ref-18][^ref-22][^ref-23][^ref-24] Sierra On-Line acquired Synergistic in 1996.[^ref-25]

**Papyrus Design Group:** Virgin Interactive published *[[1993 - IndyCar Racing|IndyCar Racing]]* (1993) in Europe and distributed the US release, which Papyrus Publishing published.[^ref-21] Virgin published *[[1994 - NASCAR Racing|NASCAR Racing]]* (1994) for MS-DOS; MobyGames also lists a US release published by Papyrus Publishing and distributed by Virgin, and another 1994 US release credited jointly to Sierra On-Line and Virgin Interactive Entertainment, Inc.[^ref-19][^ref-20] Papyrus became part of Sierra in 1995.[^ref-26] Computer Games Strategy Plus (February 1996) described Virgin Interactive as Papyrus's then-current distributor when *[[1995 - IndyCar Racing II|IndyCar Racing II]]* (1995) was released.[^ref-33] MobyGames also lists "The White Label" budget reissues, published by Virgin Interactive Entertainment (Europe) Ltd.: *IndyCar Racing* in 1995 (United Kingdom, Germany, France)[^ref-21] and *NASCAR Racing* in 1996 (Germany, France).[^ref-20]

**Distribution:** *[[1995 - Shannara|Shannara]]* (1995), published by Legend Entertainment, was distributed in the United Kingdom, Germany and France by Virgin Interactive Entertainment regional subsidiaries, according to MobyGames.[^ref-32]
