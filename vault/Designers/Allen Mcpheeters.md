# Allen McPheeters

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

L. Allen McPheeters is an American game designer and producer who worked in the games industry from 1987 to about 2005, mostly on sports simulations.[^ref-1][^ref-10] He attended Purdue University, where [[Patrick Cook]], his later collaborator, was a fraternity brother.[^ref-1][^ref-10] With Cook he created Dynamix's Front Page Sports series.[^ref-1][^ref-2] He was lead designer of Front Page Sports: Baseball '94 and co-designer of the real-time strategy game Outpost 2: Divided Destiny.[^ref-7][^ref-13][^ref-18]

His career ran from Sierra On-Line and Cinemaware in the late 1980s, through Dynamix and [[Synergistic Software]] in the 1990s, to Microsoft (1999–2001) and Exile Interactive (2004–2005).[^ref-1][^ref-10]

## Career

### Early Career

McPheeters entered the gaming industry in the summer of 1987 as a junior-level programmer at Sierra On-Line's Oakhurst, California, facility.[^ref-1] He was to port [[1987 - Mixed-Up Mother Goose|Mixed-Up Mother Goose]] from the PC to the Apple IIe. The PC version was not finished until late fall, so in the meantime he designed and programmed demonstration programs for Sierra's fall product line.[^ref-1]

In December 1987, shortly before work on the port was to begin, McPheeters accepted a job with Cinemaware as a QA analyst. His boss there was Patrick Cook.[^ref-1] In his own account he worked there as a play tester from January 1988 to April 1990, and toward the end also began writing user manuals.[^ref-20] At Cinemaware he worked mainly as a tester on ports of the company's first round of products (Defender of the Crown, King of Chicago, SDI, and Sinbad and the Throne of the Falcon) and on original versions of Rocket Ranger, The Three Stooges and Lords of the Rising Sun.[^ref-1] He moved into design by reworking arcade sequences in ports of Sinbad and the Throne of the Falcon, and into user documentation on TV Sports: Football.[^ref-1] His earliest MobyGames credit is a "Special Thanks To" on The Three Stooges (1987), followed by a producer credit on the Atari ST version of Sinbad and the Throne of the Falcon (1988).[^ref-6]

### Dynamix and Sierra (1991–1999)

After two years at Cinemaware, McPheeters grew disillusioned with making games. When he was laid off, he spent nearly a year and a half out of the industry.[^ref-1] A call from Pat Cook in July 1991 brought him back as a game designer at Dynamix, Sierra's Eugene, Oregon, subsidiary.[^ref-1] He later put it as "a little over a year after I left C'ware."[^ref-20]

Cook and McPheeters created the Front Page Sports series, first Football and then Baseball.[^ref-1][^ref-2] MobyGames credits him as assistant director on Front Page Sports: Football (1992), which Cook directed.[^ref-6] His MobyGames biography names Career Play as his most important contribution to the line. Career Play let users play multiple seasons in which players aged and retired and were replaced by rookies.[^ref-1]

Computer Gaming World named Front Page Sports: Football its 1993 Sports Game of the Year in October 1993, crediting "Patrick Cook, designer".[^ref-11] In June 1994 the magazine gave its Premier Sports Game of the Year award to Front Page Sports: Football Pro.[^ref-12][^ref-14] His MobyGames biography goes further, calling Football a four-time CGW Sports Game of the Year winner and a member of the magazine's Hall of Fame.[^ref-1] McPheeters himself put it differently in an interview for Just Adventure: the second version, Football Pro, "is in Computer Gaming World's Hall of Fame, and the Football line won three or four Sports Game of the Year awards."[^ref-20]

McPheeters took the lead in designing Front Page Sports: Baseball '94. CGW's November 1994 review lists "Designer: L. Allen McPheeters".[^ref-1][^ref-13] He later summed up his role in a 1998 Usenet letter reprinted by IGN: "I was the lead designer for Front Page Sports: Baseball '94, and I worked with Patrick Cook on the design for the original Front Page Sports: Football."[^ref-7]

In summer 1995 he accepted an offer from Viacom New Media and spent six months at the Chicago-based development house, though no products came of his time there.[^ref-1] He later called it "a short stint with Viacom NewMedia in '95."[^ref-20] The following February he rejoined Cook at Dynamix to work on [[1997 - Outpost 2 - Divided Destiny|Outpost 2: Divided Destiny]], on which MobyGames lists him among the designers and as a documentation writer.[^ref-1][^ref-18] Of Cook he said, "we both designed Outpost 2: Divided Destiny."[^ref-20] A 16 March 1997 tech-tree file from the game's pre-release demo, now reproduced as an example on the Outpost 2 community wiki, carries the header note "NOTE: IMPORTANT!!! PLEASE NOTIFY ALLEN MCPHEETERS OF ANY CHANGES TO THIS FILE."[^ref-3]

After Outpost 2 shipped, he worked on help-file localization for [[1997 - Front Page Sports - Ski Racing|Front Page Sports: Ski Racing]] and [[1997 - Red Baron II|Red Baron II]]. In February 1998 he transferred to Sierra's Renton, Washington-based Synergistic Software.[^ref-1] There he was producer of Sierra Sports: Baseball PRO 2000. In 1998 Sierra president Dave Grenewetzki "asked the tough questions and concluded that it was time to pull the plug."[^ref-7] Sierra and Rainbow Studios had begun the new Baseball PRO in summer 1997, first planning a rewrite on Rainbow's 3D engine and then deciding to reuse as much Baseball PRO '98 code as possible.[^ref-7] McPheeters explained and defended the decision publicly. His letter said "we badly underestimated how difficult it would be to have a new development team take over legacy code from the previous editions of the game."[^ref-7] It also said: "As the original designer of the game, I hate the decision—I want to see people playing this game. As a producer, and as a stockholder, I think it was the right decision."[^ref-7] He added: "Sierra has taken some criticism in the past about releasing buggy or mediocre products. Canceling this game, painful though it is, is a step in the right direction."[^ref-7]

Synergistic's [[CXL - Front Page Sports - Football Pro '99|NFL Football Pro '99]] shipped and was quickly recalled. Sierra issued the recall notice on January 21, 1999.[^ref-15][^ref-1] Most of the Synergistic staff, McPheeters included, was laid off in February 1999.[^ref-1]

### Microsoft and Exile (1999–2005)

In July 1999 Microsoft hired McPheeters as a game designer for Microsoft Baseball 2001.[^ref-1] His own later profile lists his Microsoft role as program manager, 1999–2001.[^ref-10] The game merged two code bases, Microsoft Baseball 2000 and Baseball Mogul, adding Mogul's season and career features to what had been an arcade-style game. He called it "about a 6-7 month project. In some ways, it was a miracle that it shipped at all."[^ref-9][^ref-19]

According to his MobyGames biography, sales were disappointing and Microsoft moved from Windows to Xbox development. McPheeters then became program manager for a new baseball product with an external developer, which eventually shipped as MLB Inside Pitch. He left that project after ten months because the role was not a good fit, and in August 2001 he left Microsoft and the game industry.[^ref-1]

In March 2004 the Canadian developer Exile Interactive hired him as senior designer on Nintendo Pennant Chase Baseball for the GameCube.[^ref-1][^ref-10] Nintendo announced the game in January 2005 for an April 4 release.[^ref-16] It was then delayed "indefinitely" and never released. Exile's senior engineer later said it had been "completed and passed submission."[^ref-17]

## Notable Works

### Front Page Sports: Football (1992)

McPheeters co-created the series with Cook and is credited as assistant director on the first game.[^ref-1][^ref-6] CGW named it the 1993 Sports Game of the Year.[^ref-11]

### Front Page Sports: Baseball '94 (1994)

[[1994 - Front Page Sports - Baseball Pro|Front Page Sports: Baseball '94]] was McPheeters' lead-design credit.[^ref-6][^ref-13] It brought the Front Page Sports approach to baseball.[^ref-1]

### Outpost 2: Divided Destiny (1997)

On [[1997 - Outpost 2 - Divided Destiny|Outpost 2: Divided Destiny]], a Dynamix real-time strategy game, he shares the design credit with Cook and also wrote documentation.[^ref-1][^ref-18][^ref-6]

### Microsoft Baseball 2001 (2000)

At Microsoft he designed Microsoft Baseball 2001, combining Baseball Mogul's career play with Microsoft's existing arcade baseball game.[^ref-1][^ref-9][^ref-19]
