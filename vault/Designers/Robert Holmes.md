# Robert Holmes

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Robert Holmes is a composer and producer best known for the music of the Gabriel Knight adventure games, the series created by [[Jane Jensen]].[^ref-5][^ref-10] At Sierra On-Line he was producer and composer on [[1993 - Gabriel Knight - Sins of the Fathers|Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers]] (1993) and wrote the main themes for its two sequels.[^ref-5][^ref-4] He grew up in California, played in bands around Los Angeles[^ref-11] and studied Film Scoring and Cinema History at UCLA.[^ref-5]

MobyGames credits him on 22 games and lists 19 games on which both he and Jensen are credited.[^ref-3] He is married to Jensen.[^ref-21][^ref-25] In 2012 the two launched the adventure studio Pinkerton Road.[^ref-21]

## Career

### Early Career

Holmes started out as a rock musician. His first paid gig was as a duo, "me and a drummer," who knew about six songs:  "We were paid $35 and we thought we had hit the big time."[^ref-5] When "a big record deal was not coming soon," he took day work as an ADR engineer in film post-production.[^ref-5] Among his favourite songwriters and composers he lists Harry Nilsson, Elton John, George Harrison, Dimitri Tiomkin, Max Steiner, John Barry, Tony Banks and Rick Wakeman.[^ref-5]

### Sierra On-Line

Holmes joined Sierra's music department after he "called up Mark Seibert and asked if he could use anyone with a music/production/composing background"; Seibert gave him a slot.[^ref-5] He worked as a composer on some Space Quest and King's Quest projects under Seibert.[^ref-11]

After a couple of years he became a producer. His first producer assignment was [[1993 - Hoyle Classic Card Games|Hoyle Classic Card Games]] (1993), on which he was credited as Producer, Director and Composer.[^ref-5][^ref-4] He was then assigned as producer on a project Jensen was designing; it was cancelled, but "by the time Gabriel Knight was being pitched as her next project we were a couple, and I was slotted as both the producer and composer."[^ref-5][^ref-11] He also voiced the Writing Man in [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]], and was producer and director of the King's Quest Collector's Edition (1994) and King's Quest Collection Series (1997).[^ref-10][^ref-4]

For [[1995 - The Beast Within - A Gabriel Knight Mystery|The Beast Within: A Gabriel Knight Mystery]] (1995) he wrote the main themes and the opera sequences.[^ref-4] He called being asked to write "a 'lost Wagnerian Opera'" "an exciting and elevating challenge!"[^ref-11] To prepare, "we took a trip to Germany, Vienna, and Bavaria," he recalled, to see as many operas as possible: "I remember going to Wagner's grave and apologizing for what I was about to do. But it worked out in the end."[^ref-5] On [[1999 - Gabriel Knight 3 - Blood of the Sacred, Blood of the Damned|Gabriel Knight 3: Blood of the Sacred, Blood of the Damned]] (1999), the music was by David Henry and Holmes, with Holmes credited for the main themes. Reviewer Ingrid Heyn wrote that "the sense of series cohesion between the games was due at least partly to the consistent musical treatment as well as the strong narrative connection."[^ref-9][^ref-4]

### After Sierra

Holmes scored the casual games Inspector Parker (2003) and Inspector Parker in Betrapped! (2004), the latter with Ashif Hakik.[^ref-10][^ref-4] He wrote the song "Young Goodman Brown" for Charlaine Harris: Dying for Daylight (2011), with Raleigh Holmes, Buck Wilcox and Tom Chan.[^ref-10]

He composed the music for Jensen's [[2010 - Gray Matter|Gray Matter]] (2010), "both as composer of the general score and as a musician with his band The Scarlet Furies."[^ref-11] MobyGames credits him for the game's music, the Scarlet Furies music and the song "To Forgive or to Forget".[^ref-4] He described the band's songs as "Gothic Folk": he, Buck Wilcox, Ian Francisco and Steve Hilstein had played together since school, and later re-formed the band around lead singer Raleigh Holmes. Its EP *Dark Clad Company* was produced by Rusty Anderson.[^ref-11][^ref-2] Raleigh Holmes said in 2012 that Robert Holmes "really spearheaded the core feel of all of the music in Gray Matter."[^ref-22] For Phoenix Online's [[2012 - Cognition - An Erica Reed Thriller|Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller]] he wrote one song, "The Taking."[^ref-4][^ref-10] Through Pinkerton Road he was executive producer and composer on [[2014 - Moebius - Empire Rising|Moebius: Empire Rising]] and [[2014 - Gabriel Knight - Sins of the Fathers – 20th Anniversary Edition|Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers – 20th Anniversary Edition]] (both 2014), and scored Lola and Lucy's Big Adventure.[^ref-4][^ref-2]

He has since produced albums of game themes, including *A Simple Refrain*, solo-piano versions of his themes played by Roger Hooper, and *Sequel*, an album of new themes "in the style of Gabriel Knight" launched on Kickstarter in late 2021.[^ref-7][^ref-15][^ref-20][^ref-6] By January 2023 the CD and vinyl editions were out, and he named Troels Pleimert (drums), Marco Machera (fretless bass) and Roger Hooper (piano and string arrangements) as guests on the album.[^ref-29] His Bandcamp page also lists *Second Cousin of SEQUEL* (Robert Holmes with George Napier), released October 4, 2024,[^ref-28] and *A Simple Refrain II* (Roger Hooper and Robert Holmes), a 14-track album released November 5, 2026.[^ref-27] A second Kickstarter, for *Son of Sequel*, was running in April 2023;[^ref-23] the album was released in April 2024 with 20 tracks and "an incredible reunion of The Scarlet Furies."[^ref-24] Jensen wrote a new Gabriel Knight short story, "Five Hearts," as a reward for the campaign's backers.[^ref-25][^ref-26] He has talked about his game music in video interviews with Adventure Game Hotspot and the Adventure Games Podcast.[^ref-17][^ref-18]

## Notable Works

### Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers (1993)

Holmes was producer and composer on the first Gabriel Knight.[^ref-4][^ref-5] Ingrid Heyn wrote that the music "echoed with the sombre awe of the Cathedral of the New Orleans square to the throbbing animalistic ritual beat of the secret meetings in the swamp."[^ref-9]

### The Beast Within: A Gabriel Knight Mystery (1995)

Holmes was "again called on to compose considerable portions of a 'lost' Wagner opera, which would play a pivotal role in the narrative," Heyn wrote: "Brooding and evocative, the music of the game helped to add to the patina of fascination which the game imparted."[^ref-9]

### Gray Matter (2010)

Asked about his favourite piece in Gray Matter, Holmes said: "The main theme is my favourite. It has a great melancholy nature to it, I'm a sucker for very emotional dark themes."[^ref-11]

## Design Philosophy

Holmes describes a deliberate method: "I'm a big believer in the few and the critical for creative projects. This means when I start anything, especially musical, I purposely create a set of rules, or 'legs on the table' that put me in a box as the creator. This means I usually pick four or five elements or textures, or instruments that will make up the musical language for that project."[^ref-5]

On working with Jensen: "It's important that the musical approach is working as another character in the story, so I like to understand Jane's goals and approach when working on it."[^ref-11] His favourite album is YES's *Close to the Edge*: "I want to hear it when I'm on my way off the planet."[^ref-5]

## Legacy

Author Ilana C. Myer wrote, for his Sequel Kickstarter: "Robert Holmes wove an epic tapestry for his soundtrack to the Gabriel Knight series, combining themes of setting and character with operatic themes that transcend even these. Jane Jensen's games are supernatural mysteries with colorful characters, but are also more: Each is a sweeping, emotionally charged battle for the protagonist's soul."[^ref-15]

Fans still write about his music. One Bandcamp listener wrote that "Robert Holmes accompanied my dreams and nightmares through the amazing world of adventure games," and recalled using the Gabriel Knight theme as a ringtone.[^ref-6]
