# Carlos Escobar

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Juan Carlos Escobar, usually credited as Carlos Escobar, was a programmer at Sierra On-Line in the late 1980s and early 1990s.[^ref-1] MobyGames credits him on 19 games,[^ref-12] and other credit lists add his Apple IIGS work on King's Quest I and his programming on Leisure Suit Larry 2, for a total of about twenty titles.[^ref-6][^ref-13] He was lead programmer on Mixed-Up Mother Goose, Pepper's Adventures in Time and Leisure Suit Larry 6.[^ref-11] Steve Conrad, who worked with him on Leisure Suit Larry 5, called him "Al Lowe's right hand man."[^ref-13][^ref-14]

Colleagues remembered him for his dry wit. Fellow programmer Robert Lindsley made a batch file that displayed a random "Carlos says" quote when a computer restarted, and Doug Oldfield later turned it into a program that "floated around the company for years".[^ref-3]

Escobar died on July 4, 2011.[^ref-1][^ref-5]

## Career

### Sierra On-Line (late 1980s–1995)

Al Lowe recalled in 2011: "As I recall, Carlos started at Sierra by working with me on LSL2: Leisure Suit Larry Goes Looking for Love (in Several Wrong Places), often in my home. We eventually got a small office near the Fresno airport. He and I were together for many years and games thereafter."[^ref-13] Lowe lists him on LSL2, LSL3, LSL5 and LSL6.[^ref-3]

Escobar also did Apple ports. The Apple IIGS version of [[1984 - King's Quest - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest I: Quest for the Crown]], released in April 1988, credits "Apple IIgs Version: Juan Carlos Escobar" and "Apple IIgs Version: Al Lowe".[^ref-6] He is credited with the Apple II and Apple IIgs versions of [[1987 - Space Quest II - Vohaul's Revenge|Space Quest II: Vohaul's Revenge]],[^ref-15][^ref-11] and with the Apple version of [[1987 - Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards|Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards]] (Apple IIgs, 1988).[^ref-11][^ref-12][^ref-18]

On the Leisure Suit Larry series he is credited with programming on [[1989 - Leisure Suit Larry III - Passionate Patti in Pursuit of the Pulsating Pectorals|LSL3]] and [[1991 - Leisure Suit Larry 5 - Passionate Patti Does a Little Undercover Work|LSL5]], and with lead programming on [[1993 - Leisure Suit Larry 6 - Shape Up or Slip Out!|LSL6]].[^ref-11] Mark Hood recalled that "Carlos also memorialized me forever in LSL3 as 'Mark the plumber'", placing the grossest sound effects Al would allow every time that character bent over.[^ref-13]

He was among the programmers of [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V]] (1990).[^ref-9][^ref-11] On [[1992 - King's Quest VI - Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow|King's Quest VI]] (1992), whose lead programmer was Robert Lindsley,[^ref-8] Escobar is credited with additional programming.[^ref-10][^ref-16]

His other credits run from Code-Name: Iceman (additional programming on the Amiga version) and Conquests of the Longbow (additional programming) to Space Quest IV, the VGA remakes of Police Quest and Quest for Glory I, Freddy Pharkas (special quest programming), the CD version of Police Quest: Open Season, Slater & Charlie Go Camping, King's Quest VII and Phantasmagoria.[^ref-11][^ref-12]

### The "Carlos Says" Legacy

Al Lowe wrote that Escobar "came up with so many funny lines that fellow programmer Robert Lindsley created a batch file that many of us ran in our autoexec.bat files. It would display a random quote from Carlos every time we restarted our computers (which was quite often back in those days!)." It was "later turned into a program by Doug Oldfield which floated around the company for years, always bringing a smile to those who knew and loved Carlos."[^ref-3]

"Carlos Says" quotes collected on Al Lowe's site include:[^ref-3]
- "I'm sorry I'm doing such a poor job of pretending I'm listening."
- "You must have mistaken me for someone who cares."
- "There's a fine line between my friends… and the people I get stuck working with."
- "It's not magic, it's just code!"
- "I think the problem is between the chair and the keyboard."
- "Why be nice when you can be honest?"

## Notable Works

### King's Quest I: Quest for the Crown - Apple IIGS (1988)

The Apple IIGS port of [[1984 - King's Quest - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest I]], released in April 1988, credits Escobar and Al Lowe for the Apple IIgs version.[^ref-6]

### Leisure Suit Larry Series (1988–1993)

Escobar started at Sierra on [[1988 - Leisure Suit Larry Goes Looking for Love (in Several Wrong Places)|LSL2]], working with Al Lowe,[^ref-13] and went on to program [[1989 - Leisure Suit Larry III - Passionate Patti in Pursuit of the Pulsating Pectorals|LSL3]] and [[1991 - Leisure Suit Larry 5 - Passionate Patti Does a Little Undercover Work|LSL5]] and lead the programming of [[1993 - Leisure Suit Larry 6 - Shape Up or Slip Out!|LSL6]].[^ref-3][^ref-11] He also did the Apple IIgs version of [[1987 - Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards|LSL1]].[^ref-11] Lowe said Escobar "contributed much of the silliness in those games."[^ref-13]

### Mixed-Up Mother Goose (1991) and Pepper's Adventures in Time (1993)

MobyGames credits Escobar as lead programmer on the SCI [[1990 - Mixed-Up Mother Goose (SCI Remake)|Mixed-Up Mother Goose]] and on [[1993 - Pepper's Adventures in Time|Pepper's Adventures in Time]].[^ref-11]

### King's Quest VI: Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow (1992)

Escobar is credited with additional programming on [[1992 - King's Quest VI - Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow|King's Quest VI]], alongside Doug Oldfield, under lead programmer Robert Lindsley.[^ref-8][^ref-16]

### Phantasmagoria (1995)

[[1995 - Phantasmagoria|Phantasmagoria]], Roberta Williams' live-action horror adventure, is his latest MobyGames credit (Programming).[^ref-12]

## Legacy

When news of his death spread in 2011, former Sierra colleagues posted memories of him. Josh Mandel wrote: "I loved Carlos. Really loved him."[^ref-13]

Bridget McKenna recalled: "He was such a character, and so much a part of our everyday life at Sierra. During our Sierra days, Marti and I wrote a story and put a character in it–a programmer–who said things like 'They don't pay me enough to think.' That was one of our favorite 'Carlos says' lines. Carlos, you could always make us laugh, and that was–and is–valuable beyond measure."[^ref-13][^ref-14]
