# Pablo Ghenis

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

Pablo Ghenis is a programmer who worked on the development system for Sierra On-Line's [[Sierra Creative Interpreter|SCI]] (Sierra Creative Interpreter) in the engine's early years.[^ref-1] MobyGames credits him on 22 games, nearly all for interpreter or development-system work, plus programming credits on Codename: ICEMAN and Conquests of the Longbow.[^ref-16][^ref-21]

His credits run from 1988 to 1991, from King's Quest IV and Leisure Suit Larry 2 through the VGA releases of King's Quest V and Space Quest IV.[^ref-15][^ref-16]

## Career

### Sierra On-Line (credits 1988–1991)

At Sierra, Ghenis worked on the interpreter and development system that Sierra's designers and programmers used to build [[Sierra Creative Interpreter|SCI]] games.[^ref-1] His most frequent collaborators were fellow system programmers [[Jeff Stephenson]] and [[Bob Heitman|Robert Eric Heitman]] (22 games each), [[Ken Williams]] (20) and [[Corey Cole]] (17).[^ref-16]

His development-system credits cover titles in the King's Quest, Space Quest, Police Quest, Leisure Suit Larry and Quest for Glory series.[^ref-16] On Leisure Suit Larry Goes Looking for Love (1988), the manual lists him under "Game Development System" with Jeff Stephenson, Robert Heitman and Stuart Goldstein.[^ref-14][^ref-6] He also has a PC-9801 conversion credit on Police Quest 2.[^ref-15] The printed manuals for Leisure Suit Larry 3, Police Quest 2 and Conquests of Camelot also list him among the development-system credits.[^ref-27][^ref-29][^ref-28]

Beyond systems work, Ghenis was one of three programmers on [[1990 - Codename - Iceman|Codename: ICEMAN]], with J. Mark Hood and [[Doug Oldfield]], on [[Jim Walls]]'s game; Sierra News Magazine's Autumn 1989 issue and the game's manual both list Ghenis, Mark Hood and Doug Oldfield as the game's programmers.[^ref-5][^ref-13][^ref-17] He is also credited with programming on Conquests of the Longbow (1991).[^ref-16][^ref-21]

In an interview in the same Autumn 1989 issue, Sierra programmer Stuart Goldstein said: "Pablo Ghenis, another Sierra programmer, and I are writing a sequencer program for Sierra to market."[^ref-24] Whether that sequencer program ever shipped is unverified; no source found records its release.

## Notable Works

### King's Quest V: Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder (1990)

On [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V]], Ghenis is one of ten people credited for the development system, with Dan Foy, Eric Hart, John Hartin, Robert Eric Heitman, Corey Cole, J. Mark Hood, John Rettig, Larry Scott and Jeff Stephenson.[^ref-7][^ref-18]

### Quest for Glory II: Trial by Fire (1990)

On [[1990 - Quest for Glory II - Trial by Fire|Quest for Glory II]], Ghenis is credited under "Interpreter / Development System."[^ref-8]

### Codename: ICEMAN (1990)

On [[1990 - Codename - Iceman|Codename: ICEMAN]], Ghenis is credited both as one of the game's three programmers and as part of its development-system team.[^ref-5][^ref-13][^ref-17]

### Space Quest IV: Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers (1991)

On [[1991 - Space Quest IV - Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers|Space Quest IV]], Ghenis is credited as part of the "Development Team."[^ref-9]

### Hoyle Official Book of Games Series (1989–1990)

On the Hoyle card game series, Ghenis worked on development systems. On Volume 1 he is credited under "Development System" with Stuart Goldstein, Robert Eric Heitman and Jeff Stephenson;[^ref-20] on Volume 2 he is credited alongside Jeff Stephenson, Robert Eric Heitman, John Hartin, Dan Foy, John Rettig, Corinna Abdul and Corey Cole.[^ref-10]

## Legacy

Ghenis shares the credits of all 22 of his games with fellow system programmers Jeff Stephenson and Robert Eric Heitman, a record of the small team behind the interpreter and tools of Sierra's early SCI releases.[^ref-16]
