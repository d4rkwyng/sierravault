# Douglas Herring

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Douglas Herring is a self-taught artist who worked at Sierra On-Line from 1988 to 1991, then as a freelancer, and then for nearly nine years as an art director at Microsoft Game Studios.[^ref-1][^ref-2] At Sierra he was the animator and, with Gerald Moore, one of the two artists on [[1989 - The Colonel's Bequest|The Colonel's Bequest]], one of the artists on [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V]], and co-director, with Scott Murphy, of the VGA remake of [[1991 - Space Quest I - Roger Wilco in the Sarien Encounter|Space Quest I]].[^ref-11][^ref-12][^ref-13] His own résumé describes his King's Quest V role as "Team Leader - final Art Director".[^ref-2]

A lifelong lover of science fiction and fantasy, Herring also worked on [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot]] and [[1990 - Quest for Glory II - Trial by Fire|Quest for Glory II: Trial by Fire]], where by his account he designed combat systems as well as art.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-3] After leaving Sierra in October 1991 he freelanced on games including Shannara for Legend Entertainment, and from November 1995 to October 2004 he was an art director at Microsoft Game Studios, working with franchises including Age of Empires, Close Combat and Fable, about sixteen years in games in all.[^ref-2][^ref-3]

## Career

### Early Career

According to his résumé, Herring was Art Director at KIXE-TV, the PBS affiliate in Redding, California, from August 1982 to May 1988.[^ref-2] His duties there included graphic design, set design and construction, photography, camera and lighting work, and editing. He specialized in real-time character generator work for live productions such as sports events, parades, auctions and pledge breaks, and directed projects including contract productions for the State of California.[^ref-2]

He has also been a working illustrator since high school, in mediums from watercolor and ink to oils and sculpture. His résumé lists past clients including Kimberly-Clark Corporation, The Comics Journal, Comic Buyers' Guide, Marvel Comics, Malibu Publishing and Electronic Arts.[^ref-2] "I have had little formal training as an artist, just have always drawn," he said in 2010. "My first computer was an Atari ST and it was my demo disk, done in Degas Elite on the Atari, that cemented my job at Sierra."[^ref-1]

### Sierra Years (1988–1991)

Herring joined Sierra On-Line in June 1988.[^ref-2] "Sierra had just moved its offices into the big new metal building that brought all aspects of what they did into one place," he recalled. "Sierra was growing, as was the computer game market in general, and there was a lot of pressure to remain number one."[^ref-1] He had worked on about 15% of the pictures and animation of [[1988 - Leisure Suit Larry Goes Looking for Love (in Several Wrong Places)|Leisure Suit Larry II]] before being given the art of a murder mystery designed by Roberta Williams, which became [[1989 - The Colonel's Bequest|The Colonel's Bequest]].[^ref-2][^ref-3]

The Colonel's Bequest credits him for "Animation" and, with Gerald Moore, for "Graphics / Artwork"; his résumé describes the role as "Art Designer - lead artist and sole animator."[^ref-11][^ref-2] Sierra's own InterAction magazine listed "Graphics: Douglas Herring, Jerry Moore" in its Fall 1989 issue.[^ref-5][^ref-4]

On [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot: The Search for the Grail]] he is credited among the animators and background artists; his résumé adds "In-house Art Designer - lead animator - endgame combat system."[^ref-15][^ref-2] For [[1990 - Quest for Glory II - Trial by Fire|Quest for Glory II: Trial by Fire]] his résumé lists art design for the desert section and the combat system, and the Adventure Game Fan Fair biography says he "designed the combat system and a few backgrounds."[^ref-2][^ref-3]

On [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V: Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder!]], released for the 1990 holiday season, MobyGames lists him among the game's artists, and the Fan Fair biography says he "led an animation team."[^ref-12][^ref-3][^ref-2] His résumé calls his role "Team Leader - final Art Director" and describes the game as "The best selling Adventure Game of all time" and the "First major VGA game published."[^ref-2] Those are his own descriptions; Wikipedia puts the game's sales at over half a million copies.[^ref-8]

He was co-director of the VGA remake of [[1991 - Space Quest I - Roger Wilco in the Sarien Encounter|Space Quest I: Roger Wilco in the Sarien Encounter]] with Scott Murphy, and its art designer and one of its animators.[^ref-13][^ref-9] He calls it his favorite: "Mark Crow[e] and Scott Murphy had done a wonderfully fun job with the original and it was a great honor to be allowed to re-draw and add to their work."[^ref-1] His résumé also lists art design and animation on [[1991 - Mixed-Up Fairy Tales|Mixed-Up Fairy Tales]] and [[1991 - Castle of Dr. Brain|The Castle of Dr. Brain]], and preliminary storyboards for [[1992 - Quest for Glory III - Wages of War|Quest for Glory III: Wages of War]] and [[1991 - EcoQuest - The Search for Cetus|EcoQuest: The Search for Cetus]], before he left Sierra in October 1991.[^ref-2]

### Freelance Period (1991–1995)

His first freelance project was Ringworld: Revenge of the Patriarch (1992) for Tsunami Games, based on Larry Niven's Known Space novels. He was game designer and art designer, but left the project early: "most of my design was re-worked. However, 90% of the art was done under my direction."[^ref-2]

He was then art director on Star Trek: Deep Space Nine – The Hunt for Paramount Interactive and Tachyon, doing "total art design, animation and background key frames and layout." In his words, "Art was 95% done and I had already moved on when Paramount put the project on hold."[^ref-2] Other freelance work listed on his résumé includes the user interface screens for Spectrum Holobyte's MIG-29 module for Falcon 3.0, creature animation for Free Fall Associates' Archon Ultra, and art direction and co-design on the Sega CD version of Spider-Man vs. Kingpin.[^ref-2]

His last adventure game was Shannara (1995) for Legend Entertainment, based on Terry Brooks's novels, on which he was art director.[^ref-2][^ref-3] His résumé calls it Legend's "best selling game."[^ref-2] "Legend Entertainment, which I did 'Shannara' for, were just a great group of people. Easily my most enjoyable experience as a freelancer, the developer was on the east coast and it all went very, very smoothly," he said.[^ref-1]

### Microsoft Game Studios (1995–2004)

Herring was an art director at Microsoft Game Studios from November 1995 to October 2004.[^ref-2] At first he managed a core group of "rover artists" supporting external titles, as well as large internal teams.[^ref-2]

On Age of Empires he was internal art director and content lead, working with Ensemble Studios before Microsoft acquired it; his work included the technology trees, set-up art and manual art, and on Age of Empires II he spent time in Dallas helping Ensemble re-engineer its animation pipeline.[^ref-2] On the three Close Combat games published with Atomic Games he was content lead on Microsoft's side, managing the user education team. He wrote and edited the videos in Close Combat I, produced the videos for Close Combat II and III, and directed the internal team that made the maps for Close Combat II.[^ref-2]

As senior art director he was the art representative in four acquisitions (FASA, Bungie, Ensemble Studios and Digital Anvil), leveled incoming artists, and wrote the game artist career ladder used by Microsoft's HR.[^ref-2] After a 2001 reorganization moved internal development out of his studio, he led a small team supporting externally developed titles including Fable, Rise of Nations, Tao Feng, Blinx and Kakuto Chojin.[^ref-2]

He has been frank about Microsoft's approach. "In a total programmer-centric environment, the tools were rarely if ever locked down early," he said. "In those later years I would repeat over and over that you are still in pre-production until your tools are locked down and was generally ignored." He added that Microsoft "simply did not understand that the process for making games is not the same as the one they had used for decades on productivity software."[^ref-1]

### After Microsoft

Since leaving Microsoft in October 2004, Herring has pursued art, sculpture (including fantasy helmets) and photography.[^ref-1][^ref-2] In 2018 he was credited among the Kickstarter backers of [[2018 - Hero-U - Rogue to Redemption|Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption]], by his former Sierra colleagues Corey and Lori Ann Cole.[^ref-6][^ref-14][^ref-16]

## Notable Works

### The Colonel's Bequest (1989)

Herring animated the game and drew its graphics with Gerald Moore.[^ref-11][^ref-4] One retrospective review, George Starostin's, says that "the game's artists, Doug Herring and Gerald Moore, seem to have invested much more personality into its characters than Roberta herself."[^ref-7]

### King's Quest V (1990)

MobyGames lists Herring among the game's artists; his own résumé calls him "Team Leader - final Art Director."[^ref-12][^ref-2] The game was the first King's Quest to use VGA graphics and a point-and-click interface.[^ref-8]

### Space Quest I VGA (1991)

Herring co-directed the 1991 remake with Scott Murphy and was its art designer and one of its animators.[^ref-13] He calls it "by far my favorite Sierra game."[^ref-1]

### Conquests of Camelot (1990)

MobyGames credits Herring as an animator and background artist on [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot: The Search for the Grail]]. His résumé describes him as in-house art designer and lead animator, and says he designed the endgame combat system.[^ref-15][^ref-2]

## Design Philosophy

"In the end it all comes down to the people you are working with, and there have been some great ones, in both the freelance and in-house development situations," Herring said of his career.[^ref-1]

He was critical of large-company management: "The more corporate a company becomes the more employees become mere ciphers in the minds of management; and the ability to judge, hire, and encourage the right kind of creative people just gets lost in the shuffle."[^ref-1] He also stressed locking down tools early: "you are still in pre-production until your tools are locked down."[^ref-1]

On his own work, he keeps a large reference collection: "you can never have enough reference in my opinion."[^ref-2]
