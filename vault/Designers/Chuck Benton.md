# Chuck Benton

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Chuck Benton (credited as Charles Benton on some releases) is an American programmer from Massachusetts who wrote *Softporn Adventure* (1981), the text adventure that Sierra later remade as *Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards*.[^ref-5][^ref-1][^ref-19] Sierra (then On-Line Systems) published the game, which the Sierra fan wiki notes is the only game Sierra published without graphics.[^ref-2] Benton said his goal was "to make every space have multiple features and ideally to make the game lean toward being an interactive story as opposed to a puzzle."[^ref-3]

He then worked as a contract programmer for Sierra in the early 1980s, porting and programming Frogger, Crossfire, BC's Quest for Tires, Donald Duck's Playground and other titles.[^ref-4][^ref-3] He was never a Sierra employee: "At the time, I was an independent contractor and worked out of Maine, and every couple of months I'd fly out and deliver the next product."[^ref-3] He later founded Technology Systems, Inc. (TSI), which worked mainly on simulation and networking projects for the military.[^ref-16][^ref-3]

## Career

### Early Career

Benton grew up in central Massachusetts.[^ref-15][^ref-5] By his own account he left college after six weeks, then spent two years studying aviation at the University of Illinois, where he got his pilot's license.[^ref-3][^ref-15] He had done "a little bit of coding on an IBM 360 back in my failed college attempt," and later worked as a field engineer on card-reader equipment.[^ref-3] He then worked for a flight simulator company for two and a half years.[^ref-3]

There, he said, "I talked the company into buying me an Apple, saying I'd write software that would automate all the paperwork that I had to manage."[^ref-3][^ref-17] He wrote *Softporn Adventure* on it "as an exercise in learning how to do coding, database management and so forth," and explained the premise: "Being a single 27 year old male, I decided to make the objective to get laid three times."[^ref-3] His first text adventure had been Scott Adams's *Pirate Adventure*, and he followed "Scott's screen layout and the two word parsing."[^ref-3] The game was first called "Super Stud Adventure" and was soon renamed; he sold it under his own label, Blue Sky Software.[^ref-5]

After *Softporn*, Ken Williams asked him to do "a new version of Frogger on the Atari 8-bit"; Benton already knew assembly language and "just had to learn my way around the Atari."[^ref-3] He then wrote the Commodore 64 version before the machine's release, working from "a dot matrix printout" of Commodore's draft manual.[^ref-3]

He also wrote Jump Jet (1982) for the Apple II, which he "authored … myself" and licensed to a company he met through AppleFest; it was distributed by Avant-Garde Creations.[^ref-3][^ref-9]

### Sierra Years and Contract Work

Benton met Ken Williams at a trade show where he was selling *Softporn Adventure* "in little baggies, including an instruction pamphlet"; No Dice No Glory places the meeting at the 1981 Boston AppleFest.[^ref-3][^ref-10] Williams licensed the game, and Benton said the royalties eventually exceeded his salary at the flight simulator company.[^ref-3][^ref-5]

His contract work for Sierra included:

- **Frogger** (1982–1983): MobyGames credits him on the Atari 8-bit (1982) and Commodore 64 (1983) versions.[^ref-4] Retro365 describes his Atari work as an upgrade of John Harris's version that added a two-player option, released in 1983.[^ref-18] Lemon64 credits "Charles Benton" as creator and musician of the C64 version.[^ref-20]
- **BC's Quest for Tires** (1983–1984): Programmer of the Atari 8-bit and Commodore 64 versions; Lemon64 lists "Charles Benton" as coder of the C64 release.[^ref-4][^ref-21] He told The New Yorker, "I caught up with their programmers and so began designing the second half of the game myself, even though it wasn't part of the contract."[^ref-17] Get Lamp's notes add that it was meant to be a port of the ColecoVision version and that he ended up composing the latter part of the game himself.[^ref-5] He said it "won some magazine's 'bug of the month' award," because at one spot "your character would actually land in the code as opposed to on the screen."[^ref-3]
- **Crossfire** (1983): Programmer of the Commodore 64 version, for which Lemon64 also credits him with the music.[^ref-4][^ref-19] Benton recalled, "I did the Commodore 64 and Atari versions I believe," adding that the game "requires a trance-like concentration."[^ref-3]
- **Wizard of Id's WizType and WizMath** (1984): Programmer of both Apple II titles.[^ref-4]
- **Donald Duck's Playground** (1984): MobyGames credits him with the Commodore 64 program; Benton himself said, "I think it was for the Atari and the Apple, but I'm not sure – it's over 40 years ago."[^ref-4][^ref-3] He called it the last game he did for Sierra, recalling "a panel of seven Disney child psychologists" and "about fifteen people assigned to me in total."[^ref-3]
- **The American Challenge: A Sailing Simulation** (1986): IBM programming.[^ref-4] Because the PC lacked a math co-processor, he "ended up having to write a floating point mathematics library from scratch."[^ref-3]

By Benton's account, *Softporn* "had run its course and was discontinued" three years after release; Wikipedia says it was withdrawn after a few months.[^ref-3][^ref-1] Ken Williams then asked to license the story to create Leisure Suit Larry, offering "a one percent royalty on it or … a check for 5000 dollars."[^ref-3] Benton took the check because of a tax bill of about $4,000, and later reflected, "there were years where it was doing like 35 million a year, so I could have been living on a boat in the Caribbean if I'd taken the one percent."[^ref-3]

Sierra released *Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards* in 1987; MobyGames credits Benton with its design ("Designed by" or "Original game design by", depending on the version).[^ref-4] No Dice No Glory reports that the Larry franchise had sold more than 1.4 million units by March 1996.[^ref-10] Benton said he retained the copyright to *Softporn Adventure* and has "a letter from Ken Williams saying Softporn is all mine."[^ref-3]

### Later Career

Benton's 2014 biography for a U.S. House committee hearing says that "in 1987, Chuck was lured back into the simulation business by the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency, at which time he formed Technology Systems, Inc."; Get Lamp's timeline dates TSI's start to 1985.[^ref-16][^ref-5] The New Yorker says DARPA approached him to create training games for soldiers, work that led to the SIMNET project.[^ref-17] Benton's 2003 convention keynote biography said he "helped launch the PC industry, broke new ground in the development of network protocols, and has fueled technical innovations that have produced broad and far reaching impacts in national capability."[^ref-5]

At TSI he worked on training simulators for the military and, he says, "the first large-scale voice over IP implementation", a classified project in thirteen countries.[^ref-3][^ref-16] He wrote code for the first five years, then "jokingly referred to it that I programmed in Powerpoint"; the company "bounced between ten and twenty people over the years."[^ref-3]

Now "pseudo-retired," he is lead systems engineer for an underwater power generator that runs on tidal currents.[^ref-3] At one technical conference he was introduced "as having written Softporn Adventure and Frogger," and afterwards was "mobbed by all these people wanting to take their pictures with me."[^ref-3]

He still has the Apple on which *Softporn Adventure* was written, and Get Lamp records his original development disks, Blue Sky packaging and printouts of the BASIC source code.[^ref-3][^ref-5] He has considered auctioning the material or donating it to a museum, calling *Softporn* "the first commercially successful adult software in the world."[^ref-3]

During COVID he co-wrote three thriller novels with author Steven Becker: *The Acadia Enigma* (set in Maine), *The Cape Cod Enigma* (Massachusetts) and *The Liberty Enigma* (New York).[^ref-3][^ref-15]

### Modern Recognition

*Leisure Suit Larry: Wet Dreams Don't Dry* (2018) lists Benton under Special Thanks.[^ref-4]

## Notable Works

### Softporn Adventure (1981)

*Softporn Adventure* is a text adventure in which the player tries to win over three women.[^ref-1][^ref-3] Benton sold the first version himself: "I made 1000 and sold 100," he told Get Lamp.[^ref-5] The box cover's hot tub photo was Ken Williams's idea, according to historian Laine Nooney, and was shot at the Williamses' home; the women in it were Sierra staff, Roberta Williams among them.[^ref-14][^ref-1][^ref-5] Time ran a story mentioning the game on October 5, 1981, by which point 4,000 copies had been sold; Benton recalled his mother's reaction to the article: "oh, now everybody knows…"[^ref-14][^ref-3] Get Lamp puts total sales "up past the 50,000 mark."[^ref-5] Benton said On-Line's sales rose because customers "came in and bought multiples of their product line, to hide the fact that they only wanted Softporn Adventure."[^ref-3]

Nooney writes that Benton "claims parts of the game were drawn from his own experience, but has never fessed up to which parts"; Get Lamp files one interview clip under the title "It's Semi-Autobiographical."[^ref-14][^ref-5]

### Frogger (1982–1983)

Benton's Atari 8-bit (1982) and Commodore 64 (1983) Frogger credits are listed on MobyGames.[^ref-4] He wrote the C64 version before the machine's commercial release, using draft documentation.[^ref-3] He called the C64 "an extremely impressive piece of hardware with multiple processors" but said its software was "pretty pathetic."[^ref-3]

### BC's Quest for Tires (1983)

Benton programmed the Atari 8-bit and Commodore 64 versions of [[1983 - BC's Quest for Tires|BC's Quest for Tires]] and designed the second half of the game himself.[^ref-4][^ref-17] The New Yorker reports that the work took eight weeks and that the game sold over fifty thousand copies.[^ref-17]

### Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards (1987)

Al Lowe's *Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards* began as a graphical reworking of *Softporn*, and MobyGames credits Benton with its design.[^ref-5][^ref-4] Asked about it, Benton said: "I think they did a good job with Larry and bringing it to life in graphic form."[^ref-3]

## Design Philosophy

Benton disliked "games that had an endless number of empty rooms and scenes"; his goal was "to make every space have multiple features and ideally to make the game lean toward being an interactive story as opposed to a puzzle," though "whether I achieved that is open to interpretation."[^ref-3] The Atlantic describes *Softporn* as satire, "a self-amused catalog of the unique sufferings of his species," and notes that "Benton wasn't trying to innovate on the text adventure genre so much as he was iterating from a pattern of pre-existing games."[^ref-14]

## Legacy

*Softporn Adventure* led directly to the Leisure Suit Larry series.[^ref-1][^ref-10] Benton described it as "the first commercially successful adult software in the world," and his 2003 biography called it "arguably the first commercially successful, adult-oriented software ever."[^ref-3][^ref-5] The Atlantic's Laine Nooney calls it "America's first commercially-released pornographic computer game."[^ref-14]
