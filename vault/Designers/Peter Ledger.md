# Peter Ledger

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Peter Ledger (1945–1994) was an Australian artist and illustrator who painted the art for a handful of computer games in the early 1990s, most notably as Art Designer of Sierra's [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot: The Search for the Grail]].[^ref-1][^ref-22] Born in Sydney, he won his greatest recognition in Australia for his pioneering use of the airbrush and his imaginative paintings and illustrations for advertising,[^ref-8] and he also worked in comics, including a period at Marvel Comics in New York around 1978–1979.[^ref-3][^ref-5]

His wife Christy Marx opened her biography of him with "Artist, Pilot, Adventurer. These things only begin to sum up the interests, talents and enthusiasms of a complex man who died much too soon."[^ref-8] She called him "the quintessential gentleman barbarian," and recalled that he must have said to her a hundred times, "I need to do this now, because I could be dead tomorrow."[^ref-8]

## Career

### Early Career (1970s–1988)

Ledger first made his name as an illustrator in Australian advertising.[^ref-8] Marvel colleague Eliot R. Brown recalled that a master painter Ledger had studied under taught him to isolate the work from any background and to place opposing colours of paint on either side of the brush, so that he could go "from pure to blend with great control."[^ref-5]

In 1977 he won the Art Directors Silver Award for his Surfabout poster, and his Golden Breed poster was honoured in the Graphis yearbook the same year.[^ref-8] In 1978, according to Christy Marx, he won the "King of Pop" award for Best Album Cover Design for The Angels' album *Face to Face* (Wikipedia gives the year as 1979).[^ref-8][^ref-3]

He was a big fan of Carl Barks' duck art, and hand-painted and airbrushed all the stories in the coffee-table art book *Uncle Scrooge McDuck: His Life and Times*.[^ref-3][^ref-8] He also created the first Babylon 5 logo and early character illustrations used while the series was being sold.[^ref-3]

### Computer Games (1988–1996)

Sierra's interest began when a headhunter called to ask whether Ledger would be interested in doing art for computer games; Marx asked whether Sierra also needed writers.[^ref-21] Toward the end of 1988, Ledger and Marx signed a deal to create computer games for Sierra On-Line and moved to the Oakhurst area.[^ref-8][^ref-3] The result was [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot: The Search for the Grail]] (1990), designed by Marx, with Ledger as Art Designer; The Digital Antiquarian describes him as the husband "who worked on her games as an illustrator."[^ref-22][^ref-24][^ref-16]

He went on to paint backgrounds for Tsunami Media's Ringworld: Revenge of the Patriarch (1992) and background renderings for Tsunami's [[1993 - Blue Force|Blue Force]] (1993).[^ref-3][^ref-23] His last game work, background illustrations for one campaign of Blood & Magic, appeared in 1996, two years after his death; the game was published by Interplay.[^ref-3][^ref-23]

Marx wrote that although he continued to produce paintings for computer games, "he never much cared for working with computers"; from about 1990 on he concentrated on large wall murals and trompe l'oeil paintings with British artist Susie Wilson.[^ref-8]

## Notable Works

### Conquests of Camelot: The Search for the Grail (1990)

MobyGames credits Ledger as Art Designer, one of the animators and background artists, and the cover artist; Christy Marx is the sole credited Game Designer.[^ref-22] Wikipedia summarises the split: "Marx did the majority of the design work while Ledger created the game and package art."[^ref-24] His son Julian Ledger is also credited among the background artists.[^ref-22]

### Ringworld: Revenge of the Patriarch (1992)

For Tsunami Media's Ringworld, Ledger is credited with the backgrounds.[^ref-23][^ref-3]

### Blue Force (1993)

On Tsunami's police adventure [[1993 - Blue Force|Blue Force]], Ledger is credited with background renderings.[^ref-23]

## Legacy

Ledger died on 18 November 1994.[^ref-8] The Ledger Awards, named in honour of his contribution to comic book art, were organised to "acknowledge excellence in Australian comic art and publishing."[^ref-3] The Telwynium games (2021–2023) credit him with "Special thanks and inspiration."[^ref-1][^ref-12]

Eliot R. Brown, remembering the 1979 Marvel offices, wrote that Ledger "was one of the first 'positive' people I got to know."[^ref-5]
