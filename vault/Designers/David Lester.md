# David Lester

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

David Lester is a British entrepreneur who founded Impressions Games with Edward Grabowski[^ref-24] and designed or co-designed several of its strategy games, including Caesar, Caesar II, Caesar III and Lords of the Realm II.[^ref-2][^ref-16] He trained as an accountant at Arthur Young, and a 2013 interview with him dates the founding of Impressions to 1988, when he was 22.[^ref-2] GamesNostalgia's profile of him opens: "When people think about video game creators, they always have this idea of brilliant programmers having fun, and doing what they love, making money being not really important for them. UK native David Lester is the exception to that rule."[^ref-1]

Impressions specialised in historical strategy games, and Lester sold the company to Sierra On-Line in 1995.[^ref-2][^ref-4] According to CNET Gamecenter, Impressions was the most profitable game division of Havas Interactive in 1998, with $20 million in profit, more than Blizzard.[^ref-17][^ref-4] In August 1999 Lester co-founded Firefly Studios, later known for the Stronghold series.[^ref-5]

## Career

### Early Career

After receiving accountancy training at Arthur Young, Lester found he did not want to practise as an accountant: "I started out down that road and got very bored within less than a year".[^ref-2] He explained the appeal of business: "I've always been a big fan of business. I love business, I find it interesting. It was the Thatcherite years when business was booming."[^ref-2] He had computer games contacts and decided to start a games publishing company "as a hobby with some friends".[^ref-2] Looking back, he still recommended working for someone else first: "I think the vast majority of people who leave university should go and work for someone else first. It's dangerous to start a business straightaway without that knowledge."[^ref-2]

Computer Gaming World's February 1993 company report fills in the background. It says Lester and Edward Grabowski had been working together "since 1984 when Lester coded his first game, *Just Imagine ...!* (a strategy game about managing a software house)", for which Grabowski designed the graphics.[^ref-24] Spectrum Computing lists *Just Imagine* as a 1986 ZX Spectrum release by Central Solutions, created by Lester; R&R Software had originally been due to publish it but closed in 1985.[^ref-25] According to CGW, while Lester was working with fast-growing businesses at Arthur Young, he and Grabowski, then a systems analyst and data processing manager, knew several struggling developers (Black Knight) who could not find a publisher, so Lester suggested putting up cash to publish a few titles independently.[^ref-24]

### Impressions Games Era (1988–1998)

The 2013 interview gives 1988 as the year Impressions was founded and GamesNostalgia agrees, while Wikipedia gives 1989 and MobyGames' biography of Lester gives 1991.[^ref-2][^ref-1][^ref-4][^ref-6] CGW reports that Lester and Grabowski established the company together, and that Lester set up a deal with Zeppelin Games to distribute its first titles for a fee; despite "a huge hit and a number one sales position during the summer of 1989", that relationship foundered and Impressions emerged as a distinct brand.[^ref-24] The company's first game was a football title endorsed by Kenny Dalglish, which Lester calls *Kenny Dalglish Football Manager*; this matches *Kenny Dalglish Soccer Manager* (1989), released on Impressions' Cognito label, rather than the later arcade game *Kenny Dalglish Soccer Match*.[^ref-2][^ref-21][^ref-22][^ref-23] Lester cold-called Liverpool Football Club to get Dalglish's name: "I rang him up. It was amazing, I remember ringing up Liverpool Football Club from a payphone during my coffee break and they put me through and he was there. I don't think it would happen today." The game "was number one all summer in 1989".[^ref-2]

GamesNostalgia says Lester played no role in that game beyond securing Dalglish, and calls Merchant Colony (1991) his first game, although CGW's 1993 report dates his first game, *Just Imagine*, to 1984.[^ref-1][^ref-24] GamesNostalgia describes Merchant Colony as an Amiga trading strategy game for which he wrote the concept while Simon Bradbury did the development; MobyGames credits him with its "Game Concept".[^ref-1][^ref-16]

Caesar followed in 1992, again with Bradbury; GamesNostalgia notes that it was "likened to SimCity on many levels".[^ref-1] MobyGames credits Lester with the design of Caesar and Caesar II (1995).[^ref-16] Lester says Caesar II and Caesar III "both sold about 2.5 million copies around the world".[^ref-2] After several games sold less than expected and cash ran short, the company moved into the US market. Lester recalls buying "a one way ticket out to Boston" and setting up an office there "with two people to start with and over the next three years we built that up to fifty people".[^ref-2] The period record places the early US headquarters in Connecticut: CGW's February 1993 report says Impressions located its corporate headquarters near the offices of Connecticut publisher Omnitrend, and interviewed Lester "in his Farmington, CT office".[^ref-24]

Lords of the Realm (1994) was designed with Christopher J. Foster from a concept by Lester; MobyGames credits him with its concept.[^ref-1][^ref-16] Lester later called it "probably my favorite of all the games I've designed".[^ref-18]

He advised entrepreneurs: "The best advice I can give is to get close and stay close to your customers. It's very easy to be close when you first start and very hard to stay close once you've started."[^ref-2]

### Sierra On-Line (1995–1998)

In 1995 Lester sold Impressions to Sierra On-Line; the 2013 interview calls the price a "life-changing sum".[^ref-2] Lords of the Realm II followed in 1996; MobyGames credits Lester as game designer and executive producer, and Wikipedia describes him as Impressions' "general manager and lead designer".[^ref-20][^ref-10] Lester says it sold about 2.5 million copies.[^ref-2]

Caesar III was released in 1998 with Lester credited as designer.[^ref-19][^ref-9] Lester puts its sales at around 2.5 million copies; period figures were 400,000 by January 1999, and Sierra said in April 1999 that the whole Caesar series had sold "well over one million units".[^ref-9][^ref-2] MobyGames says Lester returned to the United Kingdom in 1996 and "remained heavily involved until 1998 as the General Manager of the Impressions Division".[^ref-6]

### Post-Sierra Career

Lester co-founded Firefly Studios in August 1999 with Simon Bradbury and Eric Ouellette.[^ref-5] The studio is best known for the Stronghold series, which began in 2001, and has sold over seven million copies of its games.[^ref-5][^ref-15] MobyGames credits Lester on the Stronghold games only under Special Thanks.[^ref-6]

In February 1999, according to the 2013 interview, he launched Crimson Publishing, a magazine and website publisher whose Startups.co.uk he called "the UK's leading small business website by quite a long way"; the 2013 interview put its traffic at over 200,000 visitors a month.[^ref-2] MobyGames' biography instead says that after returning to the UK in 1996 he founded "Crimson Business", a marketing company for small and medium-sized businesses.[^ref-6] Lester also co-founded Business Community Connections, which links charities with businesses, and became a director of Watford Football Club.[^ref-6][^ref-2]

Reflecting on business, Lester said: "There's an old adage for business; 'profit is sanity, turnover is vanity' so turnover is not a factor I chase at all these days."[^ref-2]

## Notable Works

### Caesar (1992)

Caesar is a Roman city-building strategy game designed by Lester, again with the help of Simon Bradbury.[^ref-1][^ref-16] GamesNostalgia notes that although it was "likened to SimCity on many levels", it added a combat element and goals to meet.[^ref-1]

### Caesar III (1998)

[[1998 - Caesar III|Caesar III]] credits Lester as designer, and also for manual and research.[^ref-19][^ref-11] Lester's own figure for its sales is about 2.5 million copies.[^ref-2][^ref-9] It remains on sale on GOG.[^ref-13]

### Lords of the Realm II (1996)

[[1996 - Lords of the Realm II|Lords of the Realm II]] lists Lester as game designer alongside Eric Ouellette and others, and as executive producer.[^ref-20][^ref-12] In his designer's notes, dated October 4, 1996, Lester called the first game "a flawed gem" whose weaknesses lay in its interface and learning curve, and wrote: "Above all, though, I have tried to reduce the learning curve of the game dramatically, and to radically restructure the interface, in order that many more people will be able to get into the somewhat unique blend of fun this time."[^ref-18] Wikipedia notes that combining turn-based and real-time play was also meant to make the series "more multiplayer friendly".[^ref-10] The game is sold on GOG as part of Lords of the Realm: Royal Edition.[^ref-14]

## Design Philosophy

Lester's clearest statement of his design aims is his 1996 note on Lords of the Realm II. He described relying "more on other people for help with the design than ever before", and said the team hid grain farming and added a slider bar for allocating serfs, a change he initially opposed: "I couldn't see that a strategy gamer would want to give up the detailed control that the original labour panel offered."[^ref-18] He also explained cutting the first game's sheep: they "did not represent, in our minds, sufficient additional strategic choice to warrant the significant additional complexity".[^ref-18]

## Legacy

Impressions grew from a hobby business started with friends into a studio that CNET Gamecenter reported as Havas Interactive's most profitable game division in 1998, achieved "with only 40 employees".[^ref-2][^ref-17] Lester's own figures put Caesar II, Caesar III and Lords of the Realm II at about 2.5 million copies each.[^ref-2] Firefly Studios, which he co-founded, continues the historical strategy line with the Stronghold series.[^ref-5]
