# Mark Seibert

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Mark Seibert (born 1960 in California) is an American musician, composer and producer who was Sierra On-Line's music director in the early 1990s and later produced games including King's Quest VII, Phantasmagoria and King's Quest: Mask of Eternity.[^ref-1][^ref-18][^ref-20] He joined Sierra as a musician and music editor on King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella, and MobyGames credits him on 54 games.[^ref-1][^ref-5][^ref-2] He composed "Girl in the Tower," the love theme of [[1992 - King's Quest VI - Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow|King's Quest VI]], with lyrics by Jane Jensen.[^ref-19]

## Career

### Early Career

In the early 1980s Seibert toured with the Christian band Omega Sunrise. In his words: "I wrote some of their original material, played guitar and keyboards, and did some backup vocals. We did 2 album, and got some moderate airplay. My wife Debbie was the lead singer."[^ref-12] After "touring for almost 3 years non-stop," he said, "we settled in Fresno and lead music at the Vineyard for a few years."[^ref-12] He earned a Bachelor of Arts in music at California State University, Fresno, in 1983 and started a second degree in mathematics in 1986.[^ref-1][^ref-5]

He wrote his music on sheet music paper and then entered it into Sequencer Plus Gold, later moving to Cakewalk.[^ref-3]

Most accounts date his answer to Sierra's newspaper advertisement to 1987; in a 1998 interview he said, "Back in 1988 I responded to an ad in the paper. There was this company, Sierra On-Line, looking for a musician."[^ref-1][^ref-5][^ref-10] Sierra hired him as a musician and music editor for King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella (1988).[^ref-1][^ref-5]

### Sierra Years (1987–2001)

Within a few years Seibert became Sierra's music director.[^ref-1] In 1992 InterAction described him as "Music Director and head of Sierra's professional in-house recording studio," in charge of "recruiting and recording all the vocal talent."[^ref-18] He worked alongside composers such as Ken Allen, Christopher Braymen and Aubrey Hodges.[^ref-3]

"Girl in the Tower," sent to radio stations to promote King's Quest VI in 1992, was "Composed by Mark Seibert with lyrics by KQVI co-designer Jane Jensen" and performed by Bob Bergthold and Debbie Seibert.[^ref-19] In his liner notes he explained that he spun it off "from the basic motifs" of Cassima's Theme in King's Quest V, "thus tying the two games together."[^ref-17] He also chose the 20 tracks on The Sierra Soundtrack Collection (1992), drawn from King's Quest V, Codename: ICEMAN, Police Quest II, Conquests of Camelot, Quest for Glory I, Conquests of the Longbow and King's Quest VI.[^ref-17][^ref-19]

In 1992 he was promoted to producer.[^ref-1][^ref-5] His first production, Pepper's Adventures in Time (1993), "has become a cult classic amongst point 'n' click adventure gamers," according to Choicest Games.[^ref-5] MobyGames credits him as producer on King's Quest VII (1994), Phantasmagoria (1995), Torin's Passage (1995), Leisure Suit Larry: Love for Sail! (1996) and King's Quest: Mask of Eternity (1998).[^ref-2] InterAction's Holiday 1995 issue noted "the producer's credit on King's Quest VII and Phantasmagoria."[^ref-20]

For [[1995 - Phantasmagoria|Phantasmagoria]], Seibert and Jay Usher wrote the score, with Neal Grandstaff providing additional music; it mixed real instruments with synthesized and sampled sounds and, unlike earlier Sierra games, was recorded live.[^ref-8] He used the CSU Fresno concert choir for the opening chant and the Seattle vocal group the Esoterics for the rest of the game, and said: "I think Phantasmagoria was probably one of my best efforts."[^ref-12]

In 1997 he told Next Generation that he had never finished an adventure game: "I always get halfway through and get stuck someplace, and then I have to download the walk-through off the Internet and read the solution, and it's always like, 'Well that's a stupid puzzle!' I get so frustrated, I throw it down and never pick it back up."[^ref-11]

His last Sierra credits were Arcanum (2001, executive producer) and Throne of Darkness (2001, producer and dialog direction).[^ref-2][^ref-5] Wikipedia gives his departure as 2001 and VGMPF as 2002.[^ref-1][^ref-3]

### Post-Sierra Career (2001–2008)

In 2003 he was Director of Development at Gentle Revolution Software in Maryland, which had "a Space Act Agreement with NASA for the development of a PS2/PC game centered around the International Space Station."[^ref-12] He then worked at Breakaway Games as producer and audio/video director; MobyGames credits him as audio director on The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II – The Rise of the Witch-king (2006) and audio engineer on Command & Conquer 3: Kane's Wrath (2008).[^ref-5][^ref-9][^ref-2] While at Breakaway he completed a master's degree in education, and in 2008 he left the games industry.[^ref-5] As of 2015 he was teaching mathematics at Perry Hall Christian School in Maryland.[^ref-5]

## Notable Works

### King's Quest V: Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder (1990)

MobyGames lists Seibert and Ken Allen as the composers of [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V]].[^ref-14][^ref-2] His liner notes say the King's Quest V Theme and Graham's Theme "carry strong motifs from which much of the music for the game was derived."[^ref-17]

### King's Quest VI: Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow (1992)

On [[1992 - King's Quest VI - Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow|King's Quest VI]] MobyGames credits him with additional music, and he composed "Girl in the Tower."[^ref-2][^ref-19] He lists his role as "Wrote and produced 'Girl in the Tower'."[^ref-9]

### Phantasmagoria (1995)

On [[1995 - Phantasmagoria|Phantasmagoria]] MobyGames credits him as producer, one of the game directors, composer and "Movie Sequences Scored By."[^ref-2] He and Jay Usher scored the cinematic scenes after they were shot, which Wikipedia notes was a new process for Sierra.[^ref-8]

### King's Quest: Mask of Eternity (1998)

On [[1998 - King's Quest - Mask of Eternity|King's Quest: Mask of Eternity]] MobyGames credits him with production, design, music and documentation; his own list says "Producer/Musician/Co-Designer."[^ref-2][^ref-9] Before release he defended the game's genre: "King's Quest: Mask of Eternity is first and foremost an adventure game. There is a lot of story, characters, and puzzles. Just because there are other elements blended in, doesn't mean it's not an adventure game."[^ref-10]

## Design Philosophy

In the same interview he said: "People love stories. Look at the sales of books and movies. Adventure games are stories... When it comes to content, I think you'll see a reemerging of more story in games."[^ref-10]

## Legacy

Seibert's work remains associated with Sierra's early-1990s scores: VGMPF's gameography lists his music, sound or music-direction work on Sierra games from King's Quest IV (1988) to Quest for Glory V (1998), and his "Hero's Theme" for Quest for Glory reappears in Quest for Glory III, IV and V.[^ref-3][^ref-16] The 1992 Sierra Soundtrack Collection gathered his compositions on CD.[^ref-17][^ref-19]

## Personal Life

Seibert is married to Debbie Seibert, who sang lead in Omega Sunrise and later sang for Sierra games including "Girl in the Tower"; they have two daughters.[^ref-12]
