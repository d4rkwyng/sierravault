# Leslie Balfour

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Leslie Balfour (credited earlier as Leslie Wilson and Leslie Sayes Wilson) is a writer, voice actress and producer who worked at Sierra On-Line in the mid-1990s.[^ref-10][^ref-2] She voiced Laura Bow, the narrator and Yvette Delacroix in [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]] (1992), then joined Sierra in 1993, writing for its InterAction magazine, its marketing and its documentation.[^ref-12][^ref-2][^ref-3]

She edited the "Popular Janitronics" magazine packed with [[1995 - Space Quest 6 - Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier|Space Quest 6]], wrote and directed the "Making of Space Quest" video, and was co-designer with Scott Murphy on the cancelled Space Quest VII.[^ref-3][^ref-13][^ref-9] She was no longer under contract with Sierra by August 1998,[^ref-9] and later worked as a project manager.[^ref-6]

## Career

### Early Career and Voice Acting

In a 1997 interview she gave a tongue-in-cheek account of her arrival: "In 1993, I was working as a carney for a small circus called Wanda's Big Top and Catering."[^ref-3] Josh Mandel recalled that around 1993 "a good number of Sierra people, myself included, worked on or performed in one of her original musical comedies, HAMLET ON WRY."[^ref-8]

Her first Sierra work was voice acting on [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]]. Bruce Balfour, its designer, recalled: "We'd had the most trouble casting Laura Bow, with her Southern accent, Yvette Delacroix (French), and the upper-class British narrator. When Leslie Wilson showed up to audition for me and my programmer friend Dan Carver, she was able to do all three of those accents."[^ref-2] She was cast first as Yvette, and "when they had exhausted the local talent and had not found a Laura or a narrator, they asked me to do those too."[^ref-2] MobyGames lists all three roles under "Leslie Wilson."[^ref-12]

Of the Yvette sessions she said: "I was saying her lines and Bruce would say, 'More sex.' So I kept increasing the sex, and the outer door to the studio was open, and programmers could hear, I guess, and were drawn to it like programmers are drawn to sex. At the end of the session, there had to be five guys outside the studio trying to get a glimpse of Yvette. She did not emerge — I did. Scott Murphy of Space Quest fame was particularly disappointed."[^ref-2]

She met Bruce Balfour during the recordings. In 2018 she said: "I think often of that time, because I ended up falling for Bruce. The Dagger of Amon Ra is a great game, but for me, the prize was my husband of 23 years."[^ref-2] That puts their marriage at about 1995; Bruce said he "got her to accept a more permanent contract with me four years later."[^ref-2] She is credited as Leslie Sayes Wilson on InterAction articles in 1993 and 1994 and on the 1994 King's Quest Collection.[^ref-15][^ref-16][^ref-4]

### Sierra Years (1993–1998)

After the voice work she tried to get into Sierra through Bruce's friends: "I got to know a lot of Bruce's friends — Scott Murphy, Lorelei Shannon, Josh Mandel and Robert Lindsley to name a few — and I tried to get into Sierra that way, but to no avail. They were on the game side and I didn't have the background to get into Sierra that way."[^ref-2] Instead she met creative director Kurt Busch "through our children, and he put me to work on InterAction, the Sierra magazine, and later on marketing and package copy. From there I was able to work into a position as a documentation writer working for Nathan Gams, and then David Slayback hired me as a writer for The Realm."[^ref-2] Her InterAction bylines from 1993 survive in the magazine's scans.[^ref-15][^ref-16]

Her credits from this period include:

- **King's Quest Collection (1994)** — Writer and documentation, as Leslie Sayes Wilson. The King's Quest Omnipedia suggests that she or Susan Frischer, the collection's other credited writer, wrote its bonus help file *The Royal Scribe*.[^ref-4][^ref-7]
- **Space Quest: Collector's Edition (1994)** — Writer and documentation, and in the video credits.[^ref-11] She wrote and directed its "Making of Space Quest" video and appears in it as the lawyer.[^ref-3]
- **Space Quest 6 (1995)** — Documentation, and editor of the "Popular Janitronics" magazine, which Josh Mandel asked her to do.[^ref-13][^ref-3]
- **Gabriel Knight 2: The Beast Within (1995)** — Documentation.[^ref-10][^ref-7]
- **Quest for Glory Anthology (1996)** — Producer.[^ref-14]
- **The Realm** — Writer, hired by David Slayback.[^ref-2]
- **Police Quest: SWAT 2 (1998)** — Manual proofreader.[^ref-10][^ref-7]

For the 1997 Space Quest Collection she said, "I'm not involved with that product except that I proofread the documentation."[^ref-3] Josh Mandel said: "There weren't a lot of comedy writers at Sierra, and she was an active comedy writer both inside and outside of SOL's walls. She's the one who coined the phrase, 'In Space, Nobody Can Hear You Clean,' which I think is the best tagline Sierra ever had."[^ref-8]

### Space Quest VII and Departure

In early 1997 Sierra began assembling a Space Quest VII team led by Scott Murphy and Leslie Balfour.[^ref-9] SpaceQuest.Net lists the team as Murphy and Balfour (designers/writers), programmers Jay Lee and William Shockley, Mark Aro on graphics, Richard Powell on concept art and character design, and Tim Loucks on 3D.[^ref-5] In Campo Santo she described her role as producer.[^ref-2]

She told The Virtual Broomcloset: "I am a major X-Phile, and so are most of the other team members. Plus Jay Lee is a big fan of Gillian Anderson, and I think he wants to program her. So barring lawsuits, I think you can safely assume that the truth will be in there."[^ref-3]

In December 1997 Sierra put the game "on hold indefinitely." Murphy and Balfour wrote to fans: "Sierra is in the process of many changes, and we had to take a hard look at whether a Space Quest 7 project made sense. Unfortunately for those of us who love Roger and his stupid antics, other projects just have more to offer both to the company and to our customers in general at this time."[^ref-9] Murphy was laid off in February 1998, and by August 1998 Balfour was no longer under contract with Sierra. When Sierra shut its Oakhurst studio on February 22, 1999, Murphy declared the project dead.[^ref-9] Of Sierra she said: "I was there for five years, and really enjoyed about three of them."[^ref-2]

### Post-Sierra Career

After leaving Sierra in 1998 she worked as a project manager.[^ref-9][^ref-6] As of 2018 she and Bruce Balfour had been married for 23 years.[^ref-2]

## Notable Works

### The Dagger of Amon Ra (1992)

Her three voice roles in [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]] were Laura Bow, the narrator and Yvette Delacroix.[^ref-12][^ref-2]

### Space Quest 6: Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier (1995)

For [[1995 - Space Quest 6 - Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier|Space Quest 6]] she worked on the documentation and edited "Popular Janitronics," with Josh Mandel and Scott Murphy among its contributors.[^ref-13]

### Space Quest VII: Return to Roman Numerals (Cancelled)

Balfour was co-designer with Scott Murphy on [[CXL - Space Quest VII - Return to Roman Numerals|Space Quest VII]].[^ref-9] Working titles released in 1997 included "This time it's Personal," "Show me the Buckazoids" and "Return of Roman Numerals."[^ref-5]
