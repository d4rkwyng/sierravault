# Richard Tunnell

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Richard Tunnell (also credited as Rich Tunnell) is a game designer, programmer and artist credited on 21 games between 1992 and 2009, most of them made in Eugene, Oregon, by Dynamix and Jeff Tunnell Productions for Sierra On-Line.[^ref-2][^ref-1] He worked as an artist, puzzle designer, tester and manual writer on the Incredible Machine games, as a programmer on the 3-D Ultra Pinball series, and as game designer on 3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers.[^ref-3] MobyGames lists 18 games on which he and Jeff Tunnell, Dynamix's co-founder, are both credited.[^ref-2][^ref-7]

He later worked on browser and social games: he was a level designer on GarageGames' PlayLegions (2009), and his LinkedIn profile names Zynga as his employer.[^ref-3][^ref-13][^ref-6]

## Career

### Dynamix and Jeff Tunnell Productions (1992–2001)

Dynamix was founded in Eugene in 1984 by Jeff Tunnell and Damon Slye.[^ref-7] After finishing The Adventures of Willy Beamish (1991), Jeff Tunnell left to start Jeff Tunnell Productions in early 1992; the new company made The Incredible Machine games for Sierra.[^ref-15][^ref-16][^ref-4] Richard Tunnell's first credit was as an artist on [[1992 - Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science|Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science]] (1992), followed by production art on [[1993 - Turbo Learning - Mega Math|Turbo Learning: Mega Math]] (1993).[^ref-3]

On the Incredible Machine games he was one of the "Puzzlomaniacs" and wrote the manual for The Incredible Machine (1993), [[1993 - The Even More Incredible Machine|The Even More! Incredible Machine]] (1993) and [[1993 - Sid & Al's Incredible Toons|Sid & Al's Incredible Toons]] (1993).[^ref-3] On [[1994 - The Incredible Machine 2|The Incredible Machine 2]] (1994) MobyGames lists him among the designers, artists and testers.[^ref-3][^ref-4] The game's staff list, as transcribed on the Dynamix Wiki, also names him "Puzzle Director."[^ref-14] On [[1994 - The Incredible Toon Machine|The Incredible Toon Machine]] (1994) he shares the Windows & Macintosh Enhanced Design credit and is credited as an artist and manual writer.[^ref-3]

He then worked on the 3-D Ultra Pinball games. On [[1995 - 3-D Ultra Pinball|3-D Ultra Pinball]] (1995) he is credited for additional design help and as one of the programmers. On [[1996 - 3-D Ultra Pinball - Creep Night|Creep Night]] (1996) and [[1999 - 3-D Ultra Pinball - Power|Power!]] (1999) he did additional design help and additional programming. On [[1997 - 3-D Ultra Pinball - The Lost Continent|The Lost Continent]] (1997) and [[1998 - 3-D Ultra NASCAR Pinball|3-D Ultra NASCAR Pinball]] he was Game Logic Programmer.[^ref-3] He was also a quality analyst on [[1995 - Trophy Bass|Trophy Bass]] (1995), a scriptor on [[1996 - Hunter Hunted|Hunter Hunted]] (1996), and designer and documentation writer on Field & Stream: Trophy Hunting 4 (2000).[^ref-2][^ref-3]

His later Dynamix credits are game design on [[1999 - 3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers|3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers]] (1999) and on [[2000 - 3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers Deluxe - Traxxas Edition|3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers Deluxe: Traxxas Edition]] (2000), where the credit is shared with Shawn Bird and Jonathan Paton.[^ref-3][^ref-2] He also did contraption design for [[2000 - Return of The Incredible Machine - Contraptions|Return of The Incredible Machine: Contraptions]] (2000) and [[2001 - The Incredible Machine - Even More Contraptions|The Incredible Machine: Even More Contraptions]] (2001).[^ref-3] Sierra closed Dynamix on August 14, 2001.[^ref-11]

### After Dynamix

GarageGames was founded in Eugene in 2000 by Jeff Tunnell, Tim Gift, Rick Overman and Mark Frohnmayer.[^ref-5] After being acquired by IAC in 2007, it created the browser-game site InstantAction.[^ref-8] Richard Tunnell is credited as a level designer on PlayLegions, a browser game developed and published by GarageGames in 2009.[^ref-3][^ref-13] The company shut down InstantAction.com in November 2010.[^ref-9]

His LinkedIn profile names Zynga as his employer and lists projects including Social City (2009–2011), the Facebook version of ESPN Return Man (2011–2012) and Disney City Girl (from 2012).[^ref-6] Social City was a Playdom game; it won Best Social Network Game at the 2010 GDC Online Awards.[^ref-12] The profile doesn't say when he joined either company.[^ref-6]

## Notable Works

### The Incredible Machine 2 (1994)

On [[1994 - The Incredible Machine 2|The Incredible Machine 2]] Tunnell is credited as a designer, artist and tester, and the staff list names him Puzzle Director, with a team of puzzle makers under him.[^ref-3][^ref-14]

### 3-D Ultra Pinball series (1995–1999)

Tunnell worked on five 3-D Ultra Pinball games: [[1995 - 3-D Ultra Pinball|3-D Ultra Pinball]], Creep Night, The Lost Continent, NASCAR Pinball and Power!. His roles ranged from additional design help to Game Logic Programmer.[^ref-3]

### 3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers (1999)

[[1999 - 3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers|3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers]] gave him a game design credit, which he shared on the Deluxe: Traxxas Edition the following year.[^ref-3][^ref-2]
