# Guruka Singh Khalsa

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Guruka Singh Khalsa was Sierra On-Line's first producer, a creative and administrative role that gave him broad oversight of the production of the company's games.[^ref-17] MobyGames credits him with production on thirteen Sierra releases from 1989 to 1992, among them Hero's Quest, Conquests of Camelot, Quest for Glory II, Space Quest IV, Leisure Suit Larry 5 and Police Quest 3, and with voice roles in King's Quest V and Quest for Glory III.[^ref-4][^ref-5][^ref-8] Digital Antiquarian historian Jimmy Maher compares his role to that of Jon Palace at Infocom: he "made sure the designers had the support they needed, acted as a buffer between them and the more business-oriented people, and gently pushed his charges to make their games just a little bit better."[^ref-2]

Outside games, he is a Sikh minister and Kundalini Yoga teacher. In 1986 he founded the SikhNet computer bulletin board system, the forerunner of the SikhNet website, and he worked with SikhNet for fifteen years until July 2020.[^ref-3][^ref-18]

## Career

### Before Sierra

His MobyGames biography says Khalsa came "from a long career in audio engineering and television broadcast engineering and production, as well as multimedia entertainment software development." The same biography says he is "Known by many as Doctor Hi-Fi" and "loves to play with electronics, computers and words."[^ref-1]

In his own account of becoming a Sikh, he writes that he "first began to discover who I really was in the 1960's," while living in Berkeley, California. In the spring of 1971 he met Yogi Bhajan at the Earth Rebirth Festival on Earth Day at UC Davis.[^ref-6] "Well, that's how I became a Sikh. You see, I didn't really become a Sikh at all. I just remembered who I was. My true identity - Sat Nam," he writes.[^ref-6] He was married that summer at the Summer Solstice gathering in Paonia, Colorado, where Yogi Bhajan married 23 couples.[^ref-6]

### Sierra Credits (1989–1992)

At Sierra, Khalsa was the producer for many games at once. Quest for Glory designer Corey Cole recalled in 2018 that "Without Guruka, there is a good chance that there would not be Hero's Quest / Quest for Glory. Besides being our Producer, he was the game's biggest advocate within Sierra management. As he was *the* Producer at Sierra for all of the games simultaneously, his day-to-day impact on the game was minimal."[^ref-2] Josh Mandel, who met Khalsa on CompuServe's Gamers Forum, asked him about beta-testing and was later offered a job as his junior producer, described him in 2006 as the man "who, for years, was Sierra's sole Producer."[^ref-20] Adventure Gamers' Sierra retrospective describes producers like him as "deeply involved in the early stages of design," and quotes his memory of planning [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot]] with Christy Marx: "Sitting on the grass. Outside. Under a tree. Christy and I with a legal pad and pen. Sketching stuff out."[^ref-17] The finished game carries a nod to him: a man in Ismael's antiquities shop claims to have hairs from the beard of Guruka Singh, and asked about the beard he replies, "That is rare and valuable. Have you ever tried to shave a Sikh? It is most dangerous."[^ref-22]

His producer credits from 1989 are [[1989 - Quest for Glory I - So You Want to Be a Hero|Hero's Quest: So You Want to Be a Hero]] (later renamed Quest for Glory I), [[1990 - Codename - Iceman|Code-Name: Iceman]] and [[1989 - Hoyle Official Book of Games - Volume 1|Hoyle: Official Book of Games - Volume 1]].[^ref-4] In 1990 he produced Conquests of Camelot, [[1990 - Oils Well|Oil's Well]], the English version of Nihon Falcom's Sorcerian, and [[1990 - Quest for Glory II - Trial by Fire|Quest for Glory II: Trial by Fire]], which Jimmy Maher calls "the last Sierra game in 16-color EGA and the last with a parser."[^ref-4][^ref-2] In 1991 he produced [[1991 - Conquests of the Longbow - The Legend of Robin Hood|Conquests of the Longbow]], [[1990 - Jones in the Fast Lane|Jones in the Fast Lane]], [[1991 - Leisure Suit Larry 5 - Passionate Patti Does a Little Undercover Work|Leisure Suit Larry 5]], [[1991 - Police Quest III - The Kindred|Police Quest 3: The Kindred]] and [[1991 - Space Quest IV - Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers|Space Quest IV]], several of which were later released on the Amiga.[^ref-4][^ref-12] His final producer credit, in 1992, was "Production" on the CD-ROM version of Jones in the Fast Lane.[^ref-4] Retro Gamer, naming him as Sierra's producer for Jones, quoted him: "Chris Whaley came to us with the concept and we spent a lot of time tweaking and balancing the gameplay." Jones was an early CD game, and he recalled, "We had a CD burning machine that cost $140,000 and you had to burn the whole gold master disc in one go - or the disc was a coaster," adding that "each blank disc cost $140!"[^ref-21]

He also voiced The Cat and the Irate Customer in the tailor's shop in [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V: Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder]], The Wizard in [[1992 - Quest for Glory III - Wages of War|Quest for Glory III: Wages of War]], and a voice in the Jones in the Fast Lane CD-ROM.[^ref-4][^ref-5][^ref-10][^ref-13]

On August 24, 1990, Khalsa joined a CompuServe online conference on adventure-game design alongside Ron Gilbert, Noah Falstein, Bob Bates, Steve Meretzky, Mike Berlyn, Dave Lebling, Roberta Williams, Al Lowe, and Corey and Lori Ann Cole.[^ref-2] Asked about hardware, he said: "We've been doing a bit of research on who has what hardware, and an amazing number of Sierra customers have VGA cards. Looks like around 60 percent right now."[^ref-2]

Looking back on the period, he told Adventure Gamers: "There was this peak where the creative juices drove Sierra. It was very much about the soul of the game. A game that can touch you deeply and make you go wow."[^ref-17]

### SikhNet and Later Work

In 1986 Khalsa founded the first incarnation of SikhNet, a computer bulletin board system that ran from 1986 to 1994.[^ref-18] Gurumustuk Singh started the SikhNet.com website on Vaisakhi 1996, inspired by that BBS, and later led it as CEO.[^ref-18][^ref-19] Khalsa worked with SikhNet for fifteen years. His earlier work as a producer of entertainment software led to *Karma – The Ogre's Curse* (2011), which SikhNet calls "the first online Sikh spiritual adventure game"; SikhNet says its development "was led by Guruka Singh and took over a year to create."[^ref-18] SikhNet's July 2020 notice of his departure says he appears in over 160 SikhNet videos "with millions of views."[^ref-18] His older MobyGames biography describes him as SikhNet's CEO; SikhNet's own 2020 and 2022 notices do not give him that title, and name Gurumustuk Singh as the CEO stepping down in 2022. The biography also names him as one of the founders of the consulting firm Sun & Son, without a date.[^ref-1][^ref-18][^ref-19]

SikhNet describes him as one of the original 25 teachers trained directly by Yogi Bhajan. He began teaching Kundalini Yoga in 1971, became a minister of Sikh Dharma in 1973, and received the title Mukhia Singh Sahib (Head Minister) in 2004.[^ref-3] He writes that he received Amrit in 1972.[^ref-7] He translated Yogi Bhajan's Gurmukhi poems into English as *Furmaan Khalsa* (1987), published his own poetry as *The Game of Love* (2004), and wrote *Heroes, Saints and Yogis – Tales of Self-Discovery and the Path of Sikh Dharma* (2011).[^ref-3][^ref-7]

In 2013 he received a Special Thanks credit on [[2013 - Fester Mudd - Curse of the Gold|Fester Mudd: Curse of the Gold - Episode 1]].[^ref-4]
