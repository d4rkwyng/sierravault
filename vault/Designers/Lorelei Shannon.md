# Lorelei Shannon

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Lorelei Shannon is a game designer and writer who co-designed [[1994 - King's Quest VII - The Princeless Bride|King's Quest VII: The Princeless Bride]] with Roberta Williams and designed [[1996 - Phantasmagoria - A Puzzle of Flesh|Phantasmagoria: A Puzzle of Flesh]] for Sierra On-Line.[^ref-27][^ref-3] Her author biography says she was born in the Arizona desert and describes her as "a strange, fey child who kept to herself, and could often be found in the company of a big praying mantis in her mother's rose garden."[^ref-1] It also calls her the author of "numerous short stories, horror and dark fantasy novels, non-fiction books, and a computer game that was banned from three countries and Sears stores everywhere."[^ref-1] Her interests include "reading, sculpting, gaming, movies, every kind of music, learning new things, and cultivating carnivorous plants."[^ref-1]

## Career

### Early Career

Before Sierra, Shannon did writing and editing at Flying Buffalo in Arizona, where she and her husband worked.[^ref-12] After they moved to Oakhurst, she was hired as a writer for Sierra's in-house magazine, *InterAction*: "I worked on the magazine for close to a year. It was a lot of fun—I spent a lot of time playing games and writing about them. Then a spot on the design team opened up."[^ref-12] She is listed among the magazine's contributors in the Fall 1991 issue.[^ref-25] MobyGames also lists her under Special Thanks on Tunnels & Trolls: Crusaders of Khazan (1990).[^ref-3]

### Sierra Years (from 1991)

On the design team she began by writing dialog: "I started out just writing dialog for various games, including Police Quest and Dagger of Amon Ra."[^ref-12] MobyGames credits her with additional written material on [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]] (1992), where she also voiced Stinky, and on the VGA remake of Police Quest (1992), and with technical documentation for Quest for Glory III.[^ref-3] Of Dagger she said: "I mostly just wrote dialog for Dagger of Amon Ra. The game designer on that project was Bruce Balfour."[^ref-12]

Her first design work was [[1993 - Pepper's Adventures in Time|Pepper's Adventures in Time]] (1993); InterAction's Fall 1992 issue billed her as the "Twisty History co-designer," the game's working title.[^ref-26][^ref-27][^ref-12] The Adventure Gamer blog calls Pepper's "the first bigger job at Sierra" for her.[^ref-8] InterAction later wrote that Pepper's "did get the attention of Roberta Williams, who asked designer Lorelei Shannon to join her as co-designer of King's Quest VII."[^ref-27]

Shannon had moved to Seattle when her husband took a job at Microsoft, and Sierra opened a Seattle office a few months later.[^ref-12] On [[1994 - King's Quest VII - The Princeless Bride|King's Quest VII]] she was co-designer: "Basically, Roberta and I would sit across from each other at her kitchen table, brainstorm, and write out the design longhand." She described the division of work: "Roberta did more of the puzzles than I did. I wrote most of the dialog. We created the story together."[^ref-12] She cast the voice actors and directed them in Los Angeles.[^ref-12] "Working with Roberta was a lot of fun. We cracked each other up a lot. Sometimes Ken would come home and we'd be laughing about something like a couple of lunatics. He'd just roll his eyes!"[^ref-12] She also wrote the official King's Quest VII hint guide.[^ref-29]

"After successfully co-designing the game, I was given my own project, Phantasmagoria 2," she said.[^ref-12] MobyGames credits her as "Game Designer and Screenwriter" on [[1996 - Phantasmagoria - A Puzzle of Flesh|Phantasmagoria: A Puzzle of Flesh]] (1996), as well as documentation author and "Rat Wrangler."[^ref-3] In a Fall 1996 InterAction piece she introduced herself: "Hi. I'm Lorelei, the designer of Phantasmagoria: A Puzzle of Flesh."[^ref-30]

### Later Career

Shannon has published horror and dark fantasy fiction and non-fiction (see Bibliography).[^ref-1][^ref-24] She has also worked as a technical writer and editor; her LinkedIn profile describes her as "a seasoned technical content specialist with over 15 years of experience in the industry," and a colleague's recommendation there places her on the technical documentation team at Seagull Scientific.[^ref-5] Asked in 2011 whether FMV had been dismissed too early, she said: "Yes, I think FMV was dismissed much too early. I think it could still make a comeback."[^ref-12]

## Notable Works

### Phantasmagoria: A Puzzle of Flesh (1996)

In a 1996 InterAction profile, written while the game was in production, Shannon said: "I love horror in all forms. I especially like psychological horror movies like 'Jacob's Ladder,' and wanted to bring across that kind of paranoid, or very frightening, very real kind of horror.... I really admire Clive Barker for his extreme realism."[^ref-28] In a 1997 interview she named Edgar Allan Poe as "a big influence," along with Clive Barker, Kathe Koja and Thomas Ligotti, and said that "Jacob's Ladder was a big influence. So was Prince of Darkness and The Exorcist III."[^ref-22] In a later interview she said the game's look "was influenced somewhat by the movie 'Se7en,' and the artist Joel-Peter Witkin," and listed Charles Beaumont, Shirley Jackson and Poe among the authors in her head while writing it.[^ref-23]

A modern review in Only Solitaire singles out her dialogue: "Shannon really made a strong emphasis on her FMV heroes behaving like real people in a real environment — for which purpose she wrote tons of realistic, often (though not always) intelligent and occasionally funny dialogue."[^ref-11] In a 1997 interview, Andy Bellatti noted that the game "has been selling very well" and asked about the public's response; she replied, "Of course, I'm very pleased."[^ref-22]

### King's Quest VII: The Princeless Bride (1994)

MobyGames credits her on King's Quest VII for design, direction, writing and voice direction.[^ref-3] InterAction's Fall 1994 issue introduced "Roberta Williams and her co-designer Lorelei Shannon," and quoted her: "I think King's Quest VII is so special because of its quirky, lively characters."[^ref-27] By her own account Roberta did more of the puzzles, she wrote most of the dialog, and they created the story together.[^ref-12]

### Pepper's Adventures in Time (1993)

On Pepper's Adventures in Time MobyGames credits her with design, text and dialog, and documentation.[^ref-3] It was her first design job at Sierra.[^ref-12][^ref-8]
