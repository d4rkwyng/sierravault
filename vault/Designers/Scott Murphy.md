# Scott Murphy

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Scott Murphy is a game designer and programmer best known as one half of the "Two Guys from Andromeda," co-creator with Mark Crowe of Sierra On-Line's Space Quest series.[^ref-1] Wikipedia gives his birth year as 1954.[^ref-15] With Murphy programming, Crowe doing the art and both designing, the pair made four Space Quest games together, from Space Quest (1986) to [[1991 - Space Quest IV - Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers|Space Quest IV]] (1991).[^ref-1] Murphy later finished [[1995 - Space Quest 6 - Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier|Space Quest 6]] (1995), a game designed largely by Josh Mandel.[^ref-1][^ref-6]

He was laid off by Sierra in early 1999 and then left games entirely until 2012, when he and Crowe reunited as Guys from Andromeda to make SpaceVenture.[^ref-7][^ref-10][^ref-1]

## Career

### Joining Sierra

Murphy came to Sierra without any training in computers. He was cooking at a local dinner house in Oakhurst when he discovered adventure games: "I had an evening job I hated and I kept going to Sierra and bugging them until they let me come to work for them as a dealer returns person."[^ref-7] From there he became a support representative for games and then for business products, ran the support department, and did QA work.[^ref-37][^ref-7] Watching Crowe and others make King's Quest II and The Black Cauldron, he "started bugging Ken" Williams to let him try programming: "I saw people working on programming and I knew that if they could do it, I could do it."[^ref-37] He has described himself as "entirely self-taught", with "only … a high school diploma."[^ref-7]

His first programming job was [[1986 - The Black Cauldron|The Black Cauldron]]. Crowe, who worked on its animation, recalled in 1989 that it "was the first project Scott and I worked on together. I believe that was also his first job creating source code for the games."[^ref-37] Murphy said he spent nights over a summer at Ken Williams' house debugging it, and "ended up being the only one working on it."[^ref-37] MobyGames also credits him with programming ("Game Logic") on [[1985 - King's Quest II - Romancing the Throne|King's Quest II]] and a "Thanks to" credit on the 1986 King's Quest.[^ref-8][^ref-35]

### Space Quest with Mark Crowe (1986–1991)

Murphy and Crowe met on The Black Cauldron and shared a love of science fiction and a sense of humour; they came up with their own space comedy, convinced Ken Williams to let them develop it, and became the Two Guys from Andromeda.[^ref-1][^ref-7][^ref-29] [[1986 - Space Quest - The Sarien Encounter|Space Quest: The Sarien Encounter]] (1986) was followed by [[1987 - Space Quest II - Vohaul's Revenge|Space Quest II: Vohaul's Revenge]] (1987), [[1989 - Space Quest III - The Pirates of Pestulon|Space Quest III: The Pirates of Pestulon]] (1989) and [[1991 - Space Quest IV - Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers|Space Quest IV: Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers]] (1991); on each, MobyGames credits Murphy with design and programming.[^ref-8][^ref-33] Of the series' hero he said in 2012: "Roger was the antithesis of all other game characters of the time. Everyone else was noble, doing good deeds for princesses. Roger wanted none of that. It was more like me."[^ref-5]

Between Space Quests he programmed other Sierra games, including [[1987 - Police Quest - In Pursuit of the Death Angel|Police Quest]] (1987), Mixed-Up Mother Goose (1991), [[1991 - EcoQuest - The Search for Cetus|EcoQuest]] (1991) and [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]] (1992).[^ref-6][^ref-8] He was also credited as a director and game designer on the 1991 VGA remake of the first game, [[1991 - Space Quest I - Roger Wilco in the Sarien Encounter|Space Quest I: Roger Wilco in the Sarien Encounter]].[^ref-8] He was no fan of the remake's interface: "I loved the old parser interface because I felt I could have a lot more fun with the player, including insulting them based on their input."[^ref-7]

The work was hard on both men. Murphy recalled being "chewed out" over how long SQ2 and SQ3 took to ship, and said "SQ4 showed how dark we'd become as a result."[^ref-7] Crowe, for his part, called Space Quest 4 "our (Scott and I) masterpiece."[^ref-46] Space Quest IV was the last game the two made together. In 1991 Crowe moved to Dynamix, another Sierra company, and the partnership broke down for about 20 years.[^ref-19][^ref-1] Crowe designed [[1993 - Space Quest V - The Next Mutation|Space Quest V]] (1993) at Dynamix without Murphy.[^ref-15][^ref-6]

### Later Sierra Years (1991–1999)

Murphy kept programming for other Sierra projects.[^ref-1] When Sierra decided to make Space Quest 6, he was busy programming [[1993 - Police Quest - Open Season|Police Quest: Open Season]], so Josh Mandel was asked to design it. Murphy consulted, then finished the game after Mandel left Sierra.[^ref-1][^ref-6] MobyGames credits him on Space Quest 6 for design, writing and voice direction.[^ref-8] He has been harsh about the later games: "SQ's 5 and 6 were abysmal in my opinion and I've felt some guilt about 6, even though I inherited a game primarily designed by someone else."[^ref-7]

According to Choicest Games, he started work on Space Quest 7 in 1997; it was never finished.[^ref-6] He was laid off in early 1999. "I didn't actually lose my job on Chainsaw Monday. I was dumped a month and a half previous to that."[^ref-7][^ref-1]

### After Sierra (1999–2012)

Murphy left the games industry. Asked in 2000 whether he was involved with it, he answered: "Nah, not anymore. Not in any way, shape or form, other than venting road rage through my racing simulators."[^ref-10] In 2006 he said that, with no adventure-game jobs to be had, "I had to resort to doing regular work like 'normal' people," in "pretty boring jobs in office settings and the like."[^ref-7]

### Guys from Andromeda (2012–present)

In April 2012 Murphy and Crowe announced they were working together again.[^ref-28][^ref-3] With Chris Pope they formed Guys from Andromeda and launched a Kickstarter campaign for SpaceVenture, a spiritual successor to Space Quest.[^ref-15][^ref-27] In 2015, while Murphy was away from the project, Crowe and Pope released part of it as a standalone game, [[2015 - Cluck Yegger in Escape From The Planet of The Poultroid|Cluck Yegger in Escape From The Planet of The Poultroid]].[^ref-49] [[2022 - SpaceVenture|SpaceVenture]] went to Kickstarter backers on September 16, 2022,[^ref-47] reached Steam Early Access on April 1, 2025, after a troubled development,[^ref-45] and had its full release on December 23, 2025.[^ref-48]

## Notable Works

### Space Quest: The Sarien Encounter (1986)

The first [[1986 - Space Quest - The Sarien Encounter|Space Quest]] introduced Roger Wilco, a janitor who saves the galaxy. IGN notes that Crowe (artist) and Murphy (programmer) had already worked on other AGI-based adventure titles at Sierra and wanted to break away from fantasy settings.[^ref-29]

### Space Quest IV: Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers (1991)

[[1991 - Space Quest IV - Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers|Space Quest IV]] was the last game of the original partnership. MobyGames credits Murphy as Project Leader, Lead Programmer and lead designer.[^ref-8][^ref-33] Crowe called it the pair's "masterpiece."[^ref-46]
