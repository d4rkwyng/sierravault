# Josh Mandel

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Josh Mandel (born 1958) is an American video game designer, writer, producer and voice actor, best known for his work at Sierra On-Line in the early 1990s.[^ref-4][^ref-23] He came to games from theater and stand-up comedy. He joined Sierra full-time in 1990 as a junior producer, after a couple of years of beta-testing for the company and writing game reviews and walkthroughs for magazines.[^ref-4][^ref-7]

His first Sierra project was producing the 1990 SCI remake of [[1990 - Roberta Williams' King's Quest I - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest I: Quest for the Crown]], for which he rewrote most of the text and dialogue and changed a couple of puzzles.[^ref-7][^ref-4] He was the first actor to voice King Graham, in the CD-ROM release of [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V]], and he reprised the role in [[1992 - King's Quest VI - Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow|King's Quest VI]].[^ref-7][^ref-56][^ref-23] He co-designed [[1993 - Freddy Pharkas - Frontier Pharmacist|Freddy Pharkas: Frontier Pharmacist]] with Al Lowe and led the design of [[1995 - Space Quest 6 - Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier|Space Quest 6: Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier]], which he left before it was finished.[^ref-19][^ref-7][^ref-13] He describes his aim as a designer as being to "reward exploration".[^ref-16] Adventure Gamers called him "one of the most underrated designers in genre history."[^ref-7]

MobyGames lists 112 credits for him on 60 games, spanning more than three decades.[^ref-5] Beyond Sierra, his career has included designing Callahan's Crosstime Saloon (1997) at Legend Entertainment, writing for Jagged Alliance 2 at Sir-Tech, a year as Director of Game Design at Sega for the Dreamcast launch, and writing and voice work for indie and fan-made adventure games.[^ref-5][^ref-9]

## Career

### Early Career

Before entering the gaming industry, Mandel pursued acting: "Well, all my life I'd wanted to be an actor."[^ref-7] He trained at Carnegie-Mellon and the American Conservatory Theatre before he turned 18, then took a theater degree in Madison, Wisconsin, graduating in 1981.[^ref-7] In Chicago he and comedy partner Karen McVeigh took classes with Paul Sills, whose mother, Viola Spolin, wrote "*the* textbook on theatrical improvisation".[^ref-7]

The duo became the "house comedy act" at the Playboy Club in Chicago, and soon headlined at the majority of comedy clubs in the country.[^ref-7][^ref-13] When McVeigh left comedy in 1986, Mandel went into advertising, aiming to specialize in comedic ads.[^ref-7] By his own account he "racked up a lot of awards" there, but rarely felt any sense of accomplishment from the work.[^ref-4]

Mandel's first encounter with computer games was the original Colossal Cave Adventure, played on an old system with no monitor that printed its responses on paper.[^ref-16] He later spent heavily on computer games and decided to make the hobby pay for itself through a combination of beta-testing (for both Sierra and Infocom) and journalism, writing game reviews and walkthroughs for magazines.[^ref-13]

### Sierra Years (1990–1995)

Mandel met Guruka Singh Khalsa, then Sierra's sole producer, on CompuServe's Gamers Forum and asked him about beta-testing. After a couple of years of testing, Guruka offered him a job as his junior producer in early 1990.[^ref-4][^ref-7] Ken Williams came out to meet him in the parking lot when he arrived for the interview.[^ref-7] After the visit, Mandel decided: "I have GOT to be a part of all this."[^ref-7] About eight months after he started, Ken tapped him for a design position. Mandel and his comedy partner had performed at Sierra's Christmas party that first year, and Ken came up to him after the show and said, "Hey, you're an entertaining kinda guy, you could design games."[^ref-4]

His first assignment was to finish the SCI remake of King's Quest I, which had languished for at least a year. His job was primarily to produce it, but with Roberta Williams's permission he rewrote most of the text and dialogue and changed a couple of puzzles to make them fairer, most obviously the Rumplestiltskin puzzle.[^ref-4][^ref-7] The remake used SCI01's 16-color EGA graphics and kept the text parser. It was the only one of Sierra's AGI-to-SCI remakes to do so.[^ref-67] Mandel later said that only after the King's Quest I remake was he "suddenly (and unpleasantly) thrust into the world of point-and-click".[^ref-16]

Mandel became the first voice of King Graham when the series gained speech in the CD-ROM release of King's Quest V.[^ref-7][^ref-56][^ref-23] He has said he never liked that performance, but that it was "more than compensated for by the sheer selfish pleasure of being a recognizable part of what was a formative experience for so many players."[^ref-7] He returned as Graham in King's Quest VI, where he also modelled for Shamir Shamazel.[^ref-7][^ref-5]

He was also put in charge of localizing Game Arts' [[1990 - Zeliard|Zeliard]] and Fire Hawk: Thexder - The Second Contact for the American market.[^ref-4][^ref-5] He contributed writing and voice acting to numerous projects, partly because he enjoyed not being "pigeonholed on just one game."[^ref-13] Eager to be involved creatively, he asked every designer with a game in production if he could help with writing or design.[^ref-13]

For [[1991 - Space Quest IV - Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers|Space Quest IV]], Scott Murphy and Mark Crowe set Mandel to writing the Bargain Bin text, the Radio Shock online catalog text, the fake Hintbook text, and look messages for many locations on Xenon, Estros, and the Galleria.[^ref-13] As he put it, "the Two Guys had such a no-holds-barred attitude, hence the fact that Sierra's lawyer would break out in an icy sweat every time he entered the SQ area."[^ref-13] He also voiced the Monolith Burger Manager in the game's voiced CD-ROM edition, a role he has called "personally a very big deal".[^ref-65]

Mandel worked with Bruce Balfour and Lorelei Shannon on the writing of [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]],[^ref-9][^ref-7] and voiced several of its characters, including Rocco the Taxi Driver, Steve Dorian, and Rex the Talking Dinosaur.[^ref-5] He co-designed and co-wrote [[1993 - Freddy Pharkas - Frontier Pharmacist|Freddy Pharkas: Frontier Pharmacist]] with Al Lowe.[^ref-19] Under Sierra's "star system", he said, a newer designer who did well on an established designer's game was in line to get a game of their own: "how I got SQ6 after Freddy Pharkas."[^ref-7]

[[1995 - Space Quest 6 - Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier|Space Quest 6]] grew out of Mandel's proposal "Where in Corpsman Santiago is Roger Wilco?", which Ken and Roberta Williams selected. From the beginning, Mandel wanted Scott Murphy as co-designer.[^ref-7] Mandel left Sierra before the game was finished. By his account the design "was essentially finished, although we couldn't agree on how to end the damn thing", while the text "was really just beginning".[^ref-13] He has said that his "decision to depart had nothing to do with SQ6". He was due to become a contract designer and move to Bellevue, but management reneged on terms written into his contract, and he resigned.[^ref-4][^ref-7] The box credited only Murphy, although the documentation inside credited both men as designers.[^ref-4] The game was marketed as a "Scott Murphy solo job". Murphy later apologized: "I want to publicly apologize to Josh for this. It was a difficult situation for both of us. The 'solo job' thing I honestly had no clue about until I got my packaged copy of the game. Anyway, Josh did get screwed over and I hope he accepts my too-late apology for any part I may have played. I made a mistake I'm not proud of."[^ref-6]

When Mandel left, Sierra was going through "lots of new managers in a relatively short timespan", and development was being "fragmented and moved piecemeal to 'Sierra North' (Bellevue, WA)."[^ref-13] Looking back, he said: "From my perspective, I came before everything changed for the worse. From the perspective of so many other people at Sierra, everything had already changed for the worse before I got there."[^ref-7] Before moving away he did "my little guest spot in [[1995 - Phantasmagoria|Phantasmagoria]]".[^ref-7]

### Post-Sierra Career (1995–Present)

After leaving Sierra, Mandel joined Legend Entertainment, having talked with co-founder Bob Bates about it for the better part of a year.[^ref-7] As the only designer there who could not program, he felt for a long time "like I was out of my depth", but he said he learned much more about game design itself at Legend, where almost the entire company was invited to review each game design.[^ref-7] He tested Mission Critical, and late in the development of [[1995 - Shannara|Shannara]] (designed by Corey and Lori Ann Cole) he wrote additional text to make the game more responsive.[^ref-7][^ref-9] After David Eddings turned down his proposal for a Belgariad game, Mandel suggested Spider Robinson or Dean Koontz; Legend chose Robinson, and Mandel went on to design and write Callahan's Crosstime Saloon (1997).[^ref-7] He had considered adapting the Callahan's Place books since the 1980s.[^ref-35]

He then freelanced. He contributed additional case designs to Broderbund's Where in Time is Carmen Sandiego? (1997).[^ref-5] At Sir-Tech he wrote dialogue for Jagged Alliance 2 (1999),[^ref-7][^ref-9] and MobyGames also credits him as a writer on its follow-ups Unfinished Business (2000) and Wildfire (2004).[^ref-5]

He spent a year as Director of Game Design at Sega for the launch of the Dreamcast, working alongside Kurt Busch, former editor of Sierra's InterAction magazine.[^ref-9] He is credited as a script editor on Sonic Adventure (1998).[^ref-5] In 1999 he moved to Albany, New York, to work at Vicarious Visions on Game Boy and PlayStation products.[^ref-7][^ref-9] His Game Boy Color design credits there include The Wild Thornberrys: Rambler (2000) and Blue's Clues: Blue's Alphabet Book (2001).[^ref-5]

Mandel appeared on screen and in voice in Terminus (2000), and is credited as a consultant on Hungry Red Planet (2002).[^ref-5] He is credited for the Stereoscope in John Saul's Blackstone Chronicles: An Adventure in Terror (1998).[^ref-5] He also spent a long time working with Radical Entertainment on Crash of the Titans, which, he said, "by the time it got published, bore no resemblance to the game we started out creating."[^ref-7]

In the 2000s, Mandel wrote for Hour of Victory (2007–2008), a World War II first-person shooter.[^ref-5] On Insecticide (2008), a detective adventure for Nintendo DS and Windows, he handled detective game design and script writing.[^ref-46][^ref-5]

In the 2010s Mandel returned to adventure games through indie projects and remakes. From 2012 to 2013 he worked for Replay Games as designer, writer and producer on [[2013 - Leisure Suit Larry - Reloaded|Leisure Suit Larry: Reloaded]] alongside Al Lowe, and also voiced the Comedian character.[^ref-23][^ref-47][^ref-5] He is credited as Chief Creative Officer and Consulting Designer on [[2013 - Fester Mudd - Curse of the Gold|Fester Mudd: Curse of the Gold]].[^ref-5]

Mandel contributed writing and lore to Ember (2016), an RPG developed by N-Fusion Interactive, and additional writing to The St Christopher's School Lockdown (2017).[^ref-5] On [[2018 - Hero-U - Rogue to Redemption|Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption]], developed by his former Sierra colleagues Corey and Lori Cole, he "wrote all the messages for interacting with the items in the environments".[^ref-65] The game's writer credit for him reads "which scarcely begins to express his contributions".[^ref-5] For [[2022 - SpaceVenture|SpaceVenture]] he wrote "the Holotube shows, commercials, song, etc".[^ref-65][^ref-5]

Around 2011, Agustín Cordes asked Mandel for a cameo voice in Asylum, then cast him as the voice of its lead after hearing his samples.[^ref-7] Asylum was released in 2025. Adventure Game Hotspot's review notes that the game changed its main character's voice "a mere week before launch".[^ref-66] We have found no source confirming whether Mandel's performance is in the released game.

Mandel has reprised King Graham in fan remakes, including AGD Interactive's King's Quest I VGA (2001), King's Quest II+: Romancing the Stones (2002), the Infamous Adventures King's Quest III Remake (2006), and King's Quest III Redux: To Heir is Human (2011).[^ref-5] The AGD team, he said, "wanted it just like the Graham in KQ5".[^ref-4]

## Notable Works

### King's Quest I: Quest for the Crown SCI Remake (1990)

Mandel produced the 1990 SCI remake of [[1990 - Roberta Williams' King's Quest I - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest I]]; MobyGames credits him as Producer.[^ref-5] He asked Roberta Williams if he could rewrite the text, which he found "not very colorful". She agreed, provided she approved the changes. He rewrote most of the text and dialogue and changed one or two puzzles, including the "infamous Rumplestiltskin puzzle".[^ref-4][^ref-7] The remake ran on SCI01 with 16-color EGA graphics and a text parser.[^ref-67] It had no voice acting; Mandel's first turn as King Graham came in the CD-ROM release of King's Quest V.[^ref-56][^ref-7]

### Space Quest 6: Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier (1995)

Mandel's proposal became [[1995 - Space Quest 6 - Roger Wilco in the Spinal Frontier|Space Quest 6]], and he worked on its design with Scott Murphy as co-designer.[^ref-7] MobyGames credits him for design and writing.[^ref-5] He left before the text was complete, so the finished game is only partly his. He has said "it's very easy to tell what parts of the game I did and what parts I didn't": "I'm a feedback junkie, as you can tell when you play the rooms I did manage to work on before leaving (like the Turboshaft, or Boot Liquors)."[^ref-4] He was disappointed that a comic book CD he had written, which held the hints for the Datacorder puzzle, was cut, so the hints had to go in the manual. The shipped ending also came as "a total surprise" to him.[^ref-4]

### Freddy Pharkas: Frontier Pharmacist (1993)

Co-designed with Al Lowe, [[1993 - Freddy Pharkas - Frontier Pharmacist|Freddy Pharkas: Frontier Pharmacist]] is a comic Western about a former gunslinger turned pharmacist.[^ref-19] Mandel was assigned to Lowe as his apprentice. Lowe wrote the initial design, and Mandel assembled the team. He then wrote the manual and almost everything in the game except the "critical path" dialogues, plus the opening and closing ballads. The prescriptions doubled as the manual-based copy protection Ken Williams required.[^ref-7] He called it "probably the most exciting and exhausting experience I've ever had on a game."[^ref-7]

### Callahan's Crosstime Saloon (1997)

At Legend Entertainment, Mandel designed and wrote Callahan's Crosstime Saloon (1997), based on Spider Robinson's Callahan's Place stories. MobyGames also credits him with interface design and art direction.[^ref-63][^ref-5][^ref-35] He named it his personal favorite among his games, because it was "closest to my sense of humor."[^ref-13] He liked that "the jokes don't all depend on the three overused topics of most computer games: blood, shit, and various reproductive fluids."[^ref-13] Publisher Take Two shipped a beta version instead of the final one and advertised the game as a Western.[^ref-4][^ref-7]

### Jagged Alliance 2 (1999)

At Sir-Tech, Mandel wrote dialogue for the tactical strategy game Jagged Alliance 2 (1999).[^ref-7] MobyGames lists him among the writers of the main game and of Unfinished Business (2000) and Wildfire (2004).[^ref-5]

## Design Philosophy

Mandel says he avoids "working on games that don't allow me to reward exploration".[^ref-16] He has explained: "the two greatest satisfactions I get when playing a game are the satisfaction I get when I solve a puzzle, and the satisfaction I get out of exploration: looking at things, trying to use things on other things in the environment."[^ref-4] To him, "when there's no feedback, the game world feels dead".[^ref-4] One reviewer called this style "Mandellian design", which he took as "a great compliment".[^ref-16]

He prefers single-player storytelling: "I still find multiplayer games to be a miserable way to tell a story."[^ref-4] He has also criticized the move away from text parsers. Once "point-and-click became the standard", he said, most players "lost interest in puzzles that couldn't be solved immediately, on-screen, through trial-and-error".[^ref-4] More recently: "point-and-click has become the standard, which irritates me daily."[^ref-16]

On the genre's image, Mandel observed: "I think that the name 'adventure game' carries an implication that the game is going to be slow and unappealing to young players."[^ref-9]

Asked how to break into the industry, he advised: "do not be afraid to take a much lower position on the ladder and work up slowly from there."[^ref-16]

## Legacy

Adventure Gamers' 2013 interview called Mandel "one of the most underrated designers in genre history."[^ref-7] As the first voice of King Graham, he has been cast in the role again and again by fan remakes.[^ref-5][^ref-4] Many fans felt he was not fairly credited for Space Quest 6, and Scott Murphy later apologized publicly for the "solo job" billing.[^ref-4][^ref-6]

Mandel remained keen to work with his former Sierra colleagues: "Absolutely! I'd jump at a chance to work with the Sierra people again. ... To me, these people are not only great designers, but they were groundbreakers as well. The computer game field was still very young in those days, and much of what's done today owes a debt of gratitude to these pioneers. I was proud to work with them."[^ref-9]

### Recent Community Engagement (2025–2026)

Mandel appeared in the *Happy Hotspot Holidays 2025* developer's season greetings video produced by Adventure Game Hotspot, alongside other adventure-game veterans.[^ref-64]

On 27 September 2025, Mandel opened an **"AMA About Sierra!"** thread on the Adventure Game Hotspot community forum. He kept answering questions there into 2026, with replies dated as late as April 2026.[^ref-65] Notable disclosures from the thread included:

- **On Freddy Pharkas crunch:** He and others were "sleeping in our office and putting in 20-hour days". Seeing the first Freddy boxes being assembled, with relief, dread and pride, "was just a very hypnotic and overwhelming moment for me."[^ref-65]
- **On meeting Ken Williams:** His second-favorite Sierra memory: "When I pulled up in a rented car to interview there, and Ken came out and introduced himself!"[^ref-65]
- **On his Sierra roles:** Playing Graham was "kind of life-altering, really. And playing the Monolith Manager in SQ4 was also personally a very big deal."[^ref-65]
- **On creative preferences:** "I have always enjoyed the writing side more." He noted that his father had been a Golden Age TV writer.[^ref-65]
- **On Callahan's Crosstime Saloon:** "a game that was very affirming, and about friendship, was swimming against the tide."[^ref-65]
