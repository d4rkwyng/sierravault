# Randy Dersham

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Randy Dersham is a game developer and executive who built the art department at Dynamix in Eugene, Oregon, and later ran the studio.[^ref-20][^ref-13] He was art director on [[1990 - Rise of the Dragon|Rise of the Dragon]] (1990) and designed and produced the Trophy Bass fishing games.[^ref-11][^ref-21] Ken Williams, Sierra's co-founder, remembered him as "the head of our Dynamix subsidiary in Oregon. An incredibly creative guy!", adding that "he was still running Dynamix" when the Williamses sold Sierra in 1996.[^ref-3] MobyGames credits him on 35 games.[^ref-2]

After Dynamix he worked at Electronic Arts and Acclaim, ran Playdom's Eugene studio, and was a general manager at Zynga.[^ref-21][^ref-14][^ref-22] He has since turned to documentary film: *Oregon's Boat*, about the McKenzie River drift boat, premiered in December 2024.[^ref-19]

## Career

### Dynamix

Dersham studied photography at the University of Oregon: "I graduated from the University of Oregon as a photography major. Later, I got into the computer game business starting off building an art department for a new studio in Eugene, OR."[^ref-20] His earliest MobyGames credits are for photography: an "Artists' Photo" credit on Arcticfox (1986) and photography on [[1989 - Deathtrack|Deathtrack]] (1989).[^ref-21][^ref-8]

He was Director of Image Production on [[1989 - A-10 Tank Killer|A-10 Tank Killer]] and [[1989 - David Wolf - Secret Agent|David Wolf: Secret Agent]] (both 1989), [[1990 - Red Baron|Red Baron]] (1990) and [[1992 - Aces of the Pacific|Aces of the Pacific]] (1992), and Image Department Director on [[1990 - Stellar 7|Stellar 7]] (1990).[^ref-21] On [[1990 - Rise of the Dragon|Rise of the Dragon]] he was art director: "Under the art direction of Randy Dersham, Robert Caracol created all the concept art and characters," wrote Adventure Classic Gaming.[^ref-11] On [[1991 - Heart of China|Heart of China]] (1991) he is credited for image production and the Hong Kong video, and IMDb lists him in the role of "Old salt."[^ref-21][^ref-8]

He then moved into production and design. MobyGames credits him as director and designer of [[1993 - Turbo Learning - Mega Math|Turbo Learning: Mega Math]] (1993), producer of [[1994 - Lode Runner - The Legend Returns|Lode Runner: The Legend Returns]] (1994), Windows and Macintosh producer of [[1994 - The Incredible Toon Machine|The Incredible Toon Machine]] (1994), and music on [[1994 - The Incredible Machine 2|The Incredible Machine 2]] (1994).[^ref-21] He designed and produced [[1995 - Trophy Bass|Trophy Bass]] (1995), was a director and designer of [[1996 - Front Page Sports - Trophy Bass 2|Front Page Sports: Trophy Bass 2]] (1996), and executive producer and designer of [[1997 - Front Page Sports - Trophy Rivers|Front Page Sports: Trophy Rivers]] (1997).[^ref-21] On [[1998 - Police Quest - SWAT 2|Police Quest: SWAT 2]] (1998) he was Business Unit Manager.[^ref-21]

Ken Williams later described Dersham as "the studio head for Dynamix (In Oregon)" around the time of Sierra's 1996 sale to CUC.[^ref-13][^ref-3]

### Electronic Arts and Acclaim

MobyGames credits him as executive producer and a member of the "EA Design Team" on Deer Hunt Challenge (1999), Championship Bass (2000), Ultimate Hunt Challenge Pack (2000) and Matt Hayes' Fishing (2002). He also has "Original Concept" credits on [[1999 - Field & Stream - Trophy Bass 3D|Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 3D]] (1999) and [[2000 - Field & Stream - Trophy Bass 4|Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 4]] (2000).[^ref-21] He was then general manager on three games developed by Acclaim Studios Austin: NBA Jam (2003), All-Star Baseball 2005 and Showdown: Legends of Wrestling (both 2004). He was studio general manager on The Red Star.[^ref-21][^ref-3]

### Playdom and Zynga

In August 2009 Dersham opened Playdom's Eugene office, which had about 40 employees when Oregon Business profiled it. "This is the beginning of another high-growth time" for game development in Eugene, he said.[^ref-14] The studio's Facebook city-builder Social City won Best Social Network Game at the first GDC Online Awards in 2010; the award lists Alex Swanson, Dan Yue and Randy Dersham.[^ref-15] Disney had acquired Playdom by then.[^ref-14]

In January 2012 Zynga hired "Disney/Playdom's former VP-executive producer Randy Dersham as general manager."[^ref-22] There he ran the studio behind FrontierVille. A colleague's LinkedIn recommendation says the team "exceeded ambitious revenue goals over the course of five consecutive quarters" as the game became an elder title.[^ref-16] Another former colleague, Jesse Janosov, wrote: "Randy is aggressive & competitive, experienced, and a savvy business leader. Randy is great people manager & has good instincts & product sense, and I'd work with him again anytime."[^ref-16]

## Notable Works

### Rise of the Dragon (1990)

Dersham was art director on [[1990 - Rise of the Dragon|Rise of the Dragon]], Dynamix's cyberpunk adventure, whose hand-drawn art had "a real graphic-novel feel."[^ref-11]

### Trophy Bass Series (1995–2000)

Dersham designed and produced [[1995 - Trophy Bass|Trophy Bass]], co-designed and directed [[1996 - Front Page Sports - Trophy Bass 2|Trophy Bass 2]], and is credited with the original concept of the two Field & Stream sequels.[^ref-21] He is himself a keen boatman; in 2009 he wrote that he was "hooked on helping people build their own wooden boat for white water."[^ref-4]

## Personal Life and Other Interests

Around 2009 Dersham described himself as co-owner, with his son, of Tatman Wooden Boats, adding: "My day job is as an executive in the computer games industry helping great games get made by very talented people."[^ref-4][^ref-6] Explaining modern wooden drift boats that year, he said: "Today, using polyurethanes, you essentially have a fiberglass encapsulated boat."[^ref-6] His profile lists him as executive director of the McKenzie River Drift Boat Museum from 2006 to 2018.[^ref-16]

His documentary *Oregon's Boat* premiered in Springfield, Oregon, in December 2024.[^ref-19] By 2025 it had "won several awards and honorable mentions."[^ref-20]
