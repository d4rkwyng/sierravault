# Blackbird Interactive

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Blackbird Interactive is an independent Canadian video game developer based in Vancouver, British Columbia, founded by industry veterans including senior EA staff and founders of [[Relic Entertainment]]; Rob Cunningham, art director on the original Homeworld games, became its CEO.[^ref-1][^ref-7] The studio's own site gives 2007 as its founding year, while MobyGames and Cunningham himself date its start to 2010.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-18] The studio has developed two *Homeworld* games, both published by Gearbox, which bought the franchise in 2013: the prequel [[2016 - Homeworld - Deserts of Kharak|Homeworld: Deserts of Kharak]] (2016) and [[2024 - Homeworld 3|Homeworld 3]] (2024).[^ref-3][^ref-4][^ref-8]

While Blackbird is not itself a Sierra On-Line lineage studio, its continuation of the *Homeworld* franchise — originally developed by Relic and published by Sierra Studios in 1999 — places it firmly within the post-Sierra extended catalog documented by this archive.[^ref-5] The studio has worked with publishers including Gearbox Publishing, Focus Entertainment, and Prime Matter and Smilegate.[^ref-4][^ref-20][^ref-19]

## Founding and Early Years

The studio's 2015 About page says Blackbird "was founded in 2007" by "a team of experienced and successful video game industry veterans including senior EA staff and founders of Relic Entertainment".[^ref-1] MobyGames instead says "The company was founded in 2010",[^ref-2] and Rob Cunningham told Eurogamer in 2022: "When I started Blackbird it was 2010".[^ref-18] Chief technical officer Yossarian King's 2016 Unity "Made With" story bridges the two dates: it says Cunningham founded Blackbird in 2007, that King met him "over coffee in late 2009", and that "five of us set up shop in Rob's garage in October 2010".[^ref-25] The studio's 2016 team page says Cunningham, an original founding member of Relic and art director on Homeworld and Homeworld 2, left Relic in 2007 and established the eatART Foundation before "forming a new game studio with familiar faces"; its chief technical officer, Yossarian King, came from EA Canada's central tools group, and its chief creative officer, Aaron Kambeitz, was also one of the founding members of Relic and the lead artist on *Homeworld*.[^ref-7] Cunningham has said that when he started Blackbird "we weren't thinking about Homeworld 3 at all", because the franchise then belonged to THQ.[^ref-18]

The studio's first project was *Hardware: Shipbreakers*, a ground-based real-time strategy game, begun in 2010 and originally planned as free-to-play, that was inspired by *Homeworld* without carrying the IP.[^ref-1][^ref-8] In 2013 Gearbox Software won the rights to the *Homeworld* franchise at THQ's bankruptcy auction with a $1.35 million bid,[^ref-9] and licensed the IP to Blackbird so that *Hardware: Shipbreakers* could become an official *Homeworld* prequel.[^ref-8][^ref-3]

## Sierra-Adjacent Work

### Homeworld: Deserts of Kharak (2016)

Released in January 2016, [[2016 - Homeworld - Deserts of Kharak|Homeworld: Deserts of Kharak]] served as a ground-based prequel set on the homeworld of Kharak before the events of the original *Homeworld*.[^ref-3][^ref-11] It holds a Metacritic score of 79, "Generally Favorable".[^ref-12]

### Homeworld 3 (2024)

A Fig crowdfunding campaign launched in 2019 raised over $1.5 million from more than 8,400 backers,[^ref-13] and Blackbird developed [[2024 - Homeworld 3|Homeworld 3]] as a sequel to *Homeworld 2*. The game was released on May 13, 2024, published by Gearbox Publishing.[^ref-4]

## Other Notable Work

Outside the *Homeworld* franchise, Blackbird has developed:

- **Hardspace: Shipbreaker** (2022) — A spaceship salvage simulation, released May 24, 2022 and originally published by Focus Entertainment.[^ref-15][^ref-20] In August 2025 Blackbird acquired the full IP rights from Focus, and Steam now lists Blackbird as publisher.[^ref-20][^ref-15]
- **Crossfire: Legion** (2022) — A real-time strategy game set in the *Crossfire* universe, published by Prime Matter and Smilegate; early access May 24, 2022, full release December 8, 2022, delisted from Steam on August 18, 2025.[^ref-19][^ref-22]
- **Minecraft Legends** (2023) — An action-strategy spin-off of *Minecraft*, developed by Mojang Studios and Blackbird Interactive, published by Xbox Game Studios and released April 18, 2023.[^ref-21][^ref-6]

## Later Years

From September to December 2021 Blackbird trialled a four-day work week with two of its teams; in February 2022 it announced that all projects would move to four-day weeks from April 2022.[^ref-8][^ref-2]

In August 2023 the studio laid off 41 employees after "several unannounced projects" were cancelled.[^ref-23] In February 2024 it confirmed a second round of layoffs, its second in six months, while working on *Earthless* and *Homeworld 3*.[^ref-24]

## Legacy

Blackbird links the *Homeworld* series back to its origins: it was founded by Relic veterans including Homeworld's art director, and it continues a series that Relic developed and Sierra Studios published in 1999.[^ref-7][^ref-5] Rock Paper Shotgun's review of Homeworld 3 credited it with keeping "most of the atmosphere and tone" of the series, while faulting it for playing it too safe.[^ref-17]
