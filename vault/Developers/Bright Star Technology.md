# Bright Star Technology

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Bright Star Technology, Inc. was an educational software company based in Bellevue, Washington.[^ref-21] It was founded during the early 1980s by Elon Gasper and Nedra Goedert, and its best-known products included the HyperAnimation technology, Alphabet Blocks and the Talking Tutors series.[^ref-1][^ref-12] Sierra On-Line acquired the company in July 1992.[^ref-12][^ref-21]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Wikipedia and the Corum Group both date the founding only to "the early 1980s"; neither gives a year.[^ref-1][^ref-12] Wikipedia says Gasper had been teaching computer science at Caltech while trying to teach his daughter to read, and that Disney animators' lip-synchronisation charts inspired him to create animated tutors whose mouths moved realistically.[^ref-1] Encyclopedia.com's company history describes Bright Star as "an educational software firm founded by programmer Elon Gasper".[^ref-15]

### Sierra Era

Sierra acquired Bright Star in July 1992.[^ref-12] According to Encyclopedia.com, the deal came "just as the educational software market was becoming the fastest-growing segment of the software industry"; Bright Star gained improved distribution and marketing, Sierra built on its HyperAnimation, Talking Tiles and Alphabet Blocks products, and Bright Star hired 19 new employees in the first year, "quadrupling the work force".[^ref-15] After the acquisition Gasper became Sierra's VP of Educational Games.[^ref-22]

Alphabet Blocks was first released for the Macintosh on October 16, 1992, and Early Math followed in 1993.[^ref-23][^ref-24] Bright Star's other titles include Kid's Typing, Spelling Jungle, Spelling Blizzard and The Lost Mind of Dr. Brain.[^ref-1] Wikipedia also says A.J.'s World of Discovery was developed by Sierra's subsidiaries Coktel Vision and Bright Star Technology.[^ref-1]

No source found gives a date when Bright Star stopped operating.
