# Bright Star Technology

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Bright Star Technology, Inc. was an educational software company based in Bellevue, Washington.[^ref-21][^ref-26] It was founded during the early 1980s by Elon Gasper and Nedra Goedert, and its best-known products included the HyperAnimation technology, Alphabet Blocks and the Talking Tutors series.[^ref-1][^ref-12] Sierra On-Line acquired the company in July 1992 and ran it as a wholly owned subsidiary.[^ref-12][^ref-21][^ref-29][^ref-30]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Wikipedia dates the founding only to "the early 1980s" and gives no year; the Corum Group's description uses the same words as Wikipedia's lead, so it is not an independent second source.[^ref-1][^ref-12] The Washington Post reported in December 1993 that "ten years ago" Gasper had been teaching computer science at the California Institute of Technology while trying to help his daughter learn to read; a chart of the lip-synchronisation positions used in Disney animation gave him the idea for Bright Star's animated tutors.[^ref-26][^ref-1]

Gasper's US Patent 4,884,972, "Speech synchronized animation", was filed on November 26, 1986 and granted on December 5, 1989; USPTO records show Gasper and Goedert assigning it to Bright Star Technology, Inc. of Washington, effective November 22, 1988.[^ref-27] Encyclopedia.com's company history describes Bright Star as "an educational software firm founded by programmer Elon Gasper".[^ref-15]

### Sierra Era

Sierra acquired Bright Star in July 1992.[^ref-12][^ref-29] Jimmy Maher of The Digital Antiquarian gives the price as $1 million.[^ref-25] He writes that Bright Star brought technology for syncing recorded voices to the mouths of on-screen characters, and that King's Quest VI was the first Sierra game to use it.[^ref-25] According to Encyclopedia.com, the deal came "just as the educational software market was becoming the fastest-growing segment of the software industry"; Bright Star gained improved distribution and marketing, Sierra built on its HyperAnimation, Talking Tiles and Alphabet Blocks products, and Bright Star hired 19 new employees in the first year, "quadrupling the work force".[^ref-15] After the acquisition Gasper became Sierra's VP of Educational Games.[^ref-22] Popular Science (May 1993) described Bright Star's Alphabet Blocks as a DOS and Macintosh program that teaches letters and sounds through a talking tutor, using "Hyperanimation" to give the tutor humanlike facial movements while it speaks.[^ref-31]

When Sierra moved its corporate headquarters to Bellevue in 1993, Maher notes that Bright Star's presence there was one of the area's draws.[^ref-25] Sierra's InterAction magazine reported in Holiday 1993 that management was "temporarily taking refuge in the offices of Bright Star" while a new headquarters was built, and the same issue describes Bright Star Technology, Inc. as "a wholly owned subsidiary of Sierra On-Line, Inc."[^ref-30] The Washington Post's December 1993 survey of children's software placed Bright Star in Bellevue, named its "Dream Team" line, and reviewed Kid's Typing.[^ref-26]

Alphabet Blocks was first released for the Macintosh on October 16, 1992, and Early Math followed in 1993.[^ref-23][^ref-24] Bright Star's other titles include Kid's Typing, Spelling Jungle, Spelling Blizzard and The Lost Mind of Dr. Brain.[^ref-1] MobyGames credits Bright Star on seven games from 1992 to 1994, including Math and Spelling with Monker (1994), which has no vault page.[^ref-29] A February 1994 ST Report review says [[1992 - A.J.'s World of Discovery|A.J.'s World of Discovery]] "was developed by Sierra's subsidiaries Coktel Vision and Bright Star Technology", though the vault's game page lists Coktel Vision as the sole developer.[^ref-32][^ref-1]

USPTO assignment records show Bright Star's patents passing to Sierra On-Line, and record a merger of Bright Star Technology, Inc. into Sierra, both effective October 29, 1999 (reel/frame 010461/0895 and 015541/0374).[^ref-27][^ref-28] This dates the end of the legal entity, not necessarily the end of its operations; no source found says when Bright Star stopped working as a separate studio.

## Key People

- **Elon Gasper**: co-founder; inventor on Bright Star's speech-synchronised animation patents; afterwards Sierra's VP of Educational Games.[^ref-1][^ref-22][^ref-27][^ref-28]
- **Nedra Goedert**: co-founder.[^ref-1][^ref-27]
