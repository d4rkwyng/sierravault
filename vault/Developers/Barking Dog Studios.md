# Barking Dog Studios

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 15, 2026</small>

## Overview

Barking Dog Studios was a Canadian video game developer founded in 1998 by six former Radical Entertainment employees in Vancouver, British Columbia.[^ref-1] The studio gained recognition for developing Homeworld: Cataclysm, a standalone expansion to Relic Entertainment's space RTS Homeworld, which holds a Metacritic score of 89/100.[^ref-2] In addition to their Sierra work, Barking Dog contributed to Valve's Counter-Strike and developed their own tactical shooter Global Operations.[^ref-3]

The company name originated from a brainstorming session seeking a "non-corporate, non-pretentious" identity, after one of the founders recalled a pub in California called the Barking Dog.[^ref-27] The studio's logo, designed by co-founder Sean Thompson, featured a slightly deformed dog with a large head standing and barking,[^ref-4] which had not changed by the time the studio's reception area displayed it as a flashing neon logo.[^ref-27] In August 2002, Take-Two Interactive acquired Barking Dog, in a deal with an estimated total value of $9 million, and integrated it into Rockstar Games as Rockstar Vancouver.[^ref-5][^ref-28][^ref-20]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Barking Dog Studios was founded in 1998 by Glenn Barnes, Peter Grant, Michael Gyori, Christopher Mair, Brian Thalken, and Sean Thompson.[^ref-1] All six founders had previously worked at Radical Entertainment, leaving after the company faced financial difficulties in the late 1990s when their partnership with Disney faltered and they lost their ESPN license.[^ref-6] The studio's struggles prompted the formation of multiple new companies by departing employees, including Black Box Games and Barking Dog.[^ref-7]

The studio officially opened its doors on July 16, 1998, having just started its first major contract.[^ref-8] In its early years the studio briefly assisted Relic Entertainment with Homeworld (1999).[^ref-1]

### Homeworld: Cataclysm

Shortly after assisting with Homeworld, Barking Dog was greenlit to develop an expansion pack, Homeworld: Cataclysm.[^ref-1] Designer Chris Stewart later recalled that "Cataclysm existed in a weird space where Sierra wanted an expansion to the series, but Relic wanted it separated from the main line".[^ref-23] The game is set fifteen years after the original Homeworld and followed the mining vessel Kuun-Lan of Kiith Somtaaw as they encounter an ancient alien threat known as The Beast.[^ref-9] During development, Barking Dog moved into Relic Entertainment's offices, with roughly 20 employees engaged in the project.[^ref-10]

Sierra announced Cataclysm in February 2000,[^ref-11] and it was released that September.[^ref-2] The Adrenaline Vault awarded a perfect 100/100, while PC Gamer US gave it 91% and IGN scored it 9.2/10.[^ref-2] GameSpy wrote: "One of the best single-player campaigns ever makes this a great expansion to the Homeworld series."[^ref-2] The game was later re-released on GOG.com in June 2017 as "Homeworld: Emergence" after Blizzard Entertainment trademarked "Cataclysm" for their World of Warcraft expansion.[^ref-12]

### Counter-Strike Contribution

Around 1999, Valve became interested in Counter-Strike, a Half-Life mod co-created by Barking Dog employee Minh Le.[^ref-13] As Valve became more involved in the project, they hired Barking Dog to develop the mod's "Beta 5" update.[^ref-3] The studio developed approximately 90% of that update, which was released in December 1999.[^ref-14] Le subsequently joined Valve to continue Counter-Strike's development, with the finished game releasing in November 2000.[^ref-13]

### Later Projects

In November 2000, Barking Dog was rumored to be developing a tactical first-person shooter, which Crave Entertainment announced as Global Operations in December of that year.[^ref-15] Released in March 2002 and co-published by Crave Entertainment and Electronic Arts, Global Operations was a team-based tactical shooter that allowed players to engage in counter-terrorism operations across various global locations.[^ref-16]

Simultaneously, Barking Dog developed Treasure Planet: Battle at Procyon, a real-time strategy game tie-in with Disney's Treasure Planet film.[^ref-17] The game utilized ARES, a proprietary game engine the studio had been developing since January 2001.[^ref-18] Disney Interactive released the game in November 2002, after the studio's acquisition.[^ref-19]

### Acquisition and Legacy

On August 1, 2002, Take-Two Interactive announced its acquisition of Barking Dog Studios for US$3 million in cash and 242,450 shares of restricted common stock, an estimated $9 million total value.[^ref-5] The studio was integrated with Take-Two's Rockstar Games label and renamed Rockstar Vancouver, while Rockstar Canada was renamed Rockstar Toronto to avoid confusion.[^ref-20] At the time of acquisition, the studio employed roughly 50 people and was working on two games: a military action game and an original title.[^ref-5][^ref-20]

After the acquisition, several Rockstar Vancouver employees, including some of its founders, set up new studios, including Ironclad Games (2003), Kerberos Productions (2004), Slant Six Games (2005), Big Sandwich Games (2006), Hellbent Games (2006), and United Front Games (2007).[^ref-1] As Rockstar Vancouver, the studio developed Bully (2006) and co-developed Max Payne 3 (2012) before being merged into Rockstar Toronto in July 2012.[^ref-1][^ref-22]

## Key People

The six co-founders—Glenn Barnes, Peter Grant, Michael Gyori, Christopher Mair, Brian Thalken, and Sean Thompson—all came from Radical Entertainment.[^ref-1] MobyGames credits Michael Gyori as lead designer of Homeworld: Cataclysm, with Martin Cirulis, Christopher Mair and Christopher Stewart as designers.[^ref-29] Minh Le, known for co-creating Counter-Strike with Jess Cliffe, worked at Barking Dog before joining Valve.[^ref-13] Chris Stewart was later a producer at Kerberos Productions.[^ref-23]

## Legacy

Barking Dog was independent for about four years before Take-Two bought it in 2002.[^ref-1] Homeworld: Cataclysm holds a Metacritic score of 89, and GOG's description has a Hiigaran sect that "unwittingly releases an alien horror".[^ref-2][^ref-9] Studios set up by former staff include United Front Games and Ironclad Games.[^ref-1]

Cataclysm was left out of Gearbox's Homeworld Remastered Collection; Gearbox's Brian Martel said a re-release depended on "finding the original source code".[^ref-25] It returned on GOG.com in 2017 as Homeworld: Emergence.[^ref-12]
