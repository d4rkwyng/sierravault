# Phoenix Online Studios

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Phoenix Online Studios is an independent adventure game developer that grew out of the volunteer team behind The Silver Lining, a free fan game based on Sierra On-Line's King's Quest series.[^ref-15] MobyGames records that the group was originally called Phoenix Freeware Online and was formed in 2004 to work on The Silver Lining, and that Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller was its first commercial game.[^ref-3] Its 2011 and 2014 label announcements carry a San Jose dateline,[^ref-24][^ref-21] and in January 2014 PocketGamer.biz reported that Phoenix had studios in San Jose and in Mumbai, India.[^ref-17]

The studio's stated ambition was to do for Sierra what Telltale Games had done for LucasArts: "In the same way Telltale has been bringing back the LucasArts magic, we want to do the same with the Sierra magic," César Bittar told Gamasutra in 2010.[^ref-4] Its two in-house originals, The Silver Lining and Cognition, were both released episodically.[^ref-15][^ref-16] Its closest tie to a Sierra veteran was Gabriel Knight creator [[Jane Jensen]]. She was story consultant on Cognition, Phoenix developed her game Moebius: Empire Rising under her supervision, and Phoenix co-developed her Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers 20th Anniversary Edition remake with Pinkerton Road Studio, which Steam lists as its publisher.[^ref-16][^ref-20][^ref-32] In 2014 the company added a publishing label, [[Phoenix Online Publishing]].[^ref-21] Its YouTube channel still introduces it as the "creators of The Silver Lining and Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller".[^ref-12]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Production of The Silver Lining began in October 2000. After a long stretch of limited progress, the team was reorganized in January 2002 under César Bittar, who had joined as one of the project's writers. He split the crew into art, design, audio, programming and public relations departments, and that team became Phoenix.[^ref-15] Bittar had found the volunteer project while searching the web for news of the cancelled King's Quest IX, and he holds a digital media degree. Richard Flores, a 3D animator with a film background, joined as art director. By 2004 about 70 people were working on the game, some of them up to four hours a day outside their regular jobs.[^ref-4] MobyGames dates the group's formation as Phoenix Freeware Online to 2004 and lists that name for 2004–2006.[^ref-3]

In September 2005, soon after the team released its first trailer and announced a release date, Vivendi Universal sent a cease-and-desist. Phoenix halted work, and on 29 November 2005 Vivendi granted it a fan license on condition that "King's Quest" be removed from the title.[^ref-15] After Activision merged with Vivendi Games in 2008, it revoked that license and issued its own cease-and-desist. Gamasutra reported on 28 June 2010 that Activision had "at last given the go-ahead" to release the game for free.[^ref-31] The first episode was released on 10 July 2010.[^ref-4][^ref-15][^ref-29]

### Commercial Development Era

On 31 October 2011, Phoenix announced Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller, an original detective adventure that used no Activision-owned property. It was developed with the Dutch studio Khaeon Gamestudio, with Jane Jensen as story consultant, and launched on Kickstarter with a $25,000 goal.[^ref-24][^ref-26] Shacknews reported that the idea came from comic artist Romano Molenaar, Khaeon's art director, who approached Phoenix after playing The Silver Lining.[^ref-26] Many Silver Lining team members moved to paid work on Cognition after the third and fourth episodes, which stalled the free game.[^ref-15]

Phoenix then worked with [[Jane Jensen]]'s studio Pinkerton Road. Moebius: Empire Rising was crowdfunded by Pinkerton Road on Kickstarter in 2012 and programmed by Phoenix Online Studios.[^ref-18] Phoenix's own press release described Jensen as the game's "writer, designer, and creative director" and said Phoenix "developed the game under her supervision".[^ref-20] Phoenix also co-developed Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers 20th Anniversary Edition with Pinkerton Road Studio; Steam lists both studios as developers and Pinkerton Road Studio as publisher.[^ref-32] Phoenix's company page credits César Bittar as producer on both titles and Katie Hallahan as assistant designer.[^ref-2]

Phoenix Online never held a commercial King's Quest license. Activision allowed The Silver Lining to be released only as a free, non-commercial game.[^ref-15] Activision, which had owned King's Quest since its 2008 merger with Vivendi Games, revived its Sierra label in 2014 with a new King's Quest from The Odd Gentlemen, announced at Gamescom and first shown at The Game Awards that December.[^ref-15][^ref-28]

### Publishing Label (2014)

On 20 February 2014, Phoenix announced a new indie publishing label, [[Phoenix Online Publishing]]. Its first lineup was Moebius: Empire Rising, [[2014 - Quest for Infamy|Quest for Infamy]], The Last Door: Collector's Edition and Lost Civilization, alongside Cognition, Face Noir and The Silver Lining.[^ref-21][^ref-20] In January 2014, Scott Steinberg had been promoted to general manager, responsible for strategic planning and business development.[^ref-17] The company's own site describes the publisher as operating "in parallel with Phoenix Online Studios".[^ref-2] The Phoenix Online Studios name remains on its Steam developer and publisher pages.[^ref-6][^ref-10]

### Later Years

MobyGames says Phoenix Online Studios, LLC was formed after Cognition's 2013 release to handle the publishing business, and that at an unknown later point the studio was merged into Phoenix Online Publishing, with the Phoenix Online Studios name used as a label for internally developed games.[^ref-3] Its later label releases include Cornerstone: The Song of Tyrim (26 April 2016).[^ref-38] The newest post on the company's news blog, dated 3 September 2020, announces the PC release of Bleeding Moons, the debut game from IAN Games.[^ref-33][^ref-34]

**Status (checked 11 November 2026):** the newest blog post is dated 3 September 2020. No announcement of a closure was found, and this page does not know whether the studio still operates.[^ref-33]

## Notable Games

### The Silver Lining (2010)

"The Silver Lining" was Phoenix Online's debut release: a free, episodic fan game based on the King's Quest series, first released in July 2010, with four of its five planned episodes out by November 2011.[^ref-15] On 10 November 2016 Phoenix announced that the final episode would be released in 2017.[^ref-35] Its last progress report on Episode 5, dated 31 January 2019, described work on the episode's visuals, cloth simulation and puzzles.[^ref-36] Episodes 1 to 4 are offered free on the Phoenix Online Store.[^ref-37] No Episode 5 release has been found. The story follows King Graham as he seeks ancient druidic artifacts to undo the evil cast upon his children, Alexander and Rosella, and returns to the Green Isles first visited in King's Quest VI.[^ref-15]

### Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller (2012)

"Cognition" follows Boston FBI agent Erica Reed, who has postcognition, the ability to see into the past of any object she touches.[^ref-16] Funded through Kickstarter in 2011, it was released in four episodes between October 2012 and September 2013, with Gabriel Knight creator Jane Jensen serving as story consultant.[^ref-16] Episode 1, The Hangman, came out for Windows and Mac on 30 October 2012, published by Reverb Publishing.[^ref-30]

### Moebius: Empire Rising (2014)

Jane Jensen's "metaphysical thriller" follows antiquities dealer Malachi Rector. It was developed by Pinkerton Road Studio and Phoenix Online Studios and published by Phoenix Online Publishing.[^ref-18][^ref-22] Phoenix announced a 15 April 2014 PC and Mac release, and it was the first game from Jensen's crowdfunded studio Pinkerton Road.[^ref-22][^ref-23] Phoenix programmed and developed the game under Jensen's supervision.[^ref-18][^ref-20]

### Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers 20th Anniversary Edition (2014)

This remake of Jensen's 1993 Sierra adventure was co-developed by Pinkerton Road Studio and Phoenix Online Studios, with Pinkerton Road Studio listed as publisher on Steam, for PC and Mac on 15 October 2014. iOS and Android versions followed on 23 July 2015.[^ref-32][^ref-19] At launch it sold through Steam, GOG.com and the Phoenix Online Store.[^ref-27]

## Key People

- **César Bittar** (CEO). He was originally one of The Silver Lining's writers and took charge of the project team in January 2002.[^ref-15] He was producer and director on The Silver Lining and Cognition, and producer on Moebius: Empire Rising and the Gabriel Knight remake.[^ref-2] The company's profile says he previously worked with Telltale Games and Activision, on titles including Spider-Man 3, Tales of Monkey Island and Sam & Max Season 3.[^ref-2]
- **Richard Flores** (CTO). He was art director on The Silver Lining and is a 3D animator with a film background.[^ref-4] As CTO he oversees the company's art and programming.[^ref-2]
- **Katie Hallahan** (CCO and PR Director). She was co-designer of The Silver Lining and Cognition, director of Cognition's second episode, and assistant designer on Moebius and the Gabriel Knight remake. She also ran Cognition's Kickstarter campaign.[^ref-2]
- **Scott Steinberg** (GM, Publishing and Development). He was promoted to general manager in January 2014 to lead strategic planning and business development.[^ref-17][^ref-2]

## Legacy

Phoenix Online Studios grew from a volunteer fan project into a commercial developer and then a publisher: The Silver Lining began as a free King's Quest fan game, Cognition was its first commercial release, and in 2014 the company added the publishing label Phoenix Online Publishing.[^ref-15][^ref-3][^ref-21] Its clearest link to Sierra's legacy runs through Gabriel Knight creator Jane Jensen, who was story consultant on Cognition, supervised Phoenix's development of Moebius: Empire Rising, and shared the Gabriel Knight 20th Anniversary Edition remake with Phoenix.[^ref-16][^ref-20][^ref-32] The Silver Lining itself remains at four of its five planned episodes.[^ref-15][^ref-36]
