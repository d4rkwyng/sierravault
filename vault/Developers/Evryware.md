# Evryware

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

Evryware is the small game studio of brothers Dave and Barry Murry, who have described themselves as "creating computer games together since 1979."[^ref-21] It made three games that Sierra On-Line published: Sierra Championship Boxing (contracted in 1983 by the Murrys' account, and on sale by 1984) and the two Manhunter adventures, Manhunter: New York (1988) and Manhunter 2: San Francisco (1989).[^ref-21][^ref-22] Between them it made The Ancient Art of War series of strategy games for Brøderbund and MicroProse.[^ref-21]

Evryware was an independent developer, not a Sierra studio. Sierra published its games; it never owned the company. The Murrys kept the rights to The Ancient Art of War, while Sierra held Manhunter.[^ref-21] Wikipedia says the Manhunter rights are now held by Activision, following its 2008 acquisition of Sierra's intellectual property.[^ref-32] The Manhunter History page at Larry Laffer Dot Net says that "the association between Evryware and Sierra ended in 1989."[^ref-23]

The studio kept making games until 1997, when it released Pyrosaurus; MobyGames also credits it on The Ancient Art of War 2 (2014).[^ref-14][^ref-34] Its own chronology says the team then started a web design firm and later an independent record label.[^ref-22][^ref-21]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

In 1979 the Murry brothers built an air traffic control radar simulator "just for fun", modelling the traffic at McChord Air Force Base, where Barry was working as an air traffic controller.[^ref-21] About a year later they began making arcade-style games for the Heathkit H89 and, by their own account, made "about a dozen arcade games over the next couple of years."[^ref-21] Evryware's chronology lists "CP/M Games" for 1980–1982.[^ref-22] The company's 1992 manual for The Ancient Art of War in the Skies says it "developed and marketed eleven products for CP/M computers" before turning to the IBM PC.[^ref-35] A 1982 Evryware advertisement in Sextant, a magazine for Heath/Zenith users, offers "Interactive Fiction" for the H/Z89 and H8/H19, including Dragons of Hong Kong at $21.50 and Six Micro-Stories at $17.50, with a P.O. box in Sunnyvale, California; an undated Evryware catalogue scan lists further H/Z89 games such as Space Odyssey, Missile Control, Invasion and Y-Wing Fighter.[^ref-37][^ref-38] MobyGames credits Dragons of Hong Kong to Evryware in 1981.[^ref-14]

Sources differ on who founded the company. The 1997 Games Domain interview introduces Barry and Dave Murry as "the founders of Evryware," and MobyGames says the same.[^ref-21][^ref-14] Retro365 says instead that "in 1980 Dave and Joe Gargiulo, established Evryware."[^ref-16] The 1992 manual says "Dave and Barry Murry and Joe Gargiulo formed Evryware in 1980."[^ref-35] Evryware's own team page calls Dave Murry "Technical Director, Co-Founder."[^ref-6]

### Sierra Era

#### Sierra Championship Boxing

The brothers' first PC game was Championship Boxing. Microsoft planned to publish it through a new home software division but backed out at the last minute. Their contact at Microsoft was a friend of Ken Williams and told him about the game. In the brothers' words: "A few days later we had a contract with Sierra and they published Championship Boxing in 1983."[^ref-21] That 1983 date is the Murrys' recollection and the year of the Sierra contract. Evryware's chronology also lists Sierra Championship Boxing under 1983.[^ref-22] Period sources first show the game in spring and summer 1984: Computer Gaming World's Vol. 4 No. 2 lists "Sierra Championship Boxing" among Sierra On-Line's titles,[^ref-26] a mail-order ad in the April 1984 issue of PCjr Magazine lists "Championship Boxing" at $22,[^ref-27] and Computer Entertainer's July 1984 issue announced it as "planned shortly for IBM PC/PCjr, Apple, and later for C64."[^ref-28] Retro365 also dates the IBM PC release to 1984, the year it was reviewed,[^ref-16] and the 1992 manual says Dave and Barry "went on to create 'Championship Boxing' in 1984."[^ref-35] MobyGames lists the game under 1985.[^ref-14] Retro365 also notes that Sierra kept porting the game and was still marketing it in 1988.[^ref-16]

#### Manhunter

While they were working on The Ancient Art of War at Sea, "Sierra contacted us with an offer we couldn't refuse."[^ref-21] Retro365 dates Sierra's approach to late 1987.[^ref-33] Sierra offered them the game system it used for King's Quest and its other adventures, to make whatever kind of game they wanted.[^ref-21] The Murrys said that Sierra "gave us the engine and told us that we could do whatever we wanted with it. They didn't even see the game until it was almost done."[^ref-21]

The result was Manhunter: New York (1988), followed a year later by Manhunter 2: San Francisco.[^ref-21] The Murrys gave it a darker tone than other Sierra titles and used unusual camera angles, extreme close-ups, first-person views and picture-in-picture shots.[^ref-21] They said Manhunter was "the first Sierra title" with a no-text interface, adding: "We also went to EGA at this point which gave us 16 colors to use."[^ref-21] Real locations in New York and San Francisco "added realism to the game."[^ref-21] Wikipedia names Manhunter 2: San Francisco as Sierra's final title built on its Adventure Game Interpreter (AGI).[^ref-25]

According to the Murrys, both Ancient Art of War games and both Manhunter games each sold more than 100,000 copies.[^ref-21]

#### Manhunter 3 and the End of the Sierra Partnership

Manhunter 2 ended on a cliffhanger that pointed to a third game, Manhunter 3: London.[^ref-23] It was never made. The Larry Laffer Dot Net shrine says "the association between Evryware and Sierra ended in 1989 and the two companies never partnered up again," and that because no deal was ever struck to move the game into production, "it's probable that it never went beyond the conceptual stage."[^ref-23] In 1997 the Murrys said they would "like to eventually do another Manhunter," but did not have the time.[^ref-21]

### Later Years and Closure

The third Ancient Art of War game took much longer than planned. Joe Gargiulo, who had worked with the brothers in their H89 days, joined the team, and work had to restart when the graphics standard moved from EGA to VGA. MicroProse published The Ancient Art of War in the Skies in 1992.[^ref-21]

Evryware's chronology then lists SpaceKids (1994), Space Dude (1996) and Pyrosaurus (1997), a game in which players design and train their own dinosaurs and fight other players' creations over the internet.[^ref-22] The team page names the publishers: MicroProse for SpaceKids, FormGen for Space Dude, and Pyrosaurus as self-published.[^ref-6] MobyGames dates SpaceKids to 1995 rather than 1994.[^ref-14] (The team page gives 1996 for Pyrosaurus, but the Games Domain interview of June 1997 still calls it the Murrys' "latest title.")[^ref-6][^ref-21] The 1997 Pyrosaurus manual says the game "was developed and is distributed by" Evryware Inc., at a P.O. box in Olympia, Washington, and credits Dave, Barry and Dee Dee Murry, Jason Glover, Sean Farley and Todd Bishopp.[^ref-36] After Pyrosaurus the team "started a web design firm," and then created an independent record label.[^ref-22]

## Notable Games

### Sierra Championship Boxing (1983)

Players could build their own boxer or pick from historical fighters, coach from the corner between rounds, or play it arcade style.[^ref-21] InfoWorld called it "by far the most extensive sports game created for a microcomputer."[^ref-24] Retro365 says that Dave did the programming while Barry made the graphics and animation.[^ref-16] The release year is given as 1983 here to match the game's page; see the Sierra Era section for the dated evidence.

### The Ancient Art of War (1984)

Brøderbund published The Ancient Art of War in 1984, and the Murrys said it "was very successful both critically and commercially."[^ref-21] Wikipedia calls it "designed by Dave and Barry Murry of Evryware and published by Broderbund in 1984" and "one of the first real-time strategy or real-time tactics games."[^ref-29] The brothers planned a trilogy covering land, sea and air, and went straight on to The Ancient Art of War at Sea, which Brøderbund released in 1987; MicroProse published the third, The Ancient Art of War in the Skies, in 1992, on which Wikipedia credits Joe Gargiulo, Barry, Dave and Dee Dee Murry as designers.[^ref-21][^ref-30]

### Manhunter: New York (1988) and Manhunter 2: San Francisco (1989)

Evryware developed both for Sierra On-Line. Wikipedia credits Barry Murry, Dave Murry and Dee Dee Murry as designers of Manhunter: New York, with Ken Williams as producer, and gives its release as September 1988; its entry for Manhunter 2 dates that game to mid-1989 and lists the same three designers.[^ref-31][^ref-32] See the Sierra Era section for the Murrys' account of how the games were made.

## Key People

- **Dave Murry**: co-founder and technical director; programmer.[^ref-6][^ref-16]
- **Barry Murry**: co-founder; graphics and animation on Championship Boxing.[^ref-21][^ref-16]
- **Dee Dee Murry**: the brothers' sister and Evryware's lead artist. The 1997 interview places her joining around the release of Manhunter: New York ("our sister, Dee Dee joined the team"), while the team page says she has been lead artist "since 1984" and worked on the Ancient Art of War and Manhunter series. The 1992 manual says she joined in 1987.[^ref-21][^ref-6][^ref-35]
- **Joe Gargiulo**: an early collaborator from the H89 era who joined the team for The Ancient Art of War in the Skies; Retro365 names him as a co-founder. The 1997 interview spells his surname "Garguilo". The 1992 manual says he left after one IBM product and teamed up with the Murrys again in 1988 for The Ancient Art of War in the Skies.[^ref-21][^ref-16][^ref-35]
- **Mike Murry**: Creative Director and Project Manager, per the team page, which says he joined the team during the development of SpaceKids in 1994.[^ref-6]
- **Matt Murry**: Environmental Programmer, per the team page, which says he brings knowledge of game theory, shaders and scripting.[^ref-6]

## Legacy

Evryware's best-known work outside Sierra is The Ancient Art of War, which Wikipedia describes as one of the first real-time strategy or real-time tactics games.[^ref-29] The Murrys said each of their four Ancient Art of War and Manhunter games sold more than 100,000 copies.[^ref-21] Manhunter 3: London was never made, and Wikipedia says the Manhunter rights are held by Activision.[^ref-23][^ref-32] MobyGames credits the studio on The Ancient Art of War 2, released in 2014 for Windows, Mac, iOS and Android, and Evryware's website was still online with a team page listing Dave, Dee Dee, Mike and Matt Murry when checked on 11 November 2026.[^ref-34][^ref-6]
