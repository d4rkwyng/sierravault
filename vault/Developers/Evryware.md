# Evryware

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Evryware is the small game studio of brothers Dave and Barry Murry, who have described themselves as "creating computer games together since 1979."[^ref-21] It made three games that Sierra On-Line published: Sierra Championship Boxing (1983) and the two Manhunter adventures, Manhunter: New York (1988) and Manhunter 2: San Francisco (1989).[^ref-21][^ref-22] Between them it made The Ancient Art of War series of strategy games for Brøderbund and MicroProse.[^ref-21]

Evryware was an independent developer, not a Sierra studio. Sierra published its games; it never owned the company. The Murrys kept the rights to The Ancient Art of War, while Sierra held Manhunter.[^ref-21] The Manhunter History page at Larry Laffer Dot Net says that "the association between Evryware and Sierra ended in 1989."[^ref-23]

The studio kept making games until 1997, when it released Pyrosaurus. Its own chronology says the team then started a web design firm and later an independent record label.[^ref-22][^ref-21]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

In 1979 the Murry brothers built an air traffic control radar simulator "just for fun", modelling the traffic at McChord Air Force Base, where Barry was working as an air traffic controller.[^ref-21] About a year later they began making arcade-style games for the Heathkit H89 and, by their own account, made "about a dozen arcade games over the next couple of years."[^ref-21] Evryware's chronology lists "CP/M Games" for 1980–1982.[^ref-22]

Sources differ on who founded the company. The 1997 Games Domain interview introduces Barry and Dave Murry as "the founders of Evryware," and MobyGames says the same.[^ref-21][^ref-14] Retro365 says instead that "in 1980 Dave and Joe Gargiulo, established Evryware."[^ref-16] Evryware's own team page calls Dave Murry "Technical Director, Co-Founder."[^ref-6]

### Sierra Championship Boxing

The brothers' first PC game was Championship Boxing. Microsoft planned to publish it through a new home software division but backed out at the last minute. Their contact at Microsoft was a friend of Ken Williams and told him about the game. In the brothers' words: "A few days later we had a contract with Sierra and they published Championship Boxing in 1983."[^ref-21] Retro365 dates the IBM PC release to 1984, the year it was reviewed.[^ref-16]

Players could build their own boxer or pick from historical fighters, coach from the corner between rounds, or play it arcade style.[^ref-21] InfoWorld called it "by far the most extensive sports game created for a microcomputer."[^ref-24] Retro365 says that Dave did the programming while Barry made the graphics and animation.[^ref-16] Retro365 also notes that Sierra kept porting the game and was still marketing it in 1988.[^ref-16]

### The Ancient Art of War

Brøderbund published The Ancient Art of War in 1984, and the Murrys said it "was very successful both critically and commercially."[^ref-21] They planned a trilogy covering land, sea and air, and went straight on to The Ancient Art of War at Sea, which Brøderbund released in 1987.[^ref-21]

### Manhunter

While they were working on The Ancient Art of War at Sea, "Sierra contacted us with an offer we couldn't refuse."[^ref-21] Sierra offered them the game system it used for King's Quest and its other adventures, to make whatever kind of game they wanted.[^ref-21] The Murrys said that Sierra "gave us the engine and told us that we could do whatever we wanted with it. They didn't even see the game until it was almost done."[^ref-21]

The result was Manhunter: New York (1988), followed a year later by Manhunter 2: San Francisco.[^ref-21] The Murrys gave it a darker tone than other Sierra titles and used unusual camera angles, extreme close-ups, first-person views and picture-in-picture shots.[^ref-21] They said Manhunter was "the first Sierra title" with a no-text interface, adding: "We also went to EGA at this point which gave us 16 colors to use."[^ref-21] Real locations in New York and San Francisco "added realism to the game."[^ref-21] Wikipedia names Manhunter 2: San Francisco as Sierra's final title built on its Adventure Game Interpreter (AGI).[^ref-25]

According to the Murrys, both Ancient Art of War games and both Manhunter games each sold more than 100,000 copies.[^ref-21]

### Manhunter 3 and the End of the Sierra Partnership

Manhunter 2 ended on a cliffhanger that pointed to a third game, Manhunter 3: London.[^ref-23] It was never made. The Larry Laffer Dot Net shrine says "the association between Evryware and Sierra ended in 1989 and the two companies never partnered up again," and that because no deal was ever struck to move the game into production, "it's probable that it never went beyond the conceptual stage."[^ref-23] In 1997 the Murrys said they would "like to eventually do another Manhunter," but did not have the time.[^ref-21]

### Later Years

The third Ancient Art of War game took much longer than planned. Joe Gargiulo, who had worked with the brothers in their H89 days, joined the team, and work had to restart when the graphics standard moved from EGA to VGA. MicroProse published The Ancient Art of War in the Skies in 1992.[^ref-21]

Evryware's chronology then lists SpaceKids (1994), Space Dude (1996) and Pyrosaurus (1997), a game in which players design and train their own dinosaurs and fight other players' creations over the internet.[^ref-22] (The team page gives 1996 for Pyrosaurus, but the Games Domain interview of June 1997 still calls it the Murrys' "latest title.")[^ref-6][^ref-21] After Pyrosaurus the team "started a web design firm," and then created an independent record label.[^ref-22]

## Key People

- **Dave Murry**: co-founder and technical director; programmer.[^ref-6][^ref-16]
- **Barry Murry**: co-founder; graphics and animation on Championship Boxing.[^ref-21][^ref-16]
- **Dee Dee Murry**: the brothers' sister and Evryware's lead artist. The 1997 interview places her joining around the release of Manhunter: New York ("our sister, Dee Dee joined the team"), while the team page says she has been lead artist "since 1984" and worked on the Ancient Art of War and Manhunter series.[^ref-21][^ref-6]
- **Joe Gargiulo**: an early collaborator from the H89 era who joined the team for The Ancient Art of War in the Skies; Retro365 names him as a co-founder.[^ref-21][^ref-16]
