# Valve Corporation

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Valve is an American game developer, publisher, hardware and digital distribution company based in Bellevue, Washington. Former Microsoft employees Gabe Newell and Mike Harrington founded it in 1996.[^ref-16] Sierra On-Line published its debut game, Half-Life (1998), which was a critical and commercial success.[^ref-16][^ref-23] Sierra and its later owner Vivendi published Valve's games at retail until 2005. A copyright and contract lawsuit between the two companies ran from 2002 to 2005.[^ref-19][^ref-5]

Valve launched Steam in September 2003 to deliver automatic updates for its own games. Steam later expanded to third-party titles.[^ref-7] Its other franchises include Counter-Strike, Portal, Team Fortress, Left 4 Dead and Dota, and its hardware includes the Steam Deck and Valve Index.[^ref-16]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Newell and Harrington founded Valve, L.L.C. in Kirkland, Washington, on August 24, 1996.[^ref-16][^ref-5] Newell had spent the previous 13 years at Microsoft developing Windows.[^ref-16] For the company name, Newell did not want anything that suggested "testosterone-gorged muscles and the 'extreme' of anything". Names they rejected included Hollow Box, Fruitfly Ensemble and Rhino Scar.[^ref-16] Half-Life ran on GoldSrc, a heavily modified version of id Software's Quake engine, and science fiction novelist Marc Laidlaw was hired to write its plot.[^ref-23]

### Sierra Era

After struggling to find a publisher, Valve signed with Sierra On-Line.[^ref-16] Sources differ on the date: Wikipedia's Valve article gives 1997,[^ref-16] while its Sierra article dates the negotiation to November 1996, when Ken Williams secured Sierra's exclusive rights to Half-Life from Valve's founders; the game debuted at E3 in May 1997.[^ref-26] Under the agreement, Sierra owned the Half-Life intellectual property and held exclusive publishing rights to future Valve games.[^ref-16] Sierra Studios published Half-Life for Windows on November 19, 1998.[^ref-23] In 1998 Valve acquired TF Software, the Australian makers of the Team Fortress mod for Quake; GameSpot reported that Sierra Studios and Valve planned to ship Team Fortress 2 as a Half-Life expansion.[^ref-39] Valve later had Gearbox Software develop three Half-Life expansions: Opposing Force (1999), Blue Shift (2001) and Half-Life: Decay (2001), which was published by Sierra On-Line and released with the PlayStation 2 version of Half-Life.[^ref-16][^ref-40] Harrington sold his stake in Valve to Newell in 2000.[^ref-16]

On March 29, 2001, Valve signed a new Software Publishing Agreement with Sierra and its affiliates.[^ref-18] The amended deal gave Valve the Half-Life intellectual property and online distribution rights for its games. It licensed Sierra/Vivendi to distribute Valve games in the "Retail Channel" solely as "Retail Packaged Product."[^ref-16][^ref-18] Valve built Steam to distribute its games digitally and first showed it at the Game Developers Conference in March 2002. This was before any lawsuit was filed.[^ref-16][^ref-27]

### Valve v. Sierra (2002–2005)

- **August 14, 2002:** Valve filed suit against Sierra Entertainment in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington (case 2:02-cv-01683, Judge Thomas S. Zilly), as a copyright infringement case.[^ref-19]
- **The claims:** Sierra had been distributing Valve's games to Internet cafés, which Valve said its contract did not allow. Valve also claimed that Sierra/Vivendi withheld royalties and delayed Counter-Strike: Condition Zero so that it missed the Christmas 2003 retail season.[^ref-22][^ref-16] Vivendi countersued, seeking to void the publishing agreement and take ownership of the Half-Life intellectual property.[^ref-22] Wikipedia says the countersuit argued that Valve had misrepresented its Steam plans when the 2001 contract was signed.[^ref-16]
- **November 22, 2004:** The court denied Sierra/Vivendi's motions for partial summary judgment and granted Valve's cross-motions in whole or in part. A written order on the cyber-café rights followed on December 14, 2004.[^ref-19] The court held that the license covered only "Retail Packaged Product" in the "Retail Channel", so it did not cover cyber cafés.[^ref-18]
- **April 12, 2005:** After Valve's motion of April 11, the court entered a stipulation and order for voluntary dismissal with prejudice.[^ref-19] MobyGames records the settlement on April 29, 2005. VU Games would stop distributing retail packaged versions of Valve's games from August 31, 2005, and only Valve's cyber-café licenses would remain valid.[^ref-5] In July 2005 Electronic Arts was announced as Valve's new distribution partner.[^ref-16]
- **Epilogue:** The arbitrator awarded Valve $2,391,932. In April 2009 Valve sued Activision Blizzard, which had acquired Sierra Entertainment, over the unpaid $424,136 balance of that award.[^ref-16]

### Steam and Independent Growth

Steam came out of beta on September 12, 2003. In November 2004 Half-Life 2 became the first high-profile game offered digitally on Steam.[^ref-7] At retail, Vivendi Universal Games distributed Half-Life 2 through its Sierra Entertainment subsidiary.[^ref-25] In 2003 Valve moved to Bellevue and reincorporated as Valve Corporation.[^ref-16] As Valve became its own publisher via Steam, it moved to a flat organization. Outside of executive management it has no bosses, and employees can move between departments at will.[^ref-16]

### Recent Developments

Valve released Half-Life: Alyx in 2020 and the Steam Deck handheld in 2022.[^ref-16] On November 12, 2025, it announced three new hardware products: a next-generation Steam Machine, a next-generation Steam Controller, and the Steam Frame VR headset.[^ref-6] Valve opened Steam Machine pre-orders on June 22, 2026, starting at $1,050, with launch set for June 29, 2026.[^ref-31] The Verge published its Steam Machine review the same day.[^ref-36] Steam Frame pre-orders opened on September 14, 2026, at $1,059 for the 256GB model and $1,299 for 1TB, through a randomized reservation lottery with the first purchase emails expected on September 18.[^ref-37] On October 12, 2026, Road to VR reported that Valve said the Steam Frame order queue had stretched into 2027.[^ref-38]

## Notable Games

### Half-Life (1998)

In Half-Life the player controls Gordon Freeman, a theoretical physicist who must escape the Black Mesa Research Facility after a disastrous experiment lets aliens overrun it. The story is told mostly through scripted sequences rather than cutscenes, and the player keeps control of the first-person view. It won more than 50 PC "Game of the Year" awards.[^ref-23]

### Counter-Strike (2000)

Counter-Strike began as a Half-Life mod by Minh Le and Jess Cliffe. Valve acquired the game's intellectual property and partnered with Le and Cliffe to make a standalone game, which Sierra Studios published in November 2000; Sierra's own news page announced it in stores in North America on November 14, 2000.[^ref-21][^ref-16][^ref-41] That news item is the in-stores announcement, which is a different milestone from the 1.0 and Steam dates given on other pages in this vault.[^ref-41] Two teams, the Counter-Terrorists and the Terrorists, compete in objective-based modes such as bomb defusal and hostage rescue.[^ref-21]

### Portal (2007)

Portal was developed and published by Valve and first released in The Orange Box bundle. Its portal mechanic comes from the student game Narbacular Drop, and Valve hired many of that game's DigiPen team. GLaDOS, voiced by Ellen McLain, and Jonathan Coulton's end-credits song "Still Alive" were both acclaimed.[^ref-24]

## Key People

Gabe Newell co-founded Valve and is its president.[^ref-16] Mike Harrington co-founded the company and left in 2000.[^ref-16][^ref-5] Science fiction novelist Marc Laidlaw wrote Half-Life's plot.[^ref-23]

## Legacy

Steam grew from an update tool for Valve's own games into a store for third-party titles.[^ref-7] Wikipedia notes Half-Life's "lasting influence" on the first-person shooter genre.[^ref-16]
