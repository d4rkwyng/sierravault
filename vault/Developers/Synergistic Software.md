# Synergistic Software

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

Synergistic Software was an American software developer founded in 1978 in Seattle, Washington, by Robert (Bob) Clardy and Ann Dickens Clardy.[^ref-3][^ref-11][^ref-18] It published some of the earliest games and applications for the Apple II, and A.P.P.L.E. credits it with more than 160 titles over about twenty years, covering games, business, educational and utility software.[^ref-5][^ref-18] In the late 1980s and early 1990s Synergistic built the "World Builder" engine behind *War in Middle Earth*, *Spirit of Excalibur*, *Vengeance of Excalibur* and *Conan: The Cimmerian*, all published by other companies.[^ref-18][^ref-28] It also took on contract and porting work.[^ref-11][^ref-21]

Sierra On-Line acquired Synergistic in 1996, after the studio had worked for Sierra as a subcontractor for a few years.[^ref-18][^ref-21] Synergistic stayed an independent development group inside Sierra. Its titles from this period include *Birthright: The Gorgon's Alliance* and the *Diablo* expansion *Hellfire*.[^ref-11][^ref-29][^ref-30] Sierra closed the studio on February 22, 1999, as part of a wider restructuring.[^ref-3][^ref-21][^ref-22]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Bob Clardy graduated from Rice University in 1974 and worked as an electrical engineer for Boeing at the Johnson Space Center. He moved to Seattle with Ann in 1977.[^ref-18][^ref-23] In 1978 he bought an Apple II and began reworking *Dragon Maze*, a BASIC program printed in the Apple II reference manual. That work became Synergistic's first commercial game, *Dungeon Campaign* (1978), which was followed by *Wilderness Campaign* (1979).[^ref-18][^ref-21] Polygon notes that *Dungeon Campaign* came out three years before *Rogue*, making it one of the first commercial "roguelikes".[^ref-21] The two games were later expanded into *Odyssey: The Compleat Apventure* (1980).[^ref-18][^ref-21]

Softalk's 1982 profile of the company records that "It was Ann's father who came up with Synergistic."[^ref-32] Besides games, Synergistic sold business software, including a word processor, the graphics utility *Higher Graphics* and *The Modifiable Database*.[^ref-18][^ref-32] It hired its first full-time employee, programmer Mike Branham, in March 1981. The same year it moved out of the Clardys' basement into an office, and by 1982 it was based in Renton, Washington.[^ref-32] A.P.P.L.E. has since republished manuals for around twenty of the early Apple II and Atari 800 games in *Synergistic Software: The Early Games*.[^ref-5][^ref-14][^ref-17]

### Contract Work and the World Builder Games

As the IBM PC became the dominant platform, Synergistic kept going as an independent developer, and MobyGames notes that it became known for its porting work in that era.[^ref-11][^ref-23] According to Polygon, the studio took contract jobs such as *The Beverly Hillbillies* and *Homey D. Clown* to pay the bills. Publishers who were happy with that work invited Clardy to pitch original designs, which led to projects such as *Spirit of Excalibur*.[^ref-21]

None of the World Builder games were published by Sierra. *J.R.R. Tolkien's War in Middle Earth* was released under the Melbourne House label.[^ref-27] Virgin published *Spirit of Excalibur* (1990), *Vengeance of Excalibur* (1991) and *Conan: The Cimmerian* (1991).[^ref-24][^ref-25][^ref-26] The CRPG Addict notes that Synergistic adapted the same engine for *Warriors of Legend* and *The Beverly Hillbillies* (both 1993).[^ref-28]

### Sierra Era

Clardy and Sierra co-founder Ken Williams were friends as well as business partners, and Sierra's Bellevue office was close to Synergistic's headquarters.[^ref-21] Clardy told Polygon: "It was after working with Sierra as a sub-contractor for a few years that Ken broached the idea of taking us over entirely." The takeover came in 1996.[^ref-21] He also said "The major selling point was that I really enjoyed working with Ken personally."[^ref-21] Polygon adds that under Sierra's ownership the studio would no longer have to look for contract work it had no interest in.[^ref-21]

Sierra published several Synergistic titles from the mid-1990s onward. They include *Thexder 95*, a Windows 95 remake of the first *Thexder*, and *Birthright: The Gorgon's Alliance*, based on TSR's *Birthright* campaign setting for *Dungeons & Dragons*.[^ref-29][^ref-31] In February 1996, CUC International acquired Sierra, and with it Synergistic.[^ref-21] CUC management then assigned Synergistic the *Diablo* expansion *Hellfire* (1997). Clardy told Polygon it was "far and away" the studio's most financially successful project, and it was also its last.[^ref-21][^ref-30]

### Closure

According to MobyGames, the Clardys started a company called Eclectic Games in 1998, but it never developed a game. RPGWatch instead describes Eclectic Games as a 1998 renaming of Synergistic.[^ref-11][^ref-15] On February 22, 1999, Sierra closed Synergistic along with Yosemite Entertainment, Pyrotechnix and Books That Work, and moved those operations into its Bellevue headquarters.[^ref-22] IGN reported that some staff would be offered jobs in Bellevue, but not all of them.[^ref-22] Polygon reports that nearly 200 Sierra employees were laid off that day, which became known as "Chainsaw Monday."[^ref-21] Clardy blamed the parent company: "Just about every principal from all the studios quit or moved to some other firm in the next few years as none of us could stand working for Cendant."[^ref-21] Some former staff went back to the name Northwest Synergistic Software. In 2000 that company left the entertainment market to make apparel pattern-drafting software.[^ref-11]

## Notable Games

### Spirit of Excalibur (1990)

Synergistic developed *Spirit of Excalibur*, and Virgin Mastertronic published it for MS-DOS, Amiga, Atari ST, CDTV, Apple IIGS and Macintosh.[^ref-24] The player starts as Lord Constantine and eventually commands many knights and their armies, with the aim of uniting Britain under a single king. Play switches between a scrolling map of Britain and close-up "scene level" encounters.[^ref-24] See [[1990 - Spirit of Excalibur|the full game page]].

### Birthright: The Gorgon's Alliance

*Birthright: The Gorgon's Alliance* is based on TSR's *Birthright* campaign setting for *Dungeons & Dragons*. The player rules a realm in Anuire and competes with other regents through warfare, diplomacy, magic and holdings on a turn-based world map, while adventures are played in real-time 3D.[^ref-29] Wikipedia dates its North American release to July 1997. IGN reported that Synergistic worked on *Birthright* before it was folded into Sierra's sports division.[^ref-22][^ref-29]

### Conan: The Cimmerian (1991)

*Conan: The Cimmerian* is an action-adventure game with role-playing elements, based on Robert E. Howard's barbarian. Virgin Games published it in 1991 for Amiga and MS-DOS.[^ref-26] It used Synergistic's World Builder engine, and the CRPG Addict rated it above both Excalibur games.[^ref-28] See [[1991 - Conan - The Cimmerian|the full game page]].

## Key People

Bob and Ann Dickens Clardy founded the company together and jointly developed and marketed its first games.[^ref-18][^ref-21] Softalk's 1982 profile describes Ann and Bob as owning and operating the company. Bob's younger brother Will Clardy joined late in 1981 to handle marketing and advertising.[^ref-32] Bob Clardy directed *Spirit of Excalibur* and co-designed *Birthright: The Gorgon's Alliance*.[^ref-24][^ref-29]

## Legacy

Synergistic's *Wilderness Campaign* is credited with inspiring the gameplay of the 1981 electronic board game *Dark Tower*.[^ref-18] Polygon counts *Dungeon Campaign* among the first commercial roguelikes.[^ref-21] Synergistic's World Builder engine links its Tolkien, Arthurian and Conan games.[^ref-28]
