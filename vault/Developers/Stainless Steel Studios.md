# Stainless Steel Studios

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Stainless Steel Studios (SSSI) was an American video game developer in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Rick Goodman founded it in January 1998, according to the studio's own 1998 press release and Gamasutra's 2005 report.[^ref-3][^ref-4] Co-founder Dara-Lynn Pelechatz later told GameSpy that work on the company started in October 1997.[^ref-1] The studio made real-time strategy games and is best known for Empire Earth (2001), an RTS spanning 500,000 years of human history.[^ref-2] Empire Earth was GameSpy's PC Game of the Year for 2001 and had sold over one million copies by May 2002.[^ref-22][^ref-25][^ref-8] According to GameSpot, Goodman, the lead designer of the original Age of Empires, set out to "become the industry's premier developer in real-time strategy games."[^ref-23]

The company's unusual name originated during a flight back from California when Goodman opened a Sky Mall catalog and randomly pointed to an item—a stainless steel shower head—declaring it would be the company's name.[^ref-1] The studio's logo, made with Jam Design, showed a silver ball for strength with "the salamander crawling through the ball" for fun.[^ref-1] The studio closed in November 2005, while it was finishing Rise and Fall: Civilizations at War for Midway Games.[^ref-4] Goodman blamed Midway for cutting off advances. Midway did not confirm this.[^ref-17]

## History

### Founding and Vision

Goodman's May 1998 press release says he "founded Stainless Steel Studios in January 1998", after "co-founding Ensemble Studios with his brother" and creating Age of Empires, on which he was creator and lead designer.[^ref-3] Gamasutra also gives January 1998.[^ref-4] Pelechatz, the director of operations, told GameSpy the company "was started in October of 1997 by Rick Goodman and Dara-Lynn Pelechatz".[^ref-1] Its May 28, 1998 press release announced Empire Earth, planned to cover 500,000 years of history in twelve epochs.[^ref-3] The released game has fourteen.[^ref-2]

In the May 1998 press release, Goodman set out his vision: "This is a real-time strategy game for the next millennium. It will set new standards in the genre, and offer gamers an experience that goes far beyond anything that strategy games released, thus far, have offered."[^ref-3] Goodman said the studio was "designing an entirely new engine for this title".[^ref-3] The engine became known as Titan.[^ref-2][^ref-26]

### Empire Earth Development

Goodman "hand-picked" the designers, artists and programmers for the development team.[^ref-3] A notable addition to the team was Damon "Stratus" Gauthier, a StarCraft tournament veteran brought in on January 18, 2001, specifically for multiplayer balancing.[^ref-2]

The game was shown at E3 2000 and E3 2001.[^ref-2] Empire Earth was released in the United States on November 13, 2001.[^ref-2] By May 2002 Sierra reported "over one million copies sold".[^ref-25][^ref-8] In the United States alone, it generated $16.7 million in revenue by August 2006, ranking as the 41st best-selling computer game from January 2000 to August 2006.[^ref-2]

### Critical Reception and Awards

Empire Earth received generally favorable reviews. GameSpy scored it 94/100 and named it its 2001 PC Game of the Year.[^ref-9][^ref-22] GameSpy's review said that "each epoch feels fleshed out and playable, and every era has its own nuances, so it's almost like getting 14 games in one."[^ref-9] IGN gave the game 8.5/10 with an Editor's Choice award, calling it "ambitious, addictive and more than just a 3D Age of Empires II clone."[^ref-11] The game maintains a Metacritic score of 81/100 and a user score of 8.3/10.[^ref-9]

GameSpot provided a more measured assessment at 7.9/10, with Greg Kasavin noting that "Empire Earth is massive—more than most real-time strategy games would dare to be" while warning that "you can actually have too much of a good thing."[^ref-13] 

### Later Projects

While Mad Doc Software developed the Empire Earth expansion pack [[2002 - Empire Earth - The Art of Conquest|The Art of Conquest]] (September 2002),[^ref-25][^ref-27] Stainless Steel Studios moved on to Empires: Dawn of the Modern World, published by Activision in October 2003.[^ref-14][^ref-4]

The studio's final project was Rise and Fall: Civilizations at War, an RTS that let players take direct control of heroes such as Alexander the Great and Cleopatra.[^ref-15] It was being made for Midway Games, with a scheduled ship date of February 2006, and staff said it was weeks from gold disk when the studio closed.[^ref-4]

### Closure

On November 25, 2005, Gamasutra reported that Stainless Steel Studios had "closed its doors, laying off the vast majority of its staff".[^ref-4] Former employee Bob Scott confirmed the rumors on the HeavenGames Rise & Fall fan site forum.[^ref-16] Daniel Higgins also confirmed them.[^ref-4] Higgins wrote: "This is true, SSSI is no more. I can't give details as to why, but I can tell you the product is in excellent shape, the team was in high morale and plowing ahead at full steam, and we were just weeks from gold disk."[^ref-4]

GameSpot reported Goodman's account, via Gamestar.de: after the game's release slipped from October to early 2006, "Midway cut off advances to Stainless Steel, which in turn had to lay off its staff due to lack of funding." Midway's response to GameSpot's inquiries was "cryptic".[^ref-17] Midway Studios – San Diego finished Rise and Fall: Civilizations at War,[^ref-26] which was released on June 12, 2006, to mixed reviews.[^ref-15]

## Key People

Rick Goodman was the studio's president and a designer on Empire Earth.[^ref-3][^ref-18] Dara-Lynn Pelechatz was co-founder and director of operations.[^ref-1] The development team included designers Jon Alenson, Christopher Theriault, Jason Dillman, Gordon Farrell, and GJ Snyder III.[^ref-18] Composers Ed Lima and Steve Maitland created the musical scores, and Blur Studios is thanked in the credits for the game's video work.[^ref-18]

## Legacy

GameSpy's Game of the Year write-up said Empire Earth "set a new high water mark for others to follow".[^ref-22]

The series continued with Empire Earth II (2005) and Empire Earth III (2007), developed by Mad Doc Software, though the third installment was critically panned with a Metacritic score of just 50%.[^ref-20] After the Activision–Vivendi merger, Rebellion acquired the Empire Earth property, announced on July 14, 2009.[^ref-19][^ref-24]

The fan community has kept Empire Earth alive through projects like NeoEE (neoee.net), which provides replacement multiplayer servers.[^ref-21] The official servers shut down on November 3, 2008.[^ref-2]

## Games

- **2001** — [[2001 - Empire Earth|Empire Earth]] — Publisher: Sierra Entertainment, Genre: Real-Time Strategy
- **2003** — Empires: Dawn of the Modern World — Publisher: Activision, Genre: Real-Time Strategy[^ref-14]
- **2006** — Rise and Fall: Civilizations at War — Publisher: Midway Games, Genre: Real-Time Strategy[^ref-15]
