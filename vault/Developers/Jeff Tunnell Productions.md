# Jeff Tunnell Productions

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Jeff Tunnell Productions (JTP) was a small game studio that Dynamix co-founder Jeff Tunnell set up in early 1992, next door to Dynamix in Eugene, Oregon.[^ref-21][^ref-1] It was active from 1992 to 1995. In that time it made *The Incredible Machine* and its follow-ups, along with the Turbo Learning educational games.[^ref-10][^ref-13] In 1995 Tunnell returned to lead Dynamix, and Dynamix took the studio over that year.[^ref-13][^ref-10]

## History

### Founding (1992)

Tunnell has said that, "Right after completing Willy Beamish… Ken Williams supported me in founding a studio called Jeff Tunnell Productions (JTP)".[^ref-13] The Digital Antiquarian dates the founding to early 1992. Tunnell wanted to return to "the guerrilla model of game development" of the 1980s. He recruited Kevin Ryan, a programmer who had worked at Dynamix almost from the beginning, "and set up shop in the office next door with just a few other support personnel".[^ref-21] Dynamix itself had been founded in Eugene, Oregon.[^ref-1]

### The Incredible Machine and other games (1992–1995)

JTP's first game was *The Incredible Machine*, released on 1 December 1992.[^ref-21] Tunnell later said: "I left Dynamix to work on small project at Jeff Tunnell Productions where we created games like The Incredible Machine, 3D Ultra Pinball, and Trophy Bass."[^ref-3] MobyGames credits JTP on *The Incredible Machine* (1992), *Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science* (1992), *The Even More! Incredible Machine* (1993), *Turbo Learning: Mega Math* (1993), *The Incredible Machine 2* (1994) and *The Incredible Toon Machine* (1994).[^ref-10]

### Return to Dynamix (1995)

Tunnell has said that JTP's products "ended up being the most successful ever released from Dyanmix [sic], but circumstances drew me back into a leadership role at Dynamix in 1995."[^ref-13] MobyGames says that JTP's last titles, *3-D Ultra Pinball* and *The Incredible Machine 3.0*, came out in 1995, "before Dynamix took over later that year".[^ref-10] The Digital Antiquarian adds that Dynamix asked JTP "to merge back into their main fold".[^ref-21]

## Notable Games

### [[1992 - The Incredible Machine|The Incredible Machine]] (1992)

*The Incredible Machine* is a machine-construction puzzle game. The Digital Antiquarian writes: "At its heart, the game, which he would name The Incredible Machine, must be a physics simulator." It was the studio's first and best-known game.[^ref-21] Its sequels and spin-offs are listed below.

### [[1992 - Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science|Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science]] (1992)

*Turbo Science* is a science quiz-race game for DOS. The space elf Quarky O'Ghandi and his sister Quaysoo run the racing team Los Turbos, which the player joins. The player answers physics and science questions to earn money and move between checkpoints, competing against rival teams such as Das Liquidators.[^ref-22]

### [[1993 - Turbo Learning - Mega Math|Turbo Learning: Mega Math]] (1993)

*Mega Math* was the second and last title in the Turbo Learning series. MobyGames describes it as teaching "simple algebra through 3 mini games", with "tests to advance to the next level".[^ref-23]

## Key People

- **Jeff Tunnell**: founder. He had co-founded Dynamix with Damon Slye in 1984.[^ref-1][^ref-13]
- **Kevin Ryan**: programmer. He came from Dynamix with Tunnell.[^ref-21]
