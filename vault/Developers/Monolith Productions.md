# Monolith Productions

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Monolith Productions was an American video game developer founded in October 1994 by seven founders, most of them former employees of the edutainment software company Edmark.[^ref-2][^ref-20] It began in Redmond, Washington, and was later headquartered in Kirkland.[^ref-11][^ref-10] It is best known for *Blood*, the *No One Lives Forever* series, *F.E.A.R.* and the *Middle-earth* games.[^ref-2] Sierra published several of its games, including *Aliens versus Predator 2* (with Fox Interactive), *No One Lives Forever 2*, *Contract J.A.C.K.* and *F.E.A.R.*[^ref-12][^ref-23] Warner Bros. bought the studio outright in 2004 and closed it on 25 February 2025.[^ref-21][^ref-15]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

The Blood Wiki says Monolith was founded in October 1994 "by Brian Goble, Bryan Bouwman, Garrett Price, Paul Renault, Toby Gladwell, Brian Waite and Jason Hall in Redmond, Washington, nearby the headquarters of Microsoft".[^ref-11] Several of the founders, Hall among them, worked at Edmark. Microsoft hired the new company to make CDs showing off Windows 95 and DirectX, and the founders worked out of offices on Microsoft's Redmond campus. An investment from the Japanese publisher Takarajimasha later let Monolith open its own offices.[^ref-2][^ref-20] MobyGames gives the company's base as Kirkland, Washington.[^ref-10]

Co-founder Brian Goble explained the name. DOS was still the main gaming platform, so it had to be "8 characters or less (for 8.3 filenames)". "Monolith" "came up, was semi mysterious, wasn't taken, and was 8 characters. Perfect."[^ref-11][^ref-2]

Monolith's first planned game was *Claw*, a 2D platformer. In late 1996 it acquired Q Studios, which was "deep into production on a Doom-like first-person shooter called Blood". Monolith decided to finish *Blood* first.[^ref-20] *Blood* was released in March 1997 and published by GT Interactive.[^ref-20][^ref-10]

### LithTech (1998)

Monolith's in-house 3D engine, LithTech, first shipped in 1998 in *Shogo: Mobile Armor Division* and *Blood II: The Chosen*.[^ref-26][^ref-2] That was before any Sierra-published Monolith game. In 2000 a separate company, LithTech, was set up to develop and sell the engine; it was renamed Touchdown Entertainment in 2003.[^ref-10]

### Publishers: Fox and Sierra (1999–2005)

Monolith signed with Fox Interactive in late 1999.[^ref-2] *The Operative: No One Lives Forever* (2000) was published on Windows by **Fox Interactive**. Sierra Entertainment published the PlayStation 2 version.[^ref-25] Sierra On-Line and Fox Interactive published *Aliens versus Predator 2* (2001) together.[^ref-12] *No One Lives Forever 2: A Spy in H.A.R.M.'s Way* (2002) and *Contract J.A.C.K.* (2003) followed under the Sierra label, as their vault pages record. *F.E.A.R.* (2005), directed and designed by Craig Hubbard, was "released under the Sierra Entertainment brand name".[^ref-23] Hubbard was also the designer of *No One Lives Forever*.[^ref-25]

### Warner Bros. (2003–2025)

Warner Bros. Entertainment "bought about 20 percent of Monolith" in 2003, with an option on the rest. It exercised that option in August 2004, and the deal was set to close on 1 October 2004.[^ref-21] Co-founder Jason Hall had left in January 2004 to become a senior vice president at Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment. At the acquisition, Monolith's chief executive was **Samantha Ryan** and the staff numbered 150.[^ref-21]

Under Warner Bros. the studio made *Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor* (2014) and *Middle-earth: Shadow of War* (2017).[^ref-2] The team's postmortem of *Shadow of Mordor* lists the Nemesis System first under "What went right".[^ref-16] In February 2025 GameSpot, citing Bloomberg, reported that the long-delayed *Wonder Woman* game had reportedly already cost more than $100 million.[^ref-22] On 25 February 2025 Warner Bros. Games closed Monolith Productions, Player First Games and WB Games San Diego and cancelled *Wonder Woman*.[^ref-15][^ref-10]

## Key People

- **Jason "Jace" Hall**: co-founder. He left in January 2004 for Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment.[^ref-11][^ref-21]
- **Samantha Ryan**: chief executive at the time of the Warner Bros. acquisition.[^ref-21]
- **Brian Goble**: co-founder.[^ref-11][^ref-10] The Blood Wiki credits him as creator of the Windows Animation Package 32 engine used in *Claw*, *Get Medieval* and *Gruntz*.[^ref-11]
- **Craig Hubbard**: designer of *No One Lives Forever*, and director and designer of *F.E.A.R.*[^ref-25][^ref-23]
