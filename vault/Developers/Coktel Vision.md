# Coktel Vision

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Coktel Vision was a French developer and publisher founded in 1984 by Roland Oskian, a former executive at Matra Espace.[^ref-16][^ref-8] Some databases give 1985 as its founding year.[^ref-18][^ref-6] By 1993 it was best known for its "Adi" range of educational software and for interactive CD games such as Inca. Les Echos reported in 1994 that Coktel estimated that, through the Adi range, it alone held 65% of the French educational software market and 35% of the European one.[^ref-16] Among adventure-game players it is best known for the Gobliiins series.[^ref-2]

Sierra On-Line acquired the company on 29 October 1993.[^ref-22] Under the deal, Coktel distributed Sierra's games in Europe and Sierra represented Coktel's educational products in the United States under licence.[^ref-16] Coktel also localised Sierra's games and published them through its Tomahawk label.[^ref-8]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Coktel Vision was founded in 1984 by Roland Oskian, an engineer and former Matra Espace executive.[^ref-16][^ref-8] The studio was based in the Paris area.[^ref-6] Its early releases included the Jules Verne adaptation "20000 Leagues Under the Sea" (1988) and the rally game "African Raiders-01" (1988).

Muriel Tramis joined in 1986 and became the studio's leading designer, later earning the informal title of "Roberta Williams of France".[^ref-8] Coktel also released erotic games such as "Emmanuelle" (1989) and "Geisha" (1990); Wikipedia describes Tomahawk as a label Coktel used for its simulation and erotic games, to keep them apart from its educational titles.[^ref-8]

### Sierra Era

Sierra acquired Coktel Vision S.A. on 29 October 1993, for an initial purchase price of about $5.3 million.[^ref-22] When Les Echos reported the deal on 4 May 1994, Coktel had just finalised the merger; the paper reported that Coktel's revenue had risen to 75 million francs in 1993, from 30 million in 1992.[^ref-16] Ken Williams later said Sierra was more interested in expanding its European educational sales than in Coktel's games.[^ref-8]

### CUC, Havas and Vivendi

In February 1996 CUC International agreed to buy Sierra On-Line and Davidson & Associates, and Coktel became part of CUC's software division.[^ref-24][^ref-8] CUC's software business was sold to Havas, which was already a subsidiary of Vivendi; the sale was announced in November 1998 and completed on 12 January 1999.[^ref-26][^ref-27][^ref-8][^ref-3] After the commercial failure of Urban Runner (1996), Coktel turned from adventure games to educational titles, released mainly in Europe.[^ref-8][^ref-6] Oskian left the company in 1999, and Tramis left in 2003.[^ref-8] Vivendi Universal Games renamed the studio Coktel Studio in 2003.[^ref-6]

### Sale to Mindscape

In October 2005 VU Games sold Coktel and its Adibou licences to the French publisher Mindscape; eleven Coktel staff moved to Mindscape with the sale.[^ref-23] Mindscape kept Coktel as a brand until Mindscape itself closed in 2011.[^ref-8][^ref-2][^ref-6]

## Notable Games

### Gobliiins (1991)

Gobliiins was co-created by Pierre Gilhodes and Muriel Tramis.[^ref-8] Its three goblins, Asgard, Ignatius and Oups, each have different abilities, and the player must combine them to solve puzzles (see [[1991 - Gobliiins|Gobliiins]]). Coktel made two sequels, [[1992 - Gobliins 2 - The Prince Buffoon|Gobliins 2]] (1992) and [[1993 - Goblins Quest 3|Goblins Quest 3]] (1993).

### Lost in Time (1993)

In Lost in Time the player takes the role of Doralice Prunelier, a young woman living in 1992.[^ref-25] The manual lists its settings as the Manor de la Pruneliere on the Atlantic coast of Europe in 1992, and the ship Briscarde and the island of St. Cristobald in the Caribbean in 1840.[^ref-25]

### The Bizarre Adventures of Woodruff and the Schnibble (1995)

Woodruff and the Schnibble was Pierre Gilhodes's last Coktel game and carried over the visual style of Gobliiins (see [[1995 - The Bizarre Adventures of Woodruff and the Schnibble|Woodruff and the Schnibble]]).

## Key People

- **Roland Oskian**: founder, a former Matra Espace executive.[^ref-16] He left the company in 1999.[^ref-8]
- **Muriel Tramis** ([[Muriel Tramis]]): joined in 1986 and stayed until 2003; co-created Gobliiins with Pierre Gilhodes and created the Adibou educational series.[^ref-8]
- **Pierre Gilhodes** ([[Pierre Gilhodes]]): artist and designer who co-created Gobliiins with Tramis.[^ref-8]

## Legacy

Coktel's French titles reached players worldwide through Sierra's publishing.[^ref-8] Muriel Tramis explored political and social themes, such as imperialism and slavery, that were unusual for the time.[^ref-8] The Adibou educational series had sold over 1.5 million copies by 1997.[^ref-8]
