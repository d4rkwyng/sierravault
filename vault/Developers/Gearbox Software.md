# Gearbox Software

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Gearbox Software is an American game developer based in Frisco, Texas. It was founded in February 1999 by Randy Pitchford, Brian Martel, Stephen Bahl, Landon Montgomery and Rob Heironimus, five developers formerly of Rebel Boat Rocker.[^ref-3][^ref-8] It began by making expansions for Valve's Half-Life, all three published by Sierra: Opposing Force (1999), Blue Shift (2001) and Decay (2001, part of the PlayStation 2 version of Half-Life).[^ref-3][^ref-51][^ref-52][^ref-50] It later created the Brothers in Arms and Borderlands series.[^ref-3]

Gearbox Entertainment, its parent company, was acquired by Embracer Group in April 2021, and sold to Take-Two Interactive in a deal announced on March 27, 2024 and closed in June 2024.[^ref-3][^ref-22][^ref-23][^ref-48][^ref-53]

## History

### Founding

Sources differ slightly on the founding date. Wikipedia and the Counter-Strike Wiki give February 16, 1999, and Gearbox's own About page says February 1999, while MobyGames says January 1999.[^ref-3][^ref-8][^ref-1][^ref-7]

All five founders had worked together at Rebel Boat Rocker. Before that, Pitchford and Martel had worked together at 3D Realms, and Montgomery had worked at Bethesda Softworks.[^ref-3][^ref-7] The Counter-Strike Wiki says the team left Rebel Boat Rocker after its game Prax War was cancelled.[^ref-8] In a November 1999 interview, Pitchford said that Prax War was owned by EA.[^ref-55] According to Wikipedia, the company employed 15 people by 2000.[^ref-3]

### Sierra-Published Work (1999–2001)

Gearbox's first game was Half-Life: Opposing Force, an expansion pack developed by Gearbox and published by Sierra Studios on November 19, 1999.[^ref-51] Gearbox's own website described it in October 1999 as the official Half-Life expansion "created with publisher Sierra Studios in association with Valve Software".[^ref-54] Half-Life: Blue Shift followed as a standalone Windows release on June 12, 2001, also published by Sierra; it had originally been intended as part of a Dreamcast port of Half-Life that was cancelled.[^ref-52] Gearbox also ported Half-Life to the PlayStation 2, adding the two-player co-operative expansion Half-Life: Decay, which was published by Sierra On-Line in 2001.[^ref-50][^ref-7]

Gearbox says it also worked on Half-Life: Counter-Strike, the Sierra-published 2000 retail release of Counter-Strike. Its 2001 projects page listed "Half-Life: Counterstrike", and its 2017 page for the game named Gearbox Software as developer.[^ref-58][^ref-59] The game is usually credited to Valve, as on the vault's [[2000 - Counter-Strike|Counter-Strike]] page, so the extent of Gearbox's work is not clear.

### Counter-Strike: Condition Zero (2001–2002)

Gearbox took over development of Counter-Strike: Condition Zero in late May 2001, after finishing Blue Shift, and stopped work on it in July 2002.[^ref-8] Its version of the game was never released. Condition Zero was eventually finished by other studios and released in 2004; the shipped game combined Turtle Rock Studios' Tour of Duty campaign with Ritual Entertainment's Deleted Scenes, as described on the vault's [[2004 - Counter-Strike - Condition Zero|Condition Zero]] page.

### Later Years

Around 2002 Gearbox began working with other publishers. In 2005 it launched its own property, Brothers in Arms, with Brothers in Arms: Road to Hill 30, published by Ubisoft.[^ref-3][^ref-7] Borderlands followed in 2009, published by 2K, and became the company's second original series.[^ref-3] In April 2013 Gearbox won the Homeworld franchise at THQ's bankruptcy auction; Game Informer reported that it paid $1.35 million.[^ref-56][^ref-57] In May 2025 Take-Two reported that the Borderlands series had sold nearly 93 million units, according to Gamereactor's account of its quarterly report.[^ref-49]

In May 2019 Gearbox set up The Gearbox Entertainment Company as a parent for Gearbox Software and Gearbox Publishing.[^ref-3] Embracer Group acquired Gearbox Entertainment in April 2021 for $363 million, rising to as much as $1.378 billion if operational targets were met.[^ref-3] Take-Two announced on March 27, 2024 that it had agreed to buy Gearbox Entertainment from Embracer for $460 million (Embracer dated its own announcement March 28).[^ref-23][^ref-48] In a press release dated June 11, 2024, Embracer said that "the transaction has been closed today"; Game Developer reported the completed sale on June 12.[^ref-48][^ref-53]

## Key People

- **Randy Pitchford**: co-founder. On October 7, 2021, TechRadar reported that Pitchford had stepped down as president of Gearbox Software to head the newly formed Gearbox Studios, focused on film and television, and that former CTO Steve Jones had taken over the role.[^ref-47] Wikipedia says Pitchford remained president and CEO of the parent company.[^ref-3]
- **Brian Martel, Stephen Bahl and Rob Heironimus**: co-founders.[^ref-3]
- **Landon Montgomery**: co-founder; Wikipedia says he left the company around 2007 and died on March 25, 2020.[^ref-3]
