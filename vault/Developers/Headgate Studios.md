# Headgate Studios

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Headgate Studios was an American game developer founded in 1992 in Bountiful, Utah, by Vance Cook, a programmer who had worked for years at golf-simulation developer Access Software.[^ref-19][^ref-21][^ref-22] Sierra On-Line bought the studio in 1996, and Headgate made Sierra's Front Page Sports: Golf and PGA Championship Golf games.[^ref-1][^ref-19][^ref-20][^ref-21] After Cook regained ownership, Headgate developed the Windows versions of EA Sports' Tiger Woods PGA Tour.[^ref-26][^ref-24] Electronic Arts acquired the studio at the end of 2006 and renamed it EA Salt Lake.[^ref-7][^ref-19][^ref-27]

## History

### Founding

At Access Software, Cook had been project director and lead programmer of the Links golf series. According to Sierra's 1998 profile of him, he "left Access Software in 1992 to form Headgate Studios", at first to make business productivity software.[^ref-21] The Salt Lake Tribune likewise describes Headgate as "founded in 1992 in Bountiful as a business-productivity software company that later branched out into electronic games."[^ref-22] Cook served as its president. Its first product was PentaCalc, a scientific calculator for Windows.[^ref-19] When Cook turned the company to golf games, it had about six employees.[^ref-21] MobyGames' company overview says the studio was founded in California; no other source found supports that.[^ref-15]

The studio was based in Bountiful, in the Salt Lake City area. Sierra's 1998 profile says that after the acquisition "Cook and Headgate stayed in Salt Lake City", while EA's 2006 announcement places the team in Bountiful.[^ref-21][^ref-27] It stayed in Bountiful until July 2010, when EA Salt Lake opened new offices in downtown Salt Lake City.[^ref-19][^ref-7][^ref-22]

### Sierra Era (1996–1999)

Sierra bought Headgate in April 1996: Sierra's annual report for that fiscal year lists an Agreement and Plan of Merger "dated as of April 12, 1996" among Sierra, its subsidiary Birdie Acquisition Corp., Headgate, Inc. (a Utah corporation) and Headgate's shareholders.[^ref-20][^ref-19][^ref-15] According to Game Developer's "The End Game", Sierra was then expanding its Front Page Sports line: "Although Headgate had not yet shipped a game, Sierra believed that Headgate's golf technology would allow them to enter this segment of the sports market more quickly and with less risk." Headgate was paid in restricted stock, and the restrictions were lifted when Sierra was sold to CUC.[^ref-1]

Headgate's first Sierra game was Front Page Sports: Golf (1997), which Sierra said had been "nearly three years in development"; it introduced Headgate's TrueSwing mouse swing.[^ref-19][^ref-21] Three add-on course disks followed in 1997: Coeur d'Alene, Black Diamond Ranch and Pasatiempo Golf Club.[^ref-29][^ref-30][^ref-31] It then developed PGA Championship Golf 1999 Edition, released in the UK as PGA Championship Golf and catalogued by MobyGames as a 1998 game.[^ref-19][^ref-21][^ref-32]

### Return to Independence

Sources disagree on when Sierra sold the studio back to Cook:

- Wikipedia says that on February 22, 1999, Sierra announced a major restructuring and "sold the rights of the original studio back to Cook as a new corporate entity." That sentence carries no citation.[^ref-19] CBR also gives 1999.[^ref-14]
- Cook's own later company summary, on the startup site Gust, says he "bought it back in 2000."[^ref-28] The same summary dates Headgate's contract with EA Sports to 2001, while EA and Headgate's own website both say 2000.[^ref-27][^ref-26]

No period source for the sale has been found.

The vault's pages for PGA Championship Golf 2000, its Titanium Edition and the 2001 Collector's Edition all list Sierra as publisher.

### Electronic Arts (2000–2017)

From 2000, Headgate worked with Electronic Arts.[^ref-19][^ref-27] Its own website said in 2007: "In 2000, Headgate Studios Teamed up with EA SPORTS", and that it had developed Tiger Woods PGA TOUR 2001 to 2006.[^ref-26] It developed every Windows version of EA Sports' Tiger Woods PGA Tour from 2000 to 2007, starting with Tiger Woods PGA Tour 2001, along with PC versions of Madden NFL and The Godfather.[^ref-19][^ref-7] GameSpot called it "the developer of the last six PC installments in the Tiger Woods PGA Tour franchise and Madden NFL 07."[^ref-24] Tiger Woods PGA Tour 2003 was nominated for the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences' 2003 Computer Sports Game of the Year award.[^ref-18]

EA announced on November 30, 2006 that the Headgate development team had joined EA; terms were not disclosed.[^ref-2][^ref-27][^ref-24] On December 1, 2006, Game Developer reported that the team had joined EA "following a six year partnership between the two firms." The studio stayed in Bountiful, and "Vance Cook, CEO and founder of Headgate will continue to lead the studio team as executive producer."[^ref-7][^ref-25] Renamed EA Salt Lake, it was redirected toward games for Nintendo's Wii.[^ref-19][^ref-27]

As EA Salt Lake, the studio made Tiger Woods PGA Tour 07 and 08 versions, Madden NFL 08 for PC and Mac, Littlest Pet Shop games and Nerf N-Strike.[^ref-19] In 2011 it moved into EA's Maxis division and developed four expansion packs for The Sims 3.[^ref-19] After a restructuring toward mobile games, EA confirmed layoffs at the studio on January 30, 2014.[^ref-19][^ref-15][^ref-23] CBR says Cook left in 2010.[^ref-14] Wikipedia says EA closed the studio in April 2017, though that sentence is marked "citation needed"; CBR says only that it shut down three years after the 2014 shift to mobile.[^ref-19][^ref-14]

## Key People

- **Vance Cook**: founder and president of Headgate; formerly project director and lead programmer of Links at Access Software.[^ref-19][^ref-21] He stayed on as executive producer after EA's acquisition in 2006.[^ref-7][^ref-27]
