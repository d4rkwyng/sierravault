# Headgate Studios

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Headgate Studios was an American game developer founded in 1992 in Bountiful, Utah, by Vance Cook, a programmer who had worked for years at golf-simulation developer Access Software.[^ref-19] Sierra On-Line bought the studio in 1996, and Headgate made Sierra's Front Page Sports: Golf and PGA Championship Golf games.[^ref-1][^ref-19] After Cook regained ownership, Headgate developed the Windows versions of EA Sports' Tiger Woods PGA Tour. Electronic Arts acquired the studio at the end of 2006 and renamed it EA Salt Lake.[^ref-7][^ref-19]

## History

### Founding

Vance Cook founded Headgate Studios in 1992 and served as its president. Its first product was PentaCalc, a scientific calculator for Windows.[^ref-19] The studio was based in Bountiful, Utah, and stayed there until July 21, 2010, when EA Salt Lake moved to downtown Salt Lake City.[^ref-19][^ref-7]

### Sierra Era (1996–1999)

Sierra bought Headgate in April 1996.[^ref-19][^ref-15] According to Game Developer's "The End Game", Sierra was then expanding its Front Page Sports line: "Although Headgate had not yet shipped a game, Sierra believed that Headgate's golf technology would allow them to enter this segment of the sports market more quickly and with less risk." Headgate was paid in restricted stock, and the restrictions were lifted when Sierra was sold to CUC.[^ref-1]

Headgate's first Sierra game was Front Page Sports: Golf (1997).[^ref-19] An add-on course disk followed the same year (see the vault's Add-on Courses page). It then developed PGA Championship Golf (1998) and PGA Championship Golf 1999 Edition.[^ref-19]

### Return to Independence

Sources disagree on when Sierra sold the studio back to Cook:

- Wikipedia says that on February 22, 1999, Sierra announced a major restructuring and "sold the rights of the original studio back to Cook as a new corporate entity." That sentence carries no citation.[^ref-19] CBR also gives 1999.[^ref-14]
- Cook's own later company summary, on the startup site Gust, says he "bought it back in 2000."

The vault's pages for PGA Championship Golf 2000, its Titanium Edition and the 2001 Collector's Edition all list Sierra as publisher.

### Electronic Arts (2000–2017)

From 2000, Headgate worked with Electronic Arts.[^ref-19] It developed every Windows version of EA Sports' Tiger Woods PGA Tour from 2000 to 2007, starting with Tiger Woods PGA Tour 2001, along with PC versions of Madden NFL and The Godfather.[^ref-19][^ref-7] Tiger Woods PGA Tour 2003 was nominated for the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences' 2003 Computer Sports Game of the Year award.[^ref-18]

On December 1, 2006, Game Developer reported that the Headgate team had joined EA "following a six year partnership between the two firms." The studio stayed in Bountiful, and "Vance Cook, CEO and founder of Headgate will continue to lead the studio team as executive producer."[^ref-7][^ref-2] Renamed EA Salt Lake, it was redirected toward games for Nintendo's Wii.[^ref-19]

As EA Salt Lake, the studio made Tiger Woods PGA Tour 07 and 08 versions, Madden NFL 08 for PC and Mac, Littlest Pet Shop games and Nerf N-Strike.[^ref-19] In 2011 it moved into EA's Maxis division and developed four expansion packs for The Sims 3.[^ref-19] After a restructuring toward mobile games, it laid off staff in January 2014.[^ref-19][^ref-15] CBR says Cook left in 2010.[^ref-14] Wikipedia says EA closed the studio in April 2017, though that sentence is marked "citation needed."[^ref-19]

## Key People

- **Vance Cook**: founder and president of Headgate; formerly a programmer at Access Software.[^ref-19] He stayed on as executive producer after EA's acquisition in 2006.[^ref-7]
