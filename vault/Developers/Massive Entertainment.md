# Massive Entertainment

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Massive Entertainment is a Swedish video game developer founded in 1997 by Martin Walfisz.[^ref-13][^ref-23][^ref-27] When Sierra Studios signed its first game, *Ground Control*, in 1999, the studio was "located in Ronneby, Sweden".[^ref-22] Today Massive is based in Malmö and is part of Ubisoft.[^ref-1][^ref-13] Sierra published its first two games. Vivendi Universal Games owned the studio from 2002 to 2008, and Ubisoft bought it in November 2008.[^ref-21][^ref-23][^ref-19] Under Ubisoft, Massive has led *Tom Clancy's The Division* and *The Division 2*, and it made *Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora* and *Star Wars Outlaws*.[^ref-13][^ref-24]

## History

### Founding and Ground Control (1997–2000)

Martin Walfisz founded Massive Entertainment in 1997.[^ref-13][^ref-23][^ref-27] On 13 August 1999 Sierra Studios announced that it would publish *Ground Control*. The release named Walfisz as CEO and said that "Massive Entertainment is located in Ronneby, Sweden and comprised of over 20 programmers, artists, and game designers".[^ref-22] Massive released *Ground Control*, published by Sierra Studios,[^ref-13] on 31 May 2000;[^ref-31] on 1 June 2000 *Computer Games Strategy Plus* reported that "Sierra Studios has begun shipping" it.[^ref-29]

The expansion *Ground Control: Dark Conspiracy* (2000) was announced by Sierra Studios. The announcement credited it as "Developed by High Voltage Software and Massive Entertainment".[^ref-25] The expansion was mainly High Voltage's work; the [[2000 - Ground Control - Dark Conspiracy|game page]] covers the split.

### Vivendi Universal Games (2002–2008)

On 3 October 2002 Vivendi Universal Games "announced today the acquisition of Massive Entertainment by its studio, NDA Productions". Walfisz was still CEO, and Massive would "report to Dominique Cor, CEO of NDA Productions".[^ref-21][^ref-19] As a Vivendi studio, Massive made *Ground Control II: Operation Exodus* (2004) and *World in Conflict* (2007). Their publishers were Vivendi Universal Games and Vivendi Games respectively.[^ref-13] *World in Conflict* went gold as "Sierra Entertainment's World In Conflict".[^ref-26] In December 2007 the studio moved into new offices on Drottninggatan in Malmö.[^ref-13]

### Activision Blizzard and Ubisoft (2008–present)

Activision and Vivendi Games merged in 2008, and on 6 August 2008 Activision Blizzard put Massive up for sale.[^ref-13][^ref-27] Ubisoft acquired the studio on 10 November 2008, together with the rights to the *World in Conflict* franchise.[^ref-23][^ref-19] Walfisz left the company in March 2009.[^ref-13][^ref-28] Under Ubisoft, Massive collaborated on *Assassin's Creed: Revelations* (2011) and *Far Cry 3* (2012),[^ref-13] and it created the Snowdrop engine.[^ref-19] It released *Tom Clancy's The Division* on 8 March 2016[^ref-13] and was the lead developer of *Tom Clancy's The Division 2* (2019).[^ref-24] It went on to make *Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora* (December 2023) and *Star Wars Outlaws* (August 2024).[^ref-13]

On 13 January 2026 Ubisoft proposed a restructuring that "may affect approximately 55 roles" at Massive and Ubisoft Stockholm.[^ref-20][^ref-4] GamesIndustry.biz reported that Massive would continue work on The Division 3 and on the Survivors mode for The Division 2.[^ref-6]

On 30 September 2026 Ubisoft renamed its Creative House 2 business unit, home of *The Division*, *Ghost Recon*, *Splinter Cell* and the new IP *March of Giants*, as Massive Entertainment. The original Swedish studio remains part of the Creative House that takes its name, alongside Ubisoft Toronto and teams in Paris and Montreal.[^ref-30]
