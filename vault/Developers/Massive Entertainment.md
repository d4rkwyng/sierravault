# Massive Entertainment

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Massive Entertainment is a Swedish video game developer founded in 1997 by Martin Walfisz.[^ref-13][^ref-23][^ref-27] When Sierra Studios signed its first game, *Ground Control*, in 1999, the studio was "located in Ronneby, Sweden".[^ref-22] Today Massive is based in Malmö and is part of Ubisoft.[^ref-1][^ref-13] Sierra Studios published *Ground Control* (2000) and its *Dark Conspiracy* expansion.[^ref-31][^ref-25] *Ground Control II* (2004) was published by Vivendi Universal Games,[^ref-36] and *World in Conflict* (2007) by Sierra Entertainment, a division of Vivendi Games.[^ref-26] Vivendi Universal Games owned the studio from 2002 to 2008, and Ubisoft bought it in November 2008.[^ref-21][^ref-23][^ref-19] Under Ubisoft, Massive has led *Tom Clancy's The Division* and *The Division 2*, and it made *Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora* and *Star Wars Outlaws*.[^ref-13][^ref-24]

## History

### Founding and Ground Control (1997–2000)

Martin Walfisz founded Massive Entertainment in 1997.[^ref-13][^ref-23][^ref-27] On 13 August 1999 Sierra Studios announced that it would publish *Ground Control*. The release named Walfisz as CEO and said that "Massive Entertainment is located in Ronneby, Sweden and comprised of over 20 programmers, artists, and game designers".[^ref-22] Massive released *Ground Control*, published by Sierra Studios,[^ref-13] on 31 May 2000;[^ref-31] on 1 June 2000 *Computer Games Strategy Plus* reported that "Sierra Studios has begun shipping" it.[^ref-29]

The expansion *Ground Control: Dark Conspiracy* (2000) was announced by Sierra Studios. The announcement credited it as "Developed by High Voltage Software and Massive Entertainment".[^ref-25] The expansion was thus a co-development between High Voltage Software and Massive;[^ref-25] see the [[2000 - Ground Control - Dark Conspiracy|game page]].

### Vivendi Universal Games (2002–2008)

On 3 October 2002 Vivendi Universal Games "announced today the acquisition of Massive Entertainment by its studio, NDA Productions". Walfisz was still CEO, and Massive would "report to Dominique Cor, CEO of NDA Productions".[^ref-21][^ref-19] Walfisz later said that the studio, which had stayed independent after *Ground Control*, took the idea of a sequel to "a number of publishers", and that "in 2002 we were acquired by Vivendi games, who via Sierra, had published the first one".[^ref-32] As a Vivendi studio, Massive made *Ground Control II: Operation Exodus* (2004) and *World in Conflict* (2007). Wikipedia lists their publishers as Vivendi Universal Games and Vivendi Games respectively.[^ref-13][^ref-36] The 10 August 2007 release announcing that *World in Conflict* had gone gold came from "Sierra Entertainment, a division of Vivendi Games", called Massive "a Sierra Entertainment internal studio" and gave a North American retail date of 18 September 2007.[^ref-26] Its stand-alone expansion *Soviet Assault* arrived as a digital download in March 2009, and the *World in Conflict Complete* edition went to retailers the same week.[^ref-35] In December 2007 the studio moved into new offices on Drottninggatan in Malmö, when it had around 130 people.[^ref-13][^ref-34]

### Activision Blizzard and Ubisoft (2008–present)

Activision and Vivendi Games merged in 2008, and on 6 August 2008 Activision Blizzard put Massive up for sale.[^ref-13][^ref-27] Ubisoft acquired the studio on 10 November 2008, together with the rights to the *World in Conflict* franchise. MobyGames says the acquisition included the *World in Conflict* IP and the MassGate multiplayer infrastructure, but not *Ground Control*.[^ref-23][^ref-19] GamesIndustry.biz reported on 11 November 2008 that Ubisoft had obtained "the Sweden-based studio's assets and personnel" as well as those rights.[^ref-23] Walfisz left the company in March 2009.[^ref-13][^ref-28] Under Ubisoft, Massive collaborated on *Assassin's Creed: Revelations* (2011) and *Far Cry 3* (2012),[^ref-13] and it created the Snowdrop engine.[^ref-19] It released *Tom Clancy's The Division* on 8 March 2016[^ref-13] and was the lead developer of *Tom Clancy's The Division 2* (2019).[^ref-24] It went on to make *Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora* (December 2023) and *Star Wars Outlaws* (August 2024).[^ref-13]

On 13 January 2026 Ubisoft proposed a restructuring that "may affect approximately 55 roles" at Massive and Ubisoft Stockholm.[^ref-20][^ref-4] GamesIndustry.biz reported that Massive would continue work on The Division 3 and on the Survivors mode for The Division 2.[^ref-6]

On 30 September 2026 Ubisoft renamed its Creative House 2 business unit, home of *The Division*, *Ghost Recon*, *Splinter Cell* and the new IP *March of Giants*, as Massive Entertainment. The original Swedish studio remains part of the Creative House that takes its name, alongside Ubisoft Toronto and teams in Paris and Montreal.[^ref-30]

## Notable Games

### Ground Control (2000)

*Ground Control* was Massive's first game, a real-time tactics title released on 31 May 2000 and published by Sierra Studios.[^ref-31] The studio described it as taking "a new approach to the real time strategy genre", with the focus on battle tactics rather than resource gathering.[^ref-31] Walfisz later said the game "didn't do as well as we'd hoped" commercially, despite good reviews.[^ref-32] The expansion *Dark Conspiracy* was developed by High Voltage Software and Massive.[^ref-25] See [[2000 - Ground Control|Ground Control]].

### Ground Control II: Operation Exodus (2004)

The sequel was released in North America on 23 June 2004 and credited to Vivendi Universal Games as publisher; Wikipedia's note adds that it was "released under the Sierra Entertainment brand name".[^ref-36] Walfisz described the design as keeping what the studio liked about the first game while combining it "with the traditional RTS".[^ref-32] See [[2004 - Ground Control II - Operation Exodus|Ground Control II]].

### World in Conflict (2007)

*World in Conflict* is an alternate-history Cold War game that Sierra Entertainment released for Windows in North America on 18 September 2007.[^ref-26] Walfisz said it "started out as a pure multiplayer project" and that the single-player campaign was added as the ambition of the project grew.[^ref-32] See [[2007 - World in Conflict|World in Conflict]].

### Tom Clancy's The Division (2016)

Massive's page describes the first *Division* as "the fastest selling game in Ubisoft history", and says the franchise has reached "more than 40 million players worldwide".[^ref-1] Massive also led the sequel, *The Division 2* (2019).[^ref-24]

## Key People

- **Martin Walfisz**: founder (1997) and CEO. He left in March 2009.[^ref-13][^ref-22][^ref-21][^ref-27][^ref-28] In 2008 he told Rock Paper Shotgun: "I'm the president and founder of the company. I founded Massive in 1997 with a business associate."[^ref-32]
- **Christian Pérez**: named by Walfisz as the person with whom he originally started the company ("Originally I started the company with Christian Pérez, who I met when I finished my software engineering studies in 1996").[^ref-32]
- **David Polfeldt**: described by Walfisz in 2008 as "our VP and director of marketing"; Rock Paper Shotgun reported that he "joined Massive in 1995", while the studio's founding is given as 1997 elsewhere on this page.[^ref-32] By 2020 he was managing director.[^ref-34] In July 2021 Ubisoft announced that he had stepped down to take a sabbatical.[^ref-33]
- **Thomas Andrén**: appointed managing director in July 2021, effective October 2021, coming from Red Bee Media.[^ref-33]
- **Christoph Hartmann**: general manager of Massive Entertainment since June 2026, after leaving Amazon Game Studios; GamesIndustry.biz notes he previously founded and led 2K Games.[^ref-30]
- **Natalie Francis**: studio manager of the Malmö office, announced in September 2026 after senior roles at Avalanche Studios and DICE.[^ref-30]

## Legacy

Massive made its name with *Ground Control* and *World in Conflict*, then with the *Division* franchise, *Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora* and *Star Wars Outlaws*.[^ref-30] The studio developed the Snowdrop engine, which GamesIndustry.biz says is widely used across Ubisoft titles, and Massive describes itself as the home base of Ubisoft Connect.[^ref-30][^ref-1] The 2026 renaming of Creative House 2 as Massive Entertainment, with the original studio as one of its teams, carries the name into Ubisoft's new structure.[^ref-30]
