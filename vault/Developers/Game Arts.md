# Game Arts

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Game Arts Co., Ltd. is a Japanese video game developer and publisher based in Chiyoda, Tokyo, founded in 1985. It began with the action game Thexder and is best known in the West for the Lunar and Grandia role-playing series and the Gungriffon vehicle-simulation games.[^ref-1] The company was co-founded by brothers Yoichi and Takeshi Miyaji.[^ref-1][^ref-23][^ref-24]

Sierra On-Line licensed several of its early computer games for the North American market, including Thexder and Silpheed.[^ref-1][^ref-26][^ref-31] GungHo Online Entertainment acquired Game Arts in 2005.[^ref-25]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Game Arts was founded on March 2, 1985.[^ref-1][^ref-10][^ref-27] Denfaminicogamer's profile of Yoichi Miyaji says that he set up the company in 1985 together with his younger brother Takeshi Miyaji and others (「1985年に弟の宮路武氏らと株式会社ゲームアーツを設立」), and that he served as its representative director until 2005.[^ref-23] GameSpot's 2011 obituary of Takeshi Miyaji likewise says he "started Game Arts back in 1985 alongside his brother Yoichi Miyaji."[^ref-24]

The company's first game was Thexder, an action game for Japanese personal computers released in 1985.[^ref-1] Japanese Wikipedia, citing an interview with Yoichi Miyaji, dates it to April 1985 and says NEC supported the project after seeing it in development, supplying a PC-8801mkII SR half a year before that machine went on sale.[^ref-33] Shmuplations' translated developer interview credits [[Hibiki Godai]] as director/designer and Satoshi Uesaka as designer.[^ref-34] Sierra's own 1990 profile described the young company as run "from a small garage, by Miyaji and 6 employees," and claimed Thexder sold 500,000 units in Japan.[^ref-31]

Silpheed followed on the PC-8801 in December 1986, designed by Takeshi Miyaji.[^ref-28] The original version "used 3D polygonal graphics on top of a tilted third-person backdrop"; Hardcore Gaming 101 notes that every enemy craft and the player's ship "was built from simple, flat-shaded polygons, making the game genuinely three-dimensional."[^ref-28][^ref-29] The pre-rendered video backgrounds often associated with the game belong to the 1993 Sega CD version.[^ref-28]

### Sierra Era

Sierra On-Line published Game Arts games in North America in the late 1980s and 1990: Thexder (US 1987), Silpheed (US 1989), Zeliard (US 1990) and Fire Hawk: Thexder – The Second Contact (US 1990).[^ref-1][^ref-31]

Thexder came first. In the Spring 1988 Sierra Newsletter, Ken Williams wrote that it "was released in September of 1987, was Sierra's first import from Japan, and has already sold more than 100,000 units in this country."[^ref-32] (A Sierra News profile two years later instead said it "debuted in U.S. computer stores just in time for Christmas 1986," while another article in the same issue gives 1987.[^ref-31]) The same 1988 newsletter previewed Silpheed, listing it for the fourth quarter of 1988, with the IBM version programmed by Gary Butts and the Apple IIGS version by John Rettig.[^ref-32] Sierra's Fall 1990 magazine dates the US release to April 1989, and Wikipedia's games table gives 1988.[^ref-31][^ref-1]

In Fall 1990, Sierra News announced "the cementing of a long-term alliance with Game Arts of Japan, makers of Sierra's first Japanese imports Thexder and Silpheed," adding that Sierra "will begin converting and distributing all Game Arts products in the U.S. and Europe."[^ref-31] The article quoted Game Arts President Yoichi Miyaji: "Sierra On-Line is a superior software house. When we were users, Sierra was established as a company, and was already releasing wonderful games." It named Firehawk and Zeliard, "Game Arts' answer to the role playing adventure," as that year's releases.[^ref-31]

Ken Williams later described the arrangement this way:

> "Our relationship with Game-Arts was just a licensing deal. The hope was that they would help us establish our games in the Japan market, and we would help them in the US."[^ref-26]

Williams's recollection was hedged ("I think the only Game Arts' games we published were Thexder and Silpheed") and leaves out Zeliard and Fire Hawk, which Sierra also released.[^ref-26][^ref-31] He said: "Both were successful, although neither was a huge hit." He added that "everything we did in Japan failed," and that Sierra eventually closed its Tokyo office "after losing a fair amount of money there."[^ref-26]

### Later Years

In the 1990s Game Arts moved toward console role-playing games. Lunar: The Silver Star (Sega Mega-CD, 1992) was planned as a "different kind of RPG" that used the CD-ROM format for full-motion video, high-quality audio and voice acting, built around animated story sequences by artist Toshiyuki Kubooka.[^ref-21] Grandia followed on the Sega Saturn in December 1997, with a PlayStation version in 1999.[^ref-1]

On October 18, 2005, GamesIndustry.biz reported that GungHo Online Entertainment had acquired "veteran development studio Game Arts," noting that GungHo had "a long-standing relationship" with the company.[^ref-25] Game Arts developed Ragnarok Odyssey (PlayStation Vita, 2012), which GungHo published.[^ref-30] The company marked its 40th anniversary on March 2, 2025.[^ref-27]

## Notable Games

### Thexder (1985)

Game Arts' first game, released in 1985 for Japanese personal computers and published in the United States by Sierra in 1987.[^ref-1] See [[1985 - Thexder|Thexder]].

### Silpheed (1986)

A polygon-based shooter for the PC-8801, published in North America by Sierra; Sierra dated the US release to April 1989.[^ref-28][^ref-31] It was remade for the Sega CD in 1993.[^ref-28]

### Lunar: The Silver Star (1992)

Released for the Sega Mega-CD in June 1992, with a North American release by Working Designs in December 1993.[^ref-1] Its design goals and animated sequences are described above.[^ref-21]

### Grandia (1997)

Released for the Sega Saturn in Japan on December 18, 1997, and later ported to the PlayStation (Japan June 1999, US September 1999).[^ref-1]

## Key People

- **Yoichi Miyaji**: co-founder; planned, made and produced many of the company's games, and was its representative director until 2005.[^ref-23] Wikipedia names him as president and CEO in 2005.[^ref-1] Sources differ on how long he held the post: GameSpot's 2011 obituary of his brother calls him "the current CEO of the company."[^ref-24]
- **Takeshi Miyaji**: co-founder and Yoichi's younger brother; designer of Silpheed. He later became CEO of G-mode and died in July 2011, aged 45.[^ref-23][^ref-24][^ref-28]
- **[[Hibiki Godai]]** and **Satoshi Uesaka**: designers of Thexder (see the [[1985 - Thexder|Thexder]] page).
- **Noriyuki Iwadare**: composer for the Lunar and Grandia series.[^ref-22]
