# Impressions Games

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Impressions Games was a British game developer and publisher founded by David Lester with his partner Edward Grabowski, best known for historical strategy games and its City Building series: Caesar, Pharaoh, Zeus: Master of Olympus and Emperor: Rise of the Middle Kingdom.[^ref-9][^ref-36][^ref-37] Sierra On-Line bought the company in June 1995, and Vivendi Universal Games closed it in spring 2004 (May per CNN/Money; April per MobyGames and Wikipedia).[^ref-23][^ref-27][^ref-24][^ref-20][^ref-9]

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Sources differ on the founding year. Lester's own account, in a 2013 interview, is that he founded Impressions Software "in 1988 when just 22"; MobyGames and Wikipedia give 1989.[^ref-22][^ref-20][^ref-9] Lester was not previously in the games industry: he "trained as an accountant with Arthur Young," grew bored within a year, and, having "various computer games contacts," decided "to start a computer games publishing company as a hobby with some friends."[^ref-22] (See [[David Lester]].) Computer Gaming World's February 1993 company profile names Edward Grabowski as Lester's co-founder: "David Lester and Edward Grabowski established their company because they were unhappy with the European games scene." The two had been working together since 1984, when Lester coded his first game, *Just Imagine ...!*, and Grabowski designed its graphics.[^ref-36] MobyGames gives Lester as the founder but says Grabowski "was crucial to the birth of Impressions", and that he later did racing management simulations for MicroProse.[^ref-37]

Its first Dalglish-licensed game was the management title Kenny Dalglish Soccer Manager (1989), released on its Cognito label; the arcade game Kenny Dalglish Soccer Match followed, which MobyGames calls his "second licensed game".[^ref-9][^ref-22][^ref-25][^ref-26][^ref-34][^ref-35] MobyGames describes a UK company focused on strategy and business games, working with partners and freelancers including Edward Grabowski and Simon Bradbury.[^ref-20] Its early catalog included The Charge of the Light Brigade (1991), which it developed, and Merchant Colony (1991), which Wikipedia lists among the games it published.[^ref-9]

In 1992 Impressions opened a US division in Farmington, Connecticut, which later moved to Cambridge, Massachusetts.[^ref-20] Lester's own 2013 account places the first US office in Boston instead: "I bought a one way ticket out to Boston and set up an office there."[^ref-22] He recalled setting up a US office "with two people to start with and over the next three years we built that up to fifty people."[^ref-22] Caesar, the first City Building game, came out in 1992, followed by Caesar Deluxe in 1993.[^ref-9]

### Sierra Era (1995–2004)

Sierra On-Line bought Impressions in 1995.[^ref-9][^ref-22] Sierra's annual report for the year to March 1996 dates the deal: "On June 20, 1995 the Company also merged with Inspiration, a developer of strategy games, in exchange for 730,352 shares of Sierra's common stock." Software Inspiration Ltd., registered in England, was the parent of Impressions Software, Inc.[^ref-27] A Seattle Times list of Sierra acquisitions, published in January 1996, gives the date and size: "June 1995: Impressions Software Inc., Cambridge, Mass. Produces strategy games. Employees: 40."[^ref-23]

PC Gamer's September 1995 report described Impressions as "creators of the popular strategy titles Front Lines, High Seas Trader, and Powerhouse." Impressions' director of marketing, Chris Foster, said: "We've been looking for a strategic alliance to give us a better position in the marketplace for some time." Sierra chairman Ken Williams called Caesar II "one of the best strategy games I've seen in a long time," and Lester said Sierra's "top-notch development resources, together with their distribution strength, were key elements in our decision to join forces."[^ref-28]

MobyGames says that after many years running the company, Lester "passed creative leadership of the company to Chris Beatrice, who oversaw most of its remaining titles, most of which were part of the City Building series."[^ref-20] In June 1999 CNET Gamecenter's Mark Asher named Impressions, not Blizzard, as Havas Interactive's most profitable game division: "Last year, Impressions made a nice $20 million in profits," and it "did this with only 40 employees."[^ref-29]

### Later Years and Closure

In October 2001, during development of Lords of the Realm III, Mike Ryder, then president of Sierra Entertainment, forced a change in management at Impressions, and Rod Nakamoto was installed as the new director.[^ref-9] Lords of the Realm III, released in March 2004, was Impressions' final game.[^ref-9]

Reporting on June 21, 2004, CNN/Money said that Papyrus and Impressions "both had solid track records in the industry but were closed last month because they had not been living up to corporate expectations," which places the closure in May 2004.[^ref-24] MobyGames and the Wikipedia text give April 2004.[^ref-20][^ref-9]

Tilted Mill Entertainment, the studio many former Impressions staff joined, was founded before the closure, not after it. MobyGames says a management shake-up during Lords of the Realm III's development "ended with the departure of Beatrice and much of the company's leadership and senior staff, to form a new studio called Tilted Mill Entertainment."[^ref-20] Wikipedia's article on the studio says it was founded in 2002 by Beatrice, business manager Peter Haffenreffer and designer Jeff Fiske.[^ref-30] Sources differ on when Beatrice left: Tilted Mill's 2005 staff page credits him on Lords of the Realm III "through 9/2001", Wikipedia's article on Beatrice says he founded Tilted Mill in 2001, and the Tilted Mill article gives 2002.[^ref-32][^ref-31][^ref-30]

## Notable Games

### Caesar (1992) and Caesar III (1998)

Caesar began the City Building series in 1992, and Caesar III followed in 1998.[^ref-9] See [[1992 - Caesar|Caesar]] and [[1998 - Caesar III|Caesar III]].

### Pharaoh (1999)

The Egyptian entry in the City Building series.[^ref-9] See [[1999 - Pharaoh|Pharaoh]].

### Zeus: Master of Olympus (2000)

Set in ancient Greece, Zeus received "generally favorable reviews" according to Metacritic and was nominated for PC Strategy Game of the Year at the 4th Annual Interactive Achievement Awards.[^ref-21]

### Lords of the Realm II (1996)

A medieval strategy game developed by Impressions and published by Sierra On-Line for DOS, Windows and Macintosh, released in North America on 20 November 1996. Wikipedia describes it as one of the highest-selling computer games of 1997 and 1998.[^ref-33] See [[1996 - Lords of the Realm II|Lords of the Realm II]].

## Key People

- **[[David Lester]]**: founder. MobyGames says Impressions' biggest successes were the Lords of the Realm and Caesar series, "both of which came from collaboration between Lester and programmer/designer Simon Bradbury."[^ref-20] He later handed creative leadership to Chris Beatrice, and went on to form Firefly Studios in 1999 with Simon Bradbury and Eric Ouellette.[^ref-20]
- **[[Simon Bradbury]]**: programmer and designer on early titles; co-founded Firefly Studios in 1999 with Lester and Eric Ouellette.[^ref-20]
- **[[Chris Beatrice]]**: took over creative leadership from Lester and oversaw most of the later City Building games; co-founded Tilted Mill Entertainment.[^ref-20][^ref-30]
- **Rod Nakamoto**: installed as director in October 2001.[^ref-9]
