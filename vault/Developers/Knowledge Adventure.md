# Knowledge Adventure

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Knowledge Adventure was an American educational software developer and publisher founded in California in 1991.[^ref-14][^ref-5] It is best known for the *JumpStart* learning series.[^ref-21] The company was based in Glendale, California, in the 1990s and later in Torrance.[^ref-22][^ref-9] It was never a Sierra studio and Sierra did not publish its own line: from 1997 it was a **sister company** to Sierra On-Line inside CUC Software, and it later developed the Dr. Brain games that Sierra had started.[^ref-21][^ref-17] The company renamed itself JumpStart Games in 2012 and closed in 2023.[^ref-5][^ref-13]

## History

### Founding

MobyGames and TV Tropes both say Knowledge Adventure was co-founded in 1991 by Rick Gibson and the brothers Bill and Larry Gross.[^ref-14][^ref-13] CUC International's 1997 release names Bill Gross as "founder and chairman" and Larry Gross as chief executive officer.[^ref-21] Its early titles were science-exploration programs for DOS PCs, such as *Space Adventure* (DOS, 1992) and *3-D Body Adventure* (DOS, 1994).[^ref-25][^ref-26][^ref-22] The *Los Angeles Times* reported in 1996 that director Steven Spielberg "boosted the company’s profile substantially in 1994 when he bought a small equity stake", and estimated the company's annual sales at about $30 million.[^ref-22]

### CUC Software: a sister company to Sierra (1996–1998)

CUC International announced on 5 November 1996 that it would buy Knowledge Adventure.[^ref-22][^ref-5] The deal was completed on 31 January 1997. CUC issued 3,461,551 shares of its common stock, which valued the acquisition at $86.1 million.[^ref-21] Knowledge Adventure became "part of the CUC Software division, which includes Davidson, Blizzard Entertainment, and Sierra On-Line", and it stayed in Glendale.[^ref-21] The *Los Angeles Times* reported that the idea of buying Knowledge Adventure "came from employees of Davidson & Associates and Sierra Online".[^ref-22]

That shared ownership is how Knowledge Adventure came to make Dr. Brain games. Jim Winstead Jr., lead programmer of *Dr. Brain Thinking Games: IQ Adventure*, wrote that "KA and Sierra had wound up under the same corporate umbrella". The "games group" at Knowledge Adventure built *IQ Adventure* and *Puzzle Madness* using Blizzard's networking and graphics library.[^ref-17]

### Cendant, Havas and Vivendi (1997–2004)

When CUC merged with HFS in December 1997 to form Cendant, CUC Software became Cendant Software. In 1998 the French media group Havas agreed to buy Cendant Software. Havas was later acquired by Vivendi.[^ref-5][^ref-14] Knowledge Adventure moved with each sale. MobyGames summarises: "The company was shuffled to Havas Interactive and then to Vivendi as a result of acquisitions."[^ref-14] *JumpStart Phonics Learning System* won Computer Educational Title of the Year at the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences' 2000 awards.[^ref-7]

### Knowledge Holdings and JumpStart Games (2004–2023)

On 6 October 2004 Vivendi Universal Games announced that it had sold Knowledge Adventure to the venture capital firms Azure Capital Partners and Telesoft Partners. Knowledge Adventure became part of Knowledge Holdings Inc. Vivendi kept the sales and distribution role for its titles.[^ref-23] In October 2012 the company renamed itself JumpStart Games, after its best-known series.[^ref-5][^ref-13] JumpStart Games announced on 13 June 2023 that it would end support for its games, and it closed on 1 July 2023.[^ref-5][^ref-14]

## Notable Games

### JumpStart Series (1994–2023)

The first title, *JumpStart Kindergarten*, was released on 14 November 1994.[^ref-27] It was developed by the independent studio Fanfare Software and published by Knowledge Adventure, which acquired Fanfare in 1995.[^ref-24] The series grew to cover other age groups, and JumpStart Games ended support for it when the studio closed in July 2023.[^ref-24]

### 3-D Body Adventure (1994)

*3-D Body Adventure* was released for DOS in 1994, followed by Windows and Macintosh versions in 1995.[^ref-26] The *Los Angeles Times* named it as one of the company's science exploration programs.[^ref-22]

### Space Adventure (1992)

*Space Adventure* was released for DOS in 1992 and for Macintosh in 1994. It was re-released for Windows in 2014.[^ref-25]
