# Leisure Suit Larry 8: Lust in Space

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

**Leisure Suit Larry 8: Lust in Space**, also known by the alternate title **Leisure Suit Larry Explores Uranus**, was a planned sequel in the long-running adult comedy adventure series that was never released.[^ref-1] It was in early development at Sierra On-Line in 1998, with series creator [[Al Lowe]] as its designer.[^ref-1][^ref-2] The game was intended to be the first entry in the series rendered in full 3D.[^ref-1][^ref-2]

The project fell victim to the corporate upheaval that followed the acquisition of Sierra by CUC International. Al Lowe has written that "Sierra management was in turmoil after Ken and Roberta got bought out by CUC. The new management tried to get me to design my next game without a contract, saying 'we'll work it out later.'"[^ref-3][^ref-17] By his account, "At the end of 1998, while Sierra and I were negotiating a contract for *Leisure Suit Larry 8: Lust in Space*, Sierra cancelled production."[^ref-18]

Only a few test renders and a 21-second 3D test animation survive from the project.[^ref-1][^ref-18][^ref-6] Lowe has said he "refused to flesh out the design until we reached an agreement on terms. They didn't want to negotiate, so I left."[^ref-17] He gives no exact date for his departure; some secondary sources give February 22, 1999, the day Sierra closed its Oakhurst adventure studio, but no primary source confirms Lowe left that day (see Easter Eggs and Trivia).[^ref-1][^ref-19]

## Story Summary

The game was never designed in detail, but Al Lowe later published the story he had in mind, cautioning that what was "in my mind" was "far different from a finished design."[^ref-17][^ref-3] *Love for Sail!* had ended with Larry carried off in a spaceship, and its ending teased a sequel "coming in 1998."[^ref-17][^ref-15]

In Lowe's outline, Larry wakes up in what he takes for "heaven": a world of disco balls, 1970s music and beautiful women. His captors are actually Amazonian alien warrior women whose immortal civilization had killed off its males and who now need to breed in order to conquer other planets, starting with Earth. Having learned of the "Thygh's Man Trophy" competition for the sexiest man alive from transmissions from the PMS Bouncy, they kidnap Larry. They brainwash him so that their ship looks to him like a casino, disco and spa, and their own "hideous faces" look like ideal women.[^ref-17]

Eventually Larry would discover that he was being used to father a generation of children who only looked human, an invasion force able to blend in on Earth. Having escaped their mind control, he would have to stop them and "literally save the planet!" Lowe intended the whole story to be told satirically, "poking fun at the thousands of other games that have used that theme before."[^ref-17]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

No interface design was ever published. The only surviving material is a pre-rendered test movie, but Lowe has said that "in the real game you would have been able to move the camera up, down, and all around the scene, changing angles and distance at will."[^ref-18]

### Structure and Progression

No details of the game's structure were made public. Lowe refused to flesh out the design until he had a contract, and the project was cancelled before that happened.[^ref-17][^ref-18]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

No puzzles or mechanics were ever documented. Unseen64 notes that only "very early test stuff" was being done while Lowe waited for the project to be approved.[^ref-1]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

As a cancelled game that was never released, Leisure Suit Larry 8 received no contemporary reviews from gaming publications.

### Modern Assessment

The cancellation has been documented by preservation sites such as Unseen64, which records the game's planned 3D presentation and the surviving test renders.[^ref-1]

Some reviewers of later Larry games have referenced the cancelled eighth installment. A review of *Leisure Suit Larry: Wet Dreams Don't Dry* called the modern revival "very much the Leisure Suit Larry 8 you've dreamed of and never thought you'd get to actually play."[^ref-5]

## Development

### Origins

Leisure Suit Larry 8 was planned as the follow-up to *Leisure Suit Larry: Love for Sail!* (1996), whose ending teased the sequel.[^ref-1][^ref-15] Al Lowe was its designer, and the game was in early development in 1998.[^ref-1][^ref-2] It was to be titled "Lust in Space", and some references also give the alternate title "Explores Uranus".[^ref-1] Sierra's *Larry's Casino* advertised the game as *Leisure Suit Larry 8: Lust in Space*.[^ref-11]

Like a cancelled Space Quest sequel of the same period, Larry 8 was to be in full 3D.[^ref-1]

### Production

Development never progressed beyond the earliest stages. According to Unseen64, "only some very early test stuff was being done while Al Lowe waited for the project to be approved, but in the end funding was cut and the project cancelled."[^ref-1]

Lowe has said the new management after the CUC acquisition wanted him to design the game without a contract, promising to "work it out later." He refused to flesh out the design until terms were agreed, and when Sierra would not negotiate, he left.[^ref-17] At the end of 1998, while the contract was still being negotiated, Sierra cancelled production, and it later told Lowe it had no plans ever to make the game.[^ref-18]

During those months, Jason Zayas, lead animator on *Love for Sail!* and *King's Quest: Mask of Eternity*, built a prototype 3D Larry. The result was a 21-second test movie, with original music by David Henry and Larry voiced by Jan Rabson, that Lowe later published as "the only fragment of *Leisure Suit Larry 8* that you will ever get a chance to see."[^ref-18]

### Technical Achievements

The planned transition to full 3D graphics would have been a first for the series.[^ref-1][^ref-2] Only a few test renders and the short test movie survive.[^ref-1][^ref-18]

### Technical Specifications

Technical specifications for the game were never finalized or publicly released due to the early cancellation of the project.

### Cut Content

The game was cancelled before full development began, so there is little cut content to document beyond the project itself. A few test renders survive.[^ref-1] The 3D test video has been preserved by fan site Larry Laffer Dot Net as well as on Lowe's own site.[^ref-6][^ref-18]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- *Love for Sail!* referenced the full title of the game and its teaser in an easter egg triggered after completing the game.[^ref-1] Its ending advertised the sequel as "coming in 1998."[^ref-15]
- The alternate title "Leisure Suit Larry Explores Uranus" continued the series' tradition of sexual innuendo in its naming conventions.[^ref-1]
- Lowe dates the cancellation only to "the end of 1998"[^ref-17][^ref-18] and his own bio dates his departure only to the year: "Since I left Sierra On-Line in 1998".[^ref-21] Game Developer says he was laid off "during a massive layoff in February, 1999."[^ref-22] Unseen64's February 22, 1999 date is taken from Wikipedia, and Josh Mandel's "R.I.P. Sierra" column, written three days later, still listed Lowe among the staff in Bellevue who "still labor diligently on games."[^ref-1][^ref-19]
- In 2020, *Wet Dreams Dry Twice* nodded to the cancelled game with mentions of Uranus and UFO wreckage containing Larry-like aliens.[^ref-10]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

As an unreleased game, Leisure Suit Larry 8 had no direct sales or commercial impact. Its cancellation ended Al Lowe's work on the series at Sierra; he did not return to it until *Leisure Suit Larry: Reloaded* (2013).[^ref-12]

### Series Impact

The next new Larry game did not appear until 2004, when *Leisure Suit Larry: Magna Cum Laude* was released, developed by High Voltage Software.[^ref-7] It was the first game in the series without any involvement from series creator Al Lowe, and it introduced a new protagonist, Larry Lovage, Larry Laffer's nephew.[^ref-7]

Lowe has written that after cancelling Larry 8, Sierra "produced some games with the Larry name without me."[^ref-18]

### Later Developments

*Leisure Suit Larry: Box Office Bust* (2009) continued the Larry Lovage storyline, featuring voice talent including Allen Covert, Jay Mohr, Dave Attell, Shannon Elizabeth, Carmen Electra, Patrick Warburton, and Jeffrey Tambor.[^ref-8]

In 2013 Lowe returned to the series with *Leisure Suit Larry: Reloaded*, a crowdfunded remake of the first game "released in collaboration with series creator Al Lowe."[^ref-12] That year he said Leisure Suit Larry 8 was "absolutely" still in the works: "Our fans deserve to see how Larry's life plays out!"[^ref-20] No such game has been released.

The series was later revived with a more traditional approach in *Leisure Suit Larry: Wet Dreams Don't Dry* (2018), developed by CrazyBunch and published by Assemble Entertainment.[^ref-5][^ref-9] Shortly before it was delisted from Steam on October 23, 2026, it held a "Very Positive" rating there, with 85% of 1,414 user reviews positive (as captured on October 9, 2026).[^ref-9] A sequel, *Leisure Suit Larry: Wet Dreams Dry Twice*, followed in 2020.[^ref-10]

### Historical Context

Lowe ties the cancellation to the management turmoil that followed CUC's buyout of Ken and Roberta Williams.[^ref-3][^ref-17] Sierra's main adventure game studio was closed shortly afterwards, before Larry 8 could come to pass.[^ref-11][^ref-1]

The Sierra-era release *Larry's Casino* (1998), a standalone casino game, is described by Hardcore Gaming 101 as "technically the last Larry game produced with Al Lowe's involvement" at Sierra.[^ref-11]

### Collections

Leisure Suit Larry 8 was never included in any collection releases, as it was never completed. The classic Larry games have appeared in various compilations over the years, including a 1997 collection.[^ref-12]

### Fan Projects

The cancelled game has been documented by preservation communities. Unseen64 records its cancelled status and the circumstances of its demise.[^ref-1] Fan site Larry Laffer Dot Net hosts Lowe's plot outline and the test video from the development period.[^ref-3][^ref-6]

### Related Publications

No official publications were produced for Leisure Suit Larry 8 due to its cancellation.

### Series Context

The series has sold over 10 million copies as of 2011, and Sierra co-founder Ken Williams has described Leisure Suit Larry as the company's best-known franchise.[^ref-12]

Fan discussion has sometimes confused *Magna Cum Laude* with "Larry 8". In a 2004 AGDI forum thread, one poster wrote that *Magna Cum Laude* "is not much of an adventure game... there's nothing in terms of puzzles" and that "the game (especially the language) is much more raunchy than the earlier games."[^ref-4] Lowe addressed the question directly on his site under the heading "Is MCL Larry 8?"[^ref-18]
