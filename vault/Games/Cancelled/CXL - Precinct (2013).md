# Precinct

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Precinct was an ambitious crowdfunded police adventure video game announced in 2013 by [[Jim Walls]], the creator of Sierra On-Line's legendary Police Quest series[^ref-1][^ref-16]. Conceived as a spiritual successor to the classic franchise whose official name was owned by Activision, the project sought to revive the police procedural adventure genre for a new generation of players[^ref-2][^ref-12]. The game was designed to combine authentic police procedures with modern gaming technology, featuring first-person gameplay with intense action sequences including shootouts, high-speed car chases, investigations, foot pursuits, and hand-to-hand combat[^ref-2][^ref-13].

Set in the fictional corrupt town of Fraser Canyon, California, Precinct would have placed players in the role of Officer Maxwell Jones, starting as a rookie and progressing through the ranks while facing adrenaline-charged scenarios and conducting real police procedures[^ref-3]. The project launched on Kickstarter on July 16, 2013, and the creators cancelled the campaign on August 6, 2013, with $85,756 pledged of its $500,000 goal[^ref-8]. The same day, the team moved to a staged funding campaign on its own website, saying it wanted to build a playable pilot rather than rely on a tech demo video[^ref-17][^ref-18]. That campaign failed too: it was shut down by August 26, 2013, after raising around $12,000 of a $400,000 goal[^ref-19]. The game was never made.

Both crowdfunding attempts failed, and with them the project ended[^ref-19].

## Story Summary

Precinct was set in the fictional town of Fraser Canyon, California, a corrupt municipality plagued by crime and institutional decay[^ref-3]. Players would assume the role of Officer Maxwell Jones, a young police officer beginning his career in law enforcement and navigating the treacherous waters of a town where the line between law and lawlessness had become dangerously blurred[^ref-4].

The narrative was designed to follow Jones from his days as a rookie patrol officer through his advancement in the department[^ref-3]. The campaign described "a gripping crime story based on true accounts of real police cases," with "corrupt cops, greedy public officials, and a deadly struggle for control of Fraser Canyon's criminal underground"[^ref-8].

As with the original Police Quest games, players would have had to follow correct police procedure[^ref-2].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Precinct was designed to utilize a first-person perspective, marking a significant departure from the earlier Police Quest games which employed third-person views[^ref-2]. The game was being developed in the Unity3D engine, allowing for real-time 3D environments that would bring Fraser Canyon to life with modern graphical fidelity[^ref-4].

The gameplay was conceived as an adventure game with substantial action sequences, blending the investigative and procedural elements that defined Police Quest with more dynamic gameplay mechanics suitable for contemporary audiences[^ref-2].

### Structure and Progression

Precinct was planned as a five-game series, with the first game due in the third quarter of 2014[^ref-16]. The launch text called it "an episodic series"[^ref-4], but the Kickstarter FAQ later answered "Is Precinct an episodic game? No," explaining that each game in the series would be a full product[^ref-8].

After the Kickstarter was cancelled, the staged funding campaign set the following milestones[^ref-3][^ref-18]:

- **Proof of Concept ($25,000):** Initial demonstration of core gameplay mechanics
- **Vertical Slice ($90,000):** A representative playable section showcasing the full game experience
- **Game Demo ($250,000):** A substantial playable demo for public release
- **Full Game ($400,000):** The official release of Precinct

### Puzzles and Mechanics

True to its Police Quest heritage, Precinct was designed to require players to follow correct police procedure[^ref-2]. The campaign listed its action sequences as "shootouts, high speed car chases, investigations, foot pursuits, hand-to-hand combat, and more"[^ref-13].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

As Precinct was never released, there are no reviews of a finished product. However, the announcement and subsequent Kickstarter campaign generated significant coverage in gaming media:

**Kotaku** covered the Kickstarter launch in July 2013 under the headline "Guys, Police Quest Is (Basically) Back"[^ref-2]. The coverage highlighted Jim Walls' credentials as the creator of the original Police Quest series.

**Kicktraq** tracked the campaign and recorded its cancellation by the creator[^ref-1].

**Sierra Classic Gaming** provided detailed coverage of the project, documenting the funding tiers and staged development approach[^ref-3]. Their comprehensive game listing served as a primary source for campaign details.

**Larry Laffer Net** covered the project with a focus on Sierra alumni connections, featuring Al Lowe's endorsement and detailed information about the development team at Tiny Castle Studios[^ref-4].

Publications covered the campaign's launch, its cancellation, the move to staged funding, and that campaign's own cancellation[^ref-16][^ref-17][^ref-18][^ref-19].

### Modern Assessment

Precinct remains a notable example of a cancelled crowdfunding project from the adventure game revival era. The game is documented on **Sierra Classic Gaming** as an unreleased title from a legendary designer[^ref-3]. The project's failure to secure funding demonstrated the challenges of reviving classic franchises even with original creators attached.

**Wikipedia** now documents "The Precinct" (2025), an unrelated game by Fallen Tree Games. Metacritic scores it 66/100 on PC and PS5 and 75/100 on Xbox Series X/S, per Wikipedia; it is a completely separate project[^ref-5].

## Development

### Origins

Precinct emerged from Jim Walls' desire to return to the police adventure genre he had pioneered at Sierra On-Line[^ref-2]. Walls was a former California Highway Patrol officer; the campaign bio says he "left the force in 1984 after being traumatized in a violent shootout"[^ref-8], while Wikipedia dates the shootout to January 1984 and his retirement from the CHP to 1986[^ref-15]. Techzwn's 2013 interview also says he left the force in 1986, "after 15 years of service"[^ref-20]. He had brought an unprecedented level of authenticity to the original Police Quest series[^ref-4]. However, with the Police Quest name owned by Activision following Sierra's acquisition, a spiritual successor under a new title was the only viable path forward[^ref-2].

Walls first announced plans for a Police Quest successor, funded through Kickstarter, on a podcast on February 2, 2013[^ref-15]. The project was developed under the banner of Jim Walls Reloaded, Inc. of Bass Lake, California, in partnership with development studio Tiny Castle Studios[^ref-4][^ref-16].

Al Lowe, creator of Leisure Suit Larry and a programmer on the original Police Quest, endorsed the project, noting: "If you've played Police Quest, you know exactly what Jim Walls is like, because that game is Jim Walls"[^ref-4].

### Production

The development team began working with Unity3D to create a modern first-person adventure game that would honor the legacy of Police Quest while appealing to contemporary players[^ref-4]. The base plan was a DRM-free digital release for PC and Mac. A Linux version depended on the $700,000 stretch goal, and iOS, Android, XBLA and PSN on the $800,000 one[^ref-8].

The Kickstarter campaign launched on July 16, 2013, seeking $500,000[^ref-8]. With $85,756 pledged by 1,870 backers, the creators cancelled it on August 6, ten days before its scheduled end[^ref-8].

Robert Lindsley, president of Jim Walls Reloaded, told Joystiq the team wanted to build a playable pilot version, letting potential backers "experience" the vision behind the game rather than judge it from a tech demo video[^ref-17]. The same day, a staged funding campaign opened on the game's own website, with milestones at $25,000, $90,000, $250,000 and $400,000; pledges would only be charged if a stage was funded, and backer rewards were dropped because they had taken up "nearly $200K" of the original goal[^ref-18]. On August 26, 2013, PC Gamer reported that this campaign had also been cancelled after raising around $12,000 of its $400,000 goal, with no backer money taken[^ref-19]. Wikipedia's Jim Walls article gives a lower figure, saying the second campaign "did not surpass $7,000"[^ref-15].

### Technical Achievements

The game was designed to leverage the Unity3D engine to create real-time 3D environments representing the fictional town of Fraser Canyon[^ref-4]. The first-person perspective represented a significant technical evolution from the earlier Police Quest games, allowing for more immersive investigation sequences and action gameplay.

The planned distribution model included digital download via Steam, representing the shift in game distribution that had occurred since the original Police Quest era[^ref-4].

### Technical Issues

The project never reached a state where technical issues in gameplay could be documented. The primary issues were financial and organizational rather than technical, with the Kickstarter campaign failing to generate sufficient momentum before being cancelled[^ref-1].

## Legacy

### Cancellation and Aftermath

The 2013 *Precinct* Kickstarter raised $85,756 of its $500,000 goal before being cancelled on August 6, 2013[^ref-8]. The staged funding campaign that replaced it launched the same day and failed as well, cancelled around August 26, 2013, at roughly $12,000 of its $400,000 goal[^ref-18][^ref-19]. Precinct was never made.

### Posthumous Echoes

In May 2025, a separate (and unrelated) game titled **The Precinct** released from a different developer; despite the title overlap, that 2025 product has no connection to the 2013 Jim Walls / Sierra Classic Gaming project documented here[^ref-5][^ref-6][^ref-7]. The Sierra-lineage *Precinct* concept therefore exists primarily as a "what if" — a near-miss for a Jim Walls / Police Quest spiritual successor that the post-Kickstarter market never funded[^ref-2][^ref-4].
