# Front Page Sports Football Pro 99

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 27, 2026</small>

## Overview

Front Page Sports Football Pro 99, released as NFL Football Pro '99[^ref-5] and announced in September 1998 as Sierra Sports Football Pro '99,[^ref-2] was the seventh and final release in the Front Page Sports Football series.[^ref-1][^ref-8] It was released for Windows on December 22, 1998,[^ref-5][^ref-16][^ref-19] and this installment marked a departure for the Front Page Sports franchise, abandoning the series' traditional "Front Page Sports" branding in favor of the new "Sierra Sports" label as part of a company-wide restructuring.[^ref-2] The game was developed by [[Synergistic Software]], the Renton, Washington studio behind Football Pro, and published by Sierra under its Sierra Sports division;[^ref-5][^ref-1] it was rebuilt with a new 3D engine to compete with the likes of the Madden NFL series.[^ref-1]

The game attempted to modernize the venerable franchise with an all-new 3D arcade engine featuring polygonal players and stadiums, a complete financial model for team management, and comprehensive simulation features.[^ref-4] Howard David, the voice of NFL on CBS radio, provided play-by-play commentary.[^ref-4][^ref-24][^ref-25] However, the game was rushed out for the 1998 holiday season in what Wikipedia's series history calls "a completely unplayable state",[^ref-1] with reviewers documenting bugs ranging from kicking and ball handling to player AI.[^ref-4]

The game was released and sold at retail, then recalled: on January 21, 1999, Sierra Sports announced that NFL Football Pro '99, "which shipped to retailers just four weeks ago", was being pulled from shelves over "errors in the program's code", with buyers offered a full refund and a free Sierra game.[^ref-16] Customers who had purchased the game before January 20, 1999 could return it with the original receipt.[^ref-17] GameSpot called the recall "almost unprecedented".[^ref-11] Sierra president David Grenewetzki issued a public apology, admitting the company had "dropped the ball" by letting the end of the football season influence its release decision.[^ref-3][^ref-16] The game itself was therefore not cancelled; the cancelled title was its planned successor, NFL Football Pro 2000, announced at recall time for August 1999 and cancelled in early 1999 after Sierra's February 22, 1999 reorganisation, which closed four studios and consolidated Synergistic.[^ref-16][^ref-1]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

NFL Football Pro '99 represented a fundamental shift in the series' approach to football simulation. Sierra announced they were retiring the Front Page Sports logo while upgrading the graphics with an all-new 3D engine.[^ref-3] The game featured 3D polygonal players and fully rendered 3D stadiums, a significant technical leap from previous installments in the series.[^ref-4] The box feature list advertised multi-button joystick controls, a new adjustable play-calling interface and Direct3D support for 3D acceleration.[^ref-5]

The title included multiplayer options, though GameOver found play choppy on low-end systems and connections;[^ref-4] the box feature list also advertised e-mail and FTP support for remote league play.[^ref-5] Play-by-play commentary was provided by Howard David, the voice of NFL on CBS radio, adding broadcast-style audio presentation to matches.[^ref-4]

### Structure and Progression

The game featured a complete franchise mode with comprehensive team management capabilities. Players could manage all aspects of their football organization through what was marketed as a "complete financial model for team management."[^ref-4] The simulation tracked extensive statistics across hundreds of categories, continuing the series' tradition of detailed statistical tracking that had recorded more than 300 statistical categories in previous versions.[^ref-1]

One significant change from previous entries was the elimination of the FAST SIM mode, which had allowed players to quickly simulate games without watching full gameplay.[^ref-4] GameOver noted that Sierra had not previously focused on the arcade aspect of football, but that with the new 3D engine "this year promised to be different".[^ref-4] Season simulation in the new engine proved extremely time-consuming, taking approximately one hour per simulated week.[^ref-4]

### Features and Mechanics

The game continued the series' tradition of comprehensive roster management. Previous Front Page Sports football titles had featured 47-man teams with injured reserve and free agent pools, with players rated 0-99 across eight performance classifications.[^ref-1] The series was one of the first football simulations to include a career mode where players aged and retired.[^ref-1] Stock plays numbering over 200 were included along with a playbook editor for creating custom formations and strategies.[^ref-1]

The Jerry Maguire League, run by Sierra, used the game to simulate matches between the client lists of top NFL agents, even after the recall.[^ref-6][^ref-7]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The reception to NFL Football Pro '99 was overwhelmingly negative. PC Accelerator gave the game a "0" on its 1–10 scale, calling it "the worst piece of sports software we have ever seen in a shrink-wrapped box. It should have never left the building, and the decision to send it out to stores will stick with Sierra Sports for the next few years. And deservedly so."[^ref-18] A disclaimer beside the review noted that Sierra had pulled the game from shelves after the review was written.[^ref-18]

GameOver gave the game a 43% rating, with reviewer F. Krueger noting that "NFL Football Pro 99 had the potential to become the ultimate football game available today, instead we're stuck with one of the biggest letdowns of the year."[^ref-4] The review acknowledged the series' pedigree, stating "In past years, Front Page Sports Football Pro has been far and away the best football simulation available on the market. Nobody else comes close to giving a realistic feel for football both on and off the field."[^ref-4]

The contrast with previous entries in the series was stark. Front Page Sports Football had been named 1993 Sports Game of the Year by Computer Gaming World, which had called it "a better debut would be hard to imagine" and "One of the top product releases of 1992."[^ref-1] Computer Gaming World later ranked Front Page Sports Football Pro '95 as the 11th best computer game of all-time in 1996.[^ref-1] Computer Game Review had noted of the '96 version that "it's difficult to conceive how this game could be made better."[^ref-1]

### Modern Assessment

The game's legacy is defined primarily by its recall rather than its merits.

- **PC Accelerator:** 0 (on a 1–10 scale)[^ref-18]
- **GameOver:** 43%[^ref-4]

## Development

### Origins

The Front Page Sports Football series began in 1992 as a MS-DOS release from [[Dynamix]] and [[Sierra On-Line]].[^ref-1] The series was notable for being one of the first football simulations to include a career mode where players aged and retired, and for the number of statistics it offered.[^ref-1]

By the late 1990s, Sierra was restructuring their company into six distinct brands: Sierra Sports, Sierra Home, Dynamix, and others.[^ref-2] Ken Schramm of Sierra explained: "Basically we've split the company into six brands, Sierra Sports, Sierra Home, Dynamix, etc."[^ref-2] This restructuring led to the decision to retire the Front Page Sports branding, though Schramm assured fans: "We're not going to abandon the fan groups that have been loyal to the Front Page Sports brand."[^ref-2]

### Production

Development of NFL Football Pro '99 was undertaken with the ambitious goal of completely rebuilding the franchise's technical foundation. In September 1998 IGN reported that, "according to Electronic Boutique", the game would be available on November 1, under the new Sierra Sports branding.[^ref-2] The new 3D-arcade engine brought 3D polygonal players and stadiums.[^ref-4]

The game was developed by [[Synergistic Software]] of Renton, Washington.[^ref-1][^ref-5] MobyGames lists no individual credits for the '99 release.[^ref-5] After the recall, Grenewetzki acknowledged: "In '98 we rebuilt this game from the ground up and released it too early."[^ref-17]

### Technical Achievements

The game's 3D engine represented a significant technical ambition for the series. System requirements included a Pentium 200 processor minimum with 32 MB RAM, a 4X CD-ROM drive, a 2MB video card, and DirectX 6.0.[^ref-4] GameOver's reviewer called the Pentium 200 "the BARE minimum" and wrote that without "a Pentium 2-266 with a second generation 3D card, I have a hard time recommending you start playing this puppy."[^ref-4]

### Technical Specifications

**Multiplayer Options:**

- E-mail and FTP support for remote league play[^ref-5]

### Technical Issues

NFL Football Pro '99 shipped with numerous game-breaking bugs. The GameOver review documented extensive problems:[^ref-4]

**Gameplay Bugs:**
- Kickers could kick at full strength but the ball would only travel 30 yards[^ref-4]
- Running backs would sometimes stand motionless after receiving a handoff[^ref-4]
- Quarterbacks would randomly drop to their knee on first down[^ref-4]
- Running backs frequently fumbled handoffs[^ref-4]
- Receivers dropped 2 out of every 5 catchable passes even when wide open[^ref-4]
- Cornerbacks performed poorly at coverage and tackling[^ref-4]

**AI and Balance Issues:**
- The speed button allowed unrealistic player acceleration while the AI didn't use it[^ref-4]
- Blitzes were effective approximately 90% of the time[^ref-4]
- Season simulation took approximately one hour per simulated week[^ref-4]
- Statistics and team records were unrealistic[^ref-4]
- Injuries were overly severe—a hamstring injury could sideline a player for an entire year[^ref-4]
- Multiplayer performance was choppy on lower-end systems[^ref-4]

Sierra released two patches for the game, "Front Page Sports: Football 99 Update 1" and a beta update.[^ref-15] When Sierra later cancelled NFL Football Pro 2000, after the February 1999 reorganisation, Grenewetzki explained why the existing engine could not simply be fixed: "Simple patching was not going to do it. Many fundamental changes to the underlying engine were needed and there was significant risk associated with each major change."[^ref-1]

This was not the first time the series had experienced quality issues. The '96 version had crashed to desktop and required a patch.[^ref-1] The '98 version had problems including kickoff mix-ups, camera angle crashes, and incorrect stadium turf, requiring three major patches.[^ref-1]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- The game was originally titled "Front Page Sports: Football Pro '99" before being renamed to "Sierra Sports Football Pro '99" due to company rebranding.[^ref-2]
- Sierra announced the retirement of the Front Page Sports logo specifically for this game while upgrading to the new 3D engine.[^ref-3]
- The game eliminated the FAST SIM mode, which had been a popular feature allowing quick game simulation.[^ref-4]
- The Jerry Maguire League used the game to pit the client lists of the top 16 NFL agents against one another.[^ref-6][^ref-7]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

GameSpot reported that the recall reportedly involved over 100,000 units.[^ref-11] Before the recall, the Front Page Sports series had been commercially successful: combined sales of the Front Page Sports series across all sports had surpassed 500,000 units by the end of March 1996.[^ref-1]

Sierra president David Grenewetzki issued a public statement addressing the recall: "I want to apologize to all our loyal customers for releasing a product before it was ready. We came through 1998 with one great product after another only to stumble on virtually the last title we shipped in the year. We knew the potential for this product but we let the impending end of the football season influence our decision process. We figuratively 'dropped the ball' [...] Sierra Sports, like all Sierra divisions, is dedicated to producing the highest quality products possible. With this recall action we hope we can convey our intention to do right by our customers."[^ref-3]

### Awards

The series had accumulated significant accolades before its downfall:
- **1993 Sports Game of the Year** - Computer Gaming World[^ref-1]
- **11th Best Computer Game of All-Time** - Computer Gaming World, 1996 (for Front Page Sports Football Pro '95)[^ref-1]

### Collections

The Front Page Sports franchise encompassed multiple sports beyond football:
- Front Page Sports Baseball
- Front Page Sports Golf
- Front Page Sports Ski Racing
- Front Page Sports Trophy Bass
- Front Page Sports Trophy Rivers

NFL Football Pro '99 was the seventh release in the Front Page Sports Football line.[^ref-8]

### Critical Perspective

The Front Page Sports football series had spent seven years as one of the leading PC football simulations, noted for features like aging players, career modes and extensive statistics.[^ref-1] NFL Football Pro '99 was the series' final release.[^ref-1]

The disaster also highlighted the increasingly challenging economics of PC sports games in the late 1990s. The '99 edition was rebuilt to compete with the Madden NFL series, which "had won over so many football fans", but its graphics and audio were still judged inferior to Madden's.[^ref-1] GameOver contrasted the series' league depth with Madden 99's focus on arcade action.[^ref-4]

The recall's long-term impact on Sierra Sports was significant. The planned NFL Football Pro 2000 was cancelled, and the Front Page Sports Football line ended with this release, despite Ken Schramm's earlier assurance that Sierra would not abandon its Front Page Sports fan groups.[^ref-1][^ref-2] The Front Page Sports Football name was later revived by Cyanide Studio for an unrelated 2014 game.[^ref-10][^ref-14]
