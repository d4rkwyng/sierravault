# Red Baron 2013 Remake

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

*Red Baron* (no subtitle; informally the "2013 remake") was a new World War I dogfighting game from Mad Otter Games of Eugene, Oregon, led by [[Damon Slye]], co-founder of [[Dynamix]] and designer of the original [[1990 - Red Baron|Red Baron]][^ref-1][^ref-20]. It was not a Sierra or Dynamix product: Mad Otter announced in September 2009 that it had acquired the rights to Red Baron, Red Baron II and Red Baron 3D[^ref-23]. The project was pitched as a modern take on the series, with single- and multiplayer modes, rather than a straight remake of the 1990 game[^ref-7][^ref-20].

Mad Otter launched a Kickstarter on October 22, 2013, seeking $250,000[^ref-16][^ref-24][^ref-28]. A week in, with about $33,000 pledged, the studio posted that it would "cancel the Kickstarter campaign… for now" and relaunch in early 2014[^ref-17][^ref-18][^ref-26]. Kickstarter's own record, however, shows the campaign running to its November 22, 2013 deadline and ending as unsuccessful, with $40,493 from 1,118 backers[^ref-16]. The game was put on Steam Greenlight in October 2013, alongside the Kickstarter[^ref-20].

Development continued afterwards. In August 2014 Slye said the plan was to build a better prototype, grow a community and run a second Kickstarter[^ref-19]. In September 2015 he was looking to raise $50,000 for a prototype, "with a potential release in 2016"[^ref-1]. In January 2016 Mad Otter put the *original* Red Baron games on Steam "in hopes of generating interest in a new Red Baron crowdfunding campaign"[^ref-21]. No release followed, and no formal cancellation announcement has been found; the project simply went quiet. The planned game was to be DRM-free and offline in single-player, with no subscriptions and no pay-to-win[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

Mad Otter described the game in its own words: "we are not just building a simulation. We are not just building a game. We are building a time machine," a chance for players "to enter into the romanticized memories of a Great War Ace"[^ref-7]. The single-player campaign was to run "more than 30 missions played back to back from 1915 to 1918," with the player's pilot meeting historically authentic events and gaining access to new aircraft "just as the aces of the day did"[^ref-7].

An "ace AI system" was meant to recreate the behaviour of famous historical aces. The official site illustrated this with Manfred von Richthofen's line "I am a hunter. My brother Lothar is a butcher": in the game the Red Baron would not dive recklessly into battle unless he held an advantage[^ref-7]. Slye later described the aim as "a player-driven story that still offers a good authentic simulation under the hood"[^ref-1].

For the story and missions of the 1990 game, see [[1990 - Red Baron|Red Baron (1990)]].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The official site promised a "simple learning curve" so players could "jump in and play within minutes," flying fighters such as the Sopwith Camel and Fokker Triplane[^ref-7]. Immersion details were to include a visible pilot working the stick, throttle and guns, g-force blackouts and red-outs, sun blind spots, gun jams, oil splatters and dirty windscreens[^ref-7]. The team said it wanted to "hit the sweet spot" between simulation and game, and Oculus Rift support was announced[^ref-20]. In a backer update Slye rejected what he called "a false choice" between hyper-realistic simulation and arcade flying: the game would be "easy-to-learn, but under the hood it is a rich and deep simulation"[^ref-25].

### Structure and Progression

Announced features included[^ref-7][^ref-20]:
- **Single-player campaign:** 30+ missions from 1915 to 1918, offline and DRM-free, with randomly generated missions letting players "plot their own course"
- **Persistent multiplayer world:** drop-in missions "with or against your friends," on both historical and MOBA-style maps
- **Progression:** medals, promotions, new aircraft, paint and decals, and upgrades
- **Business model:** no subscriptions and no pay-to-win

Mad Otter said it wanted to take the MOBA genre "to the skies". Eurogamer noted that the pitch video showed what looked like a three-lane map, with multiplayer focused on clearing the skies of enemy fighters and destroying the enemy base[^ref-28].

## Development

### Origins

Slye co-founded Dynamix, which Sierra later bought, and designed the 1990 Red Baron, "a massive commercial and critical success"[^ref-1]. The original sold more than 500,000 copies and was Computer Gaming World's 1991 Simulation of the Year[^ref-4]. Slye left "the game and flight sim industry in 1994"[^ref-25] and stayed away for about 12 years, returning around 2007 to found Mad Otter, best known for the MMO *Villagers and Heroes*[^ref-1]. The studio also made *Ace of Aces* for InstantAction, GarageGames' web game platform[^ref-23]. In September 2009 Mad Otter announced that it now owned "all rights" to the original Red Baron games, and offered a $1,500 reward for their original C/C++ source code[^ref-23]. The team said its members had built Red Baron, Aces of the Pacific, Aces over Europe, A-10 Tank Killer and Ace of Aces[^ref-7]. At the 2013 launch Slye said of the series: "like some strange crazy boomerang it came back to me in the form of a red Fokker Triplane"[^ref-29].

### Production

The Kickstarter ran from October 22 to November 22, 2013 (30 days) with a $250,000 goal[^ref-16]. Backer reward tiers gave an estimated delivery of November 2014[^ref-16][^ref-28]. Its pitch video, with gameplay footage and team interviews, was praised by Cliqist, and Brian Fargo publicly backed it[^ref-19]. Minimum and recommended specifications were published alongside the Greenlight listing[^ref-20]. The campaign FAQ said Red Baron would support Linux and SteamOS, while OS X support was still undecided[^ref-16]. A CD Projekt Red forum post at launch passed on the Linux and DRM-free plans[^ref-10].

On October 30, 2013 Mad Otter told backers: "we are going to cancel the Kickstarter campaign… for now. We will still be moving forward with Red Baron with the plans to relaunch the campaign early next year"[^ref-18][^ref-26]. The update, signed by Damon Slye and the Mad Otter team, promised alpha access to anyone who followed the studio on social media and later backed the relaunch at a tier that included the game[^ref-26]. The campaign was never actually pulled. On November 21, the day before the deadline, Mad Otter posted a final update listing lessons learned: "It's better to Pre-Announce the product instead of just starting the Campaign and Announcing on the same day," and "Single-Player is really important"[^ref-27]. Kickstarter records the project as "Funding Unsuccessful" (not cancelled) at its deadline, with $40,493 pledged by 1,118 backers[^ref-16].

In Cliqist's 2014 postmortem, Slye said: "Our biggest mistake by far was not building up a community prior to launching the Kickstarter"[^ref-19]. In 2015 he told Polygon: "We didn't build a community before we launched the Kickstarter. We didn't present it very well. The game didn't look right. It looked a little arcade-ey. It didn't look like living history, interactive history, which is more what it should look like"[^ref-1]. He added that "flight sims are one of those things where good graphics really matter"[^ref-1].

## Legacy

### Crowdfunding Outcome

The campaign raised $40,493 of its $250,000 goal, about 16%[^ref-16]. Cliqist judged that the content itself was strong but that too few people saw it: the campaign had little pre-launch buzz and few updates after its first week[^ref-19]. Slye agreed, saying he "should have announced the campaign well before — 3 months or more even — and built up the community"[^ref-19].

### Steam Re-release of the Originals

The Red Baron games that did reach Steam in January 2016 were the old ones, not this project: the *Red Baron Pack*, with the original Red Baron, Red Baron 3D and the Mission Builder plus scanned manuals, published by Mad Otter[^ref-21][^ref-22]. GameWatcher reported they were released "in hopes of generating interest in a new Red Baron crowdfunding campaign"[^ref-21]. No such campaign has been found.
