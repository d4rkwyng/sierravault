# Manhunter: New York

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

Manhunter: New York is a post-apocalyptic adventure game released in 1988 by Sierra On-Line, developed by brothers Barry and Dave Murry, with their sister Dee Dee Murry on animated and background art, under their company Evryware[^ref-1][^ref-24]. Set in the year 2004, two years after an alien invasion by creatures called the Orbs, the game presents a dystopian vision of New York City under alien occupation[^ref-2][^ref-14]. The Orbs, described as giant floating eyeballs, have enslaved humanity and forced all humans to wear concealing robes, remain silent, and carry tracking devices[^ref-14].

Its gritty, sometimes gory visuals, unusual interface and real New York locations set it apart from Sierra's other titles, which were typically more family-oriented[^ref-14]. As the official manual puts it, "A Sierra 3-D Animated Adventure Game, simply stated, is an interactive movie where you become the main character"[^ref-6]. The player takes on the role of a human "Manhunter" - a tracker working for the alien overlords while secretly investigating the human underground resistance[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

In the year 2002, Earth was invaded by the Orb Alliance, alien creatures that appear as enormous floating eyeballs[^ref-2]. Two years later, the surviving humans live under a totalitarian regime where they are forbidden to speak, must wear hooded robes to conceal their forms, and are implanted with tracking devices that monitor their every movement[^ref-1][^ref-14]. MobyGames' user-written description says: "Brown monk robes are the only garments allowed, speaking is a capital offense, and every human has been implanted with a tracking chip"[^ref-1].

The player assumes the role of a Manhunter, one of the few humans selected to work for the Orbs as a tracker and investigator[^ref-11]. Using a device called the Manhunter Assignment Device (MAD) and an authentic map of New York City, the player must track human activities and investigate suspected resistance members[^ref-7][^ref-12].

> [!warning]- Plot Spoiler
> The Manhunter discovers that the Orbs are actually harvesting humans as a food source[^ref-14], revealing the true purpose behind the alien invasion.

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Unlike other AGI games, Manhunter: New York did not use a text parser; it used a first-person perspective and a rudimentary point-and-click interface instead[^ref-14]. Sierra's own walkthrough in the manual calls it "a unique Sierra 3-D animated adventure" that uses "an object oriented interface"[^ref-6].

Banjo's Mods, writing about the sequel, says that "like the first game, it is a huge departure from Sierra's normal adventure games, offering a dystopian horror theme with a first person interface"[^ref-16]. Players navigate through haunting locations that are replicas of a post-holocaust Manhattan, using authentic New York City geography as the foundation for the game world[^ref-12].

### Structure and Progression

The game takes place over four days, and each day the Orbs assign the player a crime or target to investigate[^ref-1]. The core mechanic involves tracking human movements using the MAD device, which displays where suspects have been and allows the player to follow their paths through the city[^ref-1][^ref-17]. As described in the manual, "Each adventure has a main goal, and yours in Manhunter is to track down humans for the Orb Alliance, learn the whereabouts of the human underground, and ultimately discover the true purpose behind the alien invasion"[^ref-7].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The game requires careful observation and memory skills, as Scorpia noted in her December 1988 Computer Gaming World column: "In spite of the arcade sequences, this is a clever game that requires you to really look at the screen and remember what you see"[^ref-26][^ref-18]. Players must piece together clues by visiting locations, examining evidence, and following the movements of suspects across New York City. The game also includes some arcade-style minigames and action sequences, such as dodging gang attacks or throwing knives between a bar patron's fingers[^ref-1][^ref-10].

### Modern Assessment

Modern retrospective reviews have been mixed. Adventure Classic Gaming gave it a 2/5 rating, with reviewer Jeremiah Kauffman describing it as "A game that is bad, yet interesting, with something in between that evades definition"[^ref-2]. On aggregate sites, the game has received varied scores: IMDb users rate it 7.2/10[^ref-20], while MobyGames shows 65% from critics and 3.5/5 from players[^ref-1].

PC Gamer's Richard Cobbett noted that "Manhunter is one of the weirdest SF adventures ever made" and observed that while "These were not particularly good adventures, but it's easy to see why people remember them so fondly"[^ref-21]. On MyAbandonware, users rate it 4.32/5 from 34 votes[^ref-10].

## Development

### Origins

Manhunter: New York was developed by Evryware, the company founded by brothers Barry and Dave Murry, rather than by Sierra's in-house team[^ref-24]. Their sister Dee Dee joined the team for this game[^ref-24]. The game was designed by Dave and Barry Murry using a modified AGI (Adventure Game Interpreter) system[^ref-11]. The sequel, Manhunter 2: San Francisco, used the same AGI engine[^ref-13].

### Production

The development team included executive producer Ken Williams, with the game system developed by Jeff Stephenson, Robert Heitman, and Chris Iden[^ref-1]. Documentation was handled by Jerry Albright and Dave Murry, animated and background scenes by Barry Murry and Dee Dee Murry, while location research was conducted by Dr. Gary Stonedahl and Julie Stonedahl[^ref-1]. Barry Murry also served as the composer for the game's music[^ref-1].

Banjo's Mods writes that the Murrys' "technical accomplishments with the AGI engine are quite impressive, making a kind of game it was definitely not designed for"[^ref-16]. The Apple II version lets players skip the sewer maze, because memory limits prevented saving progress in that section[^ref-14].

### Technical Achievements

Manhunter: New York was notable in several technical aspects. The DOS version supported CGA, MCGA, EGA, Hercules, Tandy/PCjr and VGA graphics and required 256 KB of RAM[^ref-4][^ref-8]. The Amiga version shipped on two disks[^ref-9]. Today the game runs in ScummVM, which offers limited mouse support[^ref-4].

Its use of real New York City locations helped set it apart from Sierra's other titles[^ref-14].

## Legacy

The Manhunter series ended after the second game[^ref-21]. The Murrys said in 1997 that both Manhunter games "sold very well (more than 100,000 of each)", and that they did not own the rights to Manhunter[^ref-24]. A third game, Manhunter: London, was planned but never made[^ref-13][^ref-21].

### Critical Reevaluation

The game has maintained a dedicated fan following, with users on the GOG Dreamlist expressing continued interest in seeing the game re-released. Fan comments include praise like "one of the better games from Sierra," "It's a masterpiece that should not be forgotten," and appreciation for its "superb story line and universe"[^ref-23].

### Enduring Influence

Banjo's Mods argues that "many of the things the Manhunter games did would later become popular (first-person adventures, puzzles of the kind seen in Ripper and Black Dahlia, graphic horor [sic], 'detective'-based gameplay)"[^ref-16]. Fan-made unofficial patches fix small glitches in both games[^ref-22].
