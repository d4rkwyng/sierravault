# Global Domination

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

Global Domination is a turn-based strategy game developed and published by [[Impressions Games]] in 1993 for DOS and Amiga platforms[^ref-1][^ref-4]. The game is modeled closely on the classic board game Risk, expanding upon that foundation with additional territories, unit types, intelligence operations, and unit obsolescence mechanics[^ref-3]. Set in a near-future world divided into five power nations fighting for complete planetary control, players employ tactics, spying, and diplomacy to achieve global conquest[^ref-4].

Designed by [[Simon Bradbury]] and [[David Lester]][^ref-1][^ref-7], Global Domination was, in Computer Gaming World's words, "a successor of sorts to an earlier Impressions conquest game called The Final Conflict"[^ref-22]. The game is also known as "Stratagem", which GameFAQs lists as its European title and MobyGames as its German title[^ref-5][^ref-7]. Computer Gaming World's Chuck Moss called it "a simple, workmanlike, intermediate-level, conquer-the-world game that's surprisingly enjoyable and addictive" and said it "makes a great solitaire game"[^ref-22]. An Amiga version was also released[^ref-21].

## Story Summary

In the near future, five superpowers fight for complete control of the planet, using resource management, military tactics, espionage and diplomacy; minor governments fall as the superpowers expand their borders[^ref-8]. Impressions' own product description in Computer Gaming World put it the same way: the ultimate goal of "peace" is attained "when one superpower stands alone, all others eliminated"[^ref-24].

The game employs a science fiction conceit wherein historical leaders have been cloned using advanced DNA retrieval techniques, allowing players to face off against legendary military strategists and conquerors from throughout human history[^ref-8]. The manual names nine opponents: Julius Caesar, General Custer, Genghis Khan, Henry V, Adolf Hitler, Abraham Lincoln, Napoleon, Queen Victoria and a Caveman, of whom four can be faced at a time[^ref-8]. In the game as shipped, Otto von Bismarck stood in for Hitler; Computer Gaming World noted that "Bismarck stands in for Adolf Hitler — the documentation shows Der Fuhrer's puss"[^ref-22]. A later official update put Hitler back (see Version History)[^ref-23].

According to Wikipedia, the game has four scenarios: 1914 (the year World War I broke out), 1939 (when World War II broke out in Europe), 1995, and a fictional post-apocalyptic Earth in the year 2500, after an asteroid strikes the Pacific Ocean in 2030[^ref-3].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Global Domination utilizes a top-down graphical interface with keyboard and mouse input support[^ref-7]. The game presents a world map divided into numerous territories, significantly expanding upon the original Risk board game's territory count[^ref-3]. The interface is mouse-driven, with icon menus and a pointing-finger cursor; Computer Gaming World found the menus "largely self-explanatory" but wished common commands could be given from the keyboard[^ref-22]. Each turn represents a month, and there is no naval combat; sea movement uses fleet points[^ref-22]. The multiplayer functionality supports modem connections for remote play[^ref-7].

### Structure and Progression

The game offers three complexity levels to accommodate different player skill levels: Simple, Reduced, and Full[^ref-8]. Each game has five players, human or computer: you select yourself and four other players, and up to four of them can be other humans, on one machine or by modem or serial link[^ref-8][^ref-22].

**Available Scenarios:**
- **1914:** The year World War I broke out
- **1939:** The outbreak of World War II in Europe
- **1995:** Near-present day
- **2500:** Fictional post-apocalyptic Earth[^ref-3]

### Mechanics

Unlike pure Risk, Global Domination incorporates multiple strategic systems beyond simple territorial conquest[^ref-3]. Players must manage various unit types with different capabilities and consider unit obsolescence as technology advances through different eras[^ref-3]. Intelligence operations allow for covert activities including espionage, sabotage, and information gathering about enemy positions and capabilities[^ref-3].

On the Full complexity level, a conflict can be fought as a "satellite battle", a tactical battle on a scrolling map where the player gives orders to individual units[^ref-8]. Computer Gaming World called these battles visually "stunning" but "simply not up to current standards"[^ref-22]. Revolution mechanics can trigger within controlled territories, forcing players to maintain stability in their conquered regions[^ref-10]. One reviewer described the experience as "a hybrid of Risk and Civilization," indicating the game's ambition to blend multiple strategy subgenres[^ref-10].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Computer Gaming World's Chuck Moss reviewed Global Domination in the February 1994 issue ("The Last Dictator", pp. 206 and 208)[^ref-22]. Moss called it "at once both a failure and a surprising success"[^ref-22]. He found the tactical game clunky but liked the strategic game, which he said "makes a great solitaire game". On replay value he wrote only that the article "was delayed by frequent 'fact checks' that took up to four hours each!"[^ref-22] He closed: "So give Global Domination an unexpected thumbs up."[^ref-22] He placed the game between simple games like Risk and more involved ones like Command H.Q. and Empire Deluxe[^ref-22]. MobyGames lists a critics' average of 76% from four ratings[^ref-7].

### Modern Assessment

Later assessments are mixed. SquakeNet, an undated retro-games site whose review was archived in 2021, said that "everything that could have went wrong with this game did", citing scattered interfaces and unresponsive controls[^ref-11]. A MobyGames user review from PCGamer77 in March 2002 concluded that "Global Domination isn't a terrible game, but it isn't good either," noting that "a laundry list of features does not a good wargame make"[^ref-7][^ref-12]. The same review noted that Hitler was advertised as an AI opponent but was not available in the game as shipped[^ref-12]. That matches Computer Gaming World's 1994 review; an official update later restored him[^ref-22][^ref-23].

Home of the Underdogs (HOTUD) described Global Domination as "an awful multiplayer real-time game of world conquest that is bogged down by very confusing interface and boring display of bland statistics"[^ref-10]. However, other retrospective assessments have been more charitable, with one user comparing it favorably to "a hybrid of Risk and Civilization"[^ref-10].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames Critics:** 76% average (4 ratings)[^ref-7]
- **Abandonware DOS:** 3.63/5.00 (8 votes)[^ref-1]
- **MyAbandonware:** 4.08/5[^ref-10]

## Development

### Origins

Global Domination was developed and published by Impressions Games[^ref-1][^ref-7]. Computer Gaming World described it as "a successor of sorts to an earlier Impressions conquest game called The Final Conflict". The Final Conflict had tried to combine logistics, strategy, economics and arcade-style action, and Global Domination narrowed its scope to "a simple strategic world conquest game and a detailed tactical simulation"[^ref-22]. MobyGames credits the design to Simon Bradbury and David Lester, with Bradbury also credited for programming[^ref-7]; Computer Gaming World's review box lists David Lester as designer[^ref-22].

### Production

The development team at Impressions Games worked to expand the basic Risk formula with numerous additional systems[^ref-3]. Each computer leader has its own leadership style, strong points and weaknesses, shown in its strategy and diplomacy[^ref-8]. Computer Gaming World said the personalities "resemble characters in Sid Meier's Civilization"[^ref-22]. Christopher J. Denman composed the game's music, creating original tracks including a memorable Select Players Screen theme that plays in 3/4 time signature, resembling a waltz[^ref-9].

**Development Credits:**[^ref-1][^ref-7]
- **Designer:** [[Simon Bradbury]]
- **Designer:** [[David Lester]]
- **Composer:** Christopher J. Denman

### Technical Achievements

The game utilized the AIL / Miles Sound System engine for audio playback[^ref-7]. It supports AdLib (including AdLib Gold), Aria, Roland and Sound Blaster sound cards[^ref-22]; MobyGames also lists PC speaker, Roland MT-32 and Sound Blaster Pro[^ref-7]. The strategic game ran on a 286, but Computer Gaming World said the tactical game needed a 386 to get the most out of it[^ref-22]. The multiplayer implementation supported modem connections, allowing remote players to compete in an era before widespread internet gaming[^ref-7].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- Hitler appears in the manual's list of opponents and in Impressions' pre-release product description[^ref-8][^ref-24], but the game shipped with Otto von Bismarck in his place. Computer Gaming World said "political sensitivity, or just plain common sense, prevailed"[^ref-22]. An official update dated 8/24/94 "replaces Bismarck with Hitler, for those politically incorrect wargamers"[^ref-23], and Sierra Help still hosts the "globehit" patch that adds him back[^ref-25]
- The Select Players Screen music is composed in 3/4 time, giving it a distinctive waltz-like quality that one blogger described as "so catchy I often find myself whistling the theme while I work"[^ref-9]
- A blogger recorded the track through DOSBox, describing the result as "DOSBOX's emulation of OPL3 I believe"[^ref-9]
- AI opponents include characters based on: Abraham Lincoln, Queen Victoria, Otto von Bismarck, Napoleon Bonaparte, "The Caveman," General Custer, Julius Caesar, Genghis Khan, and Henry V[^ref-9][^ref-22]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for Global Domination have been found. The game launched at $59.95[^ref-22]. It has been preserved through various abandonware archives and remains playable through DOSBox emulation[^ref-4][^ref-10]. It is not sold on any current digital store; its GOG wishlist entry has 198 votes[^ref-14].

### Collections

Global Domination was included in *Twenty Wargame Classics* (1996, Strategic Simulations, Inc. / Mindscape), a DOS CD-ROM compilation that also included Impressions' *D-Day: The Beginning of the End*, *Conquest of Japan* and *When Two Worlds War*[^ref-26]. It also remains available through abandonware distribution sites including MyAbandonware[^ref-10], Abandonware DOS[^ref-1], and ClassicReload[^ref-15]. The Internet Archive maintains a playable browser version for preservation purposes[^ref-4][^ref-13].

### Fan Projects

Community interest in Global Domination has remained modest but persistent. The game has a dedicated wishlist page on GOG.com, where fans have requested an official re-release[^ref-14]. In a November 2015 comment there, one fan said they had created the game's Wikipedia article: "And I can't believe that the wikipedia article there was created by yours truly - mind blown (I actually forgot about ever creating it)"[^ref-14].

### Related Publications

- **Game Manual:** A typed transcription of the user manual (from the Amiga release) covering complexity levels, opponents, intelligence, special forces and unit statistics[^ref-8]

### Critical Perspective

Global Domination occupies an interesting position in strategy gaming history as an ambitious but flawed attempt to evolve the Risk formula. The game's designers clearly understood the limitations of pure territorial conquest gameplay and attempted to address them through additional systems including intelligence operations, unit variety, and technological progression[^ref-3]. However, the execution proved inconsistent, with interface problems undermining the strategic depth[^ref-11][^ref-12].

Its historical-leader opponents were not new: Computer Gaming World compared them to the leaders of Sid Meier's Civilization (1991), noting that their "graphic depictions pale against those of Sid's Civ"[^ref-22]. Even so, Moss found the strategic game "surprisingly enjoyable and addictive"[^ref-22].

## Note on Related Titles

Several other games share the "Global Domination" title or concept, including:

- **Global Domination (1998 Windows, 1999 PlayStation)** – A strategy game by Psygnosis (developed by Psygnosis Leeds Studio)[^ref-27], featuring Missile Command-style gameplay with a spinning globe interface. Received poor reviews (GameSpot: 3.9/10, IGN: 3.6/10)[^ref-16][^ref-17]
- **Risk: Global Domination (2003)** – Console adaptation developed by Cyberlore Studios and published by Atari, released for PlayStation 2 on November 5, 2003[^ref-18]
- **Risk: Global Domination (2015-present)** – Mobile and PC adaptation by SMG Studio, released on Android in 2015 and on Steam in 2020, with cross-platform multiplayer[^ref-19][^ref-27]

This article focuses specifically on the 1993 Impressions Games title.
