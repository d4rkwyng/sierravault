# After Dark Games

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

After Dark Games represents a unique entry in Sierra's catalog - a collection of eleven mini-games based on the beloved After Dark screensaver series.[^ref-1] Released in 1998 by Sierra Attractions and [[Berkeley Systems]][^ref-1][^ref-2], this CD-ROM compilation transformed iconic screensaver characters into interactive gaming experiences for both Windows 95 and Macintosh platforms.[^ref-3] The collection featured games modeled after previously released screensavers from the After Dark series[^ref-4], including the famous Flying Toasters, Bad Dog, and Hula Girl characters.[^ref-1][^ref-31]

J.C. Herz of The New York Times called it "a candy box of games and puzzles" and wrote that "these vest-pocket video games are, in their own way, as engaging and addictive as the mega-selling 40-hour sagas with souped-up 3-D sound and state-of-the-art computer graphics. Part of their appeal is sheer simplicity."[^ref-31] Computer Gaming World judged it "an interesting collection that will appeal to a wide audience."[^ref-32] Jack Eastman, who had co-created the screensaver, later said Berkeley Systems' philosophy "was to remain 'aggressively stupid'".[^ref-7]

## Story Summary

Rather than featuring a single narrative, After Dark Games presents eleven individual gaming experiences, each built around the personalities and characteristics of beloved After Dark screensaver modules.[^ref-11] The games range from arcade-style action to puzzle challenges, united by the whimsical universe established by Berkeley Systems' original screensaver creations.[^ref-12]

Characters like the Flying Toasters, which Jack Eastman conceived while hunting for something "really fun" for After Dark 2.0, when the kitchen toaster caught his eye and "my sleep-deprived brain put wings on it"[^ref-7], became the stars of their own gaming adventures. The compilation embraced the irreverent spirit that made After Dark a cultural phenomenon, featuring everything from word games (Fish Shtick and Bad Dog 911) to arcade games (Hula Girl, Rodger Dodger and Mowin' Maniac).[^ref-17]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The compilation features straightforward controls optimized for casual gaming sessions.[^ref-14] Each mini-game utilizes standard keyboard and mouse inputs[^ref-10], with "no complicated set-up" required - players can "just start playing."[^ref-8] The interface maintains the playful aesthetic of the original screensavers while providing functional gaming controls.[^ref-15]

### The Eleven Games

After Dark Games includes the following titles, each with "multiple levels of difficulty to challenge any player"[^ref-8]:

| Game | Type | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| **Toaster Run** | Arcade | Isometric Glider clone featuring Flying Toaster and Super Guy; "Don't get burnt in the high-flying arcade action"[^ref-1][^ref-8] |
| **Bad Dog 911** | Word Puzzle | Word scramble based on Bad Dog modules[^ref-1] |
| **Mowin' Maniac** | Action | "Go crazy with Mowin' Man and turn some pesky gardeners into fertilizer"[^ref-8] |
| **Hula Girl** | Platformer | Endless 2D platforming game featuring the beloved Hula Girl character[^ref-18] |
| **Roof Rats** | Puzzle | Similar to SameGame; match and clear colored tiles[^ref-1] |
| **Zapper** | Trivia | Knowledge-based trivia game[^ref-1] |
| **Fish Shtick** | Word Puzzle | Aquatic-themed word scramble[^ref-1] |
| **Foggy Boxes** | Strategy | Classic dots and boxes game[^ref-1] |
| **MooShu Tiles** | Puzzle | Mahjong-style tile matching game[^ref-1] |
| **Rodger Dodger** | Arcade | Clone of the game Raimais[^ref-1] |
| **Solitaire** | Card | Classic solitaire; Computer Gaming World noted it was the only one of the eleven that "appears without some sort of mutilation" by the After Dark motif[^ref-32] |

The collection also includes "a free After Dark Games screen saver" as a bonus feature.[^ref-8]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

GameSpot granted that "the developers took time to produce sharp, clear graphics, and each game has original music," though "the short music bites loop endlessly."[^ref-17] Players are warned not to "get burnt in the high-flying arcade action of Toaster Run"[^ref-8], while other games offer varied challenges from puzzle-solving to arcade reflexes.

One reviewer spent "two hours after I first popped the After Dark Games CD into my computer I was still sitting there, mesmerized."[^ref-12] Herz noted the games "all of which take less than 10 minutes" are "miniature experiences, simple to pick up and put down."[^ref-31]

## Development

### Origins

After Dark Games emerged from the success of Berkeley Systems' screensaver empire, which began as creator Jack Eastman's personal project at Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory in 1986.[^ref-7] The Flying Toasters concept that became central to the collection came while Eastman was looking for "something really fun" for After Dark 2.0: "My sleep-deprived brain put wings on it."[^ref-7]

Berkeley Systems maintained a philosophy to remain "aggressively stupid" in their designs[^ref-7], using "charm; creativity; non-repetitiveness; stupidity" as defining features.[^ref-7] This approach carried through to After Dark Games.

The original After Dark software was created by Jack Eastman and Patrick Beard; Eastman wrote the early code in assembly language, "the only way to hack the OS calls in those days."[^ref-7] The software was later ported to Windows by Bill Stewart and Ian MacDonald.[^ref-7]

### Production

By the time of After Dark Games, Berkeley Systems belonged to CUC International, which acquired it in April 1997, the year after CUC bought Sierra On-Line.[^ref-30] Wikipedia describes the buyer as CUC's Sierra On-Line division.[^ref-33] The game was published under the Sierra Attractions label.[^ref-29]

Eastman returned as a consultant: "I worked on After Dark Games as a consultant. I was sort of Alpha Geek for that project."[^ref-7] MobyGames credits him as System Architect (Eightfold Way Consultants), with Phil Mikkelson as Game Design Consultant, Jane Scolieri (Olive Productions) as composer and sound designer, and Nick Rush as Creative Director.[^ref-13] Each game has its own original music.[^ref-17]

### Technical Achievements

After Dark Games turned screensaver characters (Flying Toasters, Bad Dog, Mowin' Man, Hula Girl and others) into eleven playable games plus a bonus screen saver.[^ref-32] Computer Gaming World found the graphics "amusing and well executed" but small, noting "the games only run at 640x480 in 256 colors"; at higher resolutions they run in windows. It called the bundled screen saver "merely a repetitive collection of short demonstrations of the different games."[^ref-32]

## Legacy

### Cultural Impact

The Berkeley Systems founders, Joan Blades and Wes Boyd, went on to found MoveOn.org in 1998.[^ref-33] The Bad Dog screensaver character also inspired an animated TV series, first broadcast by Teletoon on March 1, 1999.[^ref-1]

### Preservation Efforts

Technical preservation efforts continue through various abandonware sites and community archives[^ref-24], though users report issues with corrupted game files where "the og programming for the game isn't there."[^ref-24] Despite these challenges, the collection maintains nostalgic appeal, with players noting "This game brings back so many memories!"[^ref-24] when successful installations are achieved.

GOG wishlist requests demonstrate ongoing preservation interest, with users sharing memories: "This was one of the few games that my mother had back when I was in elementary school on our windows 2000 pc; we'd play it together at times and it's just a very simple, but cozy anthology of games that would be very nice to have preserved."[^ref-25]

Another user noted: "I'm not even here for the games. I am however, here for the massive trove/suite of screensavers/modules produced over by Berkley. To even have them running in a little window themselves would be a fascinating way to recapture a major stone of screensaver history."[^ref-25]

### Speedrunning

Speedrun.com hosts a small set of leaderboards for several games in the collection.[^ref-19] Players compete in categories including "Toaster Run - Easy" with recorded times and verification.[^ref-26]
