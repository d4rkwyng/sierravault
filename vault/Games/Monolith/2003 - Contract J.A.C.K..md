# Contract J.A.C.K.

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

Contract J.A.C.K. (where J.A.C.K. stands for "Just Another Contract Killer") is a first-person shooter developed by [[Monolith Productions]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line|Sierra Entertainment]] in November 2003.[^ref-1] Serving as a prequel to *No One Lives Forever 2: A Spy in H.A.R.M.'s Way*, the game shifts perspective from series protagonist Cate Archer to John Jack, a ruthless contract killer working for the villainous organization H.A.R.M.[^ref-2] Unlike its predecessors, which balanced stealth gameplay with action and humor, Contract J.A.C.K. strips away virtually all espionage elements to focus purely on run-and-gun shooting.[^ref-3]

The game was sold at what PC Gamer called a "budget-skewed $30 price" and was billed as a "standalone expansion" rather than a full sequel.[^ref-13] Set between the events of the original *No One Lives Forever* and its sequel, the story follows Jack as he battles a rival Italian criminal organization, Danger Danger, which plans to take H.A.R.M. down.[^ref-1][^ref-5][^ref-2] Despite using the same LithTech Jupiter engine as the critically acclaimed *NOLF 2*, the game was poorly received by critics, earning a Metacritic score of 56 out of 100.[^ref-1]

Contract J.A.C.K. represents Monolith's attempt to create a pure action experience within the *No One Lives Forever* universe. Hardcore Gaming 101 later wrote that it "leaves a bitter taste in the fans of the series," who had valued the earlier games' stealth, gadgets and humor.[^ref-6] The game remains unavailable for legal purchase today due to licensing issues between Activision Blizzard and WB Games over the rights to Monolith's back catalog.[^ref-7]

## Story Summary

The game opens with John Jack, described as the world's busiest professional killer, being drawn into the service of H.A.R.M. by Dmitrij Volkov, its Director of Executive Action.[^ref-8][^ref-27] After surviving a "job interview" against waves of H.A.R.M. troopers among Roman ruins on Malta, Jack is sent after Danger Danger, an Italian criminal organization led by Il Pazzo that has plans to take H.A.R.M. down.[^ref-1][^ref-27][^ref-2] Jack's mission takes him to Italy, Czechoslovakia, and even outer space.[^ref-10]

The narrative unfolds across seven chapters[^ref-22][^ref-27][^ref-48] as Jack carries out H.A.R.M.'s orders, battling waves of Danger Danger thugs and Czechoslovakian soldiers along the way.[^ref-4] Jack is presented as a nearly silent protagonist with only two lines of dialogue throughout the entire game.[^ref-12] The tone has been compared to *Pulp Fiction*, with lead designer Craig Hubbard noting that "there's still plenty of humor, but the style isn't as campy."[^ref-10]

The story serves primarily as a vehicle for action set pieces, including a memorable sequence where Jack pilots a machine gun-equipped Vespa through the Italian countryside.[^ref-13] The game's ending is believed to explain why Volkov appears confined to a wheelchair and covered in a full body cast during the events of *No One Lives Forever 2*.[^ref-1][^ref-5] Jack's characterization as a "rugged antihero" stands in stark contrast to the glamorous spy thriller aesthetic of the main series.[^ref-2]

Hubbard explained the protagonist's distinctive name in a developer interview: "The protagonist's real name is John Jack (instead of just one cliché action hero name, he has two!), but people call him Contract Jack because he's sort of a freelance 'problem solver' of questionable moral standing."[^ref-10]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Contract J.A.C.K. presents a straightforward first-person shooter experience, supporting keyboard, mouse, and gamepad input.[^ref-14] Players can quick save with F6, and the game also creates an automatic save at the beginning of each scene, with some levels adding checkpoint saves.[^ref-47] The talk console, accessed by pressing T, allows entry of cheat codes for those seeking assistance.[^ref-16]

Unlike its predecessors, the game completely removes the ability to search bodies for items and ammunition—dead enemies simply fade away after being killed.[^ref-17] There is no map system, and the compass rarely provides destination markers, forcing players to navigate environments through exploration.[^ref-3] The difficulty setting can be changed at any time from the game options menu, with normal difficulty recommended for first-time players.[^ref-47]

### Structure and Progression

The game consists of 15 missions spread across 7 chapters,[^ref-22] although its product description advertised "ten new single-player levels."[^ref-29] GameSpot finished the campaign in about four hours and PC Gamer in "just over five hours," making it significantly shorter than previous entries in the series.[^ref-4][^ref-13] Unlike *NOLF* and *NOLF 2*, which featured skill progression systems allowing Cate Archer to upgrade her abilities, Jack has no upgrade paths available—he cannot increase his health or armor values.[^ref-17]

The chapters take Jack through varied environments:[^ref-48]

- **Chapter 1: The Proposition** – The opening escape, beginning in Jack's flat above The Drunken Scotsman bar[^ref-18]
- **Chapter 2: Trial by Gunfire**
- **Chapter 3: Welcome to Czechoslovakia** – A Czechoslovakian military base, including a snowmobile pursued by a helicopter
- **Chapter 4: Danger! Danger!** – Includes a prisoner interrogation using a torture device
- **Chapter 5: Lunar Psycho** – The moon base
- **Chapter 6: Collision Course** – Fighting off enemies while shooting down incoming rocks
- **Chapter 7: Il Pazzo** – A motorcycle section and the final battle against Il Pazzo

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Contract J.A.C.K. explicitly abandons the stealth, gadgetry, and puzzle-solving that characterized the *No One Lives Forever* series.[^ref-6] As one walkthrough guide emphasized: "Forget almost everything you learned in NOLF and NOLF 2 - there is no stealth in THIS game. This is strictly a shooter."[^ref-19] The gameplay focuses entirely on action, with wave after wave of enemies spawning throughout levels.[^ref-3][^ref-32]

The game features a variety of weapons including the Beretta 81FS pistol, Desert Eagle Mark XIX, Sterling L2A3 submachine gun, AK-47 assault rifle, and the "Bacalov Corrector"—in reality a Thompson Center Arms Contender—shown prominently on the retail packaging.[^ref-21][^ref-8] Several weapons are anachronistic for the 1960s setting: the Desert Eagle's Mark I variant was not introduced until 1982 (the Mark XIX not until 1995), and the Beretta 81FS not until 1976.[^ref-21] Vehicle sections allow Jack to pilot a snowmobile and the aforementioned armed Vespa, though controls were criticized as awkward.[^ref-22][^ref-33]

The game includes multiplayer for 2-16 players via Internet or LAN connection,[^ref-14] featuring Deathmatch, Team Deathmatch, Doomsday, and Demolition modes.[^ref-47] However, critics noted that most of the multiplayer content was already available as a free patch for *NOLF 2*.[^ref-23][^ref-4]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Contract J.A.C.K. received predominantly negative to mixed reviews upon release, with critics universally lamenting the removal of elements that made the *No One Lives Forever* series distinctive.

GameSpot awarded the game 5.9 out of 10, with reviewer Brett Todd calling it "merely a mediocre shoot-'em-up that can be finished in four hours."[^ref-4] Todd noted that "the sense of humor, varied mission design, nifty Bond-inspired thingamajigs, and even the groovy 1960s threads have been swapped for a straight focus on action that strips the franchise of its identity and charm."[^ref-4]

IGN scored the game 6.1 out of 10, with Tom McNamara opening his review with "How the mighty have fallen."[^ref-3] He criticized the removal of core series elements: "Gone are the gadgets, the storyline, most of the humor and satire, most of the random overheard conversations, all of Cate Archer, stealth and dossiers scattered throughout the level."[^ref-3]

GameSpy rated it "Average" (recorded by Metacritic as 75/100), with Sal Accardo noting that "NOLF fans will likely find it lacking most of the series' charm, and as an action title, it feels strangely unpolished, falling victim to any number of basic problems."[^ref-22][^ref-24]

PC Gamer awarded 63%, with Norman Chan writing: "Fun, but too short and shallow to be a full-priced expansion pack."[^ref-13] He added: "I kept on wondering when the game would pick up, and it just never did."[^ref-13]

Game Informer gave 5.5 out of 10, with editor Andy McNamara stating the game "manages to take out all the good things about the series (like an entertaining story, niffy [sic] Bond-esque gadgets, and interesting gameplay), and replace it with pure, uninspired action."[^ref-23]

GMR Magazine scored it 50/100, calling it "the inferior prequel to the outstanding 'No One Lives Forever' series, having none of the charm, humor, or creative gameplay of the other games under the NOLF umbrella."[^ref-24]

ActionTrip provided one of the few positive reviews at 80/100: "For thirty bucks though, I would advise shooter fans not to miss out on the opportunity to hear some fun dialogue and engage in a very entertaining fast-paced action experience with lots of personality and gallons upon gallons of animated blood."[^ref-24]

Computer Games Magazine awarded only 40/100, stating the game "lacks the slightest spark of life or inspiration."[^ref-24]

GamerFeed gave the lowest score at 30/100, questioning: "To be brutally honest, I'm not entirely sure why Monolith wasted their time making this game, other than to cash in on the franchise."[^ref-24]

GameZone awarded 6.3 out of 10, with Mike David calling it an "incredibly inferior prequel to the No One Lives Forever games" where "gone are the stealth weapons like the piano wire, sleeping gas, gone is the clever voice-overs done by Cate Archer."[^ref-8]

Game Over Online gave 60/100, noting that "Jack may have all the guns and firepower, but he lacks finesse. And he reminds us of why we like to watch heroes like James Bond. It's not all about the big bangs and fiery explosions. It's the martinis, women and one-liners that keep us coming back for more."[^ref-24]

French publication Jeuxvideo.com scored the game 13 out of 20, with reviewer Dinowan commenting that "Monolith nous livre un soft bien bourrin, un peu trop même" (Monolith delivers a very action-heavy game, perhaps too much so).[^ref-25]

### Modern Assessment

The game's reputation has not improved with time. Contract J.A.C.K. was named the #4 Worst Game of 2003 by Computer Games Magazine.[^ref-18] Retrospective analyses consistently view the game as a low point for the franchise, with Hardcore Gaming 101 concluding that "J.A.C.K leaves a bitter taste in the fans of the series by serving them a half baked game, rushed to the market without the labor of love that characterized the earlier titles."[^ref-6]

Gaming Pastime's retrospective noted that "Contract J.A.C.K. tells the weakest and most forgettable story in the series so far" and that "almost everything that made the previous games unique is missing."[^ref-26] The Sierra Chest described the game as "a low note in the highly praised No One Lives Forever series and even seemed to be put on the store shelves in a format that lacked what was originally planned, with re-used graphics, sounds, music and hardly a story line or character depth."[^ref-27] One GameFAQs user review went further, suggesting that "this whole endeavour just smacks of a studio trying to get out from under a contractual obligation."[^ref-44]

## Development

### Origins

Contract J.A.C.K. was revealed in July 2003; a GameSpot Q&A dated July 9, 2003 introduced the "soon-to-be-announced next game in the series" as a stand-alone prequel.[^ref-10] In that interview Hubbard said most of the *NOLF 2* team had been involved in the game, which "began production at the beginning of the year," and that it was slated for release by the end of 2003.[^ref-10] Official promotional copy invited players to "explore the dark side of the No One Lives Forever universe."[^ref-2][^ref-18]

German magazine 4Players suggested that the switch to pure action was a response to the earlier games' sales. Its reviewer, Paul Kautz, wrote that both *NOLF* games had won praise from testers, yet commercial success eluded both, and that Monolith must have taken this as the occasion to thoroughly rework the gameplay (translated from the German: "Dennoch blieb beiden der kommerzielle Erfolg verwehrt").[^ref-32] Where Cate Archer's adventures had been packed with stealth sections, document collecting, a crazy arsenal and, in the sequel, a skill system, Jack "relies on pure sustained fire" (translated; original: "setzt aber auf Dauerfeuer pur").[^ref-32]

### Production

The game recycled extensive assets from *No One Lives Forever 2*, including sounds, music, weapons, items, enemies, and interface elements.[^ref-22] Most of the soundtrack was taken directly from *NOLF 2* without significant modification.[^ref-6] PC Gamer observed that "compromises were made to get Jack in stores for the holidays, and it shows."[^ref-13]

Originally, the game was conceived with a different narrative structure. In early previews, Monolith described the story as beginning "with Jack hurtling towards the sun" with "much of the game would be told through flashback."[^ref-33] This concept was scrapped at some point during development, resulting in the more straightforward linear narrative of the final product.[^ref-33]

The game went gold on October 31, 2003, more than a week before its November 11 ship date.[^ref-34] An exclusive multiplayer demo appeared on FilePlanet on October 27, 2003, using FilePlanet's Beta Protection System, which required a registration key and an internet connection on first run.[^ref-35] Sierra's gold announcement also promised a new single-player "training mission" for November 6, 2003.[^ref-34]

### Technical Achievements

Contract J.A.C.K. runs on the LithTech Jupiter engine, the same technology powering *No One Lives Forever 2*.[^ref-1] The engine supports both single-player and multiplayer modes, with online play for 2-16 players.[^ref-14] However, unlike *NOLF 2*, which had added a skill system, Contract J.A.C.K. has no upgrade system.[^ref-32][^ref-17][^ref-5]

The game shipped with level-building tools allowing players to create and share mods online.[^ref-29] Several community-created patches have since been developed to address compatibility issues with modern systems.[^ref-7]

### Cut Content

The most significant cut content involved the game's original narrative framing device. According to GameSpy's review: "when we previewed the game earlier this year, we were told that it would start with Jack hurtling towards the sun and much of the game would be told through flashback. Obviously, that idea was scrapped at some point."[^ref-33]

The German version received extensive censorship, removing all blood effects, death cries, ragdoll physics for corpses, and the ability to set enemies on fire.[^ref-18]

### Technical Issues

The game exhibits several known issues on modern systems:[^ref-7]

- **Mouse Sensitivity:** High FPS causes excessive mouse sensitivity
- **Mouse Movement:** Non-smooth, wavy movement with high polling rate mice
- **V-Sync:** Broken on Windows 10
- **Body Fading:** Corpses disappear too quickly, preventing ammo pickup
- **Catastrophic Failure Error:** Occurs on some Windows 10 systems

Community fixes exist for mouse and ammo issues through PCGamingWiki, and a widescreen fix (which also addresses the "Catastrophic failure" error) is available.[^ref-7]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

The game contains numerous references to the broader *No One Lives Forever* series:[^ref-15]

- **Cate Archer Cameo:** Cate Archer makes a brief cameo appearance, near a rabbit, during the opening scene of Chapter 3: Welcome to Czechoslovakia.[^ref-15][^ref-18]
- **Wanted Posters:** "Wanted" posters for Cate Archer appear throughout Chapter 2: Trial by Gunfire and Chapter 7: Il Pazzo.[^ref-15]
- **NOLF References:** In Chapter 1, the billboard across from the message machine contains several series references including an opera poster for Inge Wagner from *NOLF 1*, pink paper depicting three assassins from *NOLF 1*, and a reference to La Troupe de Pantomine from *NOLF 2*.[^ref-15]
- **Developer Reference:** A poster titled "How to Pick Up Classy Chicks by Steven Lee" likely references Steve Lee, a Senior 2D/3D Artist on the game.[^ref-15]
- **Gollum Reference:** In the moon base battery recharger room, a hidden panel reveals a strange creature that snarls, giggles, and occasionally says "Gollum, Gollum"—a *Lord of the Rings* reference.[^ref-15]
- **The Jade Hamster:** The fictional movie spy from *NOLF 2* returns on cinematic posters throughout the game.[^ref-18]
- **The Drunken Scotsman:** Jack's flat is located above this bar, which contains various advertisement graphics from *NOLF 1* and *NOLF 2*.[^ref-17]

**Trivia:**
- The product description advertised "ten new single-player levels," while GameSpy counted 15 missions over seven chapters.[^ref-29][^ref-22]
- Jack has only two lines of dialogue throughout the entire game.[^ref-12]
- The German package included a full version of *NOLF 2*.[^ref-32]
- The Bacalov Corrector hand cannons prominently featured on the box are only available near the end of the game.[^ref-8]
- The Man-Handler weapon was designed by Dr. Harij while trapped on the Moon.[^ref-17]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

4Players noted that although both earlier *NOLF* games had been praised by critics, neither had achieved commercial success, and suggested this prompted Monolith's change of approach.[^ref-32] The game sold at a "budget-skewed $30 price."[^ref-13] Soon after release, IGN's reviewer pointed out that *NOLF* and *NOLF 2* could be picked up in the bargain bin for less, combined, than Contract J.A.C.K. itself.[^ref-3]

Contract J.A.C.K. turned out to be the last entry in the *No One Lives Forever* series so far.[^ref-27] Monolith, absorbed into Warner Bros. Interactive, went on to release *F.E.A.R.* and *Condemned: Criminal Origins* in 2005—two "mega-hits," as TheGamer put it.[^ref-39]

### Collections

MobyGames lists the game as included in *Contract J.A.C.K.: Special Edition* (2003).[^ref-18] The German retail package also bundled a full version of *No One Lives Forever 2*.[^ref-32]

### Fan Projects

Despite receiving "discouraging ratings from reviewers," Contract J.A.C.K. remains modifiable, allowing community members to create changes to models, gameplay, and maps.[^ref-40] The modding community has produced compatibility patches for modern operating systems, widescreen fixes, and various quality-of-life improvements.[^ref-7]

### Preservation Status

The game remains commercially unavailable due to complex licensing disputes. PCGamingWiki notes: "The game remains unavailable for purchase due to licensing issues between Activision Blizzard (as successors of Sierra Entertainment) and WB Games (as current owners of developer Monolith Productions)."[^ref-7]

Night Dive Studios, known for republishing classic PC games like *System Shock 2*, has expressed interest in the *No One Lives Forever* franchise. CEO Stephen Kick has stated: "I'm never going to stop. It'll happen one way or another."[^ref-39] Rights negotiations have been complicated because Activision told Night Dive that any records of its rights predated digital storage—"a contract in a box someplace," as Night Dive's Larry Kuperman put it.[^ref-42]

Kuperman also recalled: "We weren't meeting with a lot of enthusiasm" when approaching rights holders about potential re-releases.[^ref-42] Night Dive filed trademarks for the series' games, Contract J.A.C.K. included,[^ref-49] but did not plan to re-release it, with Kick explaining: "We had filed for Contract J.A.C.K., but we don't really consider that to be part of the franchise. So we were going to just leave that by the wayside."[^ref-42] After Warner Bros. Games closed Monolith Productions in 2025, Kick told VGC that Nightdive still hoped to remaster *No One Lives Forever*: "Never give up. We don't give up."[^ref-41]

The game is available on abandonware sites[^ref-30] and in Internet Archive uploads.[^ref-36] Techdirt, which considers the series abandonware, noted that downloadable copies come with mods that let the games run on Windows 10 and 11 in widescreen.[^ref-43]
