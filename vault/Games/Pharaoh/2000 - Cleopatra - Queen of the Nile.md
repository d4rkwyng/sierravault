# Cleopatra: Queen of the Nile

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

**Cleopatra: Queen of the Nile** is an expansion pack for the city-building game Pharaoh, developed by BreakAway Games and Impressions Games and published by Sierra in July 2000[^ref-1][^ref-5]. It adds new structures, new units, four new campaigns containing 15 missions, and a scenario editor[^ref-1]. The expansion brings players into the latter years of the Egyptian Empire, "in the era of Cleopatra"[^ref-2], with its missions running from 1490 BC to 35 BC[^ref-4][^ref-6].

The expansion requires the base game Pharaoh to be present on the system[^ref-6]. It holds a Metacritic score of 77 from 13 critics[^ref-18], and GameSpot's Ron Dulin called it "both more difficult and more complex than Pharaoh, but it also makes the game seem new and fresh"[^ref-31].

## Story Summary

Cleopatra continues the Pharaoh story. StrategyWiki describes its campaigns as moving from tomb building in the Valley of the Kings, through the exploits of Ramesses II, to the fall of Egypt in the 1st century BC[^ref-4]. Unlike Pharaoh, the player is not a single dynastic character: each mission casts the player in the historical situation its briefing describes, such as fighting alongside Ramesses II or helping Julius Caesar and Cleopatra VII at Maritis[^ref-6]. Completing the original Pharaoh campaign does not lead automatically into the Cleopatra missions[^ref-6].

### Campaigns

The four campaigns and their missions, per StrategyWiki's walkthrough[^ref-4]:

| Campaign | Missions (start year) | Highlights |
|----------|----------------------|------------|
| Valley of the Kings | Thutmose in the Valley (1490 BC), Tut in the Valley (1325 BC), Seti in the Valley (1290 BC) | Building the royal tombs of Thutmose I, Tutankhamun and Seti I |
| Ramses II | Sumur (1300 BC), Qadesh (1277 BC), Abu Simbel (1270 BC), Ramses in the Valley (1250 BC) | Defeating the Hittites, building Abu Simbel and the tomb of Ramses II |
| Ancient Conquerors | Pi-Yer (1235 BC), Migdol (677 BC), Tanis (395 BC) | Fighting the Sea People and the Assyrians; rebuilding Egypt's navy after Persian occupation |
| Cleopatra's Capitol | Alexandria (331 BC), Ptolemy's Alexandria (305 BC), Maritis (50 BC), Cleopatra's Alexandria (40 BC), Actium (35 BC) | Founding Alexandria, building the Great Library, Lighthouse and Caesareum, and facing Octavian at Actium |

ModDB's summary notes that the player battles the advancing Assyrian, Persian, Phoenician and Roman empires[^ref-2].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Cleopatra plays the same way as Pharaoh in most respects, and StrategyWiki points players to the Pharaoh guide for general mechanics[^ref-4]. MobyGames lists the mouse as required and the keyboard as optional[^ref-30].

### Structure and Progression

The 15 campaign missions are joined by a scenario editor[^ref-1]. Missions follow Pharaoh's formula: the player wins by reaching set ratings and building required monuments. GameSpot found that Cleopatra "starts off at approximately the same difficulty at which Pharaoh left off, and quickly gets much harder," and that some missions add time limits[^ref-31]. Unlike in Pharaoh, Cleopatra's ranks are not successive from mission to mission[^ref-4].

Some missions let troops and monuments carry over from a previous mission. Only the highest-level troops and one fort of each type can be carried over[^ref-6][^ref-4]. One campaign mission puts the player in command of non-Egyptian forces, and the Mission Editor supports this too[^ref-6].

### New Features

- **New industries:** lamp making (pottery and imported oil) and paint making (henna), both needed for the new royal tombs[^ref-31]
- **Royal tombs:** built into the cliffs, decorated with paint, lit with lamps and stocked with luxury burial goods[^ref-31]
- **Tomb robbers:** they appear in any mission where burial goods must be dispatched to a monument, and they lower the kingdom rating if not stopped[^ref-6]
- **Zoo:** a new entertainment building, which the Impressions Games Wiki says is "only available in missions where the Culture rating is above 70"[^ref-6]
- **Plagues:** angry gods can now send rivers of blood, locusts, frogs and hailstorms[^ref-31][^ref-6]
- **Optional pyramid speed-up blessing:** gods' blessings can build part of a monument for free; this is toggled in the options menu[^ref-6]
- **Hostile creatures:** scorpions attack residents in early missions where no military can be recruited[^ref-31]

GameSpot also reported a bug in version 1.0. If burial goods were dispatched to a tomb before it was finished, construction stopped and saved games for that mission were affected[^ref-31].

| Publication | Score | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Gamer's Pulse | 88/100 | "A must-have expansion for any serious monument builder"[^ref-18] |
| Game Over Online | 81/100 | Faulted the unchanged random-walker service system[^ref-18] |
| GamePro | 80/100 | "The gameplay gets a little repetitive"[^ref-18] |

GameSpot's Ron Dulin praised BreakAway for not taking the "easy route" of expansion packs. He found the new missions both "extend the game" and "enhance it", but noted that the wandering-worker problem inherited from Pharaoh "has still not been addressed"[^ref-31].

### Modern Assessment

Metacritic's user score stands at 7.6 from 14 ratings[^ref-18]. The bundled Pharaoh + Cleopatra edition is sold on GOG and Steam[^ref-33][^ref-34].

## Development

### Origins

Computer Games Strategy Plus reported on July 6, 2000 that the expansion had gone gold and would reach retail around July 14. It noted that the pack was developed by BreakAway Games, the studio behind *Sid Meier's Antietam!*[^ref-32]. MobyGames dates the US release to July 12, 2000[^ref-1], while GameFAQs and Metacritic give July 11, 2000[^ref-5][^ref-18].

## Legacy

### Collections

MobyGames lists the expansion as included in *Pharaoh Gold*, *The Great Empires Collection*, *The Great Empires Collection II* and *The Biggest Creators of Empires*[^ref-1]. Today it is sold together with the base game as *Pharaoh + Cleopatra* on GOG and Steam[^ref-33][^ref-34].

### Pharaoh: A New Era (2023)

*Pharaoh: A New Era*, released February 15, 2023, is a remake of Pharaoh "and the expansion Cleopatra: Queen of the Nile". It was developed by Triskell Interactive and published by Dotemu[^ref-35]. TheGamer scored the remake 4/5, saying it "elevates Pharaoh to modern standards spectacularly well"[^ref-20].

### Influence on the Series

The Impressions Games Wiki describes Cleopatra's carry-over of monuments and troops between missions as a precursor to Zeus: Master of Olympus. Zeus extended the idea to whole cities, funds and military forces[^ref-6].
