# Oil's Well (1990)

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

Oil's Well (1990) is an arcade-style action game published by Sierra On-Line[^ref-20] and developed by Banana Development, Inc.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-17][^ref-18] The game serves as an enhanced remake of the original 1983 Oil's Well, which was written by Thomas J. Mitchell for Atari 8-bit computers.[^ref-3] This updated version features 256-color VGA graphics, a full soundtrack composed by Ken Allen, and animated cartoon sequences between levels starring Slater the "Petrosaur."[^ref-1][^ref-5]

The gameplay concept draws inspiration from the 1982 arcade game Anteater, but re-themes the action around an oil drilling operation rather than a hungry insectivore.[^ref-3][^ref-6] Players guide a drilling bit through underground tunnels to collect dots of oil while avoiding subterranean creatures that can destroy the drilling apparatus.[^ref-1][^ref-7] Retro365 described Anteater as "a fine cocktail of Dig-Dug, Snake, and Pac-Man elements stirred into a unique but fun and challenging game"; only the article's title applies that mix to Oil's Well ("Oil's Well, a fine cocktail of Dig-Dug, Snake, and Pac-Man").[^ref-6]

The original 1983 version became a solid hit and won Computer Games Magazine's 1984 Golden Floppy Award for Excellence as Maze Game of the Year.[^ref-3][^ref-6] Looking back on the remake in 2021, the Retro365 blog observed that "while the simple yet unique gameplay was plentiful for a commercial game in 1983, customers' expectations had changed significantly by 1990."[^ref-6]

## Story Summary

Oil's Well presents a straightforward arcade premise centered on resource extraction rather than a narrative-driven story.[^ref-7] Players take on the role of managing an oil drilling operation, extending a robotic mining arm deep below the earth's crust to collect valuable oil deposits.[^ref-7][^ref-8] The underground environment is populated with monstrous bugs and creatures that threaten the drilling operation at every turn.[^ref-7]

Between levels, players are treated to animated cartoon sequences featuring Slater the Petrosaur, a dinosaur character who finds himself frequently inconvenienced by a drillbit in various comedic situations.[^ref-1][^ref-5][^ref-12] These short movies provide entertainment rewards for completing each of the eight levels.[^ref-1] The final cartoon features a limousine with the words "Oils Well That Ends Well"[^ref-5] – a further pun on the game's title, which itself is a play on the phrase "all's well."[^ref-3]

Slater went on to star in his own game, Slater & Charlie Go Camping.[^ref-5]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Oil's Well utilizes a simple yet challenging control scheme appropriate for its arcade-style gameplay.[^ref-7] Players use four directional controls to maneuver the drill head through the underground maze while a dedicated button allows for quick retraction of the drilling apparatus.[^ref-7] The 1990 DOS version supports keyboard, mouse and joystick (or gamepad) control, chosen during installation; by default the arrow keys steer the drill bit and the space bar retracts it.[^ref-5][^ref-21]

The player controls a drilling bit that leaves behind a pipeline as it moves through the underground tunnels.[^ref-3] This trailing pipe creates the core strategic challenge: the drill bit destroys any critter it touches (earning bonus points), but a critter that touches the pipe destroys both the pipe and the bit, costing a life.[^ref-3][^ref-12][^ref-21] Land mines work the other way round: they pass through the pipe harmlessly but destroy the drill bit if it touches one.[^ref-12][^ref-21] Players can retract the pipe quickly to escape an approaching critter.[^ref-12][^ref-21]

### Structure and Progression

The game features eight distinct levels that players must navigate through, with difficulty increasing progressively as players advance.[^ref-1][^ref-3] The three difficulty levels are named after gasoline grades: "Regular" (easy), "Unleaded" (medium) and "Premium" (hard).[^ref-12][^ref-21] The player starts with three drill bits, each lasting 990 ticks (about 10 ticks per second), and earns another every 10,000 points; on Premium no goblets appear.[^ref-21]

- **Levels 1-8:** Core gameplay stages with increasing difficulty[^ref-1]
- **Cartoon Rewards:** Each completed level unlocks a short animated movie featuring Slater[^ref-1][^ref-5]

The game supports 1-2 players; the two-player mode is alternating, not co-operative, with players switching turns each time one loses a drill bit.[^ref-5][^ref-21]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core mechanic involves navigating the drill head through pre-existing tunnel networks to collect all oil pellets scattered throughout each level.[^ref-9][^ref-10] The gameplay combines elements of maze navigation with the strategic considerations of managing an extending appendage – similar to the classic Snake game mechanic but with the added complexity of enemy avoidance.[^ref-6]

Players must carefully time their movements and plan their routes to avoid the various underground pests that patrol the tunnels.[^ref-1][^ref-3] The action is "well-paced" with "progressive difficulty," requiring good "eye-hand coordination, timing, and strategy" to succeed at higher levels, as Computer Gaming World said of the 1983 original.[^ref-3]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

*The 1983 original's reception is covered in more depth on [[1983 - Oils Well|Oil's Well (1983)]]; it is summarized here for comparison.*

The original 1983 version of Oil's Well was well received across multiple platforms. ANALOG Computing described it as a "truly different and challenging" variant on the traditional dot-collecting gameplay formula.[^ref-3] Dave Stone of Computer Gaming World noted that "the action's well-paced, the difficulty progressive. While getting to a higher level is somewhat dependent on getting the right breaks — good eye-hand coordination, timing, and strategy are essential."[^ref-3]

Ahoy! magazine found the "gameplay is, in my experience, unique" and gave it a "Recommended" rating.[^ref-3] InfoWorld called the IBM PCjr version "a clever, basic game,"[^ref-3] while InfoWorld's Essential Guide to Atari Computers labeled the Atari 8-bit version "Sierra's trickiest."[^ref-3] According to Retro365, InfoWorld reviewed Ardy the Aardvark and Oil's Well in December 1983 and gave Mitchell's game the upper hand for its fast gameplay.[^ref-6]

Recorded critical response to the 1990 remake is sparse: MobyGames lists three critic ratings averaging 47%.[^ref-5] The two MobyGames player reviews split. Katakis (2013) praised a soundtrack "brilliantly composed by Ken Allen" that "blends in with the game's overall theme," and called the remake "an excellent update to the original 1983 classic."[^ref-5] Gothicgene (2002) found it "nothing special" and wrote, "I don't know why Sierra chose Oil's Well to make a remake of and not something else."[^ref-5]

### Modern Assessment

Later assessments disagree on which version is better. Home of the Underdogs' review of the 1984 original (reproduced on MyAbandonware) called it "a fun little clone of classic Digger" that is "fast, fun, but somehow missing the longevity that makes Digger a classic," and concluded: "The 1990 remake version is much better. Good for nostalgia, but little else" – the last remark aimed at the original, not the remake.[^ref-11] Retro365 took the opposite view in 2021: "while the 1990 release of Oil's Well is cute and enjoyable, the original version is the better of the two."[^ref-6]

On MyAbandonware, one 2010 commenter called the 1990 game "frustrating, annoying, and lame, even by 1990s standards."[^ref-9] Katakis described it on MobyGames as "an addictive arcade game where you control a robotic arm trying to mine oil in the underground tunnels."[^ref-5]

## Development

### Origins

Oil's Well was written by programmer Thomas J. Mitchell for Atari 8-bit computers in 1983.[^ref-3] The game concept was similar to Chris Oberth's arcade game Anteater (1982, Stern Electronics) and its home version, Ardy the Aardvark (published by Datamost in 1983), but Mitchell swapped the anthill setting for an oil-drilling operation theme.[^ref-3][^ref-6] This re-theming gave the game its own identity while maintaining the core gameplay mechanic of controlling an extending appendage through a maze-like environment.[^ref-6]

The original game was released under Sierra's short-lived "SierraVision" label before being re-released in 1984 under the generic Sierra label.[^ref-6] The game proved successful enough to warrant ports to multiple platforms including Apple II (programmed by Ivan Strand), Commodore 64, ColecoVision (programmed by Don McGlauflin), IBM PC, MSX, and Sharp X1.[^ref-3][^ref-6][^ref-8]

### Production

In 1989 Sierra got together with Banana Development after seeing Bananoid, which Retro365 calls "a brilliant public domain 256-color arcade game"; a remake of Oil's Well was then in the conceptual stage at Sierra.[^ref-6] Banana Development built the enhanced version, adding 256-color VGA graphics, mouse control and a new soundtrack for DOS.[^ref-1][^ref-6] Notably, the remake was produced without the involvement of original creator Thomas J. Mitchell.[^ref-3]

The enhanced graphics were created by Nancy Hoffelmeyer (Art Director) and Andy Hoyos (Assistant Art Director), who the same year worked on King's Quest V.[^ref-5][^ref-6] The programming for the IBM version was handled by William Rieder.[^ref-5]

### Technical Achievements

The 1990 remake featured significant technical improvements over the original versions.[^ref-6] The game showcased 256-color VGA graphics, a substantial upgrade from the limited color palettes of the 1983 releases.[^ref-6][^ref-12] Ken Allen composed an original soundtrack, which Retro365 called "an excellent soundtrack";[^ref-6] one MobyGames user review praised it as "brilliantly composed" and said it "blends in with the game's overall theme."[^ref-5]

One of the most notable additions was the inclusion of animated cartoon sequences between levels.[^ref-5][^ref-6] These featured Slater the Petrosaur in various comedic situations involving drillbits, providing entertainment incentives for level completion beyond simple score advancement.[^ref-1][^ref-5]

### Technical Issues

Users have reported several compatibility issues with the 1990 DOS version:[^ref-9][^ref-13]

- **Control Mapping Issues:** Some DOSBox configurations result in mouse controls instead of the expected arrow keys and spacebar, significantly altering gameplay[^ref-9]
- **Version Confusion:** One MyAbandonware user reported downloading the 1990 version and getting the 1984 version instead[^ref-9]
- **Vintage Hardware Compatibility:** Testing on NEC Mobile Gear MC-MK12 showed the 1990 version runs but lacks keyboard input functionality[^ref-13]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- **Title Pun:** The game's title "Oil's Well" is a pun on the phrase "all's well"[^ref-3][^ref-6]
- **Final Cartoon:** The last animated sequence features a limousine with the words "Oils Well That Ends Well"[^ref-5]
- **Debug Mode:** A debug mode can be activated by creating a file named BETA.DAT containing the string "Taito SoftwareOW" – pressing F8 grants invincibility while F7 allows skipping levels[^ref-14]
- **Difficulty Naming:** The three difficulty levels are named "Regular," "Unleaded," and "Premium" – referencing gasoline grades[^ref-12][^ref-21]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

The original 1983 Oil's Well "managed to do what Anteater couldn't and became a solid hit."[^ref-6] The game's success earned it Computer Games Magazine's 1984 Golden Floppy Award for Excellence in the Maze Game of the Year category.[^ref-3][^ref-6]

The 1990 remake was part of the run of enhanced remakes Sierra produced as the 1980s closed; most were adventure games, and Oil's Well was one of the few arcade titles given the treatment.[^ref-5][^ref-12] No sales figures for the remake have been found.

### Collections

Oil's Well (1990) was included in the compilation **Action Five** released in 1992.[^ref-5]

The game is currently preserved and playable through the Internet Archive's DOS game collection, which hosts thousands of classic games through browser-based DOSBox emulation.[^ref-1]

### Fan Projects

An unofficial conversion of Oil's Well for the Commodore PET/CBM appeared in 2013.[^ref-5][^ref-8]

### Related Publications

- **Instruction Manual (Version 1.14/1.15):** Included with the game, documentation by Marti McKenna[^ref-5][^ref-12]
- **Box Materials:** Original packaging included diskette and documentation[^ref-15]

### Critical Perspective

ANALOG Computing called the 1983 original "truly different and challenging."[^ref-3]

The 1990 remake illustrates the challenges of updating classic arcade games for modern audiences. While the technical improvements – VGA graphics, Ken Allen's soundtrack, and animated interludes – were substantial, the fundamental gameplay remained rooted in early 1980s design sensibilities.[^ref-6] At least one MobyGames reviewer questioned why Sierra chose to remake it at all, while the Slater character went on to star in his own game.[^ref-5][^ref-6]

The remake shows Sierra updating its back catalog in the same year its art staff were working on [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V]].[^ref-6]
