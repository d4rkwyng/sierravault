# Oil's Well

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Oil's Well is an arcade-style maze game published by Sierra On-Line in 1983, written for the Atari 8-bit computers by Thomas J. Mitchell.[^ref-1] The player extends an oil-mining drill through underground tunnels to collect oil while avoiding the creatures that roam the maze; one touch of a creature against the trailing pipe costs a life.[^ref-2] *Electronic Fun* called it "one of the most original arcade-style titles published this year for the home computer."[^ref-3]

The game is similar to the 1982 arcade game *Anteater*, re-themed to be about drilling for oil instead of a hungry insectivore.[^ref-1] Its title is a twist on the old saying "all's well."[^ref-4] Ports followed for the Commodore 64, Apple II, ColecoVision, IBM PC (as a self-booting disk), MSX and Sharp X1.[^ref-1][^ref-2]

In 1990 Sierra published a separate MS-DOS remake, developed by Banana Development, Inc. without Mitchell's involvement; it is covered on its own page.[^ref-1][^ref-5][^ref-6] Later clones borrowed the oil-drilling theme, including *Pipeline Run* (1990) and *Oilmania* (1991).[^ref-1]

## Story Summary

The premise is set out in the ColecoVision manual: geologists have found one of the largest oil deposits in North America on the player's land, but jealous rival oil barons have planted "oozies" and land mines in the oil fields to wreck the drilling equipment.[^ref-14] Contemporary reviews describe the same set-up, with the oil barons sabotaging the player's operation.[^ref-3][^ref-15]

As the player clears each of the eight oil fields, construction workers on the surface build a section of a refinery; clearing every field finishes it.[^ref-3][^ref-14]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The player moves the drill head through the maze with four directional controls; a pipeline trails behind it back to the base, and pressing a button quickly retracts the head.[^ref-1][^ref-11] The drill bit cannot turn back on itself or cross its own pipe, so retracting is the main way to change direction or escape an approaching enemy.[^ref-3][^ref-10] *Electronic Games* noted that the pipe can be pulled back a little with a quick press or a lot with a longer one.[^ref-3]

The Apple II version requires a joystick; *Apple User*'s reviewer noted that there is no keyboard option.[^ref-15] On the IBM PC booter, keyboard play retracts the drill with the space bar, pressed once to start retracting and again to stop, which led FRGCB to recommend a joystick.[^ref-4] The screen is a single fixed side view of the whole maze.[^ref-2]

### Structure and Progression

There are eight oil fields (mazes), each filled with oil pellets that must all be collected to advance.[^ref-1][^ref-2] *Electronic Fun* described each board as six horizontal tunnels connected by vertical tunnels.[^ref-3] On the Commodore 64 the game returns to the first level after the eighth, with faster monsters and slower land mines.[^ref-12]

The player chooses one or two players and one of three playing speeds: Regular, Unleaded or Premium.[^ref-14] The player starts with three drill bits, earns another every 10,000 points, and each bit lasts at most 990 timer ticks.[^ref-2][^ref-14]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Oil pellets are worth 10 points each. Goblets are worth 1,000 points and pass harmlessly through the pipe, and Oozies eaten by the drill bit are worth 20 to 170 points depending on depth.[^ref-2][^ref-14] Oozies destroy the pipe on contact. Land mines do the opposite: they pass harmlessly through the pipe but blow up the drill bit if it touches them.[^ref-3][^ref-14] Petromins temporarily slow the Oozies but have no effect on the land mines.[^ref-2][^ref-14]

The strategic element comes from deciding how far to extend the drill and when to retract it. As *Electronic Fun* put it, "The appeal of Oil's Well is the visual puzzle: can I get there before they do and what route should I take?"[^ref-3]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

*ANALOG Computing* called the Atari 8-bit version a "truly different and challenging" variant on the "'gobble the dots' theme", with good gameplay and graphics.[^ref-1] Dave Stone in *Computer Gaming World* wrote that "The action's well-paced, the difficulty progressive. While getting to a higher level is somewhat dependent on getting the right breaks — good eye-hand coordination, timing, and strategy are essential".[^ref-1] *Ahoy!* found the Commodore version's graphics and sound only "serviceable" but called the gameplay "unique ... Recommended". *InfoWorld* called the IBM PCjr version "a clever, basic game".[^ref-1]

*Electronic Fun* (March 1984, Phil Wiswell) rated the Atari version 4/4 and called it "one of the most original arcade-style titles published this year for the home computer."[^ref-3] *Electronic Games* (May 1984, Tracie Forman) called the retractable pipeline "the best - and most innovative - part of the game".[^ref-3]

*Apple User* gave the Apple II version 74% in October 1984. Reviewer Cliff McKnight asked, "Is this 'Pac-Man meets JR', you ask yourself?" and concluded, "Once you've got the knack, it's a great game that will keep you occupied for hours."[^ref-15] French magazines scored it 80% (*L'Ordinateur Individuel*, January 1985), 66% (*Tilt*, June 1984) and 50% (*Tilt*, December 1984).[^ref-16]

### Modern Assessment

Home of the Underdogs, quoted on MyAbandonware, wrote: "Simple concept, but quite frustrating in reality as those bugs are way too fast at higher levels. The 1990 remake version is much better. Good for nostalgia, but little else."[^ref-17] Lemon64 user comments are largely positive. One says the game "has addictive written all over it,"[^ref-12] another calls it a "modestly pleasing underground chomper," and Father Brad wrote: "We never should've put Pac-Man on a leash. Never!"[^ref-12]

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 81% critic average (7 reviews)[^ref-2]
- **Atarimania:** 7.7/10[^ref-3]
- **MyAbandonware:** 4.25/5 (8 votes)[^ref-17]

## Development

### Origins

Thomas J. Mitchell wrote Oil's Well for the Atari 8-bit computers.[^ref-1] FRGCB notes that writing it for the Atari first was a bit unusual for Sierra On-Line, which had focused its early game development on the Apple II.[^ref-4] The game is a direct reworking of *Anteater*: the anteater's tongue and the larvae become a drill pipe and oil deposits.[^ref-2][^ref-4] FRGCB notes one change: the power-up slows the enemies instead of acting as a smart bomb.[^ref-4]

### Production

According to FRGCB, the Commodore 64 version was written by R.B. Stuart and the Apple II version by Ivan Strand. Don McGlauflin did the ColecoVision and MSX versions, and John Rinck did the IBM PC version.[^ref-4] The ColecoVision manual credits "Coleco version by Don McGlauflin".[^ref-14] MobyGames lists Comptiq as porter and publisher of the MSX version in Japan.[^ref-2]

### Technical Issues

One Lemon64 user reported that on the Commodore 64 the enemies freeze completely at higher levels.[^ref-12] Home of the Underdogs complained that the enemies become far too fast at higher levels.[^ref-17]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- The title is a pun on "all's well". FRGCB notes that "Oil's Well" is also the title of a 1929 Oswald the Lucky Rabbit short.[^ref-1][^ref-4]
- The Japanese title is オイルズ・ウェル, and the MSX in-game credits misspell the title as "Oil's Wall".[^ref-2]
- Clones that borrowed the oil theme include *Pipeline Run* for the Commodore 64 (1990) and *Oilmania* for the Atari ST (1991).[^ref-1]
- Atarimania credits *Oilmania* for the Atari ST to programmers Michael Buetepage and Michael Leonhardt, with game design by Marcer Wolter.[^ref-21]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

*Computer Games* magazine gave Oil's Well its 1984 Golden Floppy Award for Excellence in the "Maze Game of the Year" category.[^ref-1] The game was ported to most major home systems of the period, from the Apple II, Atari 8-bit and Commodore 64 to the Japanese MSX and Sharp X1.[^ref-2] No sales figures have been found.

### 1990 Remake

In 1990 Sierra released a remake for MS-DOS, developed by Banana Development, Inc.[^ref-5][^ref-6] It added 256-colour MCGA/VGA graphics, a new stereo soundtrack and cartoons between levels about a dinosaur named Slater the petrosaur.[^ref-8][^ref-10] FRGCB notes that it switched to mouse control.[^ref-4] MobyGames lists a 47% critic average for the remake.[^ref-6] See [[1990 - Oils Well]].

### Collections

The Commodore 64 version appeared in Main Street Publishing's *Familiar Favorites IV* disk compilation with *Sammy Lightfoot*.[^ref-12] Aackosoft International B.V. distributed the game in the Netherlands, and Comptiq published the MSX and Sharp X1 versions in Japan.[^ref-2] MobyGames also lists the game in the *30 MSX Hits* (1988) compilation.[^ref-2]

### Fan Projects

In 2013 NOP Software made an unofficial Oil's Well demake for the Commodore PET/CBM.[^ref-2][^ref-4] Firestorm Productions made a separate fan remake, which Classic-Retro-Games describes as a 40-level game.[^ref-13] Clones continued into the 1990s, including *Oilmania* for the Atari ST.[^ref-21]
