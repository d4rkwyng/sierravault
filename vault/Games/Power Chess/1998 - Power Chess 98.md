# Power Chess 98

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

Power Chess 98 is an updated version of the original Power Chess, released by [[Sierra On-Line]] on November 3, 1997 for Windows 95 and Windows 98 platforms[^ref-1][^ref-22]. The game utilizes the WChess engine developed by David Kittinger, which had previously competed against IBM's Deep Blue at the 1995 World Computer Championship[^ref-2]. Sierra's marketing proclaimed it as "More powerful than any human opponent. More human than any computer opponent," reflecting the game's goal of creating an opponent that played chess like a human rather than a traditional number-crunching computer program[^ref-3].

Abandonware France describes the series as a serious competitor to Chessmaster, built around opponents of different strengths and personalities rather than a single engine designed to crush the player[^ref-7]. The box copy promised that the Power Chess King "always plays just a bit better than you- enough to make you stretch," and that "as you improve, so does he"[^ref-3]. International Grandmaster Larry Christiansen is quoted on the box calling it "the first innovation in chess programs in more than a decade"[^ref-3]. The Power Chess Queen "watches your games with the King and explains it all to you immediately afterward" in spoken commentary[^ref-3].

Abandonware France says the original Power Chess was designed to welcome both novices and expert players[^ref-7]; one GOG forum user later called it "chess with a 'human' side to it, not just the typical crunch-positions chess program"[^ref-6]. Sierra offered owners of the original Power Chess an upgrade to the new version for $14.95 plus shipping[^ref-26]. The original release had been named Traditional Game of the Year for 1996 by Computer Games Strategy Plus[^ref-2][^ref-15], and the line continued with Power Chess 2.0 in 1998–1999[^ref-2][^ref-7].

## Story Summary

Power Chess 98 does not feature a traditional narrative story, instead presenting gameplay within a royal court framework. The player is a chess enthusiast invited by the Queen to measure themselves against her, the King, the Prince and the Princess, as well as other opponents[^ref-7].

The Queen character serves as the player's tutor throughout the experience: she watches each game played against the King and then talks the player through it, highlighting squares and moving pieces to illustrate her spoken comments[^ref-3]. The King is the primary opponent, who adapts to the player's level of play[^ref-3].

The court is made up of the King, the Queen, the Prince and the Princess, player-created Nobles, and twenty Commoners ranging in strength from very weak to nearly as strong as the Queen[^ref-11]. Abandonware France describes the Queen as the game's most formidable opponent, above all the others[^ref-7].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game is played fully with the mouse in a typical Windows environment[^ref-10]. Players interact with a graphical chessboard, selecting pieces and indicating move destinations through point-and-click controls. The interface offers both 2D and 3D game views[^ref-10]. The box advertises dozens of chess sets and boards, drawn and rendered in 2D and 3D, for monitor resolutions up to 1280 x 1024[^ref-3].

### Structure and Progression

Power Chess 98 features an opponent roster based on a royal court theme[^ref-11]:

- **Commoners:** Twenty pre-made opponents, each with a distinct style, seven of them based on famous chess players such as Anatoly Karpov, Yasser Seirawan, Bobby Fischer, Judit Polgar and Mikhail Botvinnik[^ref-11]
- **Nobles:** Opponents the player creates by setting aggressiveness, speed of play and other factors[^ref-11]
- **The Prince and Princess:** They play at the difficulty level of the player's last game with the King, with the Princess switching to the WChess engine in the middlegame[^ref-11]
- **The King:** Primary adaptive opponent; the player's Power Chess rating rises and his play strengthens as the player improves[^ref-11]
- **The Queen:** WChess-powered opponent and post-game analyst[^ref-11]

New to Power Chess 98 was "Crack the King," billed on the box as a chance to "solve the combined puzzles of the King's battle plans"[^ref-3]. In one player's account, the King keeps using an idea while it wins; if the player beats it, the King has been "cracked"[^ref-12]. Sierra ran a "Crack the King" contest: players who forced the King to switch to a different opening book received an entry code, and the grand prize was a four-day trip to Seattle for two with a one-hour lesson from International Grandmaster Yasser Seirawan[^ref-26].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core mechanics revolve around standard chess rules with several educational enhancements:

- **Adaptive AI:** The King adjusts his playing strength during games to match the player's ability[^ref-2]
- **Post-Game Analysis:** The Queen provides spoken commentary replaying each game against the King[^ref-3][^ref-11]
- **Chess Lab:** A feature allowing players to experiment with game variations and explore alternative lines of play[^ref-13]
- **Custom Opponents:** Players can create custom AI opponents with different personality traits and playing styles[^ref-10]
- **Classic Games:** Seven classic games narrated by the Queen, plus 50 illustrative games in the bundled text of Nimzowitsch's *My System*[^ref-11]
- **PGN Support:** Import and export of games in Portable Game Notation format[^ref-7]

Sierra's announcement listed new features for this version: a window showing what the chess engine is thinking, an option to force the computer to move sooner, and analysis of all games, including those played over the Internet[^ref-26]. GameSpot's reviewer nonetheless listed "the absence of a 'force move' command to hurry up a dawdling computer opponent" among the game's annoyances[^ref-11]. The box adds move list printing, square highlighting in 3D mode, written WChess game analysis, and resignations and draw offers from the King[^ref-3].

One player's retrospective recalls that Power Chess commended players for finding good moves, which he contrasted with Chessmaster's habit of only pointing out mistakes[^ref-12].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

GameSpot awarded Power Chess 98 a score of 7.6 out of 10, with reviewer Stephen Poole noting that "Sierra On-Line set a lofty goal for this second edition of its Power Chess series: to create a computer opponent who plays chess like a human"[^ref-11]. The review praised the humanlike King while criticising the thin tutorials, the lack of an opening book and the small number of classic games. Poole concluded that "if Sierra can up the ante in terms of tutorials, game analysis, and openings, the next Power Chess will have the stuff to challenge Chessmaster 5500 for chess supremacy"[^ref-11].

Ron Dulin reviewed the original Power Chess for GameSpot, awarding 6.8 out of 10, and observed that "good chess players looking to become great will probably find Power Chess helpful," while noting that "the Queen's advice doesn't clarify much about the game beyond the opening"[^ref-13].

The German publication PC Joker rated the original Power Chess the highest in a comparison of four chess programs in March 1997[^ref-2]. According to Abandonware France, French magazine Joystick gave the original Power Chess its "Mégastar" award[^ref-7].

The original Power Chess won the Traditional Game of the Year award from Computer Games Strategy Plus for 1996[^ref-2][^ref-15]. Sierra's InterAction magazine reported that the same magazine had given the original four out of five stars, quoting it as saying "Power Chess delivers a fresh, addictive, and innovative way for the rest of us to improve our play"[^ref-26].

### Modern Assessment

Power Chess 98 holds a 3.7 out of 5 star rating on Amazon from five customer ratings, with written reviews dated 2009–2016[^ref-4]. One Amazon reviewer wrote that "there has never been a chess game that tried to create something similar to what Power Chess did so long ago"[^ref-4].

A French retrospective of the original Power Chess from Abandonware France declared that "Sierra a pris le parti d'humaniser le jeu d'échecs sur ordinateur, et c'est un pari réussi !" ("Sierra took the approach of humanizing computer chess, and it's a successful bet!")[^ref-7].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 74% (Power Chess 98)[^ref-1], 78% (Power Chess)[^ref-14]
- **Amazon:** 3.7/5 stars[^ref-4]
- **GameSpot:** 7.6/10[^ref-11]
- **GameFAQs:** "Great" rating (5 user ratings)[^ref-16]

## Development

### Chess Engine

The WChess engine by David Kittinger had drawn attention at the Fifth Harvard Cup in October 1994, where it scored an undefeated 5–1 against six American grandmasters, with four wins and two draws[^ref-27]. The engine also competed in the 1995 World Computer Championship against IBM's Deep Blue[^ref-2].

### Production

Sierra's InterAction magazine presented Power Chess 98 as the Power Chess team's follow-up to the original, "packed with new hard-core features" and shipping on two CDs[^ref-26]. GameSpot reported Sierra's claim that the King's "mood" changes during a match based on a range of positional variables and on how he perceives them[^ref-11]; the box copy says Sierra fine-tunes the King's "engine" while the player is playing him[^ref-3]. Technically, the game runs two DOS chess engines, KING.EXE and QUEEN.EXE, that communicate with the graphical Windows front end through pipes[^ref-25].

### Technical Achievements

Wikipedia notes that the program would adjust its level during the game to try to match the player's, presaging the "Friend Mode" of ChessBase's Fritz[^ref-2]. The Queen's post-game analysis is spoken aloud, with squares highlighted and pieces moved on the board to illustrate her comments[^ref-3].

The game's front end used WinG, the graphics API of the Windows 3.1x era, for display, while using DirectSound for audio[^ref-7].

- **RAM:** 12-16 MB
- **CD-ROM:** 2X speed (300 KB/s)
- **Video:** SVGA 256 colors

### Technical Issues

Power Chess 98 has significant compatibility problems with modern Windows versions because of its mixed DOS/Win32 design[^ref-7][^ref-19]. Its Windows program PCHESS.EXE calls DOS programs (KING.EXE, QUEEN.EXE, RUN386.EXE) in the background, managed by HITMAN.EXE[^ref-7][^ref-19]. One technical forum user described it as "the craziest win32 + dos connection" they had seen[^ref-19].

In a DxWnd forum thread, one user reported error 216 when trying to start what was evidently the original Power Chess on a modern system, and said the Power Chess 98 version "does not work either"[^ref-19]; players on GOG and Amazon reported that Power Chess 98 would not run on Windows XP or Vista[^ref-4][^ref-6]. One user in the same thread reported that the game had difficulties even on Windows 98 itself[^ref-19].

Two routes are now documented. Emulation works: Abandonware France recommends PCem, and a TalkChess user reported running the original 1996 Power Chess in a Windows 95 environment under PCem[^ref-7][^ref-20]. A native route also exists: install the game, apply Sierra's official 1.6.0.1 patch, then apply the unofficial pchess v1.0.1 patch[^ref-20]. The pchess patcher has been tested on 32-bit Windows XP, and on 64-bit Windows 7 and Windows 11 with NTVDMx64[^ref-25].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

The original Power Chess won the Traditional Game of the Year award from Computer Games Strategy Plus in 1996[^ref-2][^ref-15]. Abandonware France calls the series an immediate critical and financial success[^ref-7].

The Power Chess series ran to three versions: the original Power Chess (1996), Power Chess 98 (1997), and Power Chess 2.0 (1998–1999)[^ref-2][^ref-7]. According to Abandonware France, the series was shelved after Sierra was sold, because the new owners felt chess software already had a dominant player[^ref-7].

### Collections

The original Power Chess was included in Sierra's Complete Chess compilation released in 1998, alongside Maurice Ashley Teaches Chess and Extreme Chess[^ref-2].

### Fan Projects

Community efforts have focused primarily on compatibility solutions rather than modifications or remakes. The unofficial pchess patcher on GitHub patches the game's process launching and engine pipes so it can run on newer Windows versions[^ref-25]. A TalkChess user has also documented running the original 1996 Power Chess under PCem emulation with Windows 95[^ref-20].

Forum discussions on Chess.com and TalkChess continue to feature nostalgic recollections and technical assistance for users attempting to run the software on modern systems[^ref-5][^ref-9][^ref-20].

### Related Publications

No official strategy guides or hint books were identified in the research materials. The game bundled an English translation of Aron Nimzowitsch's *My System* and an easy-to-follow chess tutorial[^ref-3].

### Critical Perspective

Power Chess 98 tried to address a long-standing problem with computer chess: opponents that play nothing like humans. GameSpot judged that its humanlike King came at the cost of fundamental features such as tutorials, game analysis and an opening book, where Chessmaster 5500 was stronger[^ref-11]. Its adaptive play foreshadowed later features such as Fritz's Friend Mode[^ref-2]. Its mixed DOS/Windows design has made it hard to run on modern systems[^ref-7][^ref-19], but players still remember it fondly as a learning tool, with one Amazon reviewer writing that nothing since has tried to do what Power Chess did[^ref-4][^ref-12].
