# PGA Championship Golf 2000

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

PGA Championship Golf 2000 is a Windows golf simulation from Sierra Sports, the latest in a line of Sierra golf games that began with the Front Page Sports label[^ref-1]. Developed by Headgate Studios and released in North America on June 6, 2000[^ref-3][^ref-4], it was called by the Hartford Courant "by far the most developed and satisfying of what has become a long line of golf games from Sierra"[^ref-1]. The game holds an 88% aggregate score on GameRankings[^ref-3] and received GameSpy's 2000 Sports Game of the Year award[^ref-27].

Its headline feature was TrueSwing 3, a mouse-based swing system in which pulling the mouse back and pushing it forward swings the club in real time; a conventional three-click swing remained available as an option[^ref-8][^ref-5]. Eurogamer wrote, "Quite frankly this is the best golfing simulation on the market," and awarded it 9/10[^ref-6], while GameSpot's Stephen Poole called it "currently the unrivaled king of golf sims" with a score of 8.9/10[^ref-4].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

PGA Championship Golf 2000's defining feature was the TrueSwing 3 system, which used mouse movements to simulate an actual golf stroke; players who preferred it could still use the traditional three-click method[^ref-8][^ref-5]. GameSpot noted that "there's never been a better opportunity to make the transition to the mouse-movement style of emulating the swing," adding that it takes practice but rewards chipping and putting with more finesse[^ref-4]. CNET Gamecenter's Jason Ocampo summed the game up as "a complete, innovative, and fun golf game"[^ref-8]. MyAbandonware users wrote that "the skill involved is pretty high if you like a challenge" and called it "a great reproduction of golf"[^ref-9].

The game also included a "quick start" feature allowing players to skip technical setup and get into gameplay faster[^ref-7]. Color commentary was provided by Mark Lye and Grant Boone[^ref-4].

### Structure and Progression

Sierra announced the game as "the first golf simulation to offer both complete Season and Career tour-style gameplay"[^ref-10]. Players could create custom golfers with personalized faces, body types, and clothing, then take them onto either the amateur circuit or the pro tour[^ref-7]. The Hartford Courant counted 12 styles of play, including skins, best ball, Ryder Cup, and side games such as Nassau and closest to the pin[^ref-1]. Practice facilities included putting greens and a driving range for skill development[^ref-7].

The game shipped with 13 courses: 8 carried over from the 1999 Edition and 5 new ones[^ref-8][^ref-1]. Although it carried the PGA name, the game included no real PGA Tour players[^ref-1][^ref-5]. Using the Course Architect, players could design their own courses. User-created courses could be downloaded via WON.net, and courses made for the 1999 Edition also worked in this version[^ref-8][^ref-4]. Multiplayer was supported via modem, LAN, or the WON.net online network[^ref-8].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Rather than traditional puzzles, PGA Championship Golf 2000 challenged players with realistic golf physics and course management decisions. Rough length, green speed, and wind conditions could all be adjusted, and GameSpot noted how hard it was to work out of thick rough or dig a buried ball out of a bunker with TrueSwing[^ref-4]. However, the Game Over review found that wind "doesn't seem to affect the ball as much as it should" and that the game seemed "to misjudge driving distances by more than 100 yards", which it described as a statistical problem rather than a shot-physics one[^ref-2]. The game supported resolutions up to 1920x1600[^ref-2], though its minimum requirements were modest at a Pentium 166MHz with 32MB RAM[^ref-12][^ref-8].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

PGA Championship Golf 2000 was well received by critics on release.

| Publication | Score | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Eurogamer | 9/10 | "Quite frankly this is the best golfing simulation on the market"[^ref-6] |
| GameVortex | 95% | Impressed by extensive customization options[^ref-5] |
| GameSpot | 8.9/10 | "Currently the unrivaled king of golf sims"[^ref-4] |
| Game Over | 88% | "Arguably one of the best golf simulations ever to be released for the PC"[^ref-2] |
| GameRankings | 88% | Aggregate score[^ref-3] |
| CNET Gamecenter | 8/10 | Highlighted innovative TrueSwing technology[^ref-8] |

Stephen Poole's GameSpot review singled out the new season mode as one of the biggest improvements over the 1999 edition, while criticising the lack of a mid-round save in season play[^ref-4]. Game Over's Cody Nicholson declared it "arguably one of the best golf simulations ever to be released for the PC," noting it was one of the few Sierra Sports franchises kept alive after the NFL Football Pro 99 failure[^ref-2]. The Hartford Courant provided a comprehensive analysis praising the depth of play styles and course variety[^ref-1].

However, reviews were not without criticism. Game Over noted long loading times with constant CD checking and the lack of TCP/IP multiplayer support[^ref-2]. The absence of real PGA Tour professionals was also noted as a limitation[^ref-1][^ref-5].

### Modern Assessment

MobyGames lists a MobyScore of 8.0 for the game[^ref-7]. A GOG community user recalled, "Loved playing this, always good for a quick session when on a break, and the handling of the shots via mouse was smooth and extremely accurate for my taste"[^ref-16].

## Development

### Origins

PGA Championship Golf 2000 was developed by Headgate Studios and published by Sierra Sports, continuing a golf franchise that had evolved from Sierra's Front Page Sports line[^ref-1]. MobyGames credits Vance Cook as director and Mike Jacob as senior producer[^ref-26]. Brian Silvernail, one of the credited Course Architect design consultants, designed the Canaveral Dunes course[^ref-26][^ref-3]. The Gallery Models credits include [[Rabih AbouJaoudé|Rabih Abourjaoude]], whom MobyGames identifies as Hoyle series artist Rabih AbouJaoudé[^ref-26]. Development focused on refining the TrueSwing interface introduced in the 1999 edition, with improved physics[^ref-8]. The game carried a $29.99 price at launch[^ref-4].

### Technical Achievements

TrueSwing 3 ran in real time, so the golfer's backswing followed the player's mouse movement, and it could be used on a vertical or horizontal plane[^ref-2][^ref-1]. The Course Architect tool was a notable feature, allowing players to design their own courses and download those made by others[^ref-8][^ref-5].

## Legacy

PGA Championship Golf 2000 received GameSpy's 2000 Sports Game of the Year award[^ref-27].

Sierra released patches after launch. CD Magazine reported that "the new patch goes a long way in both stomping bugs and improving the overall mechanics of the game"[^ref-19]. An upgraded version, [[2000 - PGA Championship Golf 2000 - Titanium Edition|PGA Championship Golf 2000 Titanium Edition]], followed in December 2000[^ref-3]. A Collector's Edition was announced in June 2001, adding 12 new 18-hole courses for a total of 32[^ref-18][^ref-24]. The game was rated E for Everyone by the ESRB[^ref-23].

PGA Championship Golf 2000 is not sold on modern digital platforms such as Steam or GOG[^ref-20][^ref-16], so it is mainly available through preservation sites and abandonware collections[^ref-9][^ref-21].
