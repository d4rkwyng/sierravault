# PGA Championship Golf 2000 Titanium Edition

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

PGA Championship Golf 2000 Titanium Edition is an enhanced edition of [[2000 - PGA Championship Golf 2000|PGA Championship Golf 2000]], developed by Headgate Studios and released by Sierra Sports for Windows in December 2000[^ref-11][^ref-28]. It added seven new 18-hole courses, an expanded art library for the Course Architect, and improved multiplayer linking[^ref-11][^ref-29]. Sierra's own site described it as "all the great features in PGA 2000, plus an additional 7 courses for a total 20 courses"[^ref-34][^ref-36]. Owners of the original game could download the new content for free[^ref-11][^ref-28]. The German site Gameswelt reported the announcement on December 11, 2000, calling it "eine kräftige Erweiterung" (a substantial expansion) of PGA Championship Golf 2000[^ref-39]. A German release in 2001 carried the title "PGA Championship Golf 2001"[^ref-21]. Its course count is unresolved: MobyGames says 20 courses for the German release[^ref-29], while Gameswelt's feature list for it reads "20 komplett neue Kurse (insgesamt nun 33 Kurse)" (20 entirely new courses, 33 in total)[^ref-42]. For the US edition, Sierra's own site gives 20 as the Titanium Edition's total[^ref-34][^ref-36]. That total implies 13 base courses, and CNET Gamecenter and Wikipedia both count 13 courses in PGA Championship Golf 2000[^ref-41][^ref-27]; Sierra's own December 2000 courses page, however, lists 12 base-game courses, seven real-world and five Sierra Sports originals[^ref-35].

## Story Summary

As a golf simulation, the game has no story. The player golfs the courses of the base game plus the seven new Titanium Edition courses[^ref-28].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The Titanium Edition is built on PGA Championship Golf 2000, whose swing system is "TrueSwing 3." Eurogamer's review of the base game describes it: "simply pull the mouse back to draw the club back, and forwards to release the shot"[^ref-32]. MobyGames lists keyboard and mouse input and a required 3D accelerator[^ref-33].

### Structure and Progression

Sierra described the seven new 18-hole courses as "designed specifically for PGA Championship Golf Titanium Edition by some of the top designers in the PGA 2000 community"[^ref-35]: Beartooth Pass Golf Course (Brian Silvernail), Cutter Bay Golf Club (Tom Steenstrup), Eman Hills Golf Course (Chris Anderson), Rugged Cross Desert Course (Ken McHale), Sammamish Hills Golf Club (Jim Mattler), Saxe Gotha Golf Club (Jim Dickson) and Tulaichean Bay (Alan Toft)[^ref-35][^ref-28]. Alongside them the edition kept the base game's seven real-world courses, among them Royal Birkdale, Pasatiempo and Sahalee, and Sierra Sports' own original courses[^ref-35]. Sierra said it would post one new course per day during launch week, so existing owners could add them free[^ref-28][^ref-39]; the edition also expanded the Course Architect's art library[^ref-29]. Multiplayer supports Internet, LAN and modem play[^ref-33].

Although the game carries the PGA name, and MobyGames groups it with "PGA licensees"[^ref-29], Wikipedia notes that PGA Championship Golf 2000 "does not include any professional PGA golfers"[^ref-27].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The Titanium Edition got little separate coverage. MobyGames lists 77% from 3 ratings; individual reviews are not itemised[^ref-19]. No Titanium Edition review from GameSpot was found. The GameSpot score sometimes attached to this title belongs to the original PGA Championship Golf 2000.

Gameswelt's Tobias Straßer reviewed the German-titled release "PGA Championship Golf 2001" on January 24, 2002. He called it "für sein Genre ein wirklich sehr gutes Spiel" (a really very good game for its genre) and described the graphics as low-level, with the game running in a window, a static environment and trees that cannot be seen through. He also covered the TrueSwing and three-click swing options, the multiplayer modes and the course editor[^ref-43]. The review does not say which edition's content it covers.

The new courses themselves were well received by players. Reviewing the later Collector's Edition in November 2001, SportPlanet's John Cauthen wrote that "the Titanium Edition courses quickly established themselves as 'must haves' for online play," adding that "the fact that they were downloadable seems to have had a big bearing on their popularity"[^ref-38].

## Development

### Origins

Headgate Studios developed the Titanium Edition, and Sierra Sports published it[^ref-11][^ref-23]. Sierra had bought the studio in April 1996, but on February 22, 1999 it announced a restructuring and sold the rights of the original studio back to its founder Vance Cook as a new corporate entity; Headgate began publishing games through Electronic Arts in 2000[^ref-31], and EA later said it had "partnered with Headgate since 2000"[^ref-40]. Headgate kept its name until Electronic Arts acquired it on November 30, 2006 (reported December 1), when it "was renamed EA Salt Lake"[^ref-40][^ref-31]. Justin Kubiak, brand manager for Sierra Sports, said at launch: "We've published the Titanium Edition to present to the PC gaming community another opportunity to experience the award-winning PGA Championship Golf product line"[^ref-28].

### Production

GameSpot reported the game in stores on December 11, 2000, at about US$29.99. It added that "Owners of previous editions of PGA Championship Golf 2000 will be able to download the upgrades for free"[^ref-11]. Computer Games Strategy Plus, posting on December 16, 2000, said Sierra Sports had released it "yesterday", meaning about December 15[^ref-28]. The Sierra Chest gives a first-release date of 2001-01-01[^ref-23], which conflicts with both contemporary reports.

### Technical Achievements

The minimum requirements are Windows 95, an Intel Pentium, 32 MB of RAM and a 4X CD-ROM drive. The game runs at 640×480[^ref-33].
