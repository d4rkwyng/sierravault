# PGA Championship Golf Collector's Edition

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

PGA Championship Golf Collector's Edition is a 2001 Windows golf simulation from Sierra Sports, an updated version of PGA Championship Golf 2000 that added twelve new 18-hole courses "developed exclusively for the game, bringing the total number of courses available to 32."[^ref-2] Sierra announced it in June 2001, on the first day of the US Open, promising 12 new courses, refined gameplay and the series' TrueSwing system, with release expected in late summer.[^ref-1][^ref-2] The Sierra Chest records its first release as July 11, 2001.[^ref-32]

SportPlanet's John Cauthen summed it up as "PGA 2000/PGA Titanium Edition with 12 new courses and an up-to-date patch," a course expansion that could nonetheless be installed on its own.[^ref-33]

**Base-game context.** The game underneath is [[2000 - PGA Championship Golf 2000|PGA Championship Golf 2000]], developed by [[Headgate Studios]] and released in North America on June 6, 2000 with 13 courses. An upgraded Titanium Edition with seven new courses followed in December 2000.[^ref-7] For that game's modes, controls, reviews and development history, see its own page and the [[2000 - PGA Championship Golf 2000 - Titanium Edition|Titanium Edition]] page.

## Story Summary

As a golf simulation, the Collector's Edition has no story. Cauthen noted that it "looks and plays like the same game as the now 18 month old PGA 2000."[^ref-33]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The Collector's Edition keeps PGA Championship Golf 2000's TrueSwing mouse swing.[^ref-2] Its box led with "SmartSwing" technology, which Cauthen dismissed as a new name for an old feature: "SmartSwing is just a handle for TrueSwing's capability to adapt to different player rhythms. That's always been a part of PGA 2000."[^ref-33]

### Structure and Progression

The edition's main addition is twelve new 18-hole courses, for 32 in total.[^ref-2] SportPlanet's review names several of them and their designers, all known from the PGA 2000 course-design community:[^ref-33]

- **Normandy Heights**, by Scott Antes
- **Lochiel Golf Links**, by Mark Guiniven
- **Whisperwind G.C.**, by Jim Dickson, a companion to his Titanium Edition course Whisperwind Golf Club
- **Sammamish Hills**, by Jim Mattler, a companion to his Titanium Edition course Sammamish Plateau

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The game includes a complete course editor, the Course Architect, for designing and playing custom courses.[^ref-2] According to Cauthen, "architects get a bevy of new objects and textures."[^ref-33]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The only contemporary review located is SportPlanet's "Planet CyberGolf" column of November 30, 2001. Cauthen called the Collector's Edition "still the best playing golf game on the market" and the new courses "quite distinctive." He also criticised it on several counts:[^ref-33]

- **Installation:** it refused to install over an existing Titanium Edition and required the earlier version to be uninstalled. A full install of all 32 courses "requires 1.3 gigs of hard drive space."
- **Little that was new:** "PGACE would have had to sweeten the pot with technology or gameplay advances to have had a chance at making a commercial splash, but none of the above are to be found here."
- **Ageing graphics:** the polygonal golfers had not been improved since PGA 2000's release.

## Development

### Origins

IGN reported the announcement on June 14, 2001, noting that it "coincides with the first day of the US Open." The article opened with a joke about Sierra reviving its Front Page Sports Football and Baseball franchises, then added: "Oh wait a minute; that must be a joke."[^ref-1] GameSpot's announcement the next day gave the 12-course and 32-course totals and said the game was "scheduled for release later this summer."[^ref-2]

### Production

No production credits for the Collector's Edition were found. The Sierra Chest lists the developer as "Headgames Inc."[^ref-32] Cauthen wrote that by late 2001 Headgate Studios, "the developer of PGA 2000," had "signed-on with EA Sports," while Sierra was still supporting PGA 2000.[^ref-33]

### Version History

| Version | Date | Platform | Notes |
|---------|------|----------|-------|
| PGA Championship Golf 2000 | June 6, 2000 | Windows | Base game, 13 courses[^ref-7] |
| Titanium Edition | December 2000 | Windows | Seven new courses[^ref-7] |
| Collector's Edition | July 11, 2001 | Windows | 12 new courses, 32 total[^ref-2][^ref-32] |

The retail release came on two CD-ROMs, as preserved on the Internet Archive.[^ref-31] The Sierra Chest records a US release in a medium flip-top box with a game manual and a quick reference card (UPC 0-20626-67084-8).[^ref-32]

## Legacy

According to Cauthen, the Collector's Edition courses had not caught on in Sierra's online lobby by late 2001. Unlike the Titanium Edition courses, they were not downloadable, and players had grown used to "a wealth of free, downloadable courses of comparable quality provided by an active community of course architects."[^ref-33]
