# Outpost 2: Divided Destiny

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Outpost 2: Divided Destiny is a real-time strategy game developed by Dynamix and published by Sierra On-Line in 1997[^ref-1]. Unlike traditional real-time strategy games, Outpost 2 emphasizes colony management over the destruction of enemies[^ref-2]. The game is set on a distant planet called New Terra, where the last survivors of humanity must build sustainable colonies after Earth was destroyed by an asteroid impact known as Vulcan's Hammer[^ref-2][^ref-4].

The game focuses on the happiness and survival of the colonists - the last remaining survivors of an Earth catastrophe[^ref-5]. Players must manage complex colony systems while dealing with environmental disasters, resource scarcity, and the spreading threat of a terraforming microbe gone wrong called "the Blight"[^ref-6]. Over time, two colonies arise that have different goals in rebuilding humanity[^ref-2][^ref-3], creating the central conflict that drives the game's narrative.

## Story Summary

The story begins after Earth has been devastated by an asteroid impact, forcing the last survivors to journey to a distant planet called New Terra[^ref-9]. The game's backstory reveals that "the earth, is dead. As the asteroid fragments landed, the last few survivors departed the solar system, seeking a new home among the stars"[^ref-10]. The colonists' situation becomes desperate when their secret terraforming experiments produce a deadly side effect - a mutated microbe spreading through the planet's crust that scientists call the "Blight"[^ref-6].

The narrative is driven by survival missions where players must evacuate colonies as disasters strike.

Early missions involve urgent evacuations with briefings stating "Something has gone horribly wrong. We must evacuate the colony immediately"[^ref-6].

The story explores themes of human survival and the consequences of scientific experimentation, as the colonists must deal with contaminated expeditions where "once our units enter the infected area, they will be carriers of the Blight"[^ref-6].

The game's tagline "Extinction is not an option" underscores the desperate nature of humanity's struggle for survival[^ref-11].

The in-game storyline branches depending on whether Eden or Plymouth is chosen, with some events playing out differently or not at all[^ref-2]. The plot is filled out by a novella written by J. Steven York and included with the game, following Axen Moon of Eden and Emma Burke of Plymouth[^ref-2]; during a campaign, the STORY option lets players read the novella's chapters up to the current mission[^ref-15].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game features a top-down perspective with mouse and keyboard controls[^ref-12]. Players select and command units and structures with the mouse, and IGN's cheats page lists a "Bolster Production" tip, which says left-clicking and right-clicking a factory increases its production[^ref-13]. The interface emphasizes colony management systems over traditional military command structures found in other RTS games.

### Structure and Progression

Outpost 2 offers three game modes: Campaign, Colony, and Multiplayer[^ref-2][^ref-15]. There are separate campaigns for Eden and Plymouth, 24 missions in all, 12 for each colony[^ref-2][^ref-15]. In every mode the player can pick either colony[^ref-2]. Map sizes vary by mission; in the Eden campaign, for example, Mission 1 is a 64 x 64 map and Mission 2 is 128 x 64[^ref-6].

The ultimate goal is to build a spaceship and escape from New Terra, a planet that is ravaged by a terraforming microbe gone bad[^ref-12]. Multiplayer supports up to six players over SIGS (the Sierra Internet Gaming System), IPX or TCP/IP, and two players over modem or serial connections[^ref-15][^ref-19].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Morale is a critical factor for the colony, and because it can fluctuate rapidly, much of the player's attention is spent on ensuring that it remains at optimal levels[^ref-2]. Low morale reduces efficiency and causes worker shortages, while food shortages, disasters, and overcrowding lower it[^ref-2]. GOG's description sums up the balance as "resources, construction research, defense, morale and combat"[^ref-8].

GOG lists 140 different vehicles and buildings and 60,000 frames of animation[^ref-8], and MobyGames cites over 2,000 types of terrain[^ref-12]. Combat is secondary to survival, and one review republished on GameFabrique complained that "Everything shoots lasers" and "Everything moves slower than a sloth in a tar pit!"[^ref-18]. The factions play differently: Eden's research is more advanced and its military relies on brute force, while Plymouth's morale is more stable and its military relies on speed and trickery[^ref-2]. Each side has some units and buildings of its own[^ref-2].

Campaign missions are timed. The player must reach population, resource, construction, and research targets before the Blight or lava flows engulf the colony, so the colony keeps relocating from mission to mission[^ref-2]. Mineable ore seams on New Terra are much rarer than on Earth[^ref-2].

| Graphics | Local bus SVGA card, 1 MB video RAM, 640 x 480 in 16-bit color |
| Sound | Windows 95 compatible sound card |
| DirectX | DirectX 3 |
| Multiplayer | SIGS, IPX, TCP/IP (up to six players); modem, serial (two players) |

The game is Windows-only; the Outpost Universe wiki notes that it runs well under Wine on Linux and suggests CrossOver on macOS[^ref-4].

## Research Tree

Research happens in three buildings[^ref-2]. The Basic Lab appears only in the campaign, where it rebuilds databases lost in the evacuation. The Standard Lab mostly researches new buildings and upgrades to buildings, units and the colony. The Advanced Lab handles further colony advantages, most of the weapons program, and the space program for building the escape ship[^ref-2].

Combat units are built to order. There are three chassis, the Lynx, Panther and Tiger, and each can carry one of eleven weapon turrets, roughly half of them available to each faction[^ref-2][^ref-15]. Each chassis and weapon type must be researched before it can be built[^ref-2].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

| Publication | Score | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|

| GameSpot | 5/10 (Mediocre) | Criticized as "The Hurry Up and Wait game" due to slow pacing[^ref-19] |
| Computer Gaming World | 3.5/5 | Martin E. Cirulis called it "an excellent attempt to resurrect this franchise" but said "limited vision has damaged another promising Sierra product"[^ref-1] |

### Modern Assessment

Modern user reviews have been more favorable than contemporary professional reviews. GOG.com's store page shows a 4.4/5 user rating from 58 reviews (as of October 2026)[^ref-8]. Metacritic users rate the game 8.3/10[^ref-20], while MyAbandonware gives it 4.75/5[^ref-5]. GameFAQs users rate it as "Great" based on 24 ratings[^ref-14]. 

User reviews often praise the game's unique approach to the RTS genre. One reviewer noted it's "an RTS not centered around combat" where "the conflict here is not against some enemy force, but against nature itself"[^ref-8]. Another user called it "a classic but underrated game that expertly mixed city building with RTS elements"[^ref-20].

## Development

### Origins

Outpost 2: Divided Destiny is a sequel to the 1994 Outpost[^ref-1][^ref-17]. It reuses and refines some of the original's concepts, but the storylines and gameplay have no direct continuity[^ref-2]. GameSpot's reviewer wrote that the first game "was plagued with a massive number of problems", and that Sierra made the sequel real-time and added combat, hoping to "shake off the 'sins of the father'"[^ref-19]. The background story was written by J. Steven York, credited as Background Story Author, and his novella ships with the game[^ref-1][^ref-2].

### Production

The game was designed by Allen McPheeters and Patrick Cook, with Cook as director, Kate Kloos as producer, and Dan Duncalf as lead programmer[^ref-1]. Charles Barth composed the music, and Kathy Cullis voiced the Savant Computer[^ref-1].

### Technical Achievements

GOG's store description lists 140 different vehicles and buildings and 60,000 frames of animation[^ref-8], along with over 2,000 types of terrain and 16-bit color graphics[^ref-12]. The game ran at 640 x 480 in 16-bit color and needed DirectX 3[^ref-15]. PCGamingWiki lists it as 4:3 only, with no widescreen support[^ref-17].

## Legacy

Despite mixed contemporary reviews, Outpost 2 has maintained an active community of players and developers who continue to support and develop content for the game[^ref-21]. The Sierra Internet Gaming System (SIGS) that originally supported online play has been discontinued, but the game is now commonly played through virtual networks like Hamachi[^ref-2]. This dedicated community has kept multiplayer alive long after official support ended.

The game is sold on GOG.com, which released it in 2020 with build version 1.2[^ref-22]. Official post-release support came in 1997 with Sierra's Scenario Pack 1. It added three multiplayer maps (Peacekeeper, La Corrida and Pie Chart), let the Eden campaign's twelfth mission run longer without exhausting internal resources, and improved the program's handling of more than 256 buildings[^ref-23]. Fan work continues at the Outpost Universe (outpost2.net). Its users have developed "tools, patches and new content", and as of October 2026 its forums still carry add-on mission and mission-SDK development threads[^ref-21].

Outpost 2 stands out as an RTS built around survival and colony management rather than conquest[^ref-2].
