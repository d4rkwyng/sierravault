# Quest for Infamy

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

Quest for Infamy is a hybrid point-and-click adventure game and RPG developed by Infamous Quests[^ref-1] and published on PC by Phoenix Online Publishing on July 10, 2014[^ref-18]. Inspired by Sierra's Quest for Glory series, it casts the player as one of three anti-heroes rather than a traditional hero[^ref-2]. Built using Adventure Game Studio (AGS)[^ref-3], it features a hand-drawn world[^ref-13] and a voice cast[^ref-22].

The game follows Mr. Roehm, a scoundrel on the run from his latest scrape who is stuck in the Valley of Krasna for a while and takes on some work to pass the time[^ref-4]. Originally conceived to take place in Spielburg Valley as the villain's perspective on Quest for Glory, the final released version takes place in an original setting but maintains the inspiration from Sierra's classic adventure games and RPGs of the 1990s[^ref-5]. The project was funded through a Kickstarter campaign that ran from June 6 to July 6, 2012, marking the transition of the team from fan remake creators to commercial game developers[^ref-24][^ref-2].

## Story Summary

Quest for Infamy centers around Mr. Roehm, a man who is trying to start his life over after running away from a shady past[^ref-1]. He arrives in the little town of Volksville, only to discover there is much more going on in the valley than meets the eye[^ref-13]; the game opens with the execution of a man named Markus, and the town's law is kept by Sheriff Rayford[^ref-26]. Roehm becomes embroiled in a power struggle between several ambitious residents of the valley[^ref-4]. A lot of the plot revolves around the tragic and mysterious story of the Killington family[^ref-1], and the story builds to a confrontation with the Morroi cult, whose leader turns out to be Rayford himself[^ref-26].

The game's narrative deliberately positions Roehm as an anti-hero rather than a traditional villain. Developer Steven Alexander described Roehm as evolving from a full-on villain into an anti-hero in the vein of Han Solo: "Someone who is not a goody two shoes hero type, someone who is often on the wrong side of the law…but at heart is not a person of evil intent. Mostly just out for themselves"[^ref-7]. The game's store page invites players to "Lie, cheat, and steal your way through tales of charming villainy with multiple endings"[^ref-11].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Quest for Infamy is a point-and-click adventure built on AGS with a presentation that RPGFan said "wouldn't be out of place in the golden 90's era of Sierra questing"[^ref-12]. It is played with keyboard and mouse[^ref-11]. The game includes over 200 rooms and more than 50 NPCs to explore and interact with[^ref-13].

### Structure and Progression

Players choose their path to infamy by playing as one of three character classes: the sneaky rogue, the bold brigand, or the crafty sorcerer[^ref-4]. Each class takes the player on a different course through the quest[^ref-13], and multiple paths and branching storylines create a different experience with each playthrough[^ref-4].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Quest for Infamy combines traditional point-and-click adventure puzzles with RPG elements[^ref-2], including combat and spellcasting[^ref-13]. Several reviews singled out combat as one of the weaker elements of the game[^ref-15].

### Modern Assessment

Quest for Infamy received a Metacritic score of 63 on PC[^ref-18], indicating mixed to average reviews; RPGFan's 80/100 was among the higher scores[^ref-12]. User reviews have been more positive, with GOG.com showing a 3.9/5 rating from 43 reviews[^ref-4] and itch.io a 4.8/5 rating from 12 ratings[^ref-11]. The 2022 console ports drew mixed reviews, with TheXboxHub giving it 2.5/5 and criticizing the voice acting[^ref-20].

## Development

### Origins

Quest for Infamy originated at Infamous Quests, founded in 2012 by Steven Alexander and Shawn Mills, who had previously founded the amateur studio Infamous Adventures[^ref-2], known for its fan remakes of King's Quest III and Space Quest II[^ref-5]. The initial concept was to create a game where players could return to Spielburg in the role of the villain, inspired by Sierra's Quest for Glory series[^ref-5]. The final version moved away from direct Quest for Glory connections to an original setting while keeping the inspiration[^ref-5].

### Production

The game was funded through a Kickstarter campaign that ran from June 6 to July 6, 2012, raising $63,281 from 1,656 backers against a $25,000 goal[^ref-24]; the target had already been exceeded weeks before the campaign closed[^ref-6]. After the Kickstarter came two years of production before the game's July 2014 release[^ref-7]. Mills and Alexander wrote the game themselves before bringing in the wider team, and Alexander recalled the motto they had kept since their remake days: "JUST FINISH IT"[^ref-7]. Mills attributed the failure of many similar fan projects to their being "too 'team' driven" without one or two people pushing the vision[^ref-7].

Key development challenges included balancing the anti-hero character concept. As Shawn Mills explained: "One of the first things we realized is that evil and being a 'bad guy' are different things. Evil is by definition insanity. If we just allowed the player character to walk into a village and kill all the people, well…you can't tell much of a story from that point"[^ref-7].

### Technical Details

Quest for Infamy was created using Adventure Game Studio (AGS)[^ref-3]. GOG's store description promises "lovingly hand-crafted retro style graphics" evoking the classic era of adventure gaming[^ref-4]. The soundtrack was composed by James Mulvale; the official soundtrack album lists 98 tracks[^ref-21]. The game includes full voice acting with a cast including Steve Patrick, Sean Pratt, Christian Bell, and Matthew C. Larsen[^ref-22].

## Legacy

Quest for Infamy won four Adventure Game Studio awards in 2014, including Best Character Lead for Roehm, Best Puzzles, Best Music & Sounds, and Best Gameplay[^ref-2]. The game was also nominated for Best Writing (Comedy) at the Aggie Awards and was among the Top 100 nominations for IndieDB's 2014 Indie Game of the Year[^ref-2].

### Sales and Community Reception

Despite these accolades, Hardcore Gaming 101 reports that sales "didn't quite make up for the huge amount of work required to create the game", leaving a sequel's future uncertain[^ref-7]. User ratings on GOG (3.9/5 from 43 reviews) and itch.io (4.8/5 from 12 ratings) are higher than the mixed professional reviews[^ref-4][^ref-11]. Developer Shawn Mills expressed enthusiasm for returning to the series, stating "Quest for Infamy is one of the greatest things I've done in my life, so I'd love to go back to that well again!"[^ref-7]

### Console Ports

The game was ported to Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 4/5, Xbox One and Xbox Series X/S, published by Ratalaika Games on March 4, 2022[^ref-25][^ref-8]. Console reviews were mixed, with TheXboxHub criticizing the voice acting[^ref-20].
