# Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>


## Overview

*Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale* is a visual novel adventure game developed and published by Transolar Games, released on March 28, 2023 for PC platforms and June 15, 2023 for Nintendo Switch[^ref-1][^ref-2]. Created by legendary adventure game designers [[Lori Ann Cole]] and [[Corey Cole]]—the husband-and-wife team behind Sierra On-Line's acclaimed *Quest for Glory* series—the game represents their return to more modestly scaled game development following the financially challenging production of *Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption*[^ref-3][^ref-4].

The game is set before the events of *Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption*[^ref-12] and has been called an "airy spiritual sequel" to it[^ref-6]. It takes place at Hero University, "a school where famous adventurers teach young people how to be heroes"[^ref-1]. Players take on the role of Tilly Appleberry, a mischievous Rogue student who values people and fun above rules and authority[^ref-1][^ref-2]. The Coles deliberately chose the visual novel format to create a more accessible, lower-budget project after losing money on their previous large-scale RPG[^ref-3].

Billed by its store description as "a funny and charming visual novel adventure"[^ref-2], *Tilly's Tale* was successfully funded through Kickstarter in November 2019, with 1,639 backers pledging $106,155 USD[^ref-7][^ref-8]. The project took four years to complete, a delay that Choicest Games' reviewer noted came at a time when many studios struggled with development during COVID-19[^ref-8]. As [[Corey Cole]] explained, the visual novel format allowed them to strip out many of the more costly and time-consuming parts of game creation[^ref-3].

## Story Summary

*Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale* follows Tilly Appleberry, the adopted daughter of the Healer of Spielburg—a location fans will recognize from the original *Quest for Glory* games[^ref-3]. Tilly is a Rogue student at Hero University, a school where famous adventurers teach young people how to be heroes[^ref-1]. Unlike the typical hero-in-training, Tilly values having fun and connecting with people over following rules and respecting authority figures[^ref-1].

Facing expulsion after a prank, Tilly has two weeks to organize a successful Harvest Festival at Hero-U[^ref-3][^ref-8]. Players must help Tilly navigate her relationships with various quirky characters while using her wit, charm, and rogueish skills to make the upcoming festival a success[^ref-1]. The focus is squarely on the story and the fantasy characters she meets, among them Mooella, a self-conscious minotaur paladin, and Sitari, a shy katta bard[^ref-3], as well as a "Doggie Chef" and other potential friends and love interests[^ref-1][^ref-11].

As a visual novel, the narrative is shaped by player choices, with multiple endings and "hundreds of paths through the game"[^ref-1]. Before release, the project was described as featuring 23 characters and 25 locations at Hero-U[^ref-7]. [[Corey Cole]] called it "the opposite of a high stakes, stressful game"[^ref-6], and the store description bills it as suitable for all ages[^ref-1].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

*Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale* operates as a visual novel, presenting the story through static backgrounds and character art that is "not animated but rather a series of still images – a handful for each character, depending on their current emotion"[^ref-3]. In [[Corey Cole]]'s words, "think of it as just like a Sierra adventure game without having to walk everywhere"[^ref-12]. Players progress through the narrative by clicking to advance text and making choices when presented with dialogue options[^ref-1].

The game is fully voiced; according to Adventure Game Hotspot, "each character and every line is voiced by a professional actor"[^ref-3]. Players can use a Fast Forward button to skip through previously read text, though early versions had bugs associated with this feature[^ref-13].

### Structure and Progression

The game follows a semester at Hero University over a series of days, with class each morning and dinner each evening, building toward the Harvest Festival[^ref-3][^ref-1]. Players move around the campus using an overhead map and choose from a menu of options at each location[^ref-3].

- **Early Game:** Players meet various characters and establish relationships
- **Mid Game:** Tilly navigates social situations and festival preparations
- **Endgame:** The Harvest Festival itself, with outcomes determined by choices made throughout

A single playthrough is short: Adventure Game Hotspot's reviewer finished his first run in less than an hour[^ref-3], and the Adventure Gamer blog called it "a fun way to spend a couple of hours"[^ref-6]. The game is designed to be replayed to discover different paths and endings[^ref-1][^ref-3].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Unlike traditional adventure games from the Coles' *Quest for Glory* series, *Tilly's Tale* deliberately avoids challenging adventure game puzzles and CRPG-style combat[^ref-3]. Instead, gameplay focuses on dialogue choices and relationship building[^ref-1].

The game includes:
- **Choice-based narrative:** Player decisions affect which characters Tilly befriends and the ultimate ending[^ref-1]
- **Multiple paths:** Hundreds of narrative branches provide replay value[^ref-1]
- **Card minigame:** An optional, easily missed card game, which Hotspot called the closest thing the game has to a puzzle[^ref-3][^ref-6]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

*Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale* received a mixed reception from critics upon release. Adventure Gamers awarded the game 70/100, praising it as "an enjoyable game that boasts expert voice-overs, colorfully stylized visuals, and rollicking background melodies, all complemented by a humorous, pun-filled narrative"[^ref-15]. The review noted that "fans of Lori and Corey Cole will surely be delighted, but it may be a tougher sell for those new to their previous work"[^ref-15].

Adventure Game Hotspot gave a more reserved 60/100, with reviewer Nicholas Fuller describing it as "a pleasant but undemanding little visual novel"[^ref-3]. Fuller acknowledged that while fans of *Quest for Glory* and *Hero-U* would enjoy spending time in the Coles' universe again, "those who want the same kind of intricate gameplay might be disappointed"[^ref-3].

Choicest Games awarded 7/10, with Mark Goninon writing that "at times, Tilly's Tale feels like a high quality visual novel, with great artwork, voice acting and a punny story based on a universe rich in lore that Quest for Glory fans are very familiar with"[^ref-8]. However, he noted "it's debatable on whether this game offers anything new to visual novel fans in general and bugs can potentially ruin the experience too"[^ref-8].

### Modern Assessment

Steam user reviews show a "Mixed" rating, with 69% of 13 reviews being positive[^ref-1]. The game's small review count reflects its niche appeal to fans of the Coles' previous work.

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 67% (based on 3 ratings)[^ref-5]
- **Steam:** 69% positive (13 reviews)[^ref-1]
- **Steambase:** 62/100 (21 reviews, as of October 2026)[^ref-16]

## Development

### Origins

Following the release of *Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption* in 2018, [[Lori Ann Cole]] and [[Corey Cole]] found it "hard to consider committing to another large-scale game" after the financial losses they experienced[^ref-3]. The couple, who had been designing games together for over 30 years at Sierra On-Line and later at their independent studio Transolar Games, sought a more sustainable approach to game development[^ref-1][^ref-5].

The project was originally conceived as *Summer Daze at Hero-U*, featuring two playable characters: a rogue named Tilly and a wizard named Ifeyo[^ref-7][^ref-8]. Due to the scope of development and various delays, the project was split into two separate games, with *Tilly's Tale* releasing first and *Ifeyo's Adventure* planned for later release[^ref-7][^ref-8].

### Production

The Kickstarter campaign launched in 2019 and was successfully funded on November 6, 2019, with 1,639 backers pledging $106,155 USD[^ref-7][^ref-8]. The Coles chose the visual novel format because it allowed them to create a quality experience "with fixed sets and minimal animation" while keeping their development budget "slightly more reasonable"[^ref-17].

Development took four years[^ref-8]. In January 2022 the game was announced for a March 31, 2022 release[^ref-7], but it did not ship until March 28, 2023[^ref-1]. Choicest Games' reviewer noted that many studios struggled with development during COVID-19[^ref-8]. Voice acting management was handled by Dave Gilbert of Wadjet Eye Games, ensuring professional quality across the fully voiced cast[^ref-8].

The Coles told RPGFan that working "with fixed sets and minimal animation" kept the development budget "slightly more reasonable"[^ref-17]. The professionally voiced dialogue carries the story, while each character is drawn as a handful of still images[^ref-3].

### Cut Content

The most significant cut content involved the splitting of the original project. The game was initially designed to allow players to choose between two playable characters: Tilly (a rogue) and Ifeyo (a wizard)[^ref-7][^ref-8]. After numerous delays, Ifeyo's story was separated into a standalone sequel titled *Ifeyo's Adventure*[^ref-7][^ref-8]. It was announced for 2023 in January 2022[^ref-7]; by May 2023 Adventure Game Hotspot expected it "in a year or so"[^ref-3].

### Technical Issues

The game suffered from several technical problems at launch that affected player experience:

- **Transition Freezing:** The original release tended to freeze when moving to a new location, though this issue was reportedly fixed in patches[^ref-3]
- **Text Advancement Bug:** Some players reported clicking would not transition to the next text bubble, requiring saves and restarts to progress[^ref-8]
- **Fast Forward Crashes:** One Steam user reported that clicking the Fast Forward button while stuck on a line of dialogue crashed the game[^ref-13]
- **Linux Steam License Issue:** An itch.io user reported that the Linux build launched Steam and complained about a missing license; the developer suspected a stray "SteamSettings.ini" file that "should not be in the itch.io build" and later uploaded updated builds[^ref-10]

One Steam user reported: "But in every other dialogue between Tilly & another character, I get stuck on one line of text. If I then click on the Fast Forward button at top right, the game crashes. How can I play a game if it constantly crashes?"[^ref-13]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- **Quest for Glory Connection:** Tilly is the adopted daughter of the Healer of Spielburg, connecting her directly to the original *Quest for Glory* game world[^ref-3]
- **Card Minigame:** An easily missed card minigame is hidden in the game[^ref-3][^ref-6]
- **Character References:** The game makes several references to characters and locations from earlier Quest for Glory games, especially the Spielburg Valley[^ref-8]

### Multiple Endings

The game features multiple endings based on player choices throughout the narrative[^ref-1]. Key factors that determine endings include:

- **Relationship Building:** Which characters Tilly befriends and how those relationships develop
- **Dialogue Choices:** Selecting different conversation options opens different story paths
- **Festival Preparation:** How successfully Tilly helps prepare for the Harvest Festival

The store descriptions encourage players to "play again (and again!) to meet different people"[^ref-2][^ref-11], promising "hundreds of paths through the game"[^ref-1].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

While specific sales figures are not publicly available, the game represents the Coles' continued presence in adventure gaming after their challenging experience with *Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption*[^ref-3][^ref-4]. The lower-budget visual novel approach allowed them to continue creating games without the financial risks of larger productions[^ref-3].

With two critic reviews listed on Metacritic[^ref-15] and 13 Steam user reviews[^ref-1], the game has remained a niche title, aimed mainly at fans of the Coles' earlier work[^ref-3][^ref-15].

### Collections

*Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale* has not been included in any compilation packages as of the time of this writing. The game remains available as a standalone purchase on digital storefronts.

### Planned Sequels

The game is described as "the first game in a new series of interactive visual novels set at Hero-U"[^ref-1][^ref-12]. *Ifeyo's Adventure*, featuring the wizard character originally planned for this game, was announced as the next installment[^ref-7][^ref-8].

### Related Publications

- **Digital Manual:** Included with itch.io purchase (6.6 MB)[^ref-10]

### Critical Perspective

*Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale* represents an interesting chapter in the ongoing evolution of adventure gaming from Sierra veterans. The game demonstrates how classic adventure game designers have adapted to changing market conditions and personal circumstances—creating smaller, more focused experiences rather than attempting to compete with large-budget productions[^ref-3][^ref-4].

Jimmy Maher of The Digital Antiquarian, writing about the Coles' return to game-making with *Hero-U*, described *Tilly's Tale* as "more modestly scaled but equally warm-hearted"[^ref-4]. Reviewers noted that it lacks the puzzles of *Quest for Glory*[^ref-3] but keeps the Coles' humorous, pun-filled writing[^ref-15].

[[Corey Cole]] told Maher that he and Lori Ann have other ideas in the pipeline, but that they "are pushing 70, and spending more time on ourselves"[^ref-4].
