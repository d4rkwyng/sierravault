# Gray Matter

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

Gray Matter is a supernatural point-and-click adventure game created by designer Jane Jensen, her first adventure game in over a decade after Gabriel Knight 3: Blood of the Sacred, Blood of the Damned (1999)[^ref-1]. The game follows American street magician Samantha Everett, who takes refuge at the English manor of neurobiologist Dr. David Styles after her motorbike breaks down in a storm[^ref-2]. Gray Matter "tackles questions concerning the nature of reality and the power of the human mind in constructing the world we take for granted"[^ref-3].

The game's road to release was long. Adventure Gamers described Jensen's effort to get it made as "an odyssey that has taken seven years, two major publishers, two different developers, and numerous release delays"[^ref-1]. First announced at E3 2003 as "Project Jane-J" through The Adventure Company, it was put on hold, revived by dtp entertainment's ANACONDA label in 2006, and moved from Hungarian developer Tonuzaba to French studio Wizarbox in 2008[^ref-21][^ref-4][^ref-36]. It was released in Germany on 12 November 2010 and in North America and Europe in February 2011[^ref-6]. Adventure Gamers praised Jensen's trademark storytelling but said that "an overall lack of polish keeps it from meeting the massive expectations of an instant classic"[^ref-1].

Adventure Classic Gaming called it "a game that can easily measure up against the best of what the genre can offer"[^ref-44], while The A.V. Club praised it as "the best traditional adventure game in a decade"[^ref-6]. Metacritic aggregated the PC version to a score of 72/100 based on 27 critic reviews[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

Samantha "Sam" Everett, an American magician, is riding to London when a storm turns a signpost around and sends her toward Oxford instead; her motorcycle breaks down outside Dread Hill House[^ref-11][^ref-6]. Sam had set out to find the Daedalus Club, a secret society that only the world's greatest magicians may join[^ref-12][^ref-13]. To get out of the storm, she claims to be the new assistant that the house's owner has been seeking[^ref-11].

The manor belongs to Dr. David Styles, a reclusive neurobiologist. Three years before the events of the game, his wife Laura died in a car accident; Styles was disfigured by severe burns and withdrew from the university to his mansion[^ref-21][^ref-1]. Sam's first task is to find six people to form "The Lamb's Club" of test subjects for Styles's experiment at Oxford University[^ref-10]. Officially, the experiment studies the effects of imagined physical activity on the brain, but Styles is privately convinced he can contact Laura's spirit through experiments related to psi energy[^ref-1].

As the research begins, inexplicable and dangerous phenomena start occurring around the Oxford campus, at the same locations the students are instructed to visualize[^ref-1]. Sam suspects the work of a magician, and her investigation draws in Styles's past, his paranormal research and the Daedalus Club[^ref-1][^ref-6]. Jensen has described both leads as characters "dealt very bad hands in life" who "can help each other heal"[^ref-15]. Players control both Sam and David over the course of the mystery[^ref-16].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The PC version is played entirely with the mouse, using a contextual cursor that changes to actions such as "Talk to..." or "Pick up..." depending on the object beneath it; item labels can be toggled at any time[^ref-6]. Pressing the space bar displays all of a room's hotspots, though Adventure Gamers found that this "sometimes responds sluggishly and sometimes not at all"[^ref-1][^ref-19]. A 2006 Adventure Gamers preview reported that the game would avoid pixel-hunting[^ref-18]. On Xbox 360, interaction uses a radial menu of the nearest hotspots, which GameSpot found "fiddly"[^ref-14].

### Structure and Progression

The game is divided into eight chapters, a structure Wikipedia compares to The Beast Within; the other Gabriel Knight games were divided into "days"[^ref-6][^ref-22]. The player controls Dr. Styles in chapters 3, 5 and 7, and Sam in the rest[^ref-6]. Each chapter has required objectives with progress bars, plus optional bonus objectives; Adventure Gamers noted that the "bonus" slider brings no payoff "beyond personal satisfaction"[^ref-6][^ref-1]. A quick travel map shows locations in gold when main tasks remain there, silver for optional tasks, and gray once everything is done[^ref-1].

### Magic Tricks

As an aspiring magician, Sam often adapts tricks from her magician's handbook to the situation at hand, from creating misdirection to using gimmicks or planting objects through sleight of hand. If a step is performed incorrectly, the player can redo the trick from the last correct step[^ref-6]. Adventure Gamers was unimpressed: the book "conveniently contains directions for every trick she ends up having to do," the game "won't let you try the wrong trick on someone," and execution "requires little thought from the player"[^ref-1].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Puzzles include riddles, word games, visual puzzles, mazes and magic tricks[^ref-6]. Two scavenger hunts set by the Daedalus Club each stretch over two chapters and focus on traditional riddles such as rebuses and word play[^ref-21]. Opinions on difficulty varied. Grave Noise's Xbox 360 review found that "many of the puzzles can leave you stumped for long periods of time"[^ref-17], while GameSpot's Jane Douglas wrote that "until near the end of the game, though, no puzzle is really taxing or intricate" and that the puzzles "could stand some more flair and imagination"[^ref-22].

### Critical Consensus

Metacritic aggregated the PC version to a score of 72/100 based on 27 critic reviews, which it classifies as "mixed or average"[^ref-7], while MobyGames shows a critics score of 78%[^ref-30]. According to Wikipedia, most criticism was directed at the quality of the cutscenes, which reviewers considered out of keeping with the quality of the rendered graphics[^ref-6].

IGN's Nathan Meunier scored it 7.5/10. His verdict praised its "excellent visual flair, interesting characters, and creative storytelling," but said that the fact its puzzles "are one of its less impressive aspects is a major disappointment"[^ref-23].

### Modern Assessment

Eurogamer, reviewing the Xbox 360 version, wrote that the game is "ambitious, literate and unusual. Sadly, it's also compromised, unconvincing and often dull," and concluded that "the sheer difficulty in holding this wayward enterprise together, however, appears to have been too much"[^ref-31][^ref-6].

PopMatters gave the game a notably low 4/10[^ref-32], at the negative end of critical opinion. VGChartz users give the PC version an average community rating of 8[^ref-33].

## Development

### Origins

According to Jensen, the project "began with a Canadian publisher who approached me about doing an adventure game." She developed the game design bible and a producer was assigned, "but the company had financial issues and the project never got a team"[^ref-35]. Under the working title "Project Jane-J," the game was announced at E3 2003 through The Adventure Company[^ref-21]. MobyGames' trivia notes that production for its planned 2004 release "stalled over fears that big budget adventure games were no longer financially viable"[^ref-4]. Jensen summarized it this way: "The first publisher ended up dropping the project due to lack of funding"[^ref-15].

### The Long Road to Release

The game was picked up in 2006 by dtp entertainment's publishing label ANACONDA for a slated late 2007/early 2008 release[^ref-4]. In a January 2007 GameSpot interview, Jensen described it as the start of a new series of adventures that "could go on for many, many episodes"[^ref-9]. Development was initially outsourced to Tonuzaba in Hungary[^ref-21]. In 2008 it was transferred to French studio Wizarbox, delaying release further[^ref-36][^ref-6]. Jensen later said: "with the second publisher we had a development team change which really set the project back"[^ref-15].

### Voice Production

English voice recording was complete by October 2009. Jensen supervised the sessions, which were directed by Lorelei King[^ref-37]. Steven Pacey voices Dr. David Styles and Philippa Alexander voices Samantha Everett. The press release also named the supporting cast as "Adrienne Poster, Rupert Degas and Sacha Dahwan"[^ref-37].

Reviewers were critical of the voice work. Choicest Games found that "the emphasis is always placed on the wrong words"[^ref-2], and Eurogamer described "stilted voice acting"[^ref-31]. Adventure Gamers felt that otherwise well-written dialogue "falls flat in its delivery" because of the characters' lack of animation[^ref-1].

### Music and Sound

The soundtrack was composed by Robert Holmes, Jane Jensen's husband, who also wrote the Gabriel Knight scores[^ref-1][^ref-38]. Vocal tracks are performed by The Scarlet Furies, an indie band whose members include Holmes and his daughter Raleigh Holmes[^ref-1][^ref-38]. Adventure Gamers singled out the band's songs, especially the lead track "Safe in Arms"[^ref-1].

### Technical Achievements

Gray Matter combines static, prerendered Oxford environments with 3D character models[^ref-22][^ref-1]. Choicest Games called the in-game graphics "actually pretty good - the best I've seen in a point 'n' click adventure game." It criticized the cutscenes, however, which do not use the in-game engine[^ref-2]. Adventure Gamers described the dozen or so cinematics as a stylized cross "between a graphic novel illustration and a watercolor painting," and suggested that "budget may have been an issue"[^ref-1].

## Legacy

### Platform Availability

Gray Matter was released for Windows and Xbox 360[^ref-6]. Viva Media handled North American distribution[^ref-3], and Noviy Disk is also listed as a publisher[^ref-41]. A Steam release followed on April 1, 2014, and the game remains on sale there[^ref-10]. It was also sold on GOG.com but has since been delisted[^ref-42].

### Sequel

Jensen had envisioned a long series[^ref-9], but no sequel has been released. Gray Matter 2 was one of three options offered to backers of Jensen's 2012 Pinkerton Road Kickstarter campaign, and the backers chose Moebius instead[^ref-4][^ref-45]. In a 2012 interview, Jensen allowed that if hope for Gray Matter 2 or Gabriel Knight 4 were ever lost, "it would be a small mercy" to release their stories anyway[^ref-45].

### Jane Jensen's Continuing Work

On April 5, 2012, Jensen and Robert Holmes announced Pinkerton Road Studio and launched a Kickstarter campaign to fund its first year of development[^ref-46]. In 2014 the studio released Moebius: Empire Rising and Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers 20th Anniversary Edition, but sales were too poor for the company to continue[^ref-46].
