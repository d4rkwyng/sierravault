# Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

**Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller** is a four-part episodic point-and-click adventure game developed by [[Phoenix Online Studios]] and released beginning in 2012.[^ref-1] The game follows FBI agent Erica Reed, who possesses supernatural psychic abilities that allow her to see into the past by touching objects.[^ref-2] Alternative Magazine Online called the first episode "a good old-fashioned adventure game like they used to make"[^ref-3], and the game was created with story consultation from renowned adventure game designer [[Jane Jensen]], known for her work on the [[1993 - Gabriel Knight - Sins of the Fathers|Gabriel Knight]] series.[^ref-4]

The game was Phoenix Online Studios' first commercial project[^ref-4] and was funded through a 2011 Kickstarter campaign that raised $34,247 from 634 backers against a $25,000 goal.[^ref-1][^ref-27] Adventure Classic Gaming called it "among the earliest adventure game projects to be successfully funded on Kickstarter."[^ref-4] Each episode functions as a self-contained investigation into a serial killer while advancing the larger story of who killed Erica's brother.[^ref-7] One Metacritic user review called it "a gorgeous adventure in the spirit of genre classics like Gabriel Knight, Blade Runner and Gray Matter."[^ref-8]

## Story Summary

The game centers on Erica Reed, an FBI agent working in Boston who specializes in serial murder cases.[^ref-4] Erica possesses a rare psychic gift that allows her to see certain past events when she touches or focuses on objects.[^ref-2][^ref-12] As the game's description states: "FBI agent Erica Reed has an ability to see certain past events. A man with dark secrets has been murdered by hanging. A hidden message at the crime scene leads Erica to believe that the serial killer who killed her brother isn't dead."[^ref-13]

Throughout the episodic series, Erica learns to control three distinct psychic powers: cognition, projection, and regression.[^ref-4] God is a Geek described "an uncomfortably dark narrative"[^ref-14] centered on her investigations and her personal trauma. Each episode presents a different serial killer case while building toward the resolution of the central mystery surrounding her brother's death.[^ref-1][^ref-7]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Cognition is a point-and-click adventure played mostly in third person, with certain sequences in first person.[^ref-1][^ref-5] It can be played with the mouse or the keyboard.[^ref-18] Players control Erica as she investigates crime scenes and interacts with various characters throughout Boston.[^ref-5]

Cutscenes are presented in a graphic-novel style; Hardcore Gaming 101 wrote that "it feels like you're flipping through a comic book and playing what's on the page."[^ref-5]

### Psychic Powers

Erica's supernatural abilities serve as both investigation tools and puzzle-solving mechanisms. Adventure Classic Gaming names her three main powers as cognition, projection, and regression[^ref-4]; Wikipedia describes how she uses them:

| Power | Function |
|-------|----------|
| **Cognition** | See past events by touching objects[^ref-1][^ref-4] |
| **Regression** | Help someone recover a long-forgotten memory[^ref-1] |
| **Projection** | Named by Adventure Classic Gaming; its function is not described in the sources used here[^ref-4] |
| **Synergy** | Combine several objects to see a vision of a scene where they were together (introduced in Episode 2)[^ref-1] |

These powers allow players to reconstruct crime scenes and uncover hidden clues by interacting with relevant objects.[^ref-5][^ref-4] A Spanish walkthrough site describes them as "una ventaja a la hora de investigar pero una maldición" ("an advantage when investigating, but a curse").[^ref-18]

### Structure and Progression

God is a Geek put the first episode at "around 5 hours of straightforward gameplay"[^ref-14], and Wikipedia gives around 3–6 hours per episode.[^ref-1] Each episode functions as a complete investigative story while contributing to the overall narrative arc. According to Hardcore Gaming 101, the player can make choices at certain points that alter the path of the story and make parts of the game easier or harder, though each episode ends the same way.[^ref-5]

Adventure Gamers noted the game's built-in hint system, presented as a text-message conversation with Erica's father, and an option to highlight inventory items that can be combined.[^ref-19]

### Modern Assessment

The Game of the Year Edition's MobyGames entry shows a 90% critic average, based on a single rating.[^ref-21] Alternative Magazine Online described the series across its run: Episode 1 as "a good old-fashioned adventure game like they used to make," Episode 2 as "a masterfully woven point-and-click adventure thriller," and Episode 3 as "a potent murder mystery where questions continue to arise."[^ref-3]

Adventure Gamers concluded that "though flawed, the first episode of Cognition leaves a great final impression and sets up a series that could become a major achievement for episodic adventure gaming."[^ref-19] A Metacritic user review calling it "a gorgeous adventure in the spirit of genre classics like Gabriel Knight, Blade Runner and Gray Matter" captures the game's appeal to classic adventure fans.[^ref-8]

However, some criticism emerged. GameCritics wrote that "Cognition fails not from a lack of execution, but from a lack of imagination."[^ref-8] On release, reviewers pointed out graphical issues such as clipping and erratic walking animations, which were later patched.[^ref-1] Arcade Sushi's review of the iOS port cited graphical glitches, disappearing characters and muddy controls.[^ref-7]

## Development

### Origins

Cognition was Phoenix Online Studios' first commercial game project; the studio was previously best known for *The Silver Lining*, a freeware fan game based on Sierra's King's Quest series.[^ref-4] The studio recruited [[Jane Jensen]] as story consultant for the project.[^ref-1][^ref-4]

The game was funded through a 2011 Kickstarter campaign that raised $34,247 from 634 backers against a $25,000 goal.[^ref-1][^ref-27]

### Production

Following the campaign, the team developed the game using the Unity engine, with Skype and Dropbox used for communication within the team.[^ref-1] The store description of the iOS release calls the team an "indie dream team."[^ref-12] César Bittar is credited as director, producer, designer and writer.[^ref-34][^ref-9]

Voice acting was led by Raleigh Holmes as Erica Reed, praised for her "authentic Bostonian twang."[^ref-3] Raleigh Holmes is the daughter of Robert Holmes, Jane Jensen's husband.[^ref-3][^ref-4][^ref-20]

**Music:**
The score is by Austin Haynes, with additional music by Matthew Florianz; the song "The Taking" was written by Robert Holmes and Raleigh Holmes and performed by Raleigh Holmes.[^ref-34][^ref-13] The Phoenix Online Store describes the soundtrack: "From tense shootouts to melancholy moments, the atmospheric music of Austin Haynes sets the mood for Cognition: An Erica Reed Thriller."[^ref-25]

### Technical Notes

Cognition was developed using the Unity engine.[^ref-1] All four episodes were released for Windows and OS X, and the first two were also released for the iPad.[^ref-1] A Game of the Year Edition for PC followed in September 2013[^ref-21], and an iOS and Android release came in February 2018.[^ref-33][^ref-5]

## Legacy

During the online release party for the final episode, Phoenix suggested that a second season was planned.[^ref-1]

The Steam store description states that the game won six 2013 Reader's Choice Aggie Awards, including Game of the Year.[^ref-32] The game remains available through digital distribution platforms.[^ref-32]

### Sierra Connection

Cognition represents an important spiritual successor to Sierra's classic adventure games, particularly the [[1993 - Gabriel Knight - Sins of the Fathers|Gabriel Knight]] series. With Jane Jensen as story consultant and themes echoing Gabriel Knight's supernatural investigations, the game carries forward Sierra's legacy of narrative-driven adventure gaming. Phoenix Online Studios was previously known for *The Silver Lining*, a freeware fan game based on Sierra's King's Quest series, further cementing the Sierra connection.[^ref-4]
