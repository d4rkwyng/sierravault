# Bolt Riley: A Reggae Adventure

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

Bolt Riley: A Reggae Adventure is a classic 2D point-and-click adventure game co-designed by [[Corey Cole]] and [[Lori Ann Cole]], the creators of Sierra's Quest for Glory series[^ref-1]. It was created by Oded Sharon and his Tel Aviv studio Adventure Mob[^ref-26], and Steam credits both Adventure Mob and Sharon's earlier company Corbomite Games as developer and publisher[^ref-1]. Players take the role of Bolt Riley, a poor boy from Trenchtown, Jamaica, on his pathway to stardom[^ref-1]. Chapter 1 entered Early Access on April 20, 2016[^ref-26] and was released on February 8, 2018[^ref-1], after a development cycle that included two Kickstarter campaigns and significant financial challenges[^ref-28].

The game brings together several strands of Sierra history. Adventure Mob was originally slated to build Replay Games' Leisure Suit Larry remake in Unity before Replay switched to N-Fusion Interactive in 2012[^ref-27]; that remake became *Leisure Suit Larry: Reloaded*[^ref-4]. Bolt Riley was Adventure Mob's own original project. As Oded Sharon explained: "I've been a fan of their work forever. I was looking for experienced designers when we began work on Bolt Riley. My friend Noah Falstien introduced me to them. They were in on the game's design from day one and they are in charge of about 1/3 of the puzzles and dialog of the full game."[^ref-6]

The game features hand-drawn 2D background artwork and hand-drawn cel animation, combining all the elements of a classic point-and-click adventure with "funny dialog puzzles, exploration, wacky inventory items, and of course, a rope."[^ref-1] The first Kickstarter campaign, for $120,000, failed[^ref-2]; a shorter second campaign for a single chapter succeeded with 757 backers[^ref-25].

## Story Summary

Bolt Riley takes place in 1970s Jamaica, following a young man growing up in a very poor area of Kingston[^ref-26]; he is "a poor boy from Trenchtown"[^ref-1]. Bolt is struggling to survive and has to deal with a gang of Rude Boys[^ref-1]. As Oded Sharon described the character: "He's a good boy who grew up in a bad environment. He doesn't want to be part of a world of crime and throughout the game he realises he wants to escape it and become the good person he is."[^ref-6]

The story follows Bolt on a journey of self-discovery, love, friendship, and inspiration in which he is introduced to reggae music[^ref-1]. Players guide Bolt as he forms his band, gets inspiration, writes and produces songs, and then performs them[^ref-1]. Rather than joining the gang members who pressure him toward crime, Bolt chooses a path of peace, helping his fellow townsfolk, reuniting families, and bringing joy and harmony to his neighbors[^ref-26]. The story was planned across chapters, each focusing on a different aspect of Bolt's passage into becoming a reggae legend[^ref-1].

"This is the story of the underdog who breaks out and becomes a worldwide sensation," the store description declares[^ref-1]. GamesBeat observed that the game appears to mirror the life of Bob Marley, though the story makes no reference to him[^ref-26]. Trenchtown itself is the birthplace of rocksteady and reggae and the home of Bob Marley[^ref-13]. The art team researched 1970s Jamaica from coffee table books "with tons of pictures"; a VW van in the game is inspired by pictures of Bob Marley's actual car[^ref-25].

Bolt can use special "Inspirational powers" he learned during his journeys[^ref-1]; Sharon described him as driven "by his passion and inspirational abilities"[^ref-6].

## Development

### Origins

Oded Sharon is the CEO of Tel Aviv-based Adventure Mob[^ref-6]. In 2007 he started Corbomite Games, which made games including Zbang, Pizza Morgana, and Star Shipping Inc.[^ref-6] Adventure Mob later created the first demo for Leisure Suit Larry[^ref-6]; Sharon's Kickstarter biography says the studio "created a unique framework that help us make Adventure games in Unity3D"[^ref-2].

Replay Games raised more than $600,000 on Kickstarter to remake Leisure Suit Larry[^ref-27]. As GamesBeat reported in an exclusive in July 2012: "The plan was to have Adventure Mob, a Tel Aviv-based game studio, build a cross-platform game using the Unity Technologies game engine. But now the company will use N-Fusion Interactive, a Manalapan, N.J.-based game studio"[^ref-27]. The report gave no reason for the switch beyond Replay founder Paul Trowe calling it "a good thing for us"[^ref-27].

Sharon had always wanted to create games: "I got my first computer at the age of 6, which was an Apple. I learned coding in Logo and Basic. I made a text adventure when I was 10 years old. I've always loved adventure games – Monkey Island and Quest for Glory are my favourites."[^ref-6]

### The Cole Connection

Game designer Noah Falstein introduced Oded Sharon to Corey and Lori Cole[^ref-6]. In a November 2013 Reddit AMA with the Coles, Sharon wrote: "Two years ago we started collaborating to make a new game called Bolt Riley"[^ref-14], which would put the start of the collaboration around late 2011.

According to Sharon, "They were in on the game's design from day one and they are in charge of about 1/3 of the puzzles and dialog of the full game."[^ref-6] Rock Paper Shotgun noted that "the veteran pair designed Bolt Riley's second chapter, and seem to be generally collaborating"[^ref-5]. GamesBeat later described the Coles as having taken part "as design consultants on this project"[^ref-26].

The collaboration ran while the Coles were developing their own crowdfunded project, Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption. Rock Paper Shotgun observed: "Clearly this is primarily the project of Adventure Mob, with the Coles focused on Hero-U," adding that the developers were "hanging their Kickstarter on the Coles' involvement"[^ref-5].

### Kickstarter Campaigns

The first Kickstarter campaign ran from October 25 to November 25, 2013, seeking $120,000[^ref-2]. It failed, with $51,668 pledged by 1,082 backers[^ref-2].

As Sharon explained the financial situation, there were "forces beyond my control, like the initial investors who put in the $320k and want to see their money back. I managed to convince them to let us have one final shot to save the game and that was the Kickstarter."[^ref-6] By August 2014, Adventure Mob staff estimated the company had spent $300,000 on the game[^ref-25].

A second campaign in August 2014 ran for only a week and asked for a single chapter[^ref-25]. Its smaller goal was possible because the team could tap OUYA's Free the Games Fund for additional funding[^ref-22]. It ended with $32,104 pledged against a $31,668 goal from 757 backers[^ref-25]. Sharon said of the finish: "We got about 30 percent of the funds in the first two days... All together, that seemed to work: We got to 757 people who believed in us in that short time frame."[^ref-25]

### Technical Achievements

Adventure Mob built Bolt Riley in Unity 3D[^ref-6]. As Sharon explained: "We want the game to be everywhere and moreover that you can start playing it on one platform and finish it on another. That includes desktop, mobile and even consoles"[^ref-6]. In 2014, Sharon planned to distribute the game on Ouya first and then on desktop and tablets[^ref-25]; MobyGames lists an Ouya release in September 2016 and Windows, Mac, Linux, iPhone, iPad and Android releases on February 8, 2018[^ref-9].

The game was built with the Adventure Creator and Dialog System plugins for Unity; by 2018 Sharon wrote that the code needed upgrading to Unity 2017 or 2018 "and an additional upgrade to Adventure Creator, Dialog System and other plugins will be required"[^ref-28]. The Steam store notes that the game "is not compatible with macOS 10.15 Catalina or above"[^ref-1].

The art was made in-house by artists who were all graduates of Bezalel, "the leading school for art and design in Israel"[^ref-6]. GamesBeat identified Oran Bar-Tal as art director, and Sharon compared the style to the TV show Archer[^ref-25]. Commenting on Rock Paper Shotgun in 2013, Sharon said the team "had 10 of the most talented 2d graphic artists in Israel"[^ref-5].

### Music and Audio

Music plays a central role in Bolt Riley, both thematically and mechanically. All the music in the game was composed especially for it[^ref-1]; in 2013 Sharon said Omri Lahav "will score the rest of the soundtrack" alongside songs from other musicians[^ref-6]. "There are a few puzzles in the game that revolve around composing songs and music," Sharon said. "Imagine using point-and-click adventure mechanics (puzzles, dialogs, inventory, etc.) to make music. It's pretty neat. We're also working on something inspired by how the music worked in Monkey Island 2 with their iMuse system."[^ref-6]

The voice cast includes[^ref-26]:
- Jason Linere White as Bolt Riley
- Anthony Sardinha (also known for The Journey Down)
- Jesse Lowther as the narrator

Sharon's connection to The Journey Down developers Skygoblin helped bring in Sardinha: "I know the folks at Skygoblin who made The Journey Down. They are good people. I contacted Anthony a long while ago and he was very nice and lent us his voice for the materials you hear in the campaign, including the trailer."[^ref-6]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Bolt Riley uses a traditional point-and-click interface[^ref-1], built in Unity with the Adventure Creator plugin[^ref-28]. Sharon intended it to be cross-platform, so players could "start playing it on one platform and finish it on another"[^ref-6].

### Structure and Progression

The game was planned as an episodic adventure, with each chapter focusing on a different aspect of Bolt's journey to reggae stardom[^ref-1]. Rock Paper Shotgun reported three planned chapters, each stand-alone[^ref-5]; by 2018 Sharon wanted the whole story told in "either 2 or 3 chapters"[^ref-28]. Chapter 2 was planned with almost all new screens and characters, including the Trench Town market, a local DJ and a back alley, while Chapter 3 would revisit earlier scenes with new goals, puzzles and dialog[^ref-28].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Bolt Riley features classic adventure game puzzle design, including:
- **Inventory puzzles:** "wacky inventory items, and of course, a rope"[^ref-1]
- **Dialogue puzzles:** "funny dialog puzzles"[^ref-1]
- **Environmental exploration:** Settings in 1970s Kingston drawn from period photo research[^ref-25][^ref-26]
- **Inspirational powers:** Special powers Bolt learned during his journeys[^ref-1]
- **Music puzzles:** A few puzzles revolve around composing songs and music[^ref-6]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Bolt Riley: A Reggae Adventure received little critical coverage. Metacritic lists no critic reviews for the game[^ref-29]. Rock Paper Shotgun's preview called the project "an interesting team-up"[^ref-5].

Chapter 1 debuted as an Early Access title on April 20, 2016, on Steam, the Humble Store, and Razer ForgeTV[^ref-26]. In February 2018 Sharon released iOS and Android versions and took the game out of Early Access on Steam[^ref-22]. In a February 2018 Patreon post, Sharon reported combined net revenue from Chapter 1 sales of $1,835, and said that, with no steady income, "every single person who worked for Adventure Mob is currently busy with other projects."[^ref-28] He estimated that $42,000 was needed to complete Chapter 2 and proposed funding it through Patreon[^ref-28][^ref-22].

### Modern Assessment

Steam user reviews are mixed. Players praise the art and music, but many criticize how short and incomplete Chapter 1 is[^ref-1].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **Steam User Reviews:** "Mixed": 41% of 24 user reviews positive on the store page (checked 2026-10-09)[^ref-1]
- **Metacritic:** No critic reviews listed[^ref-29]
- **Episodic completion:** Only Chapter 1 of a planned two- or three-chapter story was released[^ref-28]
- **Crowdfunding:** First campaign failed ($51,668 of $120,000)[^ref-2]; second succeeded ($32,104 of $31,668, 757 backers)[^ref-25]

## Legacy

Bolt Riley is a footnote in the history of Sierra alumni projects. The Coles are best known for Quest for Glory and their later Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption, and Bolt Riley shows them collaborating on a project outside their own studio while Hero-U was in development[^ref-5].

The game also illustrates the challenges facing crowdfunded adventure games in the 2010s. Despite having experienced designers attached and a distinctive cultural setting, Bolt Riley failed in its first Kickstarter, barely made its second, and earned too little from Chapter 1 to fund further chapters[^ref-28]. The project's history ran from Adventure Mob's early work on the Leisure Suit Larry remake[^ref-27], through two Kickstarter campaigns, to the release of only Chapter 1.

Adventure Mob aimed for a cross-platform release on desktop, mobile and consoles[^ref-6]. Its hand-drawn art and specially composed music remain the game's most distinctive features[^ref-1].
