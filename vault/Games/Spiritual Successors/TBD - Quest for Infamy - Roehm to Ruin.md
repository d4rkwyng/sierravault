# Quest for Infamy: Roehm to Ruin

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

*Quest for Infamy: Roehm to Ruin* is a point-and-click adventure RPG developed by [[Infamous Quests]] serving as a prequel to their 2014 title *Quest for Infamy*.[^ref-1] The game follows the story behind protagonist Mr. Roehm's escape from the Baron's Castle, exploring the fateful events that led him to flee to the Valley of Krasna where the original game begins.[^ref-2] In October 2025 Steven Alexander wrote that the game "has been completed for a while, except for some voice acting" for its lead, Mr. Roehm.[^ref-3]

The prequel was announced in March 2015 alongside *Order of the Thorne: The King's Challenge*,[^ref-7] and both games were funded by a dual Kickstarter campaign (April 3 – May 3, 2015) that raised $30,943 against an $11,225 goal from 836 backers.[^ref-4] According to the campaign pitch, *Roehm to Ruin* "was born out of an Adventure Game Jam in 2014".[^ref-15] The original game was inspired by Sierra's *Quest for Glory* series and cast the player as an anti-hero; the prequel keeps the same rogue protagonist, Roehm.[^ref-5] The game promises multiple story paths with different endings and hidden secrets, offering meaningful choices that affect the storyline.[^ref-6][^ref-17]

## Story Summary

The game serves as a direct prequel to *Quest for Infamy*, revealing what transpired on the night that forced Roehm to flee to the Valley of Krasna.[^ref-1] After hearing of an illustrious gathering of wealthy and rich individuals at the Baron's castle, Roehm decides to crash the party and try to swindle a few valuables and goodies for himself.[^ref-2] However, he ends up getting more than he bargained for, setting in motion the events that would lead to his infamous adventures.[^ref-1]

The prequel tells of the "fateful night he was caught in the Baron's castle before he escaped to the Valley of Krasna";[^ref-8] *Hardcore Gaming 101* describes it as the story of "Roehm's ill-fated affair with a baron's daughter" that led him to Krasna.[^ref-5]

True to the series' emphasis on player agency, the prequel features multiple story paths with different endings depending on the choices players make throughout the adventure.[^ref-6] The store page also promises "lots of hidden secrets and special bonuses".[^ref-6]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

*Roehm to Ruin* utilizes traditional point-and-click adventure mechanics consistent with the original *Quest for Infamy*.[^ref-2] The game is built using Adventure Game Studio (AGS),[^ref-3] the same engine that powered the first game in the series.[^ref-7] The King's Quest Omnipedia notes that the prequel "plays more as a traditional KQ-style adventure game".[^ref-9]

### Structure and Progression

The game takes place primarily within and around the Baron's Castle during a single eventful night.[^ref-1] As a prequel focused on a specific incident, *Roehm to Ruin* is described as a smaller, more focused experience compared to "the sprawling epic that was the original Quest for Infamy."[^ref-3]

- **The Party:** Roehm infiltrates the Baron's gathering of wealthy nobles[^ref-2]
- **The Heist:** Attempts to swindle valuables from the partygoers[^ref-1]
- **The Escape:** Events spiral, forcing Roehm to flee the castle[^ref-8]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The game features meaningful choices that affect the storyline, offering multiple paths through the adventure.[^ref-6] Like the original *Quest for Infamy*, which allowed players to choose between brigand, rogue, or sorcerer classes with different puzzle solutions, the prequel maintains this emphasis on player agency and replayability.[^ref-10] The developer promises hidden secrets and special bonuses to find.[^ref-6]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

As of October 2026, *Quest for Infamy: Roehm to Ruin* has not been released; its itch.io page still reads "Coming in 2023" with the status "In development".[^ref-6] A playable demo was available at the start of the 2015 Kickstarter campaign, allowing potential backers to sample the prequel.[^ref-11]

The game was featured at Gamescom 2015, where it received coverage from Adventure-Treff during the German gaming convention.[^ref-2]

### Modern Assessment

The original *Quest for Infamy* received mixed to positive reception, which provides context for expectations surrounding the prequel.[^ref-7] Adventure Gamers gave the first game 3 stars, describing it as "a qualified success; the positive aspects still outnumber the negative, but the weaknesses noticeably hinder the experience."[^ref-10]

## Development

### Origins

*Quest for Infamy: Roehm to Ruin* was announced in March 2015 alongside *Order of the Thorne: The King's Challenge*, ahead of a dual-project Kickstarter campaign.[^ref-7] The campaign pitch says the prequel "was born out of an Adventure Game Jam in 2014" and that Infamous Quests was turning it into a deluxe version "complete with soundtrack and full voice acting".[^ref-15] The prequel concept emerged from the desire to explore the backstory hinted at in the original game, where Roehm arrives in the Valley of Krasna as a fugitive with an unexplained past.[^ref-1]

Infamous Quests was founded in 2012 by Steven Alexander and Shawn Mills, who previously operated Infamous Adventures, an amateur game development company that created free remakes of classic Sierra Entertainment adventure games from the early 1990s.[^ref-7] They formed the new company specifically to separate their commercial endeavors from their fan-remake work.[^ref-7]

### Production

The Kickstarter campaign launched on April 3, 2015, seeking $11,225 to fund both *Roehm to Ruin* and *Order of the Thorne: The King's Challenge*.[^ref-4] The campaign was funded quickly, eventually raising $30,943 from 836 backers by its conclusion on May 3, 2015.[^ref-4] The game was also announced via the Steam community for *Quest for Infamy* on April 7, 2015.[^ref-12]

Development proved challenging over the years. According to a 2025 update from the developer, the financial turnout on *Order of the Thorne: The King's Challenge* fell well under expectations, preventing the team from continuing as a full-time game development company.[^ref-3] Most team members moved on to other jobs, with some doing occasional work when possible, while others gradually lost contact with the project.[^ref-3] Steven Alexander continued working on the games independently when time permitted.[^ref-3]

By late 2025, the game was described as "completed for a while, except for some voice acting."[^ref-3] The remaining work involves completing recording sessions with the lead actor for Mr. Roehm, editing the voice lines, implementing them into AGS, conducting final testing, and releasing the game.[^ref-3]

### Technical Achievements

The game is built in Adventure Game Studio (AGS); Alexander's October 2025 update describes the remaining work as editing the final voice lines and "implementing them into AGS".[^ref-3] AGS was also the engine of the original *Quest for Infamy*.[^ref-7]

### Technical Issues

Development delays have been primarily attributed to resource constraints rather than technical problems.[^ref-3] The main remaining obstacle is scheduling recording sessions with the voice actor for Mr. Roehm, finding appropriate recording space, and securing funding for the sessions.[^ref-3]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- The original *Quest for Infamy* Kickstarter campaign raised $63,281, significantly more than the dual campaign's goal of $11,225.[^ref-7]
- *Quest for Infamy* was included in Time Magazine's "20 Video Games To Watch for Summer 2014" list prior to its release.[^ref-7]
- The developers previously created free remakes of *Space Quest II* and *King's Quest III* as Infamous Adventures before forming their commercial studio.[^ref-5]
- The character of Roehm was deliberately designed as an anti-hero rather than a villain, inspired by characters like Han Solo.[^ref-5]

### Multiple Endings

The game features multiple story paths with different endings based on player choices throughout the adventure.[^ref-6] The developer also promises "lots of hidden secrets and special bonuses" to find.[^ref-6]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

The Kickstarter campaign for *Roehm to Ruin* and *Order of the Thorne* demonstrated continued fan interest in Infamous Quests' Sierra-inspired adventure games, raising nearly three times the requested funding goal.[^ref-4] The campaign was described as "already well over halfway funded" shortly after launch, indicating strong initial support from the adventure gaming community.[^ref-11]

### Collections

*Roehm to Ruin* is part of the *Quest for Infamy* series, which also includes the original 2014 game.[^ref-1] The game exists within a broader catalog of Infamous Quests releases that includes the *Order of the Thorne* anthology series and the collaborative free game *Serena*.[^ref-2]

### Fan Projects

The game itself represents the continuation of a tradition rooted in fan development. Infamous Quests emerged from Infamous Adventures, which created fan remakes of classic Sierra titles.[^ref-5][^ref-7] The developers were active in the Tierra (later AGD Interactive) fan game community during the mid-2000s, participating in forums dedicated to creating adventures in the spirit of Sierra's classic games.[^ref-5]

### Related Publications

- **Kickstarter Demo:** A playable demo was provided at the start of the 2015 campaign to showcase the game's direction[^ref-11]
- **Patreon Updates:** Development progress has been shared with supporters through Patreon posts[^ref-3]

### Critical Perspective

*Quest for Infamy: Roehm to Ruin* represents an interesting case study in independent adventure game development. The extended development timeline—from 2015 announcement to a still-unscheduled release—illustrates the challenges facing small studios attempting to maintain commercial viability while delivering on crowdfunded promises.[^ref-3]

The King's Quest Omnipedia lists the series among its "King's Quest Style Games".[^ref-9] Reporting on the 2015 campaign, Cliqist's Marcus Estrada wrote that with the original *Quest for Infamy*, Infamous Quests "really channeled those influences and created a surprisingly authentic-feeling new title".[^ref-11] The series casts the player as an anti-hero rather than a traditional hero; Alexander has described Roehm as modelled on characters like Han Solo, "not a goody two shoes hero type".[^ref-5]
