# Hoyle Casino

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Casino is a 1996 casino simulation developed and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] for Windows[^ref-10]. It was the first game in the Hoyle Casino spin-off of Sierra's Hoyle's Official Book of Games card-game series, a line of virtual-casino games that ran from 1996 to 2016[^ref-2][^ref-5]. Sierra released it in December 1996, following Hoyle Solitaire and Hoyle Blackjack earlier that year[^ref-55][^ref-54].

The game puts the player in a Vegas casino with $5,000 to spend. It offers blackjack, craps, roulette, slot machines and several kinds of poker against computer opponents who comment on the play[^ref-10][^ref-53]. Several of these opponents came from Hoyle Blackjack[^ref-57]. GameSpot rated it 8.2 out of 10[^ref-53].

The Hoyle name comes from Edmond Hoyle, an 18th-century English writer on card games. His writings on whist gave rise to the phrase "according to Hoyle," meaning "full compliance with universally accepted rules and customs"[^ref-4].

## Story Summary

Hoyle Casino has no story. Instead, it simulates a trip to a casino without the financial risk[^ref-9]. You start with $5,000 and try to build up your winnings. If you lose everything, you can borrow from loan sharks[^ref-10]. GameSpot's reviewer summed up the premise as a "fully stocked game parlor with 5,000 smackers to spend away as you please"[^ref-53].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game is played with the mouse. According to GameSpot, "you can wander around the casino point-and-click fashion to choose a table or simply select a game from a pull-down menu"[^ref-53]. A GameFAQs user review of the Macintosh release says the keyboard is needed only to enter amounts at the casino's ATM[^ref-9]. MobyGames lists the perspective as first-person[^ref-10].

The computer players and croupiers comment on your play, and each has a distinct personality. A setting controls how talkative they are[^ref-10]. GameSpot noted that "you can just turn the attitude meter down and everyone will shut up"[^ref-53]. Sierra's own magazine said the opponents' skill levels could also be adjusted[^ref-57].

### Structure and Progression

The casino has five main games: roulette, slot machines, craps, a variety of poker games, and blackjack[^ref-53]. Sierra's Fall 1996 InterAction feature described them in more detail[^ref-57]:

- **Poker:** five versions, including Texas Hold'em, Seven-card Stud and Omaha Hold'em, along with Hi-Lo variations.
- **Roulette:** a fully rendered wheel, played under either Vegas or Atlantic City rules.
- **Slots:** a section of 30 themed slot machines in nickel, quarter and dollar denominations, including progressive quarter slots.
- **Blackjack and Craps.**

According to producer Steve Van Horn, poker in Hoyle Casino "is played strictly according to casino rules with fixed bets." He contrasted this with the standalone Hoyle Poker and Hoyle Blackjack, which had in-depth tutorials[^ref-59]. Hoyle Casino offered on-line help with the rules of each game instead[^ref-57].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

There are no puzzles. The money system is the game's main mechanic. An ATM in the lower-left corner of the screen lets you deposit or withdraw any amount. When you go broke, three loan sharks, including the Provoloni Brothers, offer credit[^ref-57]. The GameFAQs reviewer compared these loans to "the 3 continues you'd get in any other game"[^ref-9].

Players could also go online through Sierra's web site to play Poker or Blackjack against other people[^ref-58][^ref-57]. Van Horn called Internet play "a '90s twist on the old Friday night card game"[^ref-59].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Tim Soete reviewed the game for GameSpot in December 1996 and gave it 8.2 out of 10[^ref-53]. He praised its presentation: "Following in the tradition of the Sierra Hoyle series, everything from the roulette wheel to the craps dice has been rendered with the most tactile detail." He also liked the cast of gamblers, noting that "the gambling patrons and pit bosses you're often sharing a table with really add to the casino atmosphere." His one complaint was that "the title's only fault lies in the fact that it's just another casino game"[^ref-53].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 82% critic average, based on one review (GameSpot)[^ref-10]

### Modern Assessment

Retrospective coverage of the 1996 game is scarce. In a 2004 GameFAQs user review of the Macintosh release, the reviewer called it "a very fun little game" that could teach casino games "without it pinching you in the pocket." The same reviewer found the graphics decent but the characters "somewhat cheesy looking"[^ref-9].

## Development

### Origins

Sierra's Hoyle series began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games: Volume 1, designed and programmed by Warren Schwader in Sierra's SCI engine. Volume 1 had sold more than 250,000 copies by 1990[^ref-5]. Sierra relaunched the line in 1995 with Hoyle Classic Games, and the Sierra Chest notes that "Sierra characters were no longer included as opponents from here on"[^ref-55]. Hoyle Solitaire and Hoyle Blackjack followed in summer 1996, and Hoyle Casino followed in December[^ref-55].

### Production

InterAction's Holiday 1996 profile of the Hoyle team says producer Steve Van Horn was chosen in early 1995 to lead the relaunch of Hoyle Classic Games[^ref-59]. The same profile names these team members:

- Lead programmer Earl Malmrose
- Designer Jim Love
- Lead artist Rabih AbouJaoude and artist Heather Ivy, who drew the Casino characters

According to Van Horn, the team discussed each character design together and then tested its ideas with a focus group[^ref-59]. The article covers the Hoyle team as a whole, and MobyGames records no individual credits for Hoyle Casino[^ref-10].

In summer 1996, Sierra ran a "Design a Hoyle Casino Game Character" contest in InterAction. The grand prize was a trip to Las Vegas, and the winning entry could be included in the game[^ref-58]. The Fall 1996 issue reported that the Blackjack characters returned and that "the designers have added two more players — including one submitted by our Hoyle Casino Vegas Contest winner"[^ref-57].

### Cut Content

Sierra's Summer 1996 preview listed Baccarat among the games and promised "six gaming categories with 24 unique games"[^ref-58]. Neither Sierra's Fall 1996 feature nor GameSpot's review mentions Baccarat[^ref-57][^ref-53].

### Technical Specifications

- **Media:** CD-ROM[^ref-60]
- **Minimum CPU:** Intel i486[^ref-60]
- **Minimum RAM:** 4 MB[^ref-60]
- **Operating System:** Windows 3.1 or Windows 95[^ref-58]
- **Players:** 1 offline, plus Internet play for Poker and Blackjack[^ref-60][^ref-57]
- **ESRB:** Kids to Adults[^ref-60]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- **Sultan Fortune Teller:** The GameFAQs reviewer of the Macintosh release describes a Sultan machine that "will tell you something about the future" if you roll a quarter into its mouth. The reviewer compared it to the machine in the Tom Hanks film *Big*[^ref-9].
- **Brown & Bigelow License:** Sierra's Holiday 1996 InterAction notes that "Hoyle is used under license from Brown & Bigelow, Inc."[^ref-59]
- **Edmond Hoyle:** Hoyle (1671/72–1769) wrote *A Short Treatise on the Game of Whist* (1742), which went through 13 editions in his lifetime[^ref-4].

## Legacy

Hoyle Casino was re-released in 1997[^ref-56] and became the first entry in a virtual-casino series that ran until 2016[^ref-2]. Each later edition has its own page (see below).
