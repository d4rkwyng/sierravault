# Hoyle Slots and Video Poker 2000

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Slots and Video Poker 2000 is a casino game developed by Sierra On-Line and sold under the Sierra Attractions label[^ref-7][^ref-18]. Amazon's listing titles it "Hoyle Slots And Video Poker 2000 with Bonus Horse Racing (Win/Mac)"[^ref-3], and MobyGames catalogues it as Hoyle Slots & Video Poker, released in 2000 for Windows[^ref-4]. It belongs to Sierra's Hoyle line, which PCGamingWiki groups under Hoyle's Official Book of Games, a series that began with Volume 1 in 1989[^ref-8]. Wikipedia's Hoyle Casino article lists "Hoyle Slots & Video Poker (with Horse Racing)" among that series' spin-offs, dated 1999[^ref-9], while Sierra Chest records a first release of 2000 under the title "Hoyle Slots & Video Poker 2"[^ref-18].

The retail description promised "all the payoffs of casino Slots and video Poker without leaving home," with Las Vegas sights and sounds, quick hints and strategies, and a bonus Horse Racing game[^ref-3]. Amazon's product description gives the content as 275 variations of slots, "from classic one-armed bandits to modern video slots," and 44 variations of video poker including Joker Poker, Jacks or Better and Deuces Wild[^ref-3]. The US release came on a combined PC/Mac CD-ROM[^ref-18].

## Story Summary

As a casino game, Hoyle Slots and Video Poker 2000 has no narrative. The player starts with a stake, $5,000 by default though it can be changed, and plays the machines; when the money runs out, the game offers to reset the account and erase all statistics[^ref-4]. Only one person plays at a time, but the game supports multiple player identities and keeps a record of where each has won and lost money[^ref-4].

A bonus Horse Racing game is included alongside the slots and video poker[^ref-3][^ref-4].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames describes the game as single-player and mouse/keyboard controlled, with a first-person perspective[^ref-4]. The player inserts cash by clicking a machine's cash slot; each spin draws on the credits in the machine, and when those run out the machine takes money from the player's remaining cash pot. Winnings are added to the machine's credits, and the player may cash out at any time[^ref-4].

Amazon's listing adds that quick tips and hints give advice during play, and that a Practice Mode "alerts you to mistakes and shows you how to improve your strategy"[^ref-3].

### Structure and Progression

MobyGames describes no campaign or progression structure: the player chooses machines and stakes, and can cash out of a machine at any time[^ref-4]. Stakes are fixed per machine session; to change them, the player must cash out and start again at the higher setting[^ref-4].

- **Slot machines and video slots:** playable as 5¢, 10¢, 25¢, $1, $5, $10, $25, $50, $100, $500 or $1,000 machines[^ref-4]
- **Video poker:** playable as $1, $5, $25 or $100 machines[^ref-4]

The classic slots are multi-coin machines with a single pay line, apart from Progressive, which has three; on a $1,000 three-coin machine, a maximum bet costs $3,000 per spin[^ref-4]. Each video poker machine has its own payout characteristics. Hang 10, for example, pays 95%, with good payouts for a Full House and a Straight but a low payout for Two Pairs. Video poker payout rates run from 92% to 100%[^ref-4].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

There are no puzzles. Slot play is luck-based. Video poker involves hold/discard decisions, and the game's hints and Practice Mode are pitched at improving that strategy[^ref-3].

## Development

### Origins

The game is part of the Hoyle line that Sierra began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games: Volume 1[^ref-8]. Wikipedia classifies it as a spin-off of the Hoyle Casino series[^ref-9].

### Production

Sierra Chest and PCGamingWiki credit development to Sierra On-Line[^ref-7][^ref-8]. MobyGames lists Sierra On-Line, Inc. as publisher[^ref-4], while the US box carries the Sierra Attractions label[^ref-18]. No individual development credits have been documented.

According to the manual as quoted by MobyGames, the game can be played without the CD in the drive once installed, but "some features of the game are disabled", including "the horse racing announcer's speech and several of the animations in the various games"[^ref-4].

### Technical Achievements

No technical innovations are documented for this release. MobyGames hosts screenshots of the Windows version[^ref-21], and LaunchBox holds gameplay screenshots plus North American and UK packaging images[^ref-20].

### Technical Specifications

No system requirements for the original 2000 release were found. The requirements below come from Newegg's listing for the later Encore Software edition (first listed March 29, 2007) and may not match the original Sierra release[^ref-6]:

- **Resolution:** 640x480, 16-bit color
- **Media:** 4x CD-ROM drive
- **Disk Space:** 65 MB
- **RAM:** 16 MB (32 MB recommended)
- **Processor:** Pentium 133 MHz or faster
- **Operating Systems:** Windows 98SE/ME/2000 SP4/XP SP1
