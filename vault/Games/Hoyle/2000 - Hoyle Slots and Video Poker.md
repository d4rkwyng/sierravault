# Hoyle Slots and Video Poker 2000

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Slots & Video Poker is a casino game developed by Sierra On-Line and sold under the Sierra Attractions label[^ref-7][^ref-18]. It first went on sale in 1999: Sierra's own product page listed it in August 1999 as a "Win95/PMac CD" at $19.95, "available now"[^ref-22], and Chips & Bits mail-order ads in *Computer Games Strategy Plus* (August 1999) and *Computer Games* (December 1999) carried it at $19.99[^ref-24][^ref-25]. A revised v1.0.0.1 PC/Mac hybrid disc, with a disc volume dated February 2000, followed[^ref-28], and MobyGames records a UK release in 2000[^ref-4][^ref-30]. Amazon's listing titles it "Hoyle Slots And Video Poker 2000 with Bonus Horse Racing (Win/Mac)"[^ref-3]. It belongs to Sierra's Hoyle line, which PCGamingWiki groups under Hoyle's Official Book of Games, a series that began with Volume 1 in 1989[^ref-8]. Wikipedia's Hoyle Casino article lists "Hoyle Slots & Video Poker (with Horse Racing)" among that series' spin-offs, dated 1999[^ref-9], while Sierra Chest catalogues the v1.0.0.1 box under the title "Hoyle Slots & Video Poker 2" with a first release of 2000[^ref-18].

The retail description promised "all the payoffs of casino Slots and video Poker without leaving home," with Las Vegas sights and sounds, quick hints and strategies, and a bonus Horse Racing game[^ref-3][^ref-22]. Amazon's product description gives the content as 275 variations of slots, "from classic one-armed bandits to modern video slots," and 44 variations of video poker including Joker Poker, Jacks or Better and Deuces Wild[^ref-3]; Sierra's 1999 page put it as "Over 275 variations of Slots" and "More than 44 variations of Video Poker"[^ref-22]. Sierra's 1999 listing gave separate Windows 95/98 and Macintosh CD requirements[^ref-22], and the original v1.0.0.0 Windows disc is ring-marked "VIDEO POKER W 95/98"[^ref-27]. The v1.0.0.1 reissue combined both platforms on one PC/Mac CD-ROM[^ref-18][^ref-28].

## Story Summary

As a casino game, Hoyle Slots & Video Poker has no narrative. Each player starts with a bankroll of $5,000, or a smaller amount if the player chooses, and plays the machines[^ref-26]; when the money runs out, the game offers to reset the account and erase all statistics[^ref-4]. Only one person plays at a time, but the game supports multiple player identities and keeps a record of where each has won and lost money[^ref-4]. Each player also carries a credit card: everyone starts with a green card and can advance to higher levels of card by winning money[^ref-26].

A bonus Horse Racing game is included alongside the slots and video poker[^ref-3][^ref-4]. Sierra described it as "Hoyle's new computer Horse Racing game"[^ref-22], and the help file calls it "Hoyle Horse Derby 2000"[^ref-26].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames describes the game as single-player and mouse/keyboard controlled, with a first-person perspective[^ref-4]. The player inserts cash by clicking a machine's cash slot; each spin draws on the credits in the machine, and when those run out the machine takes money from the player's remaining cash pot. Winnings are added to the machine's credits, and the player may cash out at any time[^ref-4].

Amazon's listing and Sierra's 1999 product page add that quick tips and hints give advice during play, and that a Practice Mode "alerts you to mistakes and shows you how to improve your strategy"[^ref-3][^ref-22].

### Structure and Progression

MobyGames describes no campaign or progression structure: the player chooses machines and stakes, and can cash out of a machine at any time[^ref-4]. Stakes are fixed per machine session; to change them, the player must cash out and start again at the higher setting[^ref-4].

- **Slot machines and video slots:** playable as 5¢, 10¢, 25¢, $1, $5, $10, $25, $50, $100, $500 or $1,000 machines[^ref-4]
- **Video poker:** playable as $1, $5, $25 or $100 machines[^ref-4]

The classic slots are all multi-coin machines. On most of them each coin in play activates another pay column; the Progressive machines "use multiple paylines instead of successive pay columns", so they can pay diagonally as well as horizontally[^ref-26]. On a $1,000 three-coin machine, a maximum bet costs $3,000 per spin[^ref-4].

The help file gives a payoff rate for each video poker machine. Easy Money pays 100%, A-10 Shun 99.1% and Wild Party 99%, while Joker's Wild is "below 92%"[^ref-26]. Hang 10 is listed at "below 95%", and "offers nice payoffs for the Full House and Straight, but low payoffs for Two Pair"[^ref-26]. MobyGames summarises the range as 92% to 100%[^ref-4].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

There are no puzzles. Slot play is luck-based. Video poker involves hold/discard decisions, and the game's hints and Practice Mode are pitched at improving that strategy[^ref-3].

## Development

### Origins

The game is part of the Hoyle line that Sierra began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games: Volume 1[^ref-8]. Wikipedia classifies it as a spin-off of the Hoyle Casino series[^ref-9].

### Production

Sierra Chest and PCGamingWiki credit development to Sierra On-Line[^ref-7][^ref-8]. MobyGames lists Sierra On-Line, Inc. as publisher[^ref-4], while the US box carries the Sierra Attractions label[^ref-18]. No individual development credits have been documented. Sierra's product page was still offering the game at $19.95 in October 1999[^ref-23].

The help file says the game can be played with or without the CD in the drive, but without it "some features of the game are disabled", including "the horse racing announcer's speech and several of the animations in the various games"[^ref-26][^ref-4]. Sierra's product page likewise noted that the "hard drive version does not include character voices or animations"[^ref-22].

### Technical Achievements

No technical innovations are documented for this release. MobyGames hosts screenshots of the Windows version[^ref-21], and LaunchBox holds gameplay screenshots plus North American and UK packaging images[^ref-20].

### Technical Specifications

Sierra's 1999 product page gave these requirements[^ref-22]:

- **Windows (minimum):** Windows 95/98, 486/66, 8 MB RAM, SVGA 640x480 at 256 colours, mouse, 2X CD-ROM; a Windows-compatible sound card optional; Internet features need a 28,000 bps modem ("Internet play not available on Macintosh")
- **Macintosh (minimum):** Power PC or Power Mac G3 including iMac, System 7.5.3 or higher, 16 MB RAM (with virtual memory on), 640x480 at 256 colours, 2X CD-ROM
- **Macintosh (recommended):** Power Mac G3 including iMac, System 8.1 or higher, 32 MB RAM, 640x480 at millions of colours, 4X CD-ROM

MobyGames lists the UK Windows release as requiring Windows 95, a Pentium, 16 MB RAM and a 2X CD-ROM drive, with 256 colours at 640×480[^ref-30].

The higher requirements on Newegg's Encore Software listing (first listed March 29, 2007) belong to a different product. That listing's model number, 705381330264, is the barcode Redump records for Encore's *Hoyle Slots & Video Poker 3* disc (7 05381 33026 4)[^ref-6][^ref-29]. For reference, Newegg lists 640x480 at 16-bit colour, a 4x CD-ROM drive, 65 MB of disk space, 16 MB RAM (32 MB recommended), a Pentium 133 MHz and Windows 98SE/ME/2000 SP4/XP SP1[^ref-6].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for Hoyle Slots and Video Poker have been found.

### Publisher Transition

According to Wikipedia, publication of the Hoyle Casino series moved from Sierra Entertainment to Encore, Inc. after 2005[^ref-9]. PCGamingWiki lists Encore Software as a publisher of Hoyle Slots & Video Poker[^ref-8]. Encore's own disc, though, was a different product, *Hoyle Slots & Video Poker 3*, mastered in 2005[^ref-29], which Newegg first listed on March 29, 2007[^ref-6].

### Related Publications

- **Help file:** the Mac help file (SLOTSVP.PDF, created March 1999) is preserved in the Macintosh Garden manuals collection on the Internet Archive[^ref-26]
