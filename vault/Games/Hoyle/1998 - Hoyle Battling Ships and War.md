# Hoyle Battling Ships And War

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Battling Ships And War is a two-game compilation released by [[Sierra On-Line]] in 1998, pairing *Battling Ships*, a variant of the naval guessing game Battleship, with the card game War[^ref-1][^ref-23]. Neither game was exclusive to this release: Battling Ships had already appeared in Hoyle Classic Board Games, and War went on to appear in Hoyle Card Games the following year[^ref-1].

The title belongs to Sierra's Hoyle's Official Book of Games line, a series of card, board and puzzle games that Sierra On-Line began in 1989[^ref-18]. It shipped on CD-ROM for Windows 3.1 and Windows 95/98[^ref-2][^ref-13], and supported play against the computer, hot-seat play (War only) and two-player online play under Windows 95[^ref-2].

## Story Summary

As a compilation of a board game and a card game, Hoyle Battling Ships And War has no narrative storyline. It recreates two traditional games: Battling Ships and War[^ref-1].

In Battling Ships, two players each place five ships of different sizes on a 10-by-10 grid and take turns trying to sink the opponent's fleet with missiles. Unlike classic Battleship, the number of missiles fired per turn can be set by the player; by default it depends on how many ships the player has left[^ref-1][^ref-23].

In War, each player starts with 26 cards; both draw, and the higher card takes both. A tied pair declares "war": each player lays three cards face-down and one face-up, and the higher face-up card wins the whole pile. Sierra's version adds three tanks per player that fire at each other as cards are drawn. Losing a war costs a tank, and a player wins either by destroying all the opponent's tanks or by collecting every card[^ref-1][^ref-23].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game is played with the mouse; there is no controller support and no key remapping[^ref-2]. It runs in a window that adjusts to the desktop resolution, though the actual game area stays at 640x480[^ref-2].

The credits list ten voice actors and dedicated lip-synching staff, so the on-screen characters were voiced and animated[^ref-21].

### Structure and Progression

The game offers two distinct games within a single package:

- **Battling Ships:** Players place five ships on a 10x10 grid, then fire missiles at the opponent's grid. The number of missiles per turn is adjustable and by default tracks the number of surviving ships[^ref-1].

- **War:** A card-comparison game in which the higher card wins each round, ties trigger a "war", and each side's three tanks provide a second victory condition[^ref-1].

Play modes, per PCGamingWiki[^ref-2]:
- Single-player against the computer
- Local hot-seat play for 2 players (War only)
- Online play for 2 players, peer-to-peer, for most games; requires Windows 95. The official multiplayer service is no longer available.

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Battling Ships rests on deducing where enemy ships are from hit-and-miss feedback, with the variable missile count adding a second consideration: losing ships reduces a player's firepower by default[^ref-1]. War is mostly luck of the draw, with the tank mechanic giving a way to win before all 52 cards change hands[^ref-1][^ref-23].

## Development

### Origins

Hoyle Battling Ships And War was developed and published by Sierra On-Line[^ref-1]. Sierra had been publishing the Hoyle series since 1989, and later Hoyle titles saw yearly releases or specialized in particular types of games[^ref-18]. Both halves of this compilation were shared with other Hoyle products: Battling Ships came from Hoyle Classic Board Games and War later reappeared in Hoyle Card Games[^ref-1].

### Production

Jim Love is credited as designer (and with additional sound and music), under executive producer Stephen H. Van Horn and lead engineer Earl Malmrose; Evan Schiller composed the music[^ref-21]. Voices were recorded at Clatter and Din Studios in Seattle, Washington[^ref-21].
