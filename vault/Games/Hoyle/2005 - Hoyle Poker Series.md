# Hoyle Poker Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Poker Series is a poker simulation video game released in 2005 for Windows, developed by Sierra Entertainment and published by Encore Software[^ref-1]. The game is part of Sierra's long-running Hoyle brand of card and casino games, which began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games: Volume 1[^ref-2]. Its box copy billed it as "The #1 Card Game Brought to You by the #1 Name in Cards!" and invited players to "play the most popular poker game sweeping the nation!"[^ref-3]

IGN's catalogue summary describes it as "an engaging and highly addictive computer game offering many different playable versions of poker"[^ref-4]. The game offers 13 poker variants plus a "Dealer's Choice" option, among them Texas Hold'em, Omaha, Cincinnati, Baseball, 5 Card Draw and 7 Card Stud[^ref-1][^ref-3]; the packaging advertised "over 14" poker games[^ref-6]. It installs as two separate programs: Hoyle Friday Night Poker for single-player play against the computer, and Hoyle On-Line Poker for play against other people over the internet[^ref-1].

The series is named after the 18th-century playing-card expert Edmond Hoyle, and some titles in the series also used trademarks and designs from the Hoyle brand of playing cards under license from Brown & Bigelow[^ref-2]. The phrase "according to Hoyle" has long meant "by the book"[^ref-9].

## Story Summary

As a poker simulation, Hoyle Poker Series has no narrative storyline. In Hoyle Friday Night Poker the player creates an identity and plays against seven of the ten possible computer opponents; Hoyle On-Line Poker connects to game servers to play against other players worldwide[^ref-1]. The packaging also promoted "Hoyle's exclusive online tournaments"[^ref-6].

Each player name carries a running bank balance, which the game saves automatically along with the last selected game and settings[^ref-3]. The computer opponents each have different playing styles, such as tight or loose play, and they talk at the table; character speech can be switched off[^ref-3].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames classifies the game's perspective as third-person, and the game can be played entirely with the mouse[^ref-1]. An Amazon customer review describes the in-game graphics as "very simple 2D with very little animation", and calls the game "an amazingly fast, varied, and user-friendly Poker sim"[^ref-3].

The game includes in-game tutorials and a help function that teach the official rules for every game[^ref-6]. In tutorial mode the player can pick one of the computer players to act as a tutor[^ref-3]. Not every buyer found it approachable: another Amazon customer, who bought it for a parent, wrote that "It is way too complicated for him to play easily."[^ref-6]

### Structure and Progression

All of the variants can be played in "Friday Night" mode with a standard ante. Seven of them can also be played in "Casino" mode with structured betting (big and small blinds), and nine as tournaments[^ref-3]. Tournaments run for a set number of deals rather than as knockouts[^ref-3].

Between games the player can visit a virtual ATM to withdraw or deposit money. Large enough winnings earn a place in the "Hall Of Fame", while a player who borrows too often from loan sharks can end up in the "Hall Of Shame"[^ref-3].

### Poker Variants Included

MobyGames lists the following variants[^ref-1]:

- Texas Hold'em
- Omaha and Omaha Hi/Lo
- 7 Card Stud and 7 Card Stud Hi/Lo
- 5 Card Stud
- 5 Card Draw, 5 Card Draw Lo-ball and Jacks or Better
- Cincinnati
- Criss-Cross
- Baseball
- Hi Chicago
- Dealer's Choice (a random selection from the others[^ref-3])

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The game has no puzzles. Its challenge comes from poker strategy against computer opponents, each with a different playing style[^ref-3].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

No contemporary critic reviews of Hoyle Poker Series were found. IGN lists the game without a review ("NR")[^ref-4], and Metacritic has no critic reviews for it[^ref-7]. MobyGames records a score of 60/100[^ref-1].

Later Amazon customer reviews (2010–2016) are mixed. One 2010 reviewer called it "an excellent Poker simulation that's easy and intuitive to use"[^ref-3]. Another 2010 reviewer found that the online features no longer worked[^ref-3], and a 2015 reviewer found it too complicated for a non-computer-savvy relative[^ref-6].

## Development

### Origins

Hoyle Poker Series grew out of Sierra's long-running Hoyle series, a staple of its casual game lineup since 1989[^ref-2]. Warren Schwader submitted the original concept for the Hoyle games to Sierra co-founder Ken Williams[^ref-2]. The series began with Volume 1, which included six card games: Solitaire, Gin Rummy, Crazy 8's, Old Maid, Cribbage and Hearts[^ref-9].

According to MobyGames, the Friday Night Poker component is based on Hoyle Texas Hold 'Em, and elements of its gameplay can be traced back to Sierra's [[1997 - Hoyle Poker|Hoyle Poker]] (1997)[^ref-1].

Wikipedia lists Sierra Entertainment as the game's developer and Encore Software as its publisher[^ref-10]. Encore took over publishing the Hoyle series from 2005 to 2016[^ref-2].

### Technical Specifications

**System Requirements:**[^ref-1]

- **Operating System:** Windows 98SE or later (box lists Windows 98, Me, 2000, XP[^ref-3])
- **CPU:** Intel Pentium II
- **RAM:** 32 MB
- **Media:** CD-ROM, 4X drive
- **Resolution:** 800×600
- **ESRB Rating:** E (Everyone)[^ref-1][^ref-21]

According to an Amazon customer review, the game does not need the CD in the drive to play and has a small window that fits a netbook screen[^ref-3]. A MobyGames screenshot caption notes that the game window does not resize at higher resolutions[^ref-1].

### Technical Issues

An Amazon customer reported a bug in the Cincinnati variant: the game "doesn't show which random card is used for the 'suit split'" except in tutorial mode[^ref-3].

The online component no longer works. A 2010 Amazon review reported that no connections to the servers were possible and wrote, "This software does not appear to be supported (online features and product support) any longer by manufacturer"[^ref-3]. A secondhand seller warns that the software "May or may not work with all operating systems"[^ref-21].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

Earlier Hoyle volumes used characters from other Sierra games as opponents, including Larry Laffer from Leisure Suit Larry and Princess Rosella from King's Quest[^ref-2]. When the player took too long to decide, these characters started talking in character: Larry comments about women, while Roger Wilco wants to escape the game to save the universe again[^ref-2].

Additional trivia:
- The game installs as two separate programs: Hoyle Friday Night Poker and Hoyle On-Line Poker[^ref-1]
- The "according to Hoyle" phrase refers to the 18th-century card expert Edmond Hoyle, long before the game series[^ref-2][^ref-9]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for Hoyle Poker Series have been found. The wider Hoyle franchise sold well: Hoyle Casino 2000 sold 230,365 units and earned $6.15 million in North America from January through October 2000, according to PC Data[^ref-22]. The original Hoyle's Official Book of Games sold over 250,000 copies by 1990[^ref-2].

Encore, Inc. continued the Hoyle line from 2005 to 2016[^ref-22]. This game also appeared under other labels, including Greenstreet Software in the UK[^ref-3] and Selectsoft Publishing[^ref-23].

### Collections

Hoyle Poker Series was sold as a standalone title. Known retail releases include:

- Standard retail release by Encore Software (March 2005)[^ref-4]
- UK release by Greenstreet Software (June 2006)[^ref-3]
- Secondhand multi-game bundles from resellers[^ref-21]

### Related Publications

- **In-game tutorials:** a built-in help function teaching the official rules for every game variant[^ref-6]

### Critical Perspective

Hoyle Poker Series was a budget poker title from the mid-2000s. It drew no notice from major review outlets[^ref-4][^ref-7]. The customer reviews that do exist praise its variety and ease of use[^ref-3], but the shutdown of its online features left only the single-player game working[^ref-3].

The Hoyle series' use of Sierra adventure game characters as opponents in its early volumes gave it a crossover appeal with Sierra's other franchises[^ref-2].
