# Hoyle Board Games 2001

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Board Games 2001, also known as Hoyle Board Games 5, is a compilation of classic board games developed and published by [[Sierra On-Line]][^ref-1]. It was released in 2000 for Windows and Macintosh[^ref-1]. Macworld credited the Mac version to Sierra Attractions, Sierra's casual gaming division, and gave its price as US$29.99[^ref-3]. The collection features sixteen classic board games, augmented by multimedia and full-color graphics[^ref-3].

GameSpot's Tim Soete wrote that the games keep "the same challenging play elements as that of their real-world counterparts, but they are enhanced with graphics and sound for a more engaging PC experience"[^ref-5]. Players can compete against animated computer opponents or play with other people[^ref-1][^ref-3]; most games support up to four players on one computer, and an Internet option lets players challenge live opponents online[^ref-5]. The package includes Sierra's patented "facemaker" character creation system, allowing players to create personalized avatars that speak and are fully animated[^ref-5].

The package includes a manual, both electronic and hard copy, "detailing the rules and histories of all its games," and Macworld concluded that "players of all ages and skill levels will find something to enjoy"[^ref-3]. It is rated E for Everyone by the ESRB[^ref-17].

## Game Content

### Included Games

Hoyle Board Games 2001 contains 16 games, as counted by IGN, MobyGames, Macworld and GameSpot[^ref-6][^ref-1][^ref-3][^ref-5]. MobyGames lists them as follows, with its notes on the real-world games they resemble[^ref-1]:

- **Backgammon**
- **Battling Ships**
- **Checkers**
- **Chess**
- **Chinese Checkers**
- **Dominoes**
- **Line 'Em Up** – similar to Connect Four
- **Mahjong**, with two sub-games using the same tileset:
  - **Concentration**
  - **Gravity Tiles** – a tile-matching game
- **Mancala**
- **Master Match** – Mastermind; GameSpot called it "one of the new additions"[^ref-5]
- **Parchisi** – Parcheesi
- **Placer Racer** – similar to Puzzle Bobble (Macworld compared it to Breakout instead[^ref-3])
- **Reversi** – like Othello
- **Rummy Squares** – Rummikub; Macworld described it as a derivative of the Turkish game Okey[^ref-3]
- **Snakes & Ladders** – Sierra's version of Chutes & Ladders
- **Yacht** – Yahtzee

IGN lists Mahjong as "Mahjong Tiles"[^ref-6]. IGN adds that the package includes "three card games" as well[^ref-6].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames classifies the interface as point and select[^ref-1]. In GameSpot's review, any of the games "are accessible from a pull-down menu at the bottom of the screen," and "the interface for each game is simple and intuitive"; most games can be played entirely with the mouse, with the keyboard as an option[^ref-5]. Macworld wrote that "pointing, clicking, and dragging are the only skills needed to enjoy the games provided"[^ref-3]. Soete singled out the backgammon move preview: running the cursor over a piece "brings up a pop-up graphic on the board" showing its possible moves[^ref-5].

### Opponents and Customization

For solo players the game includes computer opponents, which Macworld found "much less obnoxious than typical computer players"; they are "creatively voiced and often comment on specific action in the game"[^ref-3]. GameSpot noted that "the characters each have unique animations"[^ref-5], and each computer opponent has its own biography screen[^ref-8]. GameSpot described their personalities as "pretty odd," ranging "from a schizophrenic puppeteer to a Jamaican jazz bass player," and noted that the game lets players adjust the opponents' chattiness[^ref-5].

The game features Sierra's patented "facemaker" character creation system, allowing players to build customized avatars[^ref-5]. These player-created characters can be fully animated and can speak during gameplay[^ref-7]. Personalized characters also carry over into the game's Internet play[^ref-5].

### Multiplayer Options

The collection supports multiple play modes[^ref-1]:
- **Solo Play** – Against computer-controlled opponents[^ref-3]
- **Local Multiplayer** – Most games can be played by up to four players on one computer[^ref-5]
- **Internet Play** – Challenge and chat with live players over the Internet[^ref-5]; Macworld's reviewer found the in-game Internet button did not work for him, and noted the option was not mentioned in the manual[^ref-3]

Some games allow choice between solo play and multiplayer with other people or computer controlled characters[^ref-7].

### Rules and Layouts

Rules are explained in the manual rather than in the game: "Don't know the rules to Reversi or Pachisi? That's not a problem, as this collection also includes an excellent, interesting manual"[^ref-3]. The Mahjong component includes a layouts editor for creating custom tile arrangements[^ref-8].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

MobyGames lists an 85% critic average from two reviews[^ref-1]. GameSpot's Tim Soete gave the game a score of 7 out of 10 (Good) in his December 14, 2000 review, praising the collection while noting its limited appeal to existing Hoyle fans[^ref-5]. Soete wrote that "the games have the same challenging play elements as that of their real-world counterparts, but they are enhanced with graphics and sound for a more engaging PC experience"[^ref-5].

The review noted that "board game fans will appreciate the graphics and easy interface found in Hoyle Board Games, though some of the opponents' chatter may be a bit off-putting"[^ref-5]. However, Soete concluded that "the only real drawback is that if you're not new to the Hoyle Board Game series, you may not find much reason to upgrade to this version"[^ref-5].

Macworld's Danilo Campos called it "a job very well done," noting that play is "simple and intuitive" and that the collection includes a manual, both electronic and hard copy, "detailing the rules and histories of all its games"[^ref-3].

### Later Editions Reception

GameSpot's Gord Goble reviewed a later edition (the one following Hoyle Board Games 2002) on October 22, 2002, giving it a score of 7.5 out of 10 (Good)[^ref-10]. Goble noted that the series "proves just how enduring and intriguing some classic strategy and board games can be," while acknowledging that "it may only appeal to players who are unfamiliar with any of the previous editions"[^ref-10]. He concluded that "although the package is a great choice for newcomers, it probably won't be as satisfying to old hands"[^ref-10].

## Development

### Origins

Hoyle Board Games 2001 (also known as Hoyle Board Games 5) followed the 1998 edition of Hoyle Board Games in Sierra's series[^ref-1][^ref-14]. Sierra's Hoyle board game compilations date back at least to 1991's *Hoyle: Official Book of Games – Volume 3*, which brought six board games to the computer[^ref-20].

### Production

The game was developed by Sierra On-Line, Inc.[^ref-1] Its four credited designers – Rabih AbouJaoudé, Paul Horn, Robert L. Jerauld and Michael Katz – were also credited with the design of the 1998 edition[^ref-1][^ref-14].

**Development Credits:**[^ref-1]
- **Producer:** Robert L. Jerauld
- **Associate Producer:** Eric Hook
- **Sr. Software Engineer-Project Lead:** Roger Key
- **Creative Director:** Herb Perez
- **Lead Artists:** Rabih AbouJaoudé, Julian Love
- **Designers:** Rabih AbouJaoudé, Paul Horn, Robert L. Jerauld, Michael Katz

### Technical Specifications

**Macintosh minimum requirements:**[^ref-7][^ref-3]
- **Resolution:** 640 x 480
- **Color Depth:** 16-bit color
- **Processor:** PowerPC (Macintosh)
- **RAM:** 32 MB available
- **Hard Disk:** 90 MB available
- **CD-ROM Drive:** 4X speed required
- **Operating System:** System 7.5.3 minimum; listed as running up to Mac OS 9.2

Macworld noted the game can be played without its CD, though without it the game animations and character voices are lost[^ref-3].

### Localization

The game was released in both English and German versions as a hybrid Mac/PC release[^ref-7].

### Technical Issues

Users have reported various compatibility issues when running the game on modern systems[^ref-12]:

- Install screen appears but play option shows spinning wheel then nothing
- Facemaker feature crashes the game in some configurations
- Some users report 'NOT RESPONDING' during installation
- One user wondered whether a failed launch was because the game wanted a CD drive their laptop lacked

However, some users have reported that the game runs well on Windows 10[^ref-12].

## Legacy

### Collections and Related Titles

The Hoyle Board Games series continued after this release, with Sierra Entertainment and later [[Encore Software]] publishing additional editions:

- **Hoyle Board Games 2002** – Very similar to the 2001 edition[^ref-10]
- **Hoyle Board Games 2003** – Goble wrote that Sierra "added two more games to last year's list for a grand total of 18," while also describing four new Sierra-devised games and captioning a screenshot "one of 16 different board games," so the count is uncertain[^ref-10]
- **Hoyle Board Games 2005** – Published by Encore Software[^ref-16]

The game was also part of a larger Hoyle ecosystem that included card game collections, which shared the same avatar system[^ref-9]. The edition GameSpot reviewed in October 2002 added bonus PDA versions of mahjong tiles and backgammon for Palm or Windows handhelds[^ref-10].

### Related Publications

- **Manual:** Electronic and hard-copy manual covering the rules and histories of all included games[^ref-3]

### Critical Perspective

Both period reviews were favourable: Macworld's Campos called it "a job very well done"[^ref-3], and GameSpot's Soete scored it 7 out of 10[^ref-5]. Soete felt existing Hoyle owners might not find much reason to upgrade, but called the CD "a good value if you don't already own a collection of these games for the PC"[^ref-5].

Reviewing a later edition in 2002, GameSpot's Gord Goble observed that Hoyle Board Games 2002 "was almost a dead ringer for Hoyle Board Games 2001," and that the newer package "may only appeal to players who are unfamiliar with any of the previous editions"[^ref-10].
