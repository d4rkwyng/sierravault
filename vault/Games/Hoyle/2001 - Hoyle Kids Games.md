# Hoyle Kids Games 2001

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Kids Games 2001 is the second edition of [[Sierra On-Line]]'s children's compilation in the Hoyle series, sold at retail as *Hoyle Kids Games 2002*.[^ref-26] GameFAQs dates the US PC release to September 25, 2001, with the Macintosh version following in 2002,[^ref-26] while Sierra Chest, which lists the game as *Hoyle Kids Games 2*, gives a first release date of October 26, 2001.[^ref-9] It followed the 2000 original, [[2000 - Hoyle Kids Games]]; Wikipedia's Hoyle series list records Hoyle Kids Games editions in both 2000 and 2001.[^ref-5]

The package collects fifteen games for players aged five and up.[^ref-2][^ref-26] Sierra's own product page introduced "a new game for 2002 called Bump 'em!" alongside fourteen other games,[^ref-23] and the box advertised it as a "Great new board game".[^ref-2] Sierra's pitch asked: "Have you ever played Crazy 8s with an alien? Or tried to sink a dinosaur's Battling Ship?"[^ref-23] The box text uses a slightly different line: "Have you ever played Go Fish with an alien? Or sunk a dinosaur's sub?"[^ref-2]

Children can play against friends and family on one computer or against Hoyle's animated characters, create their own character with the Facemaker tool, and call on the Hoyle Genie for rules and hints.[^ref-2][^ref-23]

## Story Summary

As a compilation of classic children's games, Hoyle Kids Games has no narrative storyline. Its personality comes from its cast of animated opponents. Sierra's character page gave several of them short biographies: Penny is "a show horse from England", Pepper is "a Dalmatian puppy" and "firedog in training", and Red is "the real Red Riding Hood, but a bit older and wiser now".[^ref-24] Players can also build their own character with Facemaker, "a wacky figure from out of this world, or one that looks just like you".[^ref-23]

## Games Included

Sierra listed the fifteen games as Battling Ships, Checkers, Chinese Checkers, Crazy 8s, Go Fish, Hangman, Memory Match, Old Maid, Placer Racer, Snakes & Ladders, Speedy Racer, The Music Game, Tic Tac Toe, War and the new Bump 'em.[^ref-23] The same list appears on the GameFAQs retail description.[^ref-26]

Descriptions on the box add detail on several of them:[^ref-2]

- **Bump 'Em:** "This new game will have you driving your bumper car around the track to victory... just don't get bumped back!"
- **Battling Ships:** "Enter the strategic command center and select where to position your fleet."
- **Checkers:** "King me! Let the Genie show you how to be a Checkers champ."
- **Chinese Checkers:** "Jump your marbles across the board to claim victory."
- **Go Fish:** Played with animated opponents "on the sea floor".
- **Hangman:** "Try all four great variations of Hangman: Snow, Beach, Western and Medieval."
- **Old Maid:** "Be the first to pair up the cards in your hand- but watch out for that Old Maid!"
- **Placer Racer:** "Shoot down the balls before your time is up."
- **Snakes and Ladders:** "Climb and slide with up to 3 friends."

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls
Games are chosen from a bookshelf in a bedroom setting, and winning games earns trophies.[^ref-2] Players can pick the music and background they like best.[^ref-2]

### Structure and Progression
Each of the fifteen games is a separate mini-game.[^ref-8] Sierra advertised adjustable skill levels "tailor-made for kids and fun for everyone", automatic saving so games can be finished later, and laptop play "with or without your CD".[^ref-23]

### Character System
Sierra described "a talkative cast of 12 animated players" who are "always ready for a challenge", naming Penny the horse, Pepper the Dalmatian, Harley the bear, Marvin the dinosaur, Roswell the alien, Ethel, Red, Jasper, Captain Scurvy and Miss Tardee.[^ref-23] The box counts "10 talkative opponents including Pepper the Dalmatian and Roswell the Alien".[^ref-2] The Facemaker tool lets players create their own character.[^ref-2][^ref-23]

### Multiplayer Options
Sierra's product page promised "10 multiplayer games that host up to four players on the same computer".[^ref-23] Gamepressure likewise describes play for up to four people at the same computer.[^ref-8] Neither Sierra's 2001 product page nor the box text mentions internet play.[^ref-2][^ref-23]

### Tutorial and Help System
The Hoyle Genie, "just a lamp rub away", provides spoken rules and hints, and answers questions such as "show me where?", "What now?" and "show me how?" during games.[^ref-23]

## Reception

No contemporary press reviews or aggregate scores for this edition were found. GameFAQs lists an ESRB rating of E (Everyone) with the descriptor "Mild Violence".[^ref-26]

## Development

### Origins
The game belongs to Sierra's Hoyle series, which ran from 1989 to 2016.[^ref-5] Sierra's 2001 Hoyle catalog listed Hoyle Kids Games for both PC and Mac alongside Hoyle Board Games, Card Games, Casino and Word Games, describing it as "fifteen children's favorites".[^ref-25] Sierra's product page credited the game to Sierra On-Line, with the Sierra Attractions trademark.[^ref-23] No credits list for this edition was found.
