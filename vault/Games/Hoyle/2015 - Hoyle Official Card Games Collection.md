# Hoyle Official Card Games Collection 2015

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Official Card Games Collection 2015 represents a modern continuation of the venerable Hoyle gaming franchise, which traces its roots back to Sierra On-Line's original card game simulators of the late 1980s[^ref-1]. Developed by Webfoot Technologies, Inc. and published by Viva Media, LLC, this collection was released on Steam on November 18, 2015 for both Windows and macOS platforms[^ref-2][^ref-21]. A boxed US retail edition from Encore Software is dated November 6, 2015[^ref-20]. Marketing itself as "The #1 card game collection," the game promised an updated experience with a new visual design, improved artificial intelligence, and new computer opponents to challenge players[^ref-3].

The 2015 edition was advertised with "over 70 new and classic card games," including titles never before seen in the Hoyle series such as Fan Tan, Oh Hell, and Whist[^ref-4]. The publisher's own list on the Steam forum names 23 card games and 50 solitaire games[^ref-17]. The collection includes a Face Creator feature allowing players to customize their in-game avatar, along with dozens of backgrounds and card deck designs to personalize the playing experience[^ref-2]. The game supports widescreen monitors natively and offers large, easy-to-read cards designed for accessibility[^ref-5].

The series is named after 18th century playing card expert Edmond Hoyle, and some titles also used trademarks and designs from the Hoyle brand of playing cards under license from Brown & Bigelow[^ref-1]. The game's own marketing calls Hoyle "the most trusted name in gaming for over 200 years"[^ref-2][^ref-12]. Encore Software took over publishing the Hoyle series from 2005 to 2016[^ref-1], and Viva Media, the Steam publisher, was at some point bought by WD Encore Software[^ref-9].

## Story Summary

As a card game simulation collection, Hoyle Official Card Games Collection 2015 does not feature a traditional narrative storyline. Instead, the game provides a virtual environment where players can engage with computerized opponents across a variety of card games[^ref-2].

The early Sierra volumes used humorous computer opponents drawn from other Sierra series, such as Leisure Suit Larry and Princess Rosella[^ref-1]. Those characters are not in this edition. When a player on the game's Steam forum asked in April 2017 for Hoyle releases with the old characters and voices, Encore's community moderator replied that "due to licensing restrictions we're unable to include the original voices and other likenesses that Sierra owns the IP to"[^ref-16].

The game instead offers a variety of characters and speed of play options for opponents, allowing players to customize their gaming experience according to preference[^ref-2]. Players compete in individual games with no overarching storyline or progression narrative, simply enjoying the card games themselves.

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Hoyle Official Card Games Collection 2015 utilizes a top-down perspective with a fixed flip-screen visual presentation typical of card game simulations[^ref-2]. The game supports keyboard and mouse input, with no controller support available[^ref-5]. Players navigate through menus to select their desired card game and customize settings including opponent selection, game speed, and visual preferences.

The interface was designed for widescreen monitors with support for both wide window (16:9) and narrow window (4:3) display modes[^ref-5]. The game advertises "large, easy-to-read cards"[^ref-2].

### Structure and Progression

The collection offers over 70 individual card games[^ref-2]. The publisher's Steam forum list splits them into two groups[^ref-17]:

- **Card Games (23):** Bridge, Canasta, Crazy Eights, Cribbage, Euchre, Fan Tan, Gin Rummy, Go Fish, Hearts, Memory Match, Oh Hell, Old Maid, Pinochle, Pitch, Poker, Rummy 500, Skat, Solitaire, Spades, Spite and Malice, Tuxedo, War and Whist[^ref-17]. Fan Tan, Oh Hell and Whist were advertised as new to the Hoyle series[^ref-4]
- **Solitaire Games (50):** from Aces and Kings, Aces Up and Alhambra through Klondike, Spider and Yukon[^ref-17]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The gameplay mechanics center on accurate simulation of card game rules with computer-controlled opponents that utilize artificial intelligence to provide competitive play[^ref-3]. The 2015 edition specifically advertised "better AI" compared to previous versions[^ref-3].

The Face Creator feature allows players to design custom avatars, providing a personalization element beyond the card games themselves[^ref-2]. Players can also select from dozens of backgrounds and card deck designs to customize the visual presentation[^ref-2].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

No professional reviews of the 2015 collection have been found; the available reception is from players. On Steam, SteamDB records a user review score of 52.12% positive based on 68 total reviews[^ref-7]. The Amazon UK listing shows 4.0 out of 5 stars from 708 global ratings[^ref-4].

On the day of release, a Steam forum poster opened a thread titled "It's refreshing..." with: "to see that your graphics and interface haven't changed a bit from Hoyle Card Games 2002/2003 and you have the face to peddle this on Steam for 20 Euro!"[^ref-19]

Amazon customers raised similar concerns about visual quality, with one noting: "This is not the original Hoyle Card Games at all. Graphics are poor and blurry"[^ref-4]. Other Amazon reviews reported installation and compatibility problems[^ref-4].

## Development

### Origins

The Hoyle series began at Sierra On-Line with Warren Schwader's concept for *Hoyle's Official Book of Games: Volume 1*, a spiritual sequel to Sierra's *Hi-Res Cribbage* (1981)[^ref-1]. Volume 1 sold over 250,000 copies by 1990[^ref-1].

### Production

By 2015 the series was no longer made by Sierra. Webfoot Technologies, Inc. developed the 2015 collection[^ref-2][^ref-6][^ref-12]. Viva Media, LLC, described as "a privately owned New York company dedicated to publishing a wide array of interactive content of the highest standards," published it on Steam[^ref-9][^ref-21]; MobyGames notes that Viva Media was at some point purchased by WD Encore Software[^ref-9].

Boxed retail copies carried the Encore name: GameFAQs lists a US release by Encore Software on November 6, 2015 (UPC 705381412502)[^ref-20], and the Amazon UK listing gives a release date of October 7, 2015 with Encore as manufacturer[^ref-4]. The Steam forum's community moderator signed posts as "Community Moderator at Encore Software"[^ref-8].

**Development Credits:**
- **Developer:** Webfoot Technologies, Inc.[^ref-2][^ref-6]
- **Publisher (Digital):** Viva Media, LLC[^ref-9]
- **Publisher (Retail):** Encore[^ref-4][^ref-20]

Specific individual credits for designers, artists, and programmers were not documented in available sources.

### Technical Achievements

The launch announcement highlighted "a different interface, native wide screen support, amongst other new features"[^ref-8]. The game was advertised with high-resolution graphics and widescreen monitor support[^ref-4], and offers 'Wide Window' (16:9) and 'Narrow Window' (4:3) modes[^ref-5].

The same post noted that "Hoyle Bucks are no longer part of the game," and called it "our very first release in the Hoyle Series on Steam"[^ref-8]. Patch 1.07, posted on December 9, 2015, added French and German localization, more options for some solitaire games, and bug fixes[^ref-22].

### Technical Issues

The 2015 Hoyle collection has experienced notable compatibility issues, particularly with newer operating systems. Users have reported that the game does not function properly on Windows 11[^ref-4]. A reviewer explicitly warned: "not for windows 11"[^ref-4].

Windows 10 users have also encountered problems following system updates. In March 2017 a Steam forum poster reported: "Since the recent update to Windows 10, when I get to the settings page none of the buttons work anymore. I cannot play the game anymore."[^ref-18]

Installation difficulties and graphics quality were also raised in Amazon reviews[^ref-4]. The last public Steam build is dated June 23, 2017[^ref-7].

PCGamingWiki lists the Steam version as unavailable[^ref-5], and SteamDB records the app as restricted in Germany[^ref-10].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No official sales figures have been published. For the Steam version, SteamDB shows owner estimates of about 3,200 (VG Insights) and about 16,700 (PlayTracker)[^ref-10], while SteamSpy gives a range of 20,000 to 50,000 owners[^ref-14]. All are third-party estimates.

For comparison, Sierra's Volume 1 sold over 250,000 copies by 1990[^ref-1].

As of this writing, gg.deals lists the game on Amazon.com at $19.99 as a Steam code and as a download[^ref-15].

### Critical Perspective

The 2015 collection continues a series that began at Sierra, but Sierra had no part in making it. Encore said it could not use the Sierra-owned voices and characters[^ref-16], so the game keeps the Hoyle name but not the Sierra cast that set the early volumes apart[^ref-1].

Its marketing promised "a new look, better AI and new opponents"[^ref-3]. At least one Steam forum poster saw little change from the 2002/2003 Hoyle card games[^ref-19], and Steam user reviews are split roughly evenly[^ref-7]. The last public Steam build dates from June 2017[^ref-7], and PCGamingWiki now lists the Steam version as unavailable[^ref-5].
