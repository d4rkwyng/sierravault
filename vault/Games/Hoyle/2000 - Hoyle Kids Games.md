# Hoyle Kids Games 2000

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Kids Games 2000 is a compilation of classic children's games published by [[Sierra On-Line]] under its Sierra Attractions brand and developed by Sierra's Casual Entertainment Division.[^ref-19] Sierra announced its release on September 7, 2000, for Windows 95/98/2000/ME and Macintosh at an MSRP of $29.95, for children aged five to twelve.[^ref-19] GameFAQs and Sierra Chest also give September 7, 2000, while MobyGames dates the release to August 7, 2000.[^ref-2][^ref-5][^ref-1] The collection has twelve games: Battling Ships, Checkers, Chinese Checkers, Snakes & Ladders, Crazy Eights, Go Fish, Memory Match, Old Maid, War, Hangman, Placer Racer, and Speedy Racer.[^ref-19][^ref-20] MobyGames also lists two bonus games, The Music Game (similar to Simon) and Tic Tac Toe.[^ref-1]

Players can play with friends and family or against a cast of eight animated computer characters.[^ref-19][^ref-20] Sierra's product page describes "10 multiplayer games that host up to four players on the same computer".[^ref-20] MobyGames' description and specifications also mention four-player internet play, but Sierra's own 2000 press release and product page describe only same-computer play.[^ref-1][^ref-26][^ref-19][^ref-20] The Facemaker utility lets players create their own character, and the Hoyle Genie explains the rules and gives play tips.[^ref-19][^ref-20]

Hoyle Kids Games is one of the titles listed in Wikipedia's overview of the Hoyle series, which began with Hoyle's Official Book of Games: Volume 1 in 1989.[^ref-3] Sierra's press release billed it as the first Hoyle game "designed just for kids", though Wikipedia's list of Hoyle titles includes an earlier Hoyle Children's Collection (1996).[^ref-19][^ref-3] Sierra's Catalogue 2000-2001 included it on page 19, a "Hoyle Range" page tagged with nine Hoyle titles.[^ref-4]

## Story Summary

As a compilation of classic board and card games, Hoyle Kids Games 2000 does not feature a narrative storyline. Players instead play the collected games against built-in animated computer characters or other human players, using an avatar made with the Facemaker utility.[^ref-1]

Sierra's characters page introduced some of the computer opponents: Penny, a show horse from England; Pepper, a Dalmatian puppy who is "a firedog in training"; and Red Riding Hood, "a bit older and wiser now".[^ref-21] The press release also mentions "a wise-cracking alien" among the eight characters.[^ref-19]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Games are chosen from the bookshelf of an interactive bedroom, which also holds other interactive surprises for children to explore.[^ref-19] UVList tags the Macintosh version as using the mouse.[^ref-10] The game saves automatically so a session can be finished later, and Sierra described it as "Laptop compatible - play with or without your CD".[^ref-20]

### Structure and Progression

The game includes fourteen different activities organized as follows:[^ref-1]

**Main Games:**
- **Battling Ships:** A strategy guessing game
- **Checkers:** Classic board game
- **Chinese Checkers:** Star-shaped board variant
- **Snakes & Ladders:** Dice-based racing game
- **Crazy Eights:** Card shedding game
- **Go Fish:** Card collecting game
- **Memory Match:** Card matching game
- **Old Maid:** Card game
- **War:** Simple card comparison game
- **Hangman:** Word guessing game
- **Placer Racer:** Racing game
- **Speedy Racer:** Racing game

**Bonus Games:**
- **The Music Game:** Similar to Simon, a memory sequence game[^ref-1]
- **Tic Tac Toe:** Classic X and O game[^ref-1]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Players can customize their experience using the Facemaker utility, which allows creation of personalized character avatars.[^ref-1] Screen background environments where games are played can be altered to the player's liking, and difficulty levels are adjustable.[^ref-1] Checkers, for example, can be played on a regular board or with frogs on a lily pad board.[^ref-19][^ref-22] The Facemaker in this game was made for children, with "hundreds of features from realistic to outlandish".[^ref-19]

Ten of the games are multiplayer, for up to four players on the same computer.[^ref-20] MobyGames also lists four-player online play, which Sierra's own 2000 materials do not mention.[^ref-26]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

*Computer Gaming World*'s January 2001 "Holiday Hit List" placed Hoyle Kids Games first of its four family and kids picks, calling it "a collection of 12 classic games for all ages".[^ref-23] The magazine wrote that "Graphics are pretty good, and computer opponents are entertaining with their snappy banter", noted that most games have adjustable skill levels with rules and help on every screen, and gave an age recommendation of 5–12.[^ref-23]

MobyGames records a critic average of 85%, based on three critic ratings.[^ref-1]

### Modern Assessment

GameFAQs users rate the game "Good", based on two user ratings.[^ref-2]

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames Critics:** 85% (3 ratings)[^ref-1]
- **GameFAQs User Rating:** Good (2 ratings)[^ref-2]

## Development

### Origins

The game was developed and published in-house by Sierra On-Line, Inc.[^ref-1] GameFAQs lists the developer and publisher under the later Sierra Entertainment name.[^ref-2] Sierra's press release credited the game to its Casual Entertainment Division and quoted Steve Van Horn, the division's senior vice president and business unit manager: "Our internal development group has produced a fine new addition to the very popular Hoyle line of products, and created a new game designed just for kids."[^ref-19]

### Production

Michael Katz produced the game and is also credited as one of its designers, as principal engineer and project lead, and as a script writer.[^ref-18] His MobyGames profile lists credits on many other Hoyle titles, through Hoyle Puzzle & Board Games (2009).[^ref-8] The design team also included Rabih AbouJaoudé, Dana Armstrong, Eric Fleming, and Robert L. Jerauld.[^ref-1][^ref-18]

Jeff Hoyt was one of six credited script writers and also one of the voice actors.[^ref-18] His MobyGames credits on Hoyle titles run from Hoyle Poker and Hoyle Classic Card Games (1997) to Hoyle Puzzle & Board Games (2009).[^ref-9]

### Technical Achievements

Sierra highlighted the Facemaker avatar utility and the Hoyle Genie, which "appears to help with game rules and tips".[^ref-19] Sierra also offered a 14 MB downloadable demo with two of the twelve games, Checkers and Crazy Eights, plus the Hoyle Genie.[^ref-22]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for this game have been found. In its launch press release Sierra said the Hoyle CD-ROM series as a whole "sold over 1.3 million copies in 1999, according to PC Data".[^ref-19] Sierra's Catalogue 2000-2001 included the game on page 19, a "Hoyle Range" page tagged with nine Hoyle titles.[^ref-4]

### International Release

In Spain, Havas Interactive released the game in its "Casual Games" collection alongside Hoyle Board Games, Hoyle Casino Games and Hoyle Card Games. The Spanish magazine *PCManía* said it gave children "doce divertidos juegos" (twelve fun games) and a Facemaker for creating characters.[^ref-24]

### Collections

Sierra Chest lists the game in its Hoyle Series group.[^ref-5]

### Successor

Sierra followed it with Hoyle Kids Games 2002, which GameFAQs dates to September 25, 2001 in the US for PC. Its 15 games included the new Bump'em!.[^ref-27] Sierra Chest lists the follow-up as a separate game, Hoyle Kids Games 2, with a first release date of 2001-10-26.[^ref-28] See [[2001 - Hoyle Kids Games]].
