# Hoyle Card Games 2002

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Card Games 2002 is the fifth release of Sierra's *Hoyle Card Games* title (MobyGames lists it as "Hoyle Card Games 5"), part of the Hoyle line that began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games and is named after the 18th-century card-game authority Edmond Hoyle (died 1769).[^ref-1][^ref-13][^ref-20] Despite the "2002" in its name, the product shipped in autumn 2001; as with the rest of Sierra's 2002 Hoyle line, the year in the product name ran ahead of the release. Amazon.com's listing in August 2001 said the game "will be released on September 25, 2001," and Macworld reported on 30 September 2001 that Sierra On-Line "has released the 2002 series of Hoyle games for PC and Mac."[^ref-22][^ref-3] All titles in that line shipped as hybrid PC/Mac games on one CD-ROM.[^ref-3]

Sierra's product page said the game "brings you 18 of your favorite card games": "16 authentic favorite card games plus two new International Games."[^ref-21] The main new feature was the "Hoyle International Game Section", which added Skat, "a three-player game from Germany", and Tarot, "the national game of France".[^ref-21][^ref-3] Sierra promised that "Lively, interactive play and crisp, colorful graphics make Hoyle Card Games the number one selling game available."[^ref-21] The package also included a version of Hoyle Solitaire for Palm OS handhelds, which Macworld described as four solitaire variations.[^ref-21][^ref-3]

The game was published by [[Sierra On-Line]] (copyright 2001 Sierra On-Line, Inc.) and sold under its Sierra Attractions label; the ESRB, which rated it E for Everyone with a Gambling descriptor, lists the publisher under the company's later name, Sierra Entertainment.[^ref-21][^ref-22][^ref-3][^ref-6] Players faced ten animated characters — Bart, Elayne, Ethel, Gax, Harley, Jasper, Marvin, Maurice, Robin and Roswell — and could build their own likeness with Facemaker; player identities carried over to other Hoyle products such as Board Games and Casino.[^ref-21][^ref-1]

## Games Included

Sierra's product page lists eighteen games: sixteen standard card games plus the two new International Games, Skat and Tarot.[^ref-21] GameGenie's review also counts eighteen; MobyGames' description lists the sixteen and does not count Skat and Tarot.[^ref-5][^ref-1]

### Card Games
The full lineup, per Sierra, Macworld and GameGenie:[^ref-21][^ref-3][^ref-5]

- **Bridge**
- **Canasta**
- **Crazy Eights**
- **Cribbage**
- **Euchre**
- **Gin Rummy**
- **Go Fish**
- **Hearts**
- **Memory Match** – a concentration-style game[^ref-1]
- **Old Maid**
- **Pinochle**
- **Pitch**
- **Poker**
- **Solitaire**
- **Spades**
- **War**
- **Skat** – German card game (new to the series)[^ref-3]
- **Tarot** – French card game (new to the series)[^ref-3]

### Solitaire Collection
The solitaire count also varies by source: MobyGames gives "forty three varieties," while GameGenie's review says "FIFTY different kinds of Solitaire."[^ref-1][^ref-5] Separately, the CD included Hoyle Solitaire for Palm OS handhelds; Macworld lists its four variations as Klondike, Pyramid, 4 Free and 3 Towers.[^ref-21][^ref-3]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls
MobyGames classifies the game as a top-down, fixed-screen, point-and-select card and tile game.[^ref-1] GameGenie's James Allen described the graphics as "headless players and floating cards," clear and functional but "standard fare."[^ref-5] A printed Hoyle rulebook and strategy guide came in the box, containing "card game history, rules, playing strategies and helpful diagrams"; in-game, "Rules, hints and tips are also just a mouse click away."[^ref-21][^ref-1][^ref-5]

### AI Opponents and Customization
The game featured ten animated characters, "each with adjustable skill levels and attitudes."[^ref-21][^ref-1] GameGenie noted that each AI character has "several different sayings" and that the AI "know the games very well."[^ref-5] Facemaker let players create a personalized character, "choosing his or her hairstyle, outfit, and facial features," and those identities could be used in Hoyle Board Games and Hoyle Casino.[^ref-21][^ref-1] Players could also choose the music, background and game variations.[^ref-21]

### Multiplayer Options
Sierra's product page lists play "with up to four people on the same computer" and Internet Play; Facemaker characters could represent players "on the computer or over the Internet."[^ref-21] GameGenie's review also praised the online play.[^ref-5]

### Palm OS Integration
Sierra's product page promised "a version of Hoyle Solitaire for Palm OS handhelds", and Macworld reported four Palm OS solitaire variations.[^ref-21][^ref-3] Neither source mentions Palm Spades or Windows CE support for this edition.

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews
GameGenie.com's James Allen concluded that "Hoyle Card Games is a very good compilation of almost every card game that you'd ever want to play."[^ref-5] He praised the game selection, online play and Palm OS solitaire, but found the graphics similar to "those found on countless freeware card games," and noted that the game runs without the CD but "you'll need the disc in the drive to fully appreciate the sound."[^ref-5]

No other contemporary critic review of this edition has been verified.

### User Reception
Period Amazon.com customer reviews were mixed. By February 2002 the listing had 7 reviews and a sales rank of 85.[^ref-23] One reviewer enjoyed Facemaker ("Create a near life-like picture of yourself to play cards") but "couldn't get the sound to come on"; a long-time Hoyle player reported that Canasta bugs were "still there"; another called it an "awkward piece of junk."[^ref-23]

Today Amazon India shows 4.2 out of 5 stars from 4 global ratings.[^ref-4] GameFAQs users rate it "Good" (3 ratings), and MobyGames shows 5.0 out of 5 from 2 ratings.[^ref-1][^ref-7] All of these samples are very small.

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 5.0/5 (2 ratings)[^ref-1]
- **Amazon:** 4.2/5 (4 ratings)[^ref-4]
- **GameFAQs:** Good (3 ratings)[^ref-7]

## Development

### Origins
The Hoyle series began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games and was initially developed and published by Sierra On-Line. Sierra Entertainment developed and published the series from 1996 to 2003.[^ref-13]

### Production
Hoyle Card Games 2002 was one of five titles in Sierra's 2002 Hoyle line, alongside Hoyle Casino 2002, Hoyle Board Games 2002, Hoyle Word Games 2002 and Hoyle Kids Games 2002. All shipped as hybrid PC/Mac CD-ROMs, and every title except Kids Games sold for US$29.95.[^ref-3] The main new feature for the card games edition was the International Games Selection, which added Skat and Tarot.[^ref-1][^ref-3]

### Technical Specifications

**System Requirements (Sierra's official specifications):**[^ref-21]
- **Windows:** Windows 95/98/00/ME, Pentium 133 or higher, 16 MB RAM, 4x CD-ROM, 640x480 at 16-bit color, 60 MB hard disk space
- **Macintosh:** PowerPC or Power Mac G3 including iMac, System 7.5.3 or higher, 32 MB unused RAM, 4x CD-ROM, 640x480 at thousands of colors, 100 MB hard disk space
- **Media:** Hybrid PC/Mac CD-ROM[^ref-3]
- **Local multiplayer:** up to four players on one computer[^ref-21]

GameGenie's review lists slightly different figures (70 MB of hard disk space and Windows 95/98/2000/Me/XP).[^ref-5]

**Display and Interface:**[^ref-1]
- **Perspective:** Top-down
- **Visual Style:** Fixed / flip-screen
- **Gameplay Type:** Cards / tiles
- **Interface:** Point and select
- **Business Model:** Commercial

### Technical Issues
Period Amazon.com reviewers reported problems: one wrote in December 2001 that "Even though the instructions specify that the software can be used on the iMac, it wouldn't work after being installed," and another said Canasta bugs from earlier versions were "still there."[^ref-23]

A 2017 Amazon reviewer wrote: "I had this for 6 years and even though it does not work on Windows Vista, 7, and 8, it worked again on Windows 8.1 and Windows 10."[^ref-4]

### Trivia

- **Historical Namesake:** The series is named after Edmond Hoyle, the English writer on card-game rules who died in 1769.[^ref-13][^ref-20]
- **Cross-Product Compatibility:** Player identities created in the Facemaker could be used in Hoyle Board Games and Hoyle Casino, and vice versa.[^ref-1]
- **International Expansion:** Skat (Germany) and Tarot (France) were new in this edition.[^ref-21][^ref-3]
- **Game Boy Color Tie-in:** Sierra's product page for this edition also advertised a Game Boy Color version.[^ref-21][^ref-12]

## Legacy

### Publisher Transition
Sierra Entertainment published the Hoyle series from 1996 through 2003. Encore took over publishing from 2005 to 2016.[^ref-13] Encore Software developed Hoyle Card Games 2005 (published by VU Games), and Freeverse developed Hoyle Card Games 2009 for publisher Encore.[^ref-11][^ref-15]

### Digital Distribution
A later, different product, *Hoyle Official Card Games Collection*, came to Steam. Its developers called it "our very first release in the Hoyle Series on Steam" and noted that "Hoyle Bucks are no longer part of the game."[^ref-8] One Steam user wrote that its "graphics and interface haven't changed a bit from Hoyle Card Games 2002/2003."[^ref-8]

### Platform Legacy
The wider Hoyle series also reached the Dreamcast.[^ref-13] A separate Game Boy Color *Hoyle Card Games* (2000) was developed by Sandbox Studios and published under Sierra's Sierra Attractions label. In that version, each player picks an avatar, such as a crocodile or a teddy bear.[^ref-12]
