# Hoyle Card Games 2002

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Card Games 2002 is the fifth release of Sierra's *Hoyle Card Games* title (MobyGames lists it as "Hoyle Card Games 5"), part of the Hoyle line that began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games and is named after the 18th-century card-game authority Edmond Hoyle (died 1769).[^ref-1][^ref-13][^ref-20] Despite the "2002" in its name, the product shipped in autumn 2001: Macworld reported on 30 September 2001 that Sierra On-Line "has released the 2002 series of Hoyle games for PC and Mac," and Amazon lists a release date of 25 September 2001.[^ref-3][^ref-4] All titles in that line shipped as hybrid PC/Mac games on one CD-ROM.[^ref-3]

The main new feature was an "International Games Selection" introducing Skat, a three-player game from Germany, and Tarot, the national game of France.[^ref-1][^ref-3] The manufacturer's box copy promised "Lively, interactive play and crisp, colorful graphics" that "make it the number one game in its class."[^ref-4] The package also included Palm OS versions of four solitaire variations.[^ref-3]

Hoyle Card Games 2002 was published by Sierra Entertainment (Macworld's announcement credited [[Sierra On-Line]]) and rated E for Everyone by the ESRB, with a Gambling descriptor.[^ref-3][^ref-6] Players faced ten animated AI opponents and could build their own likeness with the Facemaker function; player identities carried over to other Hoyle products such as Board Games and Casino.[^ref-1]

## Games Included

Sources count the games differently. GameGenie's review counts eighteen games, including Solitaire, Skat and Tarot; MobyGames' description lists sixteen games and does not count Skat and Tarot in that list.[^ref-1][^ref-5]

### Card Games
The full lineup, per Macworld and GameGenie:[^ref-3][^ref-5]

- **Bridge**
- **Canasta**
- **Crazy Eights**
- **Cribbage**
- **Euchre**
- **Gin Rummy**
- **Go Fish**
- **Hearts**
- **Memory Match** – a concentration-style game[^ref-1]
- **Old Maid**
- **Pinochle**
- **Pitch**
- **Poker**
- **Solitaire**
- **Spades**
- **War**
- **Skat** – German card game (new to the series)[^ref-3]
- **Tarot** – French card game (new to the series)[^ref-3]

### Solitaire Collection
The solitaire count also varies by source: MobyGames gives "forty three varieties," while GameGenie's review says "FIFTY different kinds of Solitaire."[^ref-1][^ref-5] Separately, Macworld lists four Palm OS solitaire variations: Klondike, Pyramid, 4 Free and 3 Towers.[^ref-3]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls
MobyGames classifies the game as a top-down, fixed-screen, point-and-select card and tile game.[^ref-1] GameGenie's James Allen described the graphics as "headless players and floating cards," clear and functional but "standard fare."[^ref-5] Full rules for each game are included, along with a strategy guide.[^ref-1][^ref-5]

### AI Opponents and Customization
The game featured ten animated AI opponents.[^ref-1] GameGenie noted that each AI character has "several different sayings" and that the AI "know the games very well."[^ref-5] The Facemaker let players create characters in their own likeness, and those identities could be used in Hoyle Board Games and Hoyle Casino.[^ref-1]

### Multiplayer Options
According to the box copy, up to four people could play on one computer, and Free Internet Play let players challenge and chat with other Hoyle players online. Internet play was not available on Macintosh.[^ref-4]

### Palm OS Integration
Macworld reported that the game included four Palm OS solitaire variations.[^ref-3] Amazon's listing describes "Solitaire and Spades for use on Palm OS and Windows CE Handhelds" as bonus games "available in specially marked versions."[^ref-4]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews
GameGenie.com's James Allen concluded that "Hoyle Card Games is a very good compilation of almost every card game that you'd ever want to play."[^ref-5] He praised the game selection, online play and Palm OS solitaire, but found the graphics similar to "those found on countless freeware card games," and noted that the game runs without the CD but "you'll need the disc in the drive to fully appreciate the sound."[^ref-5]

No other contemporary critic review of this edition has been verified.

### User Reception
Amazon shows 4.2 out of 5 stars from 4 global ratings.[^ref-4] GameFAQs users rate it "Good" (3 ratings), and MobyGames shows 5.0 out of 5 from 2 ratings.[^ref-1][^ref-7] All of these samples are very small.

**Aggregate Scores:**

- **Amazon:** 4.2/5 (4 ratings)[^ref-4]
- **GameFAQs:** Good (3 ratings)[^ref-7]

## Development

### Origins
The Hoyle series began in 1989 with Hoyle's Official Book of Games and was initially developed and published by Sierra On-Line. Sierra Entertainment developed and published the series from 1996 to 2003.[^ref-13]

### Production
Hoyle Card Games 2002 was one of five titles in Sierra's 2002 Hoyle line, alongside Hoyle Casino 2002, Hoyle Board Games 2002, Hoyle Word Games 2002 and Hoyle Kids Games 2002. All shipped as hybrid PC/Mac CD-ROMs, and every title except Kids Games sold for US$29.95.[^ref-3] The main new feature for the card games edition was the International Games Selection, which added Skat and Tarot.[^ref-1][^ref-3]

### Technical Specifications

**System Requirements:**[^ref-5]
- **Processor:** Pentium 133
- **RAM:** 16 MB
- **Hard Drive:** 70 MB
- **Operating System:** Windows 95/98/2000/Me/XP or Mac OS 7.5.3
- **Media:** Hybrid PC/Mac CD-ROM[^ref-3]
- **Local multiplayer:** up to four players on one computer[^ref-4]

**Display and Interface:**[^ref-1]
- **Perspective:** Top-down
- **Visual Style:** Fixed / flip-screen
- **Gameplay Type:** Cards / tiles
- **Interface:** Point and select
- **Business Model:** Commercial

### Technical Issues
A 2017 Amazon reviewer wrote: "I had this for 6 years and even though it does not work on Windows Vista, 7, and 8, it worked again on Windows 8.1 and Windows 10."[^ref-4]

### Trivia

- **Historical Namesake:** The series is named after Edmond Hoyle, the English writer on card-game rules who died in 1769.[^ref-13][^ref-20]
- **Cross-Product Compatibility:** Player identities created in the Facemaker could be used in Hoyle Board Games and Hoyle Casino, and vice versa.[^ref-1]
- **International Expansion:** Skat (Germany) and Tarot (France) were new in this edition.[^ref-1][^ref-3]

## Legacy

### Publisher Transition
Sierra Entertainment published the Hoyle series from 1996 through 2003. Encore took over publishing from 2005 to 2016.[^ref-13] Encore Software developed Hoyle Card Games 2005 (published by VU Games), and Freeverse developed Hoyle Card Games 2009 for publisher Encore.[^ref-11][^ref-15]

### Digital Distribution
A later, different product, *Hoyle Official Card Games Collection*, came to Steam. Its developers called it "our very first release in the Hoyle Series on Steam" and noted that "Hoyle Bucks are no longer part of the game."[^ref-8] One Steam user wrote that its "graphics and interface haven't changed a bit from Hoyle Card Games 2002/2003."[^ref-8]

### Platform Legacy
The wider Hoyle series also reached the Dreamcast.[^ref-13] A separate Game Boy Color *Hoyle Card Games* (2000) was developed by Sandbox Studios and published under Sierra's Sierra Attractions label. In that version, each player picks an avatar, such as a crocodile or a teddy bear.[^ref-12]
