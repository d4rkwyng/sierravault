# Hoyle Puzzle Games 2004

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hoyle Puzzle Games 2004 is a collection of puzzle and arcade games from Sierra Entertainment, released on September 2, 2003 for Windows[^ref-1][^ref-4][^ref-6][^ref-20][^ref-24][^ref-32]. Although it came out in 2003, "2004" is part of the edition's name: GameSpot grouped it with the other "Hoyle Games 2004" titles in a demo posted that September[^ref-32]. GameZone's listing gives the North American release date as 09/03/2003, one day later[^ref-31]. It gathers 17 games, one of which is the full version of *The Incredible Machine: Even More Contraptions*[^ref-4][^ref-8][^ref-20]. Two of the 17, Rays and Word Searches, were billed as new games[^ref-4][^ref-20][^ref-29]. Sierra Chest catalogues it as "Hoyle Puzzle Games 2", the follow-up to [[2002 - Hoyle Puzzle Games|Hoyle Puzzle Games]] (2002)[^ref-24].

The box advertised 1,000 crossword puzzles, high score lists, adjustable skill levels (Beginner, Intermediate or Expert), the ability to save and print word searches and crosswords, a free Hoyle rulebook and strategy guide, and the Facemaker tool for building a character "from hundreds of features"[^ref-20][^ref-33]. GameSpot's 2003 product page described "hundreds of variations of 17 inventive puzzle games"[^ref-32]. GameSpy listed the publisher as Sierra Attractions and the ESRB rating as E for Everyone[^ref-8]; the ESRB's own entry, "Hoyle: Puzzle Games 2004", is rated E with no descriptors and lists the platforms as Palm Pilot, Windows PC and Pocket PC[^ref-34].

## Game Content

### Included Games

The 17 games are listed on the box and in retail listings[^ref-4][^ref-8][^ref-20]. The descriptions below follow the game's manual[^ref-29].

**Word Games:**
- **Anagrams** – A six-letter word is scrambled at the top of the screen. Players have 90 seconds to find it and the other three- to six-letter words that can be made from its letters.
- **Crosswords** – Puzzles labelled Easy, Medium or Hard, with a different puzzle for each day of the year plus bonus puzzles; play alone or against another person. The box says 1,000 crossword puzzles[^ref-20][^ref-33]; the manual says 1,200[^ref-29].
- **Hangman** – Three versions: Classic Hangman, Hangman Challenge and two-player Head to Head Hangman, each with a choice of four game environments.
- **Word Searches** (new) – Hidden-word puzzles on topics such as hobbies, animals, food, science and cities, played alone or head to head.

**Tile Games** (all five use mahjong tiles):
- **Edge Tiles** – Match free tiles on opposite edges of a rectangular grid.
- **Gravity Tiles** – Remove groups of matching tiles; the tiles above them fall into the gap.
- **Mahjong Tiles** – Match exposed tiles in pairs from a choice of layouts; a Layout Maker lets players design their own.
- **Memory Tiles** – A concentration-style game: tiles are dealt face down and turned over two at a time to find pairs.
- **Slide Tiles** – Slide tiles around the board to make matches, removing more than one pair at a time for extra points.

**Arcade and Strategy Games:**
- **Maze Racer** – Escape a maze before the goal time runs out, or race another player to the exit.
- **Maze Raider** – Collect each maze's goal items while avoiding monsters, in Greek or Haunted House themes.
- **Placer Racer** – Fire balls at a ceiling of coloured balls; three or more of the same colour that touch disappear. Two players can compete on one keyboard.
- **Rays** (new) – A hide-and-seek game compared in the manual to Battling Ships: players fire rays of light into a grid from its edge and, from where the rays come out, work out where the hidden "nodes" are, with only eight rays to use.
- **Solitaire Arcade** – Five timed solitaire games: 3 Towers, Best 21, Fast 21, Pick 2 and Sum 11.
- **Star Collector** – Move pieces from a rack onto a grid, matching by picture or colour, to collect enough stars to reach the next level.
- **Time Breaker** – A block-breaking game in which a time-travelling ship clears blocks across four ages: Primitive, Egyptian, Medieval and Future.
- **[[2001 - The Incredible Machine - Even More Contraptions|The Incredible Machine: Even More Contraptions]]** – The Professor's Rube Goldberg-style contraption puzzles, included as a complete game[^ref-24][^ref-29].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Players sign in by typing a name and picking a character image. From the main screen they choose a game by clicking its icon or through the Go To menu. Each game opens with a "Getting Started" screen that explains the basics and lets players change players and settings, and fuller instructions are in the in-game help[^ref-29].

### Structure and Progression

All the games can be played alone, and several, including Hangman, Maze Racer and Placer Racer, let two people play against each other on the same computer[^ref-29]. Players can set game speed, difficulty, background music and game variations. The game keeps statistics for each player, and games can be saved and continued later[^ref-29]. The 2004 edition added a new high score list[^ref-8][^ref-20][^ref-33].

### Customization Features

The **Facemaker Tool** lets players create characters "from hundreds of features"[^ref-6][^ref-8]. A GameSpot user reviewer noted options for the music and difficulty level, and the ability to print some puzzles, but found fewer choices than in Hoyle's card and table games[^ref-7].

Sierra Chest lists 32 songs that can be taken straight from the CD as WAV or MP3 files, including an intro, a Puzzle Games theme and themes for Placer Racer, Time Breaker, Anagrams and Maze Raider, and calls them "a whole set of new songs for a Hoyle game"[^ref-35]. The partial game rip on KHInsider has five tracks totalling 4 minutes 18 seconds[^ref-9].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

GameZone's reviewer Aceinet scored the game **6.6 out of 10** in a review dated 10/28/2003, with sub-scores of 6.5 for gameplay, 6 for graphics, 7 for sound, 6.5 for concept and 7 for multiplayer[^ref-30]. The review praised the create-a-character mode, noting that a character saved in another Hoyle game could be used here, and compared Time Breaker to Arkanoid/Breakout and Placer Racer to Puzzle Bobble. It complained that choosing the rules option exits the game to the help screen, that some games which mention mouse control can only be played with the keyboard, and that the on-screen Hangman clues were "useless". It concluded that the game "is an entertaining collection of classic puzzle games that should please most gamers", but "doesn't offer anything in a puzzle collection that hasn't been done before"[^ref-30]. GameZone's listing shows it as the only review on file there[^ref-31].

A GameSpot user review dated April 4, 2006 scored it **8 out of 10**, summing it up as "Something for everyone's taste but you might want to get the 2005 version." The reviewer said the 2005 version sounded better because it was combined with the table games and could be fully installed without the CD[^ref-7].

### Modern Assessment

On Amazon the game holds **3.7 out of 5 stars** from 11 ratings[^ref-4]. One customer, KGReads, wrote: "I love (most of) these games. A couple of the games seem rather pointless but my favorite is the Star Collector game. I also like Rays and a couple of the Tile games"[^ref-4]. GameFAQs shows a user rating of "Outstanding" from 2 ratings[^ref-1].

## Development

### Origins

Sierra Chest catalogues the game as "Hoyle Puzzle Games 2", the second release of [[2002 - Hoyle Puzzle Games|Hoyle Puzzle Games]][^ref-24]. Its box, retail listings and Sierra's own product page presented Rays and Word Searches as the new games in this edition[^ref-4][^ref-20][^ref-33]. GameSpot posted a "Hoyle Games 2004 Demo" on September 5, 2003, sampling games from the new Hoyle card, casino, puzzle and table releases, with Star Collector representing this one[^ref-32].

### Production

The manual credits Executive Producer Stephen H. Van Horn, Producer Cara Ely, and Eric Fleming as Senior Software Engineer and Project Lead. Mike Caviezel and Ben Houge are credited as composers, with Caviezel also the sound engineer, and Pat Cashman voiced the talking skulls[^ref-29]. The manual carries a 2003 Sierra Entertainment copyright and a 2003 Dell Magazines copyright, and notes that the game uses the Miles Sound System[^ref-29].

### Technical Issues

In a December 2003 PCReview forum thread, a user reported that on Windows XP the CD spun but nothing appeared on screen. A Microsoft MVP replied with general advice for games that ask for the disc, but the thread does not record a fix[^ref-12]. An Amazon reviewer wrote in 2017 that the game did not work for them on Windows Vista, 7 and 8, but worked again on Windows 8.1 and 10[^ref-4].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures have been found. Best Buy listed the game for Windows (SKU 5884457), and it is now marked as no longer available new[^ref-13]. Amazon lists it as discontinued by the manufacturer[^ref-4].

### Subsequent Releases

A GameSpot user reviewer pointed buyers to a 2005 version, which combined the puzzle games with the table games and could be fully installed[^ref-7]. See [[2004 - Hoyle Puzzle and Board Games 2005|Hoyle Puzzle & Board Games 2005]].

### Trivia

- Amazon's product description says the game includes a "FREE Hoyle Rulebook and Strategy Guide for advanced insider information"[^ref-4]
- The box invites players to print puzzles "for proof that you're a pro"[^ref-6][^ref-20]
- Sierra's 2003 product page linked its product registration under SKU 72013[^ref-33]
