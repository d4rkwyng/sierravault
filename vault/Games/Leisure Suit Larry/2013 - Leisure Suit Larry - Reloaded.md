# Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards: Reloaded

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards: Reloaded is a 2013 HD remake of Al Lowe's classic 1987 Sierra adventure game[^ref-18]. The project was successfully crowdfunded through Kickstarter, raising $655,182 from 14,081 backers against a $500,000 goal[^ref-42]. Developed by N-Fusion Interactive and published by Replay Games, with original creator Al Lowe as designer[^ref-18], the remake features new high-resolution graphics[^ref-79], a voice cast led by Jan Rabson as Larry[^ref-8][^ref-12], and a new big-band soundtrack by Grammy-nominated composer Austin Wintory[^ref-59]. It was released for PC and Mac on June 27, 2013, priced at $19.99[^ref-83].

The game follows the misadventures of Larry Laffer, a "40-year-old virgin in pursuit of losing his virginity"[^ref-40] as he navigates the nightlife of Lost Wages, a parody of Las Vegas[^ref-18]. While maintaining the core gameplay and humor of the original, Reloaded introduces new content including an entirely new character named Jasmine[^ref-21], expanded locations, and additional puzzles[^ref-15]. As Al Lowe explained, "One of our goals with doing this remake was not to do a remake. We didn't just want to port the game over with higher-res and better graphics. We wanted to do a much better game and it gave me a chance to fix some of the puzzles that I've hated for twenty five years"[^ref-76].

## Story Summary

The game centers on Larry Laffer, described as one of "gaming's more infamous characters"[^ref-24] and "one of the most iconic characters in the history of computer gaming"[^ref-15]. IMDb's synopsis calls him a "lovable loser"[^ref-40], and his quest is straightforward: when the remake was greenlit on Steam, the Financial Post summed up the 1987 original as a game about helping its hero find a sexual partner[^ref-9]. Set in the fictional city of Lost Wages, the game has five buildings to explore: Lefty's Bar, a mini-mart, a wedding chapel, a multi-floor casino and a disco[^ref-18].

The remake expands on the original's narrative with additional character interactions and locations. The credited cast includes the women Larry pursues (Fawn, Faith, Eve and the new Jasmine) along with the Hooker outside Lefty's, and several voiced "Kickstarter Patron" characters[^ref-8]. Jasmine is a new character, absent from both the 1987 original and the 1991 SCI remake[^ref-21]. As one walkthrough noted, "this game differs slightly from the original. Most puzzles are the same, but a few of them are completely different, not to mention that Jasmine didn't even exist in the original game"[^ref-21].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Like the 1991 SCI remake, Reloaded replaces the original's text parser with point-and-click controls[^ref-18]. Puzzles are solved by looking at, picking up, licking, talking to or using items on the game's objects and people[^ref-18].

Mash Those Buttons singled out the interaction system: in this remake, "the writers wrote a specific reaction (and narrated it) for every single command used on every single item", so trying an unexpected action on an object still gets its own narrated response[^ref-44].

### Structure and Progression

A returning fan finished it in about two and a half hours[^ref-1]; another reviewer logged six hours[^ref-12]. The same returning fan noted that the developers "did add a few more puzzles" and a new girl[^ref-1]. Larry gets around Lost Wages by taxi; a GameFAQs board thread notes "You can get through using the taxi just seven times"[^ref-23] for efficient completion.

The Steam release has 40 achievements, and collecting them all takes two playthroughs[^ref-74]; Steam Community guides walk through the requirements[^ref-67]. Easter eggs include a payphone that can dial the original Sierra hint line[^ref-74].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Al Lowe used the remake to "fix some of the puzzles that I've hated for twenty five years"[^ref-76]. Mash Those Buttons felt that not everything was fixed, observing that "some rotten things followed this game on its trip to modernity"[^ref-44].

The age-check quiz returns: players must answer trivia that only an adult should know before they can play[^ref-13], with new questions on subjects such as Steve Fossett and Hannibal Lecter[^ref-21] and Y2K[^ref-84]. The old CTRL + ALT + X shortcut still skips the quiz; the game simply brands you "CHEATER, CHEATER. CHEATER!!!" and lets you play[^ref-13].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The 2013 remake received "mixed or average" reviews, with a Metacritic score of 56/100[^ref-18]. GameSpot awarded it 4/10, with reviewer Jason Venter writing that "there's not enough new gameplay to hide the fact that the original Leisure Suit Larry game was every bit as shallow as its sexually frustrated protagonist"[^ref-31]. Game Informer's Jeff Cork gave it 6/10, observing that "Newcomers may find the gameplay and core concept as chafing as a pair of sweaty polyester slacks"[^ref-24].

### Modern Assessment

User reception among GOG customers was more positive, with 66 reviews averaging 4/5 stars (October 2026)[^ref-37]. On the GameFAQs boards, some Kickstarter backers were enthusiastic, with one stating "I contributed $125 to make this game happen, 6 times the price of the game itself, and I'm so happy I did it"[^ref-22]. Another backer claimed "I have not heard of a SINGLE kickstarter backer who was disappointed in Al Lowe's work here"[^ref-22].

JayIsGames reviewer Tricky wrote that "these guys get Larry... the obliviousness, the dorkiness, and all those other attributes that ensure this loser stays just that much more loveable than pathetic"[^ref-41]. GamesRadar was less taken, judging that its "gameplay doesn't translate nearly as well to the modern gaming era"[^ref-33].

## Development

### Origins

The remake project began when Replay Games, founded by Paul Trowe[^ref-55], reached a licensing agreement with IP holder Codemasters[^ref-18]. However, traditional publishers showed no interest in funding development[^ref-34]. As Al Lowe explained, "Replay Games had the rights to Leisure Suit Larry for months and was unable to find a publisher interested in funding development. Had Paul Trowe and I not stumbled into Tim Shaffer at a conference, we might still be hunting for funding"[^ref-34].

The Kickstarter campaign, titled "Make Leisure Suit Larry come again!"[^ref-42], launched in April 2012[^ref-83]. The campaign successfully raised $655,182 from 14,081 backers, exceeding the $500,000 goal[^ref-42]. This funding model proved crucial, as Al Lowe noted that Kickstarter made the project possible when traditional funding had failed[^ref-34].

### Production

Development was handled by N-Fusion Interactive, with the remake headed by original designer Al Lowe and co-designer Josh Mandel[^ref-83]. Replay billed it as more than a graphics update, promising "thousands of hilarious new lines" written by Mandel and "new characters, new locations, new puzzles"[^ref-79]. Jan Rabson provided the voice of Larry Laffer and Brad Venable voiced the Narrator[^ref-8][^ref-12], and the credited cast also includes voiced Kickstarter patrons[^ref-8].

The game faced multiple delays during development, with Paul Trowe explaining "Due to the pure number of bugs found in the PC version alone (Mac version is being tested now, but not via Steam), we're having to delay the game, yet again, until the end of June"[^ref-55]. Trowe added "We have ONE SHOT at getting this game right, and it will set the bar for quality for all future Larry games to come"[^ref-55].

Austin Wintory composed a new 21-track big-band score built on Al Lowe's original 1987 theme, recorded live with an 18-piece band, The Late Night Jazz Orchestra[^ref-59]. Wintory saw the game as romantic at heart, explaining "This game, at its core, is very innocent, sweet and romantic. Despite the fact that its loaded with raunchy and silly on-the-nose humor, this character of Larry Laffer is like a 40-year-old virgin. He's like a little boy"[^ref-59]. Choicest Games praised the score, writing that "Austin Wintory has done a sensational job with the Leisure Suit Larry: Reloaded soundtrack which goes to prove he's not a one trick pony"[^ref-12].

### Technical Achievements

The remake was built using the Unity engine[^ref-57] and released for Windows, macOS and Linux on June 27, 2013, followed by Android and iOS on July 17[^ref-18]. The Android version was pulled from Google Play the day after it went live to fix an unspecified error, and Replay said there was "no confirmed day" for its return[^ref-60]; MobyGames dates the Android release to August 13, 2013, which fits a relaunch[^ref-85].

The game was rebuilt and animated from scratch[^ref-24]. Game Informer's Jeff Cork observed "The game looks pretty darn good overall, though it lacks consistency"[^ref-24].

## Legacy

Replay Games billed Reloaded as "the first major game to be released with funding by Kickstarter"[^ref-79], and Al Lowe told GamingBolt that, "to the best of my knowledge", it was "the very first game 'out the door'" among Kickstarter projects[^ref-34]. Both are the makers' own claims.

Some backers were delighted with the result[^ref-22], while GamesRadar concluded that it "only really works as a pure nostalgia play"[^ref-33].

The game was delisted from Steam on November 24, 2017[^ref-15], though it remains available on GOG for Windows[^ref-37]. Delisted Games attributes the removal most likely to an expired license from Codemasters, rather than to the contemporaneous controversy around Replay's president[^ref-15].
