# Leisure Suit Larry Goes Looking for Love (in Several Wrong Places)

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

*Leisure Suit Larry Goes Looking for Love (in Several Wrong Places)* is the second game in [[Al Lowe]]'s landmark comedy adventure series, released by Sierra On-Line in October 1988.[^ref-1] The sequel marked a significant technical leap forward, being one of the first games to use Sierra's new Creative Interpreter (SCI) engine,[^ref-7] which brought full 320x200 resolution, mouse control for movement, and sound card support.[^ref-6] "For better or worse, Larry Laffer is among the most recognized cult heroes in adventure games," noted Adventure Classic Gaming's retrospective review.[^ref-3]

Sierra intentionally toned down Larry's sexual escapades for the sequel, which instead offers a more elaborate plot involving the KGB, a cruise ship, and tropical islands.[^ref-6][^ref-9] The sequel itself had not been assured: IGN notes that "a Leisure Suit Larry sequel was not always a sure thing," because a number of retailers had refused to sell the first game and its initial sales were among the lowest in Sierra's history.[^ref-5] Al Lowe has said that each game in the series, this one included, sold over 250,000 copies.[^ref-6]

Adventure Classic Gaming judged it "at least 3 times longer than the original,"[^ref-3] and Last Day Deaf counts 75 unique locations, "nearly doubling that of the previous game."[^ref-7] However, this expansion came with a controversial design decision: the game actively punishes Larry for flirting with any woman he meets, invariably leading to his violent death—a marked departure from the rest of the series.[^ref-6] "This lack of Leisure Suit Larry's trademark humor was an oft-cited criticism of the game and the series returned to its original formula in all later games."[^ref-9]

## Story Summary

The game opens with Larry Laffer discovering that his romantic triumph from the first game was nothing more than a fleeting encounter. "We find our hero, Larry Laffer, mowing Eve's lawn (not like that!) as Eve pulls up and screams at Larry to leave (and that, what he thought was true love, turns out to only be a one night stand – one, that Eve barely even remembers!)"[^ref-10] Cast out into the streets of Los Angeles, penniless and womanless, Larry's luck suddenly takes an unexpected turn.[^ref-1]

After being dumped by Eve, Larry finds his fortune steadily improving as he wins the lottery, a blind date contest, and a vacation on a cruise ship.[^ref-20][^ref-6] However, Larry unwittingly becomes entangled in international intrigue when he accidentally comes into possession of a Peruvian "onklunk"—an instrument holding microfilm that the KGB and the villainous Dr. Nonookee both want.[^ref-15][^ref-6] The language barrier between Larry and the woman at the music store means he has no idea of the significance of what he's carrying or that he's being pursued by dangerous operatives.[^ref-1]

The adventure takes Larry from Los Angeles to a luxury cruise liner, through a tropical resort, and ultimately to the volcanic island lair of the villainous Dr. Nonookee—whose name is itself a pun on "no nookie," reflecting Larry's perpetual romantic misfortune.[^ref-40][^ref-6] Along the way, Larry encounters numerous femme fatales, all of whom are either undercover agents wanting his microfiche, secretly want him dead for wacky reasons, or will inevitably lead him to some sort of fatal hijinx.[^ref-7] Unlike the first game where the objective was straightforward—get Larry laid—here Larry never learns why the KGB is chasing him, and the plot simply unfolds around him.[^ref-1]

The story culminates with Larry infiltrating Dr. Nonookee's volcano to win the hand of Kalalau, the chief's daughter; his accidental defeat of Nonookee frees the women he had hypnotized, among them "Polyester Patti," who returns as Passionate Patti in later games.[^ref-6] Interestingly, Patti appears as a blonde in this game but would become dark-haired in future installments.[^ref-9] The evil doctor and his volcanic lair are direct parodies of James Bond films, particularly *Dr. No* and *You Only Live Twice*.[^ref-15]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game utilizes Sierra's newly developed SCI0 engine, representing a significant advancement over the AGI engine used in the original game.[^ref-2] "LSL2 is more advanced than the original, due to the use of Sierra's new SCI engine which boasts a higher resolution, fancier graphics, mouse support, and support for common sound cards of the time."[^ref-1] Players move Larry with the cursor keys, a mouse or a joystick, while a text parser is still used for all other actions.[^ref-6]

The technical presentation runs at 320x200 resolution in 16-color EGA graphics, a notable improvement over the original's more limited visuals.[^ref-2] "Larry now consists of at least eight pixels," joked one reviewer about the graphical upgrade.[^ref-17] The DOS version supports the AdLib, Game Blaster, IBM Music Feature and Roland MT-32 sound devices,[^ref-41][^ref-42] and Al Lowe composed the soundtrack himself, scoring it for the MT-32.[^ref-18]

### Structure and Progression

Unlike the original's more open-ended design, the sequel follows a linear story progression similar to Sierra's other adventure games, particularly later entries in the King's Quest series.[^ref-6] The game takes Larry through several distinct areas:

- **Los Angeles:** Larry's starting point, featuring familiar locations like the Quikie Mart and music store where the fateful onklunk is obtained
- **Cruise Ship:** A luxury liner where Larry must navigate social situations and avoid deadly women
- **Tropical Resort:** Features a vegetation maze containing a peacock from King's Quest IV's Genesta's island[^ref-15]
- **Airport:** Where Princess Rosella from King's Quest IV appears as a barbershop attendant in a cross-promotional cameo[^ref-15]
- **Airplane:** A fellow passenger recognises Larry from Lefty's Bar[^ref-20]; a chatterer barber parodies Sierra CEO Ken Williams[^ref-19]
- **Nontoonyt Island:** Dr. Nonookee's base of operations, inside a volcano, and the game's climax[^ref-6][^ref-15]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

There are fewer puzzles in this installment than in the previous game; however, the number of ways to die has increased, with danger awaiting Larry in most places he visits.[^ref-1] A YouTube compilation of death scenes reportedly runs approximately 40 minutes.[^ref-7] The game's inventory system is accessed via Ctrl+I, and points are awarded for completing puzzles and discovering secrets, with rank changes occurring at score milestones.[^ref-20]

The game features numerous dead-end situations where players can become stuck if they forget to perform certain actions before proceeding to the next location.[^ref-1] "Having unintuitive puzzles, dead ends and repeated game overs are just staples of these early Sierra Online games," observed one modern reviewer.[^ref-2] Al Lowe's own advice, printed in the official book, was simply: "Save early and often!"[^ref-22]

The text parser carries over the first game's tradition of humorous responses to typed commands. IGN observes that the series' parser responses, "as seen in the first game and especially the second," are "quite wordy and not afraid to stretch the humour to its absolute limit."[^ref-5]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The game received strong reviews upon release, with critics praising its expanded scope and improved technology. Bob Guerra of *Compute!* called it a "terrific sequel to one of the best adventure games ever written" and found it "thoroughly entertaining from beginning to end. The story is as interesting as anything you're likely to find on network television, and less predictable to boot."[^ref-6][^ref-43]

*Commodore User* awarded the game 9 out of 10, describing it as "Not only an adventure, but a complete entertainment package."[^ref-24][^ref-6] *Computer and Video Games* gave it 90%, calling it "A superb piece of all-round entertainment, particularly, but not exclusively, suited to adventure players."[^ref-24][^ref-6] *PC Format* noted that "The three Larry games so far plumb new depths in computer entertainment — they're crude, suggestive, full of innuendo and double entendres and designed to appeal to the worst aspects of human nature — you'll love 'em."[^ref-6]

*Amiga Action* awarded 74%, while *CU Amiga Magazine* gave the 1994 budget re-release 84%.[^ref-6]

### Modern Assessment

Modern critics have been more mixed in their assessment, particularly regarding the toned-down humor. Adventure Classic Gaming's Zack Howe concluded that while it "may not be the best game in the Leisure Suit Larry series, it is still a humorous and an entertaining title to play," awarding it 3 out of 5 overall with 4 out of 5 for gameplay.[^ref-3] GameFAQs user Syrain gave it 7/10, calling it "Better, Longer and Funnier than the first."[^ref-17]

A 2023 retrospective awarded the game 64%, praising its technical improvements but criticizing its "frustratingly vague puzzles" and design requiring exact character positioning for interactions.[^ref-2] One MobyGames user review put it bluntly: "The biggest problem with this game is WHY is Larry doing any of the things he is doing? In the first game, the objective was simple: get Larry laid. All the actions & jokes stemmed from that. In this game, Larry has no idea what is happening."[^ref-1]

## Development

### Origins

The existence of a sequel was far from guaranteed. The original *Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards* faced significant challenges at retail—"a number of retailers refused to sell the game based on its perceived content" and "initial sales were some of the lowest in Sierra's history."[^ref-5] The game sold only 4,000 copies in its first month, growing significantly only after word of mouth spread.[^ref-23] The first game was also heavily copied: according to MobyGames, Sierra claims it sold more hint books for it than actual games.[^ref-23]

Al Lowe faced pressure to clean up Larry's act for the sequel. "After the original was claimed to be too dirty by some, Al Lowe decided to make Larry go looking for true love, as opposed to cheap sex (as stated in the manual of the collection)."[^ref-28]

### Production

Development utilized Sierra's new SCI engine, making this one of the earliest titles to showcase its capabilities.[^ref-7] The game shipped on six 5.25" 360 KB or three 3.5" 720 KB disks;[^ref-41] according to Just Games Retro, Lowe knew he would need six floppies' worth of space and built six distinct areas visited one after another, with no way back.[^ref-29] Al Lowe served as designer, programmer, and composer, writing a score for the Roland MT-32.[^ref-18]

"In Larry 2 we tried something a little different from our original protection of requiring a distribution disk," Al Lowe explained. "We included the pictures of girls from Larry's 'little black book.' The game shows you one when it starts up, and you enable play by completing her phone number. The pictures, by the way, are intentionally bad in the book so that they can't be Xeroxed easily."[^ref-3]

After the game's release, Sierra ran a CompuServe contest for the funniest Trite Phrase. According to MobyGames' trivia, the winning line, "Do you want fries with that?", came from [[Josh Mandel]], who later joined Sierra.[^ref-1] Mandel's own accounts trace his hiring to beta-testing rather than the contest: he had asked producer [[Guruka Singh Khalsa]], whom he knew from CompuServe's Gamers Forum, about testing for Sierra, and after a couple of years of testing Guruka offered him a job as his junior producer in early 1990.[^ref-46][^ref-47]

### Technical Achievements

The game represented a significant technical leap for Sierra. It was the first Larry game to support sound cards including AdLib, Roland MT-32, and the Game Blaster.[^ref-1] The SCI engine enabled 320x200 resolution graphics and full mouse support for navigation, though the text parser remained for commands.[^ref-2]

The game's 75 unique locations nearly doubled the content of its predecessor.[^ref-7] However, deadline pressures forced compromises: Lowe had wanted the player "much more in control of the action at the climax," but as shipping deadlines neared he "was forced to go for a more 'auto pilot' ending."[^ref-1] According to Just Games Retro, Lowe has admitted that he ran out of time and floppy space and made the entire ending a non-interactive cut scene.[^ref-29]

### Cut Content

The game's ending was originally planned to give players more control during the climactic sequences, but shipping deadlines forced Al Lowe to implement an "auto pilot" ending with minimal interactivity.[^ref-1] The time and floppy disk space constraints meant the final area became largely a non-interactive cutscene.[^ref-29]

### Technical Issues

The game shipped with a notorious parser bug that was still generating support emails years later. "The day before the game shipped, the system programmer in charge of 'the parser' fixed a bug that I'd been complaining about for days. He assured me he changed nothing else. I foolishly added the new code to the game," Al Lowe explained. The bug causes commands like "PUT BAG BOTTLE" to fail because the parser incorrectly treats "bag" as a verb rather than a noun—players must type "PUT THE BAG IN BOTTLE" instead.[^ref-32] "16 years later, I'm still answering emails about a bug I swear I didn't create!"[^ref-32]

Other documented issues include:
- **Dead-end situations:** Player can become permanently stuck if they forget certain items before proceeding[^ref-1]

Al Lowe ruefully notes that modern technical support offers no help: "Once upon a time you could have phoned Sierra's technical support staff if you had trouble with a game. Now, Vivendi Games' tech desk will just laugh at you if you need help with anything before Magna Cum Laude."[^ref-35]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

The game is packed with references to other Sierra games and inside jokes:

- **Copy Protection Bypass:** Entering the phone number 555-0724 (Al Lowe's birthday) skips the copy protection, and the intro with it; Al Lowe says it works only in version 1.002.000, not the original 1.000.011[^ref-17][^ref-31][^ref-32]
- **Boss Key:** Pressing Ctrl-B, the game's boss key, makes Larry disappear[^ref-3]
- **Police Quest Reference:** Looking through a fence hole at the Quikie Mart shows people playing Police Quest[^ref-15]
- **Space Quest IV Reference:** One background is described as looking like it belongs in Space Quest IV[^ref-20]
- **King's Quest IV Cameos:** Princess Rosella appears as a barbershop attendant at the airport; a peacock from Genesta's island appears in the resort maze[^ref-15]
- **Ken Williams Parodies:** The chatterer barber on the airplane and Chief Keneewauwau of Nontoonyt Island are both parodies of Sierra's CEO[^ref-19]
- **The Maid's Brother:** If Larry tries to have sex with the hotel maid, her overprotective brother walks in with a gun and kills him[^ref-44][^ref-45][^ref-40]
- **"Hairy Reams" Salon:** A reference to adult film star Harry Reems[^ref-40]
- **Recurring Barbershop:** The same barbershop appears in every area as a running gag[^ref-29]
- **Goldfinger Homage:** If Larry follows Dr. Nonookee's henchwomen, he ends up tied to a table and cut in half by a laser beam[^ref-15]
- **Original Larry Alley:** One location "looks exactly like the alley from the original Leisure Suit Larry game"[^ref-20]
- **Tribal Programming Test:** Larry's initiation test uses partially disassembled 8088 code from MS-DOS 3.3 COMMAND.COM[^ref-1]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Al Lowe has said the game sold over 250,000 copies.[^ref-6] The entire Larry series surpassed 1.4 million units by March 1996, and the first five Larry games exceeded 2 million copies by the launch of *Love for Sail!*[^ref-6]

### Collections

The game has been included in numerous compilation releases:

- **Leisure Suit Larry's Greatest Hits and Misses** (1994): Included alongside *Softporn Adventure*, Larry 1 (original and VGA), Larry 3, 5 and 6, and *The Laffer Utilities*[^ref-33]
- **Leisure Suit Larry Collection Series** (1997): Five-game compilation with Larry 1 VGA remake[^ref-34]
- **Leisure Suit Larry Collection** (2006): Windows compilation released by Sierra Entertainment[^ref-36]
- **Steam Release** (December 2017): Digital distribution by Assemble Entertainment (since delisted)[^ref-27]

### Fan Projects

The game maintains an active speedrunning community, with the current Any% record standing at 22 minutes 22 seconds held by swimfan.[^ref-37] The leaderboard lists 16 runs by 7 players; the 100% record is 23:01 by SantaClaus.[^ref-37]

### Related Publications

- **Larry's Little Black Book:** Included with the game as copy protection; featured intentionally low-quality photos of women with phone numbers to be referenced during startup[^ref-3]
- **The Official Book of Leisure Suit Larry:** Contains Al Lowe's advice including the famous "Save early and often!" guidance[^ref-22]
- **Game Manual:** Eight-page instruction booklet covering interface, commands, and gameplay basics[^ref-39]

### Critical Perspective

Sierra deliberately toned down Larry's sexual escapades for the sequel in favour of a more elaborate plot,[^ref-6][^ref-9] and the lack of the series' trademark humor became an oft-cited criticism; later games returned to the original formula.[^ref-9] "While the first game in the series was full of adult humour and jokes, Leisure Suit Larry 2 features less of an adult theme and more of an excellent plot," observed one walkthrough author.[^ref-12]

"This commitment to the 'you can never have enough jokes' school of comedy is one of the hallmarks of the Leisure Suit Larry series," noted IGN's retrospective, which singles out the parser's wordy responses "as seen in the first game and especially the second."[^ref-5]
