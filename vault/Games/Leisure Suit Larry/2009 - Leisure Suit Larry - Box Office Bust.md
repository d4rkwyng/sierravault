# Leisure Suit Larry: Box Office Bust

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Leisure Suit Larry: Box Office Bust stands as one of the most critically reviled video games ever released, earning the dubious distinction of being called "the worst game of the decade" by JustAdventure[^ref-3]; GamesRadar later ranked it 28th in its list of the 100 worst games of all time[^ref-76]. Released in 2009 for PC, PlayStation 3, and Xbox 360[^ref-8], this action-adventure platformer marked a radical departure from the series' traditional point-and-click adventure roots, featuring Larry Lovage (nephew of series protagonist Larry Laffer) navigating a 3D open-world Hollywood studio lot[^ref-57].

The game was developed by Team17 Software Limited[^ref-48] and announced by Sierra Entertainment in January 2008[^ref-67]; after Vivendi Games merged with Activision to form Activision Blizzard, the project was dropped[^ref-66][^ref-8]. Codemasters eventually picked up publishing duties under its Funsta label in February 2009[^ref-70][^ref-8]; Joystiq observed that while other titles orphaned by the merger drew concern, the game's sudden jeopardy "went undiscussed, and largely uneulogized"[^ref-70]. The final product featured voice acting from notable celebrities including Jeffrey Tambor, Carmen Electra, Shannon Elizabeth, Jay Mohr, and Patrick Warburton[^ref-34], but the star cast could not save it from overwhelmingly negative reviews[^ref-40][^ref-41][^ref-42].

## Story Summary

Box Office Bust follows Larry Lovage, nephew of series mainstay Larry Laffer, as he works a summer job at his uncle's movie studio[^ref-23]. The game's narrative centers on Larry's attempts to help his uncle Larry Laffer by doing odd jobs and uncovering a mole from a rival studio who is trying to sabotage Laffer Studios[^ref-8]. Set within the confines of a movie studio backlot, players guide Larry through various Hollywood-themed environments while completing missions that parody film industry tropes[^ref-20].

The story unfolds through 45 main story missions[^ref-60], with Larry interacting with studio staff voiced by the game's celebrity cast[^ref-34]. The plot leans heavily on sexual innuendo and crude humor; GameSpot's Chris Watters wrote that the game's humor "has all but forsaken wit and cleverness"[^ref-22].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Unlike the classic point-and-click Leisure Suit Larry games, Box Office Bust adopted a third-person 3D action-adventure format using the Unreal Engine 3[^ref-54]. The game featured open-world sandbox gameplay with exploration, platforming, racing, and puzzle-solving elements[^ref-67]. Players controlled Larry Lovage through various studio environments, completing missions that IGN described as a mix of fetch quests, timed challenges, platforming, stealth and shooting galleries[^ref-30].

The controls were one of the game's most criticized aspects: GameWatcher found the PC version "extremely frustrating to control"[^ref-27], and GameSpot called the controls "floaty"[^ref-22]. One GameFAQs walkthrough author recommended a PC trainer to get through timed and poorly controlled sections, writing that "this game is horribly made and we need to compensate for it"[^ref-13].

### Structure and Progression

The game contained 45 story missions alongside numerous side activities[^ref-60]. Players could collect 100 "Larry Awards" scattered throughout the game world, participate in 9 racing sequences, and complete 7 seduction mini-games[^ref-60]. Before release, executive producer John Melchior promised that players "will not be gated" and could explore the sandbox "at their own pace"[^ref-31].

Achievement hunters noted particular difficulty in completing the game's objectives, with one guide author stating they had "played this game five times and have never succeeded getting all achievements in a single playthrough"[^ref-59]. GamesRadar described a camera that regularly flipped itself 180 degrees for no reason, sending Larry off ledges to his death[^ref-25].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The gameplay mechanics departed significantly from the series' traditional adventure game puzzles, instead focusing on platforming, racing, combat and mini-games such as directing scenes and seducing women[^ref-67][^ref-22]. The seduction sequences, a hallmark of the franchise, became a simple conversation minigame of choosing among dialogue options, which IGN found "impossible to lose"[^ref-30].

IGN's Charles Onyett argued that even the script seemed aware of how pointless many missions were, quoting Larry's line "Oh that's right, introduce a timer to place me under undue and unnecessary pressure"[^ref-30]. The platforming was particularly problematic, with IGN citing Larry's "imprecise handling" and a temperamental camera, and GamesRadar a camera that flipped 180 degrees without warning[^ref-30][^ref-25].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Box Office Bust received overwhelmingly negative reviews on all platforms; Al Lowe noted that GameRankings had placed it "next to last place" among all games it tracked, at 16%[^ref-3]. Metacritic aggregated scores of 25/100 for Xbox 360, 20/100 for PC, and 17/100 for PlayStation 3[^ref-40][^ref-42][^ref-41]. GameRankings showed similarly dismal scores of 26.08%, 20.22%, and 16.00% respectively[^ref-73][^ref-71][^ref-75].

Charles Onyett of IGN delivered one of the most scathing reviews, stating "The lowest rating numbers here at IGN are reserved for games with nearly no redeeming qualities or interesting ideas, with next to nothing enjoyable to offer players, and which under no circumstances should be purchased by anyone. Leisure Suit Larry: Box Office Bust is, without a doubt, one of those games"[^ref-30].

### Modern Assessment

A Reddit user who played and ranked all 12 Leisure Suit Larry releases placed Box Office Bust dead last, criticizing the decision not to involve Al Lowe: "This was a big mistake and a blatant quick cash cow off a known franchise"[^ref-55]. My Abandonware users nonetheless give it a surprisingly high 4.21/5 (34 votes, October 2026), though comments remain divided[^ref-50].

Series creator Al Lowe was never part of the project: Melchior said in 2008 that "Al Lowe is not involved with this project"[^ref-31], and Lowe wrote that the companies behind it "did not consult me nor discuss anything about this game with me"[^ref-3]. Lowe responded to the game's poor reception by sarcastically thanking "VU Games, for keeping me completely away from this latest disaster!"[^ref-3].

## Development

### Origins

Box Office Bust was initially announced by Sierra Entertainment on January 17, 2008[^ref-67][^ref-72], as part of an attempt to revitalize the dormant Leisure Suit Larry franchise. Executive Producer John Melchior explained that the development team wanted to address criticisms of the previous game, Magna Cum Laude, stating "The last game was torn apart and I think it was just the wrong approach for this franchise"[^ref-31].

The development philosophy centered around creating "a good solid story, clever set ups, and great pay offs"[^ref-8], with Melchior emphasizing that "Nudity is a mask for poor gameplay. There is no Nudity in this version of Larry"[^ref-31]. The switch from adventure-game design to 3D open-world gameplay broke with the formula of the Al Lowe-era games[^ref-57].

### Production

Development was handled by Team17 Software Limited[^ref-48], best known for the Worms games[^ref-72]. The game utilized the Unreal Engine 3[^ref-54].

The production faced significant challenges when Activision Blizzard, which inherited Sierra's slate in the July 2008 merger, dropped the project[^ref-66][^ref-8], leaving the game in limbo until Codemasters picked up publishing rights[^ref-23]. One IMDb user reviewer later called the whole production "rushed"[^ref-32].

The voice cast featured established comedians and actors including Jeffrey Tambor as Larry Laffer, Josh Keaton as Larry Lovage, Carmen Electra as Ginger Vitus, Shannon Elizabeth as Amy Loveheart, and Patrick Warburton as Damone LeCoque[^ref-34]. GamesRadar's John Walker called Tambor and Jane Lynch "great actors" and said Tom Arnold gave a strong performance, but added that "the words they're saying are gibberish"[^ref-25].

### Technical Achievements

The game's technical implementation proved to be its greatest weakness rather than achievement. Built on Unreal Engine 3[^ref-54], the game suffered from glitchy movement, barely functioning interaction and a camera that regularly flipped itself 180 degrees[^ref-25].

The PlayStation 3 version fared worst: GameSpot's Chris Watters cited a choppy frame rate, screen tearing and slow-loading textures, summing it up as "poor technical performance"[^ref-24], and IGN wrote that the PS3 version's graphical performance "veers into unacceptable territory, with an erratic framerate"[^ref-30].

## Legacy

Although Sierra announced the game, it did not live to release it: by the time it shipped under Codemasters' Funsta label, GameSpot was describing Sierra as Vivendi's "now-defunct" label[^ref-23][^ref-21].

Trophy hunters still take it on; one PlayStation player who earned its platinum trophy wondered how it had been released "with how broken it is"[^ref-56]. GamesRadar's list of the 100 worst games of all time placed it 28th, citing "menial missions and borderline broken controls"[^ref-76].
