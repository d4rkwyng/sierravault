# Leisure Suit Larry 6: Shape Up or Slip Out!

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

Leisure Suit Larry 6: Shape Up or Slip Out! is the fifth installment in Al Lowe's comedic adult adventure series, despite its number (there was no Larry 4)[^ref-10][^ref-5]; Computer Gaming World's 1994 review noted that Larry had "appeared in five adventures, only four of which have been published"[^ref-41]. Released by Sierra On-Line in 1993, this entry marked a significant technical leap for the franchise, being the first Larry game to feature full voice acting and SVGA graphics in its enhanced CD-ROM version[^ref-2]. The game represents what Al Lowe described as "a return to the original" Larry formula, focusing on "more babes, more silly situations to humiliate Larry, and more babes"[^ref-3].

Set entirely at the luxurious La Costa Lotta health spa resort, the game follows Larry Laffer as he pursues the women staying there: eight spread around the resort, plus Shamara Payne in the penthouse[^ref-7]. After losing on the television game show "Stallions" and taking home the runner-up prize, a trip to La Costa Lotta, Larry finds himself in what the narrator describes as an "exclusive health spa, filled with gorgeous women"[^ref-5]. Walkthrough author Tom Hayes called it "one of the very best Larry games"[^ref-6], and Hardcore Gaming 101 judged it "definitely one of the better games in the series"[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

The game opens with Larry Laffer having won a stay at La Costa Lotta as the consolation prize for losing on the game show "Stallions"[^ref-8]. The game starts with no connection to Leisure Suit Larry 5[^ref-10]: Larry is single again, and Passionate Patti is off doing her own thing. She is mentioned only briefly by the narrator, and appears in the calendar packaged with the game[^ref-8].

Larry's goal at the resort is straightforward: meet and try his luck with the women staying at La Costa Lotta[^ref-8]. Each of these encounters provides Larry with an object that he can offer to Shamara Payne, after which the game ends[^ref-8]. The women Larry meets are creatively named after varieties of wine, including Burgundy Bodine, Charlotte Donay (Chardonnay), Rose, and Gammie Boysulay (Gamay Beaujolais)[^ref-17]. The Adventure Gamer blog treats Cavaricchi Vuarnet as the one exception, named for a line of 1980s sunglasses[^ref-14], though MobyGames reads "Cav" as Cabernet[^ref-17].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Larry 6 utilizes Sierra's SCI (Sierra Creative Interpreter) engine, specifically SCI 1.1 for the original VGA version and SCI 2.1 for the SVGA CD-ROM release[^ref-11]. The game features a point-and-click interface where players move the cursor around the screen and click to perform actions[^ref-20]. Unlike most Sierra games of the era, the icon bar remains visible at all times[^ref-8].

The interface was refined from Larry 5, with the "Use" command being divided into separate "Use" and "Pick Up" commands[^ref-7]. The resort also has a tram, driven by a character named Art[^ref-5].

### Structure and Progression

Although the narrator promises "two weeks" at the spa[^ref-5], the game itself is split into four days[^ref-24]. The women's tasks can be done in any order except for the last one[^ref-8]. Walkthrough author Tom Hayes preferred this to the previous game: "Instead of a dull adventure spread over a variety of locations, this is a fun adventure based in one large location"[^ref-6].

Deaths returned after their absence in Larry 5[^ref-2][^ref-81], although Adventure Classic Gaming counted it among the first Sierra adventures "in which you cannot die!"[^ref-27] The deaths are there for humor's sake, since the "Try Again" button lets the player reverse the mistake without restoring a saved game[^ref-8]. One modern reviewer felt the option takes away "some of the fear of getting a bad ending when you haven't saved your game in a while"[^ref-29]. Even so, at least one dead end remains from which the game cannot be completed[^ref-8].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The puzzles have drawn particular praise. MobyGames user reviewer Unicorn Lynx called them "by far the best puzzles in the series so far", with "a nigh immaculate system with convoluted, intricate tasks woven together in a clever and yet appropriately twisted, comedic way"[^ref-30]. The puzzles focus primarily on solving each woman's personal problems or desires, requiring Larry to explore the resort and gather appropriate items or information[^ref-31].

The difficulty was deliberately relaxed compared to earlier Sierra titles, and it is impossible to get stuck without the proper items[^ref-8]. Al Lowe's design philosophy emphasized accessibility, stating in the game manual his intention to "provide an area where the player could roam around and see almost anything within the first few minutes of play, and yet provide enough depth of play that the game would challenge most players"[^ref-3].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Larry 6 received generally positive reviews from gaming publications, though critics were sharply divided, some enjoying it and others unable to stand it[^ref-18].

| Publication | Score | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Electronic Games | 92% | Sara Slaymaker[^ref-18] |
| PC Gamer (UK) | 83% | Phil South, January 1994[^ref-8] |
| Joystick | Technique 65, Intérêt 80 (average 72.5/100) | #52, September 1994, p. 109; review of the SVGA CD-ROM version[^ref-87] |
| Hyper | 50% | David Wildgoose, #3, February 1994[^ref-18][^ref-82] |
| PC Format | 45% | Richard Longhurst[^ref-18] |
| Next Generation | 3/5 stars | #9, September 1995, pp. 99-100; Macintosh version[^ref-88] |
| White Wolf | 3/5 | James V. Trunzo, #49, November 1994[^ref-8] |

Computer Gaming World's Allen Greenberg provided a nuanced assessment, describing the game as "a very clever game that will easily offend the puritan-minded, just as it might offend the experienced gamer looking for a serious puzzle challenge"[^ref-18]. He concluded: "For the novice who enjoys a touch of risqué entertainment and good, off-color jokes, *LSL6* is a very novel and enjoyable source of Titillation and Amusement"[^ref-41].

The more negative reviews focused on the series' perceived stagnation. David Wildgoose of Hyper magazine dismissed it as "really desperate stuff," arguing that "Larry was funny when he had some novelty value. Now he's just a sad old sexist pervert"[^ref-18][^ref-82]. Similarly, PC Format's Richard Longhurst wrote, "If you're amused by flatulence, tampons, vibrators and masturbating, then Larry 6 is for you"[^ref-18].

### Aggregate Scores

MobyGames lists a Moby Score of 7.7 from 108 votes (read via the MobyGames API on November 11, 2026)[^ref-2].

### Modern Assessment

Several retrospective write-ups have been warmer. A 2025 review at 3rd World Geeks noted that "out of all the original Leisure Suit Larry games, this nearly is my favorite game in the series"[^ref-29]. The reviewer particularly praised the character graphics, stating "I honestly believe these are some of the best looking pixelated pictures of women in video games"[^ref-29].

Unicorn Lynx's MobyGames review also compared it favorably with its predecessor: "Larry 6 is everything its predecessor was not: it is large, difficult, very funny, and rich in interaction and exploration"[^ref-30].

## Development

### Origins

Al Lowe set out his design goals in the game's manual: "In some ways, a return to the original." He continued: "No long auto-pilot cartoons. No Passionate Patti to provide political-correctness. No involved heavy plot. Just more babes, more silly situations to humiliate Larry, and more babes."[^ref-3]

### Production

The game's development team included several key Sierra personnel. Al Lowe served as designer, writer, director, and producer[^ref-51]. The voice cast was led by Jan Rabson as Larry Laffer and Neil Ross as the narrator[^ref-52]. The supporting cast included notable voice actors such as Mary Kay Bergman, who voiced multiple characters including Cav Vuarnet and Char Donay[^ref-53].

Bil Skirvin was art director[^ref-51]. Lowe later wrote that he came up with "The Girls of Larry 6" calendar, packed in the box, "after seeing the beautiful women Bil Skirvin and his artists created" for the game[^ref-9]. Adventure Classic Gaming's review noted that "The contrast between hand-drawn background art and cartoonish characters adds a distinctive flavor to the game"[^ref-27]. Lowe's design document planned this look: the game "juxtaposes 'Toon-like' buildings set in photo-realistic backgrounds", with "Unrealistic Toon people" set against realistically drawn women, adding that "This contrast of photographs, rendered art, and Toon characters even looks like fun"[^ref-18].

Dan Kehler composed and arranged the music; Al Lowe wrote the Larry theme and played alto saxophone on the opening[^ref-8][^ref-57]. Chris Braymen is credited for trombone solos among the game's "MIDIfied Musicians"[^ref-51]. Lowe and Josh Mandel wrote the words and music for "Cell Block Love", sung by the country singer Char Donay; Mandel's concept had Marie from the Police Quest games tell how she fell in love with Sonny, the star of that series[^ref-57].

### Technical Achievements

The original 1993 floppy release ran in 320x200 VGA; the 1994 CD edition added an SVGA 640x480 mode and also kept the lower-resolution VGA mode[^ref-8]. That made Larry 6 the first Larry game with SVGA graphics[^ref-2]. The VGA version was also re-released on CD-ROM in 1994 with added speech[^ref-11].

The CD-ROM version also added voice acting, a first for the series; Hardcore Gaming 101 found "most of which is quite excellent"[^ref-7].

Adventure Classic Gaming lists minimum requirements of MS-DOS 5.0 or Windows 3.1, a 486DX33 processor, 8 MB RAM, and a double-speed CD-ROM drive[^ref-27]. Sources differ: Sierra Help gives MS-DOS 5.2, any 486, and 4 MB RAM[^ref-64]. Both list a SoundBlaster-compatible sound card[^ref-27][^ref-64]. For the original floppy-era release, Computer Gaming World's review data box listed a price of $69.95 and these requirements: DOS version, a 286 at 16MHz or better, 10MB of hard drive space and a mouse; Windows version, a 386SX or better, 2MB RAM and Windows 3.1; supported sound cards were AdLib, General MIDI, PAS-16, Roland, Sound Blaster and UltraSound[^ref-41].

### Cut Content

Al Lowe's design document called for a tenth woman, Frau Milchlieb, described as an "excessively rich, over-weight, over-sexed, middle-aged, married, Germanic tourist" at La Costa Lotta, who was to sit in the Dining Room. She was cut. A leftover remains in the finished game: the room service tray from which Larry picks up the mineral water stands in front of the room that was to be hers[^ref-17]. The same design document also had the gate guard coming from Sweden and the tram driver from Hawaii, a more multicultural cast than the finished game's[^ref-17]. The released game has nine women in all, a count that matches Computer Gaming World's description of Larry striving to fulfil the requests of "nine different women"[^ref-17][^ref-41].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- The title is a play on the navy saying "Shape up or ship out"[^ref-17].
- The artists drew the tram driver as a likeness of Al Lowe, who then renamed him "Art", in allusion to a friend of his who looks like him, and to spite the art department[^ref-17].
- Eight of the nine women are named after wines; Shamara Payne is Champagne[^ref-17].
- MobyGames lists the alternate titles "Larry 6", "Leisure Suit Larry 6", the Israeli title "Larry in the Jacuzzi", the German "Leisure Suit Larry 6 - Reiss auf oder schieb' ab" and the Polish "Larry 6: Z impetem w głąb"[^ref-2].
- Larry 6 ends in a way that continues at the start of Leisure Suit Larry 7: Love for Sail![^ref-10]
- MobyGames records three versions of the game: the Windows version at 640x480 in 256 colours, the DOS 320x200 version, and a DOS hi-res version identical to the Windows one; the 1994 CD release added full speech[^ref-17].

## Legacy

Larry 6 sold well. It debuted at #6 on PC Research's computer game sales chart for November 1993 (Computer Gaming World's "What's Hot" list) and then ranked seventh in December 1993, sixth in January 1994 and seventh in February 1994[^ref-83][^ref-84][^ref-85][^ref-86]. Sierra's 1996 annual report said it had released five Leisure Suit Larry titles plus an anthology and sold more than 1.4 million copies in the series[^ref-66]. Al Lowe later wrote that his latest Larry game, "like all of its predecessors", had "sold over a quarter-million copies"[^ref-67].

Larry 6 has its own speedrun.com leaderboard[^ref-70]. The Any% record stands at 22:08 (swimfan, 2024); the linked 2019 100% run by chopheavymetal (23:57) ranks second in its category[^ref-70][^ref-71].

The game remains on sale at GOG, with both its VGA and SVGA versions included in GOG's Leisure Suit Larry package (Leisure Suit Larry's Greatest Hits and Misses)[^ref-72][^ref-74][^ref-11]. Assemble Entertainment released it on Steam on December 18, 2017; that listing has since been delisted[^ref-89]. GOG's re-release runs the VGA version through ScummVM and the SVGA remake through DOSBox[^ref-11]; the DOSBox wiki rates the game "Seems fine, but haven't fully tested" under DOSBox 0.74[^ref-73].

### Collections

The Sierra Chest lists Larry 6 in these compilations: Al Lowe's Abenteuerliche Adventure Schatztruhe, Leisure Suit Larry's Greatest Hits and Misses (VGA non-talky release), the Leisure Suit Larry Collection Series (VGA non-talky release), Leisure Suit Larry: Ultimate Pleasure Pack (SVGA talky release), Best of Sierra Nr.14 (German release) and the Leisure Suit Larry Collection (XP) (VGA non-talky release)[^ref-10]. The GOG.com and ZOOM Platform bundles are based on the 1997 Leisure Suit Larry: Collection Series; the ZOOM Platform copy was delisted on March 31, 2023[^ref-11].
