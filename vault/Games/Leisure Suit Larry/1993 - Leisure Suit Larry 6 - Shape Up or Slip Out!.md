# Leisure Suit Larry 6: Shape Up or Slip Out!

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Leisure Suit Larry 6: Shape Up or Slip Out! is the fifth installment in Al Lowe's comedic adult adventure series, despite its number (there was no Larry 4)[^ref-10][^ref-5]. Released by Sierra On-Line in 1993, this entry marked a significant technical leap for the franchise, being the first Larry game to feature full voice acting and SVGA graphics in its enhanced CD-ROM version[^ref-2]. The game represents what Al Lowe described as "a return to the original" Larry formula, focusing on "more babes, more silly situations to humiliate Larry, and more babes"[^ref-3].

Set entirely at the luxurious La Costa Lotta health spa resort, the game follows Larry Laffer as he pursues the women staying there: eight spread around the resort, plus Shamara Payne in the penthouse[^ref-7]. After losing on the television game show "Stallions" and taking home the runner-up prize, a trip to La Costa Lotta, Larry finds himself in what the narrator describes as an "exclusive health spa, filled with gorgeous women"[^ref-5]. Walkthrough author Tom Hayes called it "one of the very best Larry games"[^ref-6], and Hardcore Gaming 101 judged it "definitely one of the better games in the series"[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

The game opens with Larry Laffer having won a stay at La Costa Lotta as the consolation prize for losing on the game show "Stallions"[^ref-8]. The game starts with no connection to Leisure Suit Larry 5[^ref-10]: Larry is single again, and Passionate Patti is off doing her own thing. She is mentioned only briefly by the narrator, and appears in the calendar packaged with the game[^ref-8].

Larry's goal at the resort is straightforward: meet and try his luck with the women staying at La Costa Lotta[^ref-8]. Each of these encounters provides Larry with an object that he can offer to Shamara Payne, after which the game ends[^ref-8]. The women Larry meets are creatively named after varieties of wine, including Burgundy Bodine, Charlotte Donay (Chardonnay), Rose, and Gammie Boysulay (Gamay Beaujolais)[^ref-17]. The Adventure Gamer blog treats Cavaricchi Vuarnet as the one exception, named for a line of 1980s sunglasses[^ref-14], though MobyGames reads "Cav" as Cabernet[^ref-17].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Larry 6 utilizes Sierra's SCI (Sierra Creative Interpreter) engine, specifically SCI 1.1 for the original VGA version and SCI 2.1 for the SVGA CD-ROM release[^ref-11]. The game features a point-and-click interface where players move the cursor around the screen and click to perform actions[^ref-20]. Unlike most Sierra games of the era, the icon bar remains visible at all times[^ref-8].

The interface was refined from Larry 5, with the "Use" command being divided into separate "Use" and "Pick Up" commands[^ref-7]. The resort also has a tram, driven by a character named Art[^ref-5].

### Structure and Progression

Although the narrator promises "two weeks" at the spa[^ref-5], the game itself is split into four days[^ref-24]. The women's tasks can be done in any order except for the last one[^ref-8]. Walkthrough author Tom Hayes preferred this to the previous game: "Instead of a dull adventure spread over a variety of locations, this is a fun adventure based in one large location"[^ref-26].

Deaths returned after their absence in Larry 5[^ref-29], though so gently, with an instant "Try Again", that one reviewer counted it among the first Sierra adventures "in which you cannot die!"[^ref-27] The deaths are there for humor's sake, since the "Try Again" button lets the player reverse the mistake without restoring a saved game[^ref-8]. One modern reviewer felt the option takes away "some of the fear of getting a bad ending when you haven't saved your game in a while"[^ref-29]. Even so, at least one dead end remains from which the game cannot be completed[^ref-8].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The puzzles have drawn particular praise. MobyGames user reviewer Unicorn Lynx called them "by far the best puzzles in the series so far", with "a nigh immaculate system with convoluted, intricate tasks woven together in a clever and yet appropriately twisted, comedic way"[^ref-30]. The puzzles focus primarily on solving each woman's personal problems or desires, requiring Larry to explore the resort and gather appropriate items or information[^ref-31].

The difficulty was deliberately relaxed compared to earlier Sierra titles, and it is impossible to get stuck without the proper items[^ref-8]. Al Lowe's design philosophy emphasized accessibility, stating in the game manual his intention to "provide an area where the player could roam around and see almost anything within the first few minutes of play, and yet provide enough depth of play that the game would challenge most players"[^ref-33].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Larry 6 received generally positive reviews from gaming publications, though scores varied significantly based on the reviewer's tolerance for the series' adult humor.

| Publication | Score | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Electronic Games | 92% | Sara Slaymaker[^ref-34] |
| PC Gamer (UK) | 83% | Phil South, January 1994[^ref-35] |
| Joystick | 72.5/100 | September 1994[^ref-36] |
| Hyper | 50% | David Wildgoose[^ref-37] |
| PC Format | 45% | Richard Longhurst[^ref-38] |
| Next Generation | 3/5 stars | September 1995[^ref-39] |
| White Wolf | 3/5 | James V. Trunzo, #49, November 1994[^ref-8] |

Computer Gaming World's Allen Greenberg provided a nuanced assessment, describing the game as "a very clever game that will easily offend the puritan-minded, just as it might offend the experienced gamer looking for a serious puzzle challenge"[^ref-40]. He concluded: "For the novice who enjoys a touch of risqué entertainment and good, off-color jokes, *LSL6* is a very novel and enjoyable source of Titillation and Amusement"[^ref-41].

The more negative reviews focused on the series' perceived stagnation. David Wildgoose of Hyper magazine dismissed it as "really desperate stuff," arguing that "Larry was funny when he had some novelty value. Now he's just a sad old sexist pervert"[^ref-42]. Similarly, PC Format's Richard Longhurst wrote, "If you're amused by flatulence, tampons, vibrators and masturbating, then Larry 6 is for you"[^ref-43].

### Modern Assessment

Several retrospective write-ups have been warmer. A 2025 review at 3rd World Geeks noted that "out of all the original Leisure Suit Larry games, this nearly is my favorite game in the series"[^ref-44]. The reviewer particularly praised the character graphics, stating "I honestly believe these are some of the best looking pixelated pictures of women in video games"[^ref-45].

Unicorn Lynx's MobyGames review also compared it favorably with its predecessor: "Larry 6 is everything its predecessor was not: it is large, difficult, very funny, and rich in interaction and exploration"[^ref-46].

## Development

### Origins

Al Lowe set out his design goals in the game's manual: "In some ways, a return to the original." He continued: "No long auto-pilot cartoons. No Passionate Patti to provide political-correctness. No involved heavy plot. Just more babes, more silly situations to humiliate Larry, and more babes."[^ref-50]

### Production

The game's development team included several key Sierra personnel. Al Lowe served as designer, writer, director, and producer[^ref-51]. The voice cast was led by Jan Rabson as Larry Laffer and Neil Ross as the narrator[^ref-52]. The supporting cast included notable voice actors such as Mary Kay Bergman, who voiced multiple characters including Cav Vuarnet and Char Donay[^ref-53].

Bil Skirvin was art director[^ref-51]. Lowe later wrote that he came up with "The Girls of Larry 6" calendar, packed in the box, "after seeing the beautiful women Bil Skirvin and his artists created" for the game[^ref-54]. Adventure Classic Gaming's review noted that "The contrast between hand-drawn background art and cartoonish characters adds a distinctive flavor to the game"[^ref-55]. Lowe's design document planned this look: the game "juxtaposes 'Toon-like' buildings set in photo-realistic backgrounds", with "Unrealistic Toon people" set against realistically drawn women, adding that "This contrast of photographs, rendered art, and Toon characters even looks like fun"[^ref-18].

Dan Kehler composed and arranged the music; Al Lowe wrote the Larry theme and played alto saxophone on the opening[^ref-8][^ref-57]. Lowe and Josh Mandel wrote the words and music for "Cell Block Love", sung by the country singer Char Donay; Mandel's concept had Marie from the Police Quest games tell how she fell in love with Sonny, the star of that series[^ref-58].

### Technical Achievements

The original 1993 floppy release ran in 320x200 VGA; the 1994 CD edition added an SVGA 640x480 mode and also kept the lower-resolution VGA mode[^ref-8]. That made Larry 6 the first Larry game with SVGA graphics[^ref-2]. The VGA version was also re-released on CD-ROM in 1994 with added speech[^ref-11].

The CD-ROM version also added voice acting, a first for the series; Hardcore Gaming 101 found "most of which is quite excellent"[^ref-61].

Adventure Classic Gaming lists minimum requirements of MS-DOS 5.0 or Windows 3.1, a 486DX33 processor, 8 MB RAM, and a double-speed CD-ROM drive[^ref-27]. Sources differ: Sierra Help gives MS-DOS 5.2, any 486, and 4 MB RAM[^ref-64]. Both list a SoundBlaster-compatible sound card[^ref-27][^ref-64].

## Legacy

Larry 6 sold well. It debuted at #6 on PC Research's computer game sales chart for November 1993 and placed sixth or seventh for the next three months[^ref-8]. Sierra's 1996 annual report said it had released five Leisure Suit Larry titles plus an anthology and sold more than 1.4 million copies in the series[^ref-66]. Al Lowe later wrote that his latest Larry game, "like all of its predecessors", had "sold over a quarter-million copies"[^ref-67].

Larry 6 has its own speedrun.com leaderboard[^ref-70]. The Any% record stands at 22:08 (swimfan, 2024); the linked 2019 100% run by chopheavymetal (23:57) ranks second in its category[^ref-70][^ref-71].

The game remains available through digital distribution platforms, included with both its VGA and SVGA versions in GOG's Leisure Suit Larry package (Leisure Suit Larry's Greatest Hits and Misses)[^ref-72][^ref-74][^ref-11]. GOG's re-release runs through ScummVM[^ref-11]; the DOSBox wiki rates the game "Seems fine, but haven't fully tested" under DOSBox 0.74[^ref-73].
