# Leisure Suit Larry: Wet Dreams Don't Dry

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Leisure Suit Larry: Wet Dreams Don't Dry is the ninth installment in the Leisure Suit Larry series[^ref-3] and is, as The Cutting Room Floor puts it, "the first new Larry title in over a decade," returning to the 2D point-and-click format[^ref-22]. Released in 2018 by German developer CrazyBunch and published by Assemble Entertainment[^ref-2] (November 7, 2018 for Windows and Mac; June 13, 2019 for Switch and PS4; September 15, 2020 for Xbox One[^ref-1]; an iOS version followed on August 7, 2019[^ref-50]), the game catapults the iconic Larry Laffer from the late 1980s directly into the modern era of smartphones, social media, and dating apps[^ref-3]. One user reviewer called it "a classic fish out of water story"[^ref-12], and God is a Geek described bringing Larry into the 21st century as "an interesting choice, and one that certainly needs delicately balancing"[^ref-4].

The game received mixed reviews, with Metacritic scores of 72 for the PC version and 52 for the Nintendo Switch version[^ref-5], 71 for PlayStation 4[^ref-52] and 72 for Xbox One[^ref-53]. Critics praised the return to classic point-and-click gameplay and hand-drawn visuals while expressing concerns about whether Larry's brand of humor could translate appropriately to modern sensibilities[^ref-6]. Notably, series creator Al Lowe was not involved in this revival, learning about the project only when it was publicly announced in May 2018[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

The narrative begins with Larry Laffer awakening in the 21st century after being held in an underground laboratory since the 1980s[^ref-6]. Finding himself in the modern world of New Lost Wages, Larry discovers that "many of the people I knew are dead... everything has changed... I don't know this world"[^ref-3]. The central plot revolves around Larry's quest to win the affections of Faith, the assistant to Prune boss Bill "BJ" Jobs, who "only dates men with a perfect score on the dating app Timber"[^ref-1].

To achieve his romantic goals, Larry must navigate the complexities of modern dating culture, including mastering the Timber app (a parody of Tinder)[^ref-3][^ref-9]. The game's structure follows Larry as he attempts various schemes to improve his dating profile score, leading him through encounters with numerous characters across New Lost Wages[^ref-10]. The story culminates in Larry discovering that Faith has been using a male figurehead named BJ to run her tech company, as "the business would not have thrived if it was known a woman was behind everything"[^ref-1].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Wet Dreams Don't Dry returns to the classic point-and-click adventure format that made the original series famous[^ref-11]. In place of the old Sierra verb-based interface it uses modern point-and-click controls, with gamepad and touch controls on Switch[^ref-12], and items in Larry's inventory can be combined to solve puzzles[^ref-16]. Central to the experience is the smartphone Larry is given near the start, with apps parodying social networks such as "Farcebook" (Facebook) and "Instacrap" (Instagram)[^ref-3][^ref-14]; Screen Rant found some of these app names among the game's weakest jokes[^ref-13].

At PAX West 2018 the developer said there were over a hundred items to find, which Operation Rainfall expected would lead to "very strange ways that they need to be used and/or combined"[^ref-14]. The game's visual style is entirely hand-drawn[^ref-14].

### Structure and Progression

The game is structured around Larry's quest to achieve a perfect Timber rating of 90 points[^ref-3][^ref-15]. Marooners' Rock estimated that the adventure lasts around eight to ten hours[^ref-16], and the publisher's store description promises over 30 hand-drawn venues and over 30 hand-drawn characters[^ref-2].

Parts of the story are told through cut-scenes resembling Instagram stories in the Instacrap app[^ref-14]. The PC version has 31 Steam achievements[^ref-18]; the PlayStation 4 version has 32 trophies, and its trophy guide notes that the game has no autosave and no chapter select[^ref-11].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The puzzle design follows traditional adventure game logic, though Common Sense Media found that "many descend into the realm of frustratingly nonsensical, requiring seemingly endless trial and error"[^ref-20]. Each Timber date asks Larry to complete a task before his profile score goes up, and the order in which those tasks are done is left to the player[^ref-15]. One section has the player control the past Larry "in all his eight-bit glory," which Destructoid saw as part of the game confronting the series' "questionable legacy"[^ref-6].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The game received mixed reviews[^ref-5][^ref-48]. Professional reviewers generally praised the visual presentation and return to classic adventure gaming while expressing reservations about the humor's modern relevance.

| Publication | Score | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Ragequit.gr | 95/100 | "Extraordinary puzzle design and brilliant writing"[^ref-23] |
| PC Games | 82/100 | "Keeps the charm and humor of the first games"[^ref-23] |
| Adventure Gamers | 80/100 | "Excellent addition to the Larry brand"[^ref-23] |
| Destructoid | 7/10 | "Solid and definitely has an audience"[^ref-6] |
| Screen Rant | 6/10 | "a bit of fun, and most of that comes at Larry's expense"[^ref-13] |
| Gaming Respawn | 5/10 | "Feels like a relic of what gaming used to be"[^ref-15] |

### Modern Assessment

Contemporary criticism focused on the challenge of updating Larry's character for modern audiences. Common Sense Media noted the game was "content to more or less disregard the \#MeToo movement, not only allowing Larry to get away with making inappropriate comments to and hit on just about every woman he meets"[^ref-20]. However, Screen Rant's Rob Gordon observed that the game "feels much less predatory than it could do, with characters - both men and women - more accepting to Larry's 'charms'"[^ref-13].

A GameFAQs user review rated it 8/10, stating: "While it is a shame that Al Lowe couldn't be a part of this game, I think Crazy Bunch has done an excellent job bringing an old game like Leisure Suit Larry into the 21st century"[^ref-12]. The visuals were a common bright spot: Gaming Respawn praised its "amazing hand-drawn art style"[^ref-15], and even the critical Indie Game Reviewer acknowledged "the attention to detail in the environments"[^ref-47].

## Development

### Origins

The project began when German publisher Assemble Entertainment acquired the rights to the original Larry games from Codemasters and was allowed to produce its own new Larry title[^ref-7], developed by CrazyBunch[^ref-2]. At PAX West 2018, Operation Rainfall reported that the game's director described himself and his team as fans of the classic Sierra and LucasArts adventure games who hoped to live up to that legacy[^ref-14]. The story picks up shortly after the events of the original Land of the Lounge Lizards[^ref-16]; one GameFAQs guide writer describes it as "retconning anything that came after LSL1"[^ref-21].

Notably absent from development was series creator Al Lowe, who "only found out about the game when it was first announced to the public in May 2018, six months before the game's release"[^ref-7]. Lowe expressed his feelings about the project's title, stating: "if that title is the best they could come up with, I'm glad I'm not."[^ref-9]

### Production

The game was developed using Unity engine (specifically build 2017.4.29f1)[^ref-26] with a team of 61 people credited, including 33 developers and 28 acknowledgments[^ref-8].

After fan backlash to the new voice heard in the first trailer, the producers recast the role at the last minute with original Larry voice actor Jan Rabson; the replaced actor had already recorded around 25% of Larry's dialogue[^ref-7]. The soundtrack was composed by Johannes Semm and Kai Rosenkranz[^ref-28] and is sold separately as DLC on Steam[^ref-37].

### Technical Achievements

The game was released for Windows, macOS, PlayStation 4, Nintendo Switch and Xbox One[^ref-1], plus iOS[^ref-50]. PCGamingWiki lists Windows Vista as the minimum PC operating system[^ref-26], while GOG lists Windows 7/8/10[^ref-36]. Post-release support included patches rolled out differently between Steam and GOG.com platforms, with Steam typically receiving updates first[^ref-31].

After fan comments that the original ending seemed abrupt[^ref-7], a free "Happy Ending Update" was released on Steam on November 7, 2019 and on GOG.com on November 14, 2019[^ref-31]; it reached the Switch version by January 2020[^ref-32]. This content served as an epilogue expanding upon Larry's misadventures and providing closure to the Faith storyline[^ref-32].

## Legacy

Wet Dreams Don't Dry returned Larry to point-and-click adventures more than a decade after Magna Cum Laude and Box Office Bust[^ref-33]. A sequel, "Leisure Suit Larry: Wet Dreams Dry Twice," followed in October 2020[^ref-34]. Looking back in 2026, PC Gamer's Andy Chalk called the first game "a genuinely pleasant surprise, in that it's actually good"[^ref-51]. Critics and fans generally agreed that despite its flaws, the game "got much closer to the classic games in terms of story, humor, and gameplay than the rather lackluster Magna Cum Laude and Box Office Bust"[^ref-35].

Reviewers framed the game around whether Larry could still work for modern audiences: God is a Geek concluded that it "manages to avoid being completely out of touch and irrelevant to modern audiences"[^ref-4], and Destructoid called it "Solid and definitely has an audience"[^ref-6].

In October 2026, Assemble announced that both Wet Dreams games would leave the Steam store after October 23, 2026, saying only that Larry was "getting ready to hang up his leisure suit"[^ref-49].
