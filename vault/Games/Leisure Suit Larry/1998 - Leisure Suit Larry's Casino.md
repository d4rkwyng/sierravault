# Leisure Suit Larry's Casino

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

*Leisure Suit Larry's Casino* is a casino simulation game developed and published by Sierra On-Line, released on June 30, 1998 for Windows (MobyGames gives July 17, 1998 for the US release).[^ref-1][^ref-2] The game is a spinoff from the main Leisure Suit Larry adventure series: Sierra's promotional site announced that Larry Laffer "has been named manager of his very own Vegas-style gaming resort."[^ref-17] Series creator [[Al Lowe]] is credited as designer, writer and director,[^ref-2] and his 1997 design document describes the game as using "the existing Hoyle's Casino code base as a starting point for a totally new product."[^ref-18]

The official description lists five casino games, three party games, a full gift shop, three restaurants, three bars, and "a few surprises."[^ref-2] Players picked a persona from characters of earlier Larry games and could play against computerized opponents drawn from those games, or against live opponents via WON, which Sierra's site called "Cendant's on-line gaming service."[^ref-17] Cendant was Sierra's parent company at the time,[^ref-19] and online play was free.[^ref-6][^ref-17]

The game's roots go back to "LarryLand," a Lowe concept that was eventually realized only in part on The Sierra Network (TSN), later known as ImagiNatioN (INN).[^ref-3][^ref-18] The game was included in *Leisure Suit Larry: The Ultimate Pleasure Pack* (1999), a four-CD compilation that added *Larry's Casino* and the full *Love for Sail!* to the contents of the earlier Larry collection.[^ref-7][^ref-16]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames classifies the game as first-person with an isometric visual presentation.[^ref-2] Players can move through the casino scene by scene, which Hardcore Gaming 101 notes are "just static pictures," or use a map that jumps straight to any location.[^ref-14][^ref-17] The game is controlled with mouse and keyboard.[^ref-2][^ref-17]

Off-line, four randomly selected characters from earlier Larry games sit across the table, with Larry himself as "dealer, croupier or stickman." Online, up to four other real players, each using one of ten personas, can join through WON. Chat appears in cartoon balloons, and typed emoticons (or eight emotion buttons) change the persona's facial expression.[^ref-17]

### Casino Games

Sierra listed five casino games, each with three tables at different betting limits; blackjack and poker add two off-line tournament tables.[^ref-17]

- **Blackjack** – The classic card game where players attempt to reach 21 without going over
- **Poker** – Variations include Seven Card Stud, Seven Card Stud Hi-Lo, Texas Hold'em, Omaha Hold'em, Omaha Hold'em Hi-Lo, and an online-only Five Card Draw[^ref-5]
- **Roulette** – The traditional wheel-spinning betting game
- **Craps** – Dice-based casino betting
- **Slots** – Quarter, dollar and five-dollar machines[^ref-17]

The game offers a Wheel of Fortune option, but IGN reported that the game "was shoved out the door before the WOF game could be programmed."[^ref-5] A player walkthrough confirms it does not work and that the game's configuration file ships with `WheelOfFortune=OFF`.[^ref-15]

### Party Games and Social Features

Sierra described "three on-line only party games," playable by up to 15 players. Everyone votes for their favorite entry, and votes award the writer Larrybucks from the casino's money, not the voters' own:[^ref-17]

- **Say What?!** – Players invent funny definitions for submitted words
- **Pickup Master** – Players write pick-up lines on a submitted topic; Sierra warned that all lines became Al Lowe's property, and that he promised to use the best (and worst) in a future Larry adventure
- **Larry's Comedy Club** – An online joke-telling contest

Lowe's 1997 design document had planned four party games, including a story-writing game called "The Bottom Line."[^ref-18]

### Virtual Economy

Off-line gambling is "practice only," with unlimited money. Online, the bankroll is tracked between sessions, and a player who goes broke must earn money back in the party games.[^ref-17] Online winnings are paid in "Larrybucks," which can be spent on:[^ref-17]

- Virtual gifts, drinks and food
- Hotel room upgrades at the front desk
- Gifts from the casino gift shop to give to other players
- A visit to the Quiki-Wed Chapel for a virtual wedding, with Larry doing his Elvis impersonation

The chat areas include three restaurants (The Food Trough, Planet Larry, and Molto Dinnero) and three bars (Shooters, Le Trapeze Bar, and The Cock and Bull). Each hotel room has a CYBERLARRY 2000 machine that tells jokes.[^ref-17]

### Characters

Players could select their online persona from a roster of characters drawn primarily from *Leisure Suit Larry 7: Love for Sail!*, with Cavaricchi Vuarnet from *Leisure Suit Larry 6: Shape Up or Slip Out!* also available.[^ref-5] Jan Rabson voices Larry Laffer, as in the adventure games.[^ref-2]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

IGN's Chris Buckman scored the game 6.2/10 ("Okay") in a review dated September 2, 1998.[^ref-5] He found it "much better than those budget titles," meaning the Larry casino games Sierra had made a few years earlier, but cautioned that "how much you enjoy it depends on what you're looking for in a gambling simulation."[^ref-5] He called it "a decent buy" for players who liked the characters from the recent Larry adventures and enjoyed chatting with people online for free. He criticized the lack of a way to set the number of players at a table in single-player mode, and the way choosing to play online dumped players into a general game lobby.[^ref-5] Players who wanted a full casino simulation, with options for customizing each game and tutorials on winning strategy, were "probably better off looking elsewhere."[^ref-5]

Metacritic has no Metascore for the game. Its summary blurb calls the pairing of the Larry franchise and a casino game "a match made in sleaze heaven."[^ref-8]

### Modern Assessment

Hardcore Gaming 101 found that "other than the constantly-playing Larry theme song, there's very little of the usual humor, and not much reason to play it beyond any other simple casino sim, especially since the online functions are nerfed."[^ref-14]

**Known Scores:**
- **IGN:** 6.2/10[^ref-5]
- **IMDb:** 6.4/10 (33 ratings)[^ref-1]
- **MobyGames:** critics 69% (10 ratings); players 3.4/5 (11 ratings)[^ref-2]

## Development

### Origins in LarryLand

Al Lowe's 1997 design document traces the game to LarryLand. Eight years earlier, LarryLand had been "a modem-based multi-player adventure game" that was "the impetus behind the birth of INN." It was dropped after six months of waiting for a working multiplayer engine.[^ref-18] Three years later, the network "implemented a shadow of the original design creating a Larry-theme casino with adult on-line chat, but little more. Still, for its time, it was extremely popular."[^ref-18] That TSN area offered casino games and a virtual bar for adults.[^ref-9]

Lowe then built a standalone retail product on Sierra's Hoyle casino software. His design document says *Larry's Casino* "includes all the Hoyle's stand-alone gambling games, but with total Internet involvement," and that "it is aimed at an audience Hoyle's has missed completely."[^ref-18][^ref-3]

### Production

Al Lowe designed, wrote and directed the game, and also served as voice casting director.[^ref-2] It is the last Sierra On-Line title in Wikipedia's list of his game credits.[^ref-10] Funding for *Leisure Suit Larry 8* was cut, Sierra's adventure game department was disbanded, and Lowe left Sierra on February 22, 1999.[^ref-16] The credits list Cheryl Sweeney as producer, Steve Conrad as programming lead, Tracy Boyd as art lead, and Gary Spinrad for music and sound effects.[^ref-2]

Development took place while Sierra was owned by CUC International, whose acquisition of Sierra closed in July 1996. CUC merged with HFS in December 1997 to become Cendant, which announced the sale of its consumer software division to Havas in November 1998.[^ref-19]

The online component used WON (World Opponent Network). Sierra had rebranded its Sierra Internet Gaming System (SIGS) as WON in December 1997, and the service officially launched in April 1998.[^ref-19] Lowe's design document set the goal to "completely integrate SIGS into all the games."[^ref-18]

### Technology

PCGamingWiki documents a fixed 640x480 window that cannot be resized, DirectDraw 5, and CRI Middleware audio. Settings and saved data are kept in `LCASINO.INI`.[^ref-13]

### Easter Eggs

Al Lowe appears in the game as a man in a barrel running past the slot machines.[^ref-1] Clicking the in-game credits shows an advertisement for *Larry 8: Lust in Space*, which was never completed or released.[^ref-2]

## Legacy

### Collections

*Leisure Suit Larry's Casino* was included in the following compilation releases:[^ref-2]
- **Leisure Suit Larry: The Ultimate Pleasure Pack** (1999) – A four-CD collection that added *Larry's Casino* and the full *Love for Sail!* to the earlier Larry collection's contents[^ref-7][^ref-16]
- **Best of Sierra Nr. 17** (2000)[^ref-2]

### Related Products

The game has connections to Sierra's earlier Larry-themed casino software:

- **Crazy Nick's Software Picks: Leisure Suit Larry's Casino** (1992) – An earlier budget release containing only three games (Slot Machine, Blackjack, and Poker), where money earned carried over between games[^ref-3][^ref-20]
- **LarryLand** (1992) – The Larry-themed casino and chat area on TSN[^ref-9][^ref-16]

### Online Service Shutdown

The WON service is no longer available, so the game's online features no longer work.[^ref-13] Activision closed the remaining WON servers on November 1, 2008.[^ref-19] Players today can only play the casino games off-line against computer opponents.[^ref-3]

### Promotional Materials

When Sierra updated their Larry website to promote the game, they had Jan Rabson (the voice of Larry Laffer) record special audio jokes for the promotional campaign.[^ref-3]

## Technical Information

### System Requirements

From Sierra's official product page:[^ref-17][^ref-13]
- **Operating System:** Windows 95
- **Processor:** Pentium 90 or better
- **RAM:** 16 MB
- **Hard Disk:** 120 MB
- **CD-ROM:** 2X
- **Online play:** 28.8 modem or better with an Internet connection

### Known Issues

- Online multiplayer is no longer available because the WON service is gone[^ref-13]
- The Wheel of Fortune game was never programmed and does not work[^ref-5][^ref-15]
- No option to set the number of players at a table in single-player mode[^ref-5]
