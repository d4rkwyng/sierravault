# Lords of the Realm II: Siege Pack

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Lords of the Realm II: Siege Pack is the official expansion pack to Lords of the Realm II, developed by Impressions Games and published by Sierra On-Line[^ref-1]. It features several new maps and castles, an all-battle mode where players can defend or attack castles, improved AI, and a map editor[^ref-1]. GameSpot reported the add-on as out on June 10, 1997[^ref-21], and Wikipedia dates its release to June 1997[^ref-6]. The marketing blurb put it plainly: "Declare all-out war with the Lords of the Realm II Siege Pack. Now you can concentrate on just fighting - this is pure combat"[^ref-2].

Sierra's release announcement listed a skirmish-only mode, new battle locations, a map editor, head-to-head combat on the Internet, 10 new castle designs, 20 all-new battle maps and more aggressive artificial intelligence[^ref-20]. The Siege Pack adds 20 new maps to the 24 maps of the basic game[^ref-4], and its marketing copy promised "50 new battle arenas"[^ref-2]. It requires a registered copy of Lords of the Realm II to play[^ref-20][^ref-5].

## Story Summary

The Siege Pack has no new story of its own; it extends Lords of the Realm II, whose GameFAQs description opens: "When the throne of England sits empty, the sword rules"[^ref-9]. That description places the player in thirteenth-century England as one of five nobles who must "manage crops, build weapons, construct a castle, and raise an army to conquer neighboring realms before they conquer you"[^ref-9]. It closes: "Because in the end, you're either king - or you're dead"[^ref-9].

## Gameplay

### Skirmish Mode

The expansion's headline addition is Skirmish Mode, which lets players jump straight into battle without the turn-based county management of the base game[^ref-20]. Home of the Underdogs called it "the best feature" of the pack: players choose either open-field battles or castle sieges, then fight with a computer-generated army or their own composition[^ref-13]. Lead designer David Lester said in Sierra's announcement: "The new skirmish mode gives many more gamers access to the hugely popular siege and combat experience"[^ref-20].

### Campaign and Battle Additions

Home of the Underdogs lists the additions as a more challenging AI, a custom series of games, twenty new maps, a map editor, faster Internet support, Skirmish Mode, sieges with ten new castle designs, enhanced diplomacy and more random events[^ref-13]. It notes that the designers "turned the difficulty up a notch for the new campaign," and that new random events include embezzling[^ref-13]. Battle maps gain a new terrain feature, pitch, which can be set aflame[^ref-13].

GameFAQs' Siege Pack page also documents a happiness integer-rollover glitch: a province losing around 160 happiness per season from taxes sees the -160 roll over to about +120[^ref-12].

### Map Editor

The included editor is for battle maps. Home of the Underdogs says players "can customize skirmishes, and even build battle maps using the intuitive map editor," but adds: "Unfortunately, you cannot design new castles or strategic-level maps, making it impossible to create new campaigns"[^ref-13]. The castles in the pack are the ten new designs supplied by Impressions[^ref-20]. The map editor needs 1 MB of video memory and 16-bit colour[^ref-17].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

MobyGames records a critics' average of 69% from five ratings for the Siege Pack[^ref-1]. The individual contemporary reviews behind that average have not yet been sourced for this page.

### Modern Assessment

Home of the Underdogs reviewed the pack favourably, calling it "An excellent expansion to one of the best medieval strategy games ever made" and concluding: "If only more expansion packs today gave as much bang for your buck. Two thumbs up, way up!"[^ref-13] Its user rating stands at 8.19 from 142 votes[^ref-13]. On Amazon, the product listing shows 4.0 out of 5 stars from 9 customer ratings[^ref-5].

## Development

Lords of the Realm II, released in November 1996, was described by Sierra as its best-selling strategy game ever, and the Siege Pack was created by Impressions as an add-on for its owners[^ref-20]. Sierra sold it on CD-ROM for Windows 95 at a street price of $29.95[^ref-20].

MobyGames credits David Lester, Chris Beatrice, Simon Bradbury and Eric Ouellette as game designers, with Eric Ouellette as producer, Simon Bradbury on programming and Chris Beatrice as creative director[^ref-22]. Music and sound effects are credited to Edward Saltzman and Keith Zizza[^ref-22]. A rip of the soundtrack on KHInsider runs to 65 files totalling 52 minutes and 5 seconds[^ref-16].

A fan-made Windows XP/Vista installer covering Lords of the Realm II and the Siege Pack scales the game to the full screen using Direct3D bilinear interpolation[^ref-17].

## Legacy

In November 1997 Impressions announced the Lords: Royal Collection, bundling Lords of the Realm, Lords of the Realm II and the Siege Pack[^ref-6]. A sequel, Lords of the Realm III, was developed by Impressions Games and published by Vivendi Universal in 2004[^ref-6].

The Siege Pack remains available today as part of digital re-releases: the Steam edition of Lords of the Realm II includes the Siege Pack expansion[^ref-23], and GOG's Lords of the Realm Royal Edition includes Lords of the Realm, Lords of the Realm II and the Siege Pack[^ref-24].
