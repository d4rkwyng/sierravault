# 3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown Deluxe

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown Deluxe is a railroading puzzle game developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by Sierra Attractions for Windows, released on September 30, 2000[^ref-9][^ref-23][^ref-15]. It is a stand-alone reissue of [[1999 - 3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown]]: the D.I.C.E. database describes it as enhancing "the previous game by adding 50 new missions and a new track creation system"[^ref-8]. A modern retrospective sums it up as an enhanced version "that has more levels than the standard version and a level editor"[^ref-2].

The Deluxe box promises "more than 80 jobs, from building an Eiffel Tower at the North Pole, to feeding a baby T-Rex, to rescuing a crashed UFO," set across "10 fantastic worlds"[^ref-25]. Review Corner's contemporary review counted "80+ puzzles, 3 new train worlds (for a total of 10), and a newly designed track layout system"[^ref-27]. The game was designed for anyone but, in ModDB's words, "will mostly appeal to those interested in trains and/or puzzles"[^ref-17].

For the original game's history, the 1999 Interactive Achievement Award and its reception, see [[1999 - 3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown]].

## Story Summary

There is no single plot. Each job has its own narrated scenario: in one, "the mayor's chair broke and we're entrusted with delivering a brand new chair to the mayor, as well as his lunch"; others have the player delivering golf balls for the queen or rescuing cows from "udder space"[^ref-2]. Players work up from Caboose Washer to Whistle Blower[^ref-27].

Deluxe's box singles out a new storyline in which the new Phantom locomotive helps "save the 'cowsmonauts' in 'Cows from Udder Space'"[^ref-25]. Review Corner lists jobs ranging from delivering fish to the anglers' club to "saving Planet X from alien invaders", and "saving Santa's Village from a runaway train filled with presents"[^ref-27].

The Tourist Project, an episodic story in which Oak Valley's mayor builds a Roman Colosseum to attract tourists, comes from the original game[^ref-12] and carries over into Deluxe, ending with the job "#6: Opening Day"[^ref-11].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game is played with the mouse: players click a train, then pick one of three speeds forward or backward[^ref-2]. It uses a 2D isometric style[^ref-2], with a diagonal-down perspective[^ref-13]; forum reviewer sb1254 called it technically a 2D sidescroller that does not feel like one[^ref-14]. Players follow their trains by scrolling across the screen and can call up maps of each world[^ref-27]. GameFAQs reviewer spiceworm noted that the pacing "was completely down to the player for the most part"[^ref-15].

### Structure and Progression

Deluxe is divided into six job rosters[^ref-2]. Review Corner reports that "each level includes a roster of 14 jobs, the last of which can only be unlocked once the previous 13 have been mastered"[^ref-27]. IGN's guide numbers levels for both editions and confirms that the final job of each roster is the 14th in Deluxe and the 12th in the original[^ref-11]. Jobs need not be played in sequence, and good runs earn Blue Ribbons[^ref-27][^ref-25].

### Worlds

The box describes "Ten wildly warped and wonderful worlds including the Big City and the Moon"[^ref-25]. Review Corner names three new ones (making ten)[^ref-27]. IGN's guide marks Kitchen, Basement and Big City as new in Deluxe, and notes that the original's Planet X becomes "Purple Moon in Deluxe"[^ref-11]. sb1254 lists the same Deluxe worlds: Big City, Purple Moon, Kitchen and Basement[^ref-14]. Returning settings include Oak Valley, Cactus Valley, the Backyard, North Pole and Living Room[^ref-11].

### Layout Tinkerer

The main new feature is the Layout Tinkerer, a new job bin for building your own layouts and puzzles[^ref-11][^ref-25]. Review Corner found it "now easier to use", and said its Smart Track Selector "makes laying out track child's play"[^ref-27]. Mex's 2025 review was less impressed, writing that pieces "have to be placed piece by piece" and that this "feel kind of clunky"[^ref-2]. IGN notes Deluxe also adds large curves "for added realism"[^ref-11]. Finished layouts "can be played as well as posted on the Internet for others to download"[^ref-8]; sb1254 noted in 2010 that the sharing website is long gone[^ref-14].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Most jobs mean picking up and delivering loads with freight and passenger cars, while dealing with switches, semaphores, broken track and oncoming trains[^ref-2]. Hazards include rockslides, avalanches and animals on the track[^ref-11][^ref-17]. Job types carried over from the original include:

**School Daze:** Players line up train cars "just so" to form valid mathematical equations[^ref-27]. TV Tropes, describing the original, says the levels range up to fourth grade[^ref-12], while Wikipedia says kindergarten to fifth grade[^ref-9]. IGN's guide explains the difference: the original's fifth-grade School Daze job was "taken out of the Deluxe version and 5th Grade was relabeled 4th"[^ref-11].

**Little Sister:** Players sort train cars by type by switching tracks, or "little sister" gets to keep the trains[^ref-27][^ref-12].

**Word and Memory Games:** Deluxe keeps the hangman-style and concentration jobs[^ref-27][^ref-2]. The Tower of Hanoi job "Tower of Power" uses four cars in Deluxe instead of five[^ref-11].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Review Corner (reviewed November 2000) scored Deluxe 9.6 out of 10 and called it "extraordinarily fun and provides an excellent workout for the brain at the same time"[^ref-27]. It added that "You don't have to be a train buff to enjoy the program's variety of puzzles, zany missions, and gorgeous graphics." The site put the game on its Best Children's Educational Software list and gave it its Award of Excellence[^ref-27].

The publisher's description, quoted on MyAbandonware, says: "3-D Ultra Lionel Traintown Deluxe may well be everything both a parent and a child could want. It's engaging, well designed, and informative. Best of all, playing it is a lot of fun"[^ref-13].

### Awards

Deluxe itself won no award. The award belongs to the **original** game. D.I.C.E. calls Deluxe "a stand-alone reissue of 3D Ultra Train Town, which was voted '2000 Family Game of the Year'"[^ref-8]. The original won PC Family Entertainment Title of the Year at the 3rd Annual Interactive Achievement Awards, held on May 11, 2000 at E3[^ref-26]. See [[1999 - 3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown]].

### Modern Assessment

Forum user sb1254 gave Deluxe 9/10, writing "This game is a recommendation from me. It's very original and very fun to play"[^ref-14].

GameFAQs reviewer spiceworm (2024) gave it 6/10, writing "The easy controls and fun puzzles were a chilled and much more relaxing way for me to spend my hours gaming." They faulted the lack of checkpoints: "I would have liked a checkpoint feature of some type, as I found that failing a mission at the half-hour point or right near the end was very frustrating"[^ref-15].

Mex's Articles and Reviews (2025) praised the game's variety and voice acting. The reviewer disliked the mix of realistic towns with toy-train settings, saying "This mix breaks the immersion"[^ref-2].

**User Ratings:**
- **MyAbandonware:** 4.41/5 (46 votes, as of October 2026)[^ref-13]

## Development

### Origins

Deluxe was developed by Dynamix as an expanded follow-up to its 1999 Lionel-licensed puzzle game[^ref-8][^ref-9]. The new content, per the box: more than 80 jobs, ten worlds, the Phantom locomotive, the Layout Tinkerer, and "over 45 locomotives and cars, all beautifully rendered from highly accurate, detailed Lionel models"[^ref-25]. D.I.C.E.'s description mentions "over 30 authentic models from Lionel's own line of miniature locomotives that span the ages of steam through diesel"[^ref-8]. IGN marks the Phantom Engine, three toy steam engines and toy-train wagons as new in Deluxe[^ref-11].

### Shared with the Original

Much of the game's material comes from the 1999 original:
- The locomotives include a Union Pacific EMD SW1500 switcher, a Santa Fe F3A diesel, a 2-8-0 steam locomotive and a 1950s passenger railcar[^ref-9].
- The original's opening used clips from Lionel videos such as "The History of Lionel Trains"[^ref-19].
- The original used sound effects from the Hollywood Edge and Sound Ideas libraries, and in Europe it was known simply as "TrainTown"[^ref-20].

### Technical Specifications

**System requirements (box):**[^ref-25]
- Windows 95 or 98, Pentium 120, 32 MB RAM (64 MB recommended)
- 70 MB free hard disk space, 4X CD-ROM, SVGA 256 colors

Review Corner also lists Windows 95/98/2000 and DirectX 7.0, and gives a retail price of $29.95 US[^ref-27]. The box product ID is 20626-70999[^ref-25].

**Archive file sizes:** MyAbandonware offers a 111 MB setup, a 169 MB full-rip and a 319 MB disc image[^ref-13]. Old Games Download lists a 273.42 MB ISO and 148 MB rips[^ref-22].

### Cut Content

A user-submitted GOG Dreamlist entry for the original game claims that "A port was planned for the Sega Dreamcast, but was cancelled for unknown reasons"[^ref-3]. No other source we found confirms this.

### Technical Issues

MyAbandonware's notes for running Deluxe on modern systems[^ref-13]:
- Set TrainDlx.exe to compatibility mode with Windows 2000 or Windows XP
- If it still fails, close TrainDlx.exe in Task Manager but leave rundll32.exe running
- Tested on Windows 7 64-bit and Windows 10; the display must support 800x600

CrossOver rates the game "Runs Great" on macOS (version 24.0.3) and Linux (version 25.1.0)[^ref-16].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- Cheat codes are typed after pressing the pipe ("|") key[^ref-11]
- Using cheats in Deluxe gives a red check instead of a blue ribbon on completed levels: "Cheaters don't prosper, ya know!"[^ref-11]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Deluxe was sold at retailers including Best Buy[^ref-21]. It has become scarce on the collector's market. sb1254 wrote in 2010: "3D Ultra Lionel Traintown Deluxe is probably the rarest game I have. Used versions alone sell for about 60 dollars", and "If you ever find this game for 10 to 20 dollars, you are a very lucky person"[^ref-14].

spiceworm notes that the TrainTown games "did lead to further Lionel Train set games being developed, the most notable of which was a Nintendo DS game, Lionel Trains: On Track, that was released in mid-March 2007"[^ref-15]. This was a separate Lionel-licensed game, not a direct sequel.

### Fan Interest

The game is not sold on major digital stores. GOG's Dreamlist entry for the original draws votes for a re-release[^ref-3]. One voter hopes for the expanded version: "It turns out there is an expanded version of this game, that I hope is the one that is added to the store"[^ref-3]. Another calls it "an edutainment classic that I've had the fondest recollection of," with "such a calming atmosphere"[^ref-3].
