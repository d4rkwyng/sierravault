# Gobliiins 4

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 27, 2026</small>

## Overview

Gobliiins 4 is the fourth installment in the beloved puzzle-adventure series that began in 1991, marking the franchise's return more than fifteen years after Goblins Quest 3 in 1993[^ref-1]. Developed by Société Pollene and produced by the Russian company Snowball Studios, the game was designed by series co-creator [[Pierre Gilhodes]][^ref-38][^ref-8]; his original co-creator [[Muriel Tramis]] is credited only in the thanks[^ref-38]. The game returns to the original formula of controlling three goblins with unique abilities, as indicated by the three "i"s in the title—a naming convention that reflects the number of playable characters[^ref-3].

Released in March 2009[^ref-36], the game features 3D graphics while maintaining the classic gameplay mechanics that defined the series[^ref-2]. Players take on the roles of Detective Tchoup and his companions Stucco and Perluis, who are hired by King Balderon VIII to locate his missing pet aardvark named Riri[^ref-4]. For many gamers, the Gobliiins series represents "the gaming equivalent of marmite: either you love it or you hate it," with its challenging puzzles and distinctive humor dividing audiences[^ref-5].

The game received mixed reviews upon release, with critics praising its puzzle design while criticizing its dated presentation and lack of modern conveniences[^ref-6]. A further sequel, Gobliiins 5, followed in 2023[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

Set in Goblinland, the story concerns King Balderon VIII, whose favourite pet, the orycterope (aardvark) Riri, has run away—with a hint of involvement from the ambitious Grand Steward Galantz[^ref-11]. To ease the king's depression, Detective Tchoup and his two associates are tasked with finding the escaped pet[^ref-11][^ref-4]. Young Riri can only hope that the three Gobliiins will solve the riddles before him and ultimately save both him and his fiancée[^ref-10].

The player controls three distinct goblin characters, each with their own unique personality and limited skillset. Tchoup serves as the brain of the operation, investigating clues and gathering information from the various characters encountered throughout their journey[^ref-11]; he can also pick up items and keep them in his pockets[^ref-35]. Stucco provides the muscle, making up for poor deductive skills with superior strength for lifting and climbing[^ref-35]. Perluis rounds out the trio as the magic user, possessing the ability to make plants grow and bewitch objects with his mystical powers[^ref-8].

True to goblin nature, the three protagonists are not especially bright, and each is capable of performing only one type of action[^ref-8]. This limitation forms the core of the gameplay, as players must strategically combine the talents of all three goblins to overcome obstacles and progress through the adventure. The story unfolds across sixteen levels as the trio travels through increasingly bizarre and challenging environments in pursuit of Riri[^ref-13].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Gobliiins 4 is a point-and-click game[^ref-16] built on the classic concept of the first Gobliiins: three characters, each with unique abilities, directed by the player[^ref-13]. The game uses a simple cursor-based interaction system where players select a goblin and then click on objects or characters within the environment to perform that goblin's unique action. Unlike modern adventure games, the title does not feature an integrated hint system—a significant omission that led one reviewer to note "If there is a game that ever needs a hint system, this game will be it!"[^ref-5].

The game utilizes an archaic save system that provides only a single save slot per level, relying on a password code system rather than traditional save files[^ref-15]. This design choice was criticized for feeling outdated, with one reviewer describing it as "very archaic; from the sub-par graphics, the level-by-level gameplay, even the codes used instead of a save file"[^ref-15]. The intrusive auto-save feature and limited save options were noted as sources of frustration[^ref-16].

### Structure and Progression

The game is structured around sixteen self-contained levels, each consisting of a single screen packed with interactive elements and puzzles[^ref-13]. This level-based approach was compared to "a point and click version of The Lost Vikings," requiring players to combine the three siblings' talents to find solutions[^ref-16]. Players must complete all puzzles within a level before receiving a code to access the next area.

- **Levels 1-13:** Each hides a golden tooth[^ref-13]
- **Bonus Level:** One of the sixteen levels is locked, and is unlocked by finding the hidden golden tooth on each of the first thirteen levels[^ref-13][^ref-5]

The game compels repetition, forcing players to replay levels if certain actions are not completed correctly[^ref-14]. This design philosophy reflects the series' classic approach but proved divisive among modern players accustomed to more forgiving game structures.

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core gameplay revolves around solving multi-stepped puzzles using the unique abilities of each goblin character[^ref-14]. Tchoup's investigative skills allow him to speak with NPCs and gather crucial information—sometimes requiring multiple conversations with the same character[^ref-17]. Stucco's strength enables physical manipulation of objects, while Perluis's magic can transform and enchant elements of the environment.

IT Reviews criticized the puzzle solutions as "annoyingly strait-jacketed at times," requiring very specific sequences of actions[^ref-40]. Trial and error became a common approach, with one reviewer noting "I don't mind hard puzzles, but I hate it when the player is reduced to just using trial and error to progress"[^ref-18]. However, fans of the series argued that "every puzzle is reasonable" and encouraged players to attempt solving them independently[^ref-11].

Unlike earlier entries, the game no longer counts mistakes: the characters may make as many mistakes as they need, as there is no mistakes counter anymore[^ref-13]. This removes the pressure of a failure meter but does not diminish the challenge of the puzzles themselves.

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Gobliiins 4 received highly polarized reviews upon release, with Metacritic-listed scores ranging from 91 (Just Adventure) to 11 (PC Gamer UK)[^ref-6].

Just Adventure awarded the game a remarkable 91/100, declaring it "a cleverly designed and entertaining game"[^ref-6]. Adventure Gamers gave it 4 out of 5 stars (80 on Metacritic), with reviewer Austin Boosinger calling it "one of the most likeable titles this year"[^ref-39][^ref-6] and noting that "unless you play adventures only for their stories, I can safely say that this game is worth the time of any point-and-click adventurer"[^ref-6]. GameStar provided a mixed 68/100, observing "The series continues after 15 years and it's true to its predecessors. That's good and bad at the same time. It's a colorful slapstick adventure, but today a game like this needs a real story, characters and some more functions"[^ref-6].

Adventure Classic Gaming rated the game 2/5 (Fair), with reviewer Martin Mulrooney concluding "Sadly, for other adventure game fans, a recommendation cannot be offered with a clear conscience"[^ref-5]. GameGrin awarded 5/10, criticizing the disconnect between the child-friendly presentation and adult-level puzzle difficulty[^ref-15]. LEVEL (Czech Republic) gave 50/100, describing it as a "sequel to the famous trilogy changed into a kid's entertainment game with uncomplicated logical puzzles. The sound and graphics departments should be fired anyways"[^ref-6].

Total PC Gaming scored it just 30/100, summarizing "Gobliiins 4 has a lot of character, it just doesn't have enough depth"[^ref-6]. PC Gamer UK was particularly harsh, awarding only 11/100 with the dismissive assessment "Deliberately obtuse is just frustrating"[^ref-6].

### Modern Assessment

The game maintains a contested legacy among adventure gaming enthusiasts. Some fans continue to champion it as a worthy successor that preserved the spirit of the originals, while others view it as a missed opportunity for modernization.

## Development

### Origins

Pierre Gilhodes returned to game development after a decade of absence to resurrect his beloved franchise[^ref-23]. Having created the original Gobliiins characters alongside [[Muriel Tramis]] at Coktel Vision in the early 1990s, Gilhodes maintained a vision for continuing the series[^ref-24].

Gilhodes continued working as a graphic artist and author of animations for various audiovisual productions during the intervening years before launching the production of Gobliiins 4[^ref-25]. His artistic background since a very early age informed the game's distinctive visual style, which aimed to preserve the whimsical aesthetic that defined the series[^ref-25].

### Production

To bring Gobliiins 4 to life, Gilhodes teamed up with Société Pollene, a small studio from the south of France[^ref-13]. Financial backing came from Snowberry Connection, an independent production fund based in Moscow[^ref-13]. This international collaboration between French creative talent and Russian production resources enabled the project to move forward despite the franchise's long dormancy.

The development embraced 3D graphics technology while consciously maintaining the gameplay mechanics that had defined the series[^ref-2]. The decision to preserve the classic formula was deliberate, though it would later draw criticism for feeling outdated. Société Pollene described the game as "for all ages"[^ref-15], while Gilhodes described its colourful, somewhat childish art design as "the kidults style"[^ref-13].

### Technical Achievements

The transition to 3D graphics represented the most significant technical evolution from the original trilogy[^ref-2]. However, reviews were divided on whether this constituted an improvement, with some critics finding the graphics "sub-par" despite the technological upgrade[^ref-15]. The game maintained the single-screen level design of its predecessors while rendering environments and characters in three dimensions.

The game supported an impressive array of languages for localization: English, French, Italian, German, Spanish, Russian, Polish, Czech, and Hungarian[^ref-35]. This extensive localization effort reflected the franchise's international appeal and the publishers' commitment to reaching global audiences.

### Technical Issues

The game exhibited stability problems, with one reviewer noting it could crash during play[^ref-16]. Compatibility issues emerged with newer operating systems, with users reporting that the game would install correctly but fail to launch—appearing in task manager without actually running[^ref-28]. The buggy translations from French to English also drew criticism[^ref-5].

The limited save system proved particularly frustrating, offering only a single save per level through an intrusive auto-save mechanism or password system[^ref-16]. This archaic approach stood in stark contrast to modern gaming conventions and contributed to player frustration.

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- Hidden golden teeth are concealed on each screen; collecting all teeth from the first thirteen levels unlocks a secret bonus level[^ref-5][^ref-13]
- The number of "i"s in the series titles directly corresponds to the number of playable goblin characters: Gobliiins (3 goblins), Gobliins 2 (2 goblins), Goblins 3 (1 goblin), Gobliiins 4 (3 goblins)[^ref-3]
- The US release of the original Gobliiins renamed the characters to Hooter, Dwayne, and BoBo, whereas European versions used Oups, Ignatius, and Asgard[^ref-30]
- Pictures of previous games' goblins appear in scenes throughout the series[^ref-3]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Gobliiins 4 launched at a retail price of £19.99 in the UK[^ref-8] and was available for digital download through GamersGate[^ref-35]. One reviewer suggested that "this is a full price retail game, yet I think it would have worked far more effectively as a cheaper, digital download"[^ref-18].

In 2014–2015 the game was not sold on GOG.com, and a commenter on its wishlist page wrote in August 2014 that "a lot of people do not even know this game exists"[^ref-22][^ref-43].

### Collections

The game was also released in a "Platinová Edice" (Czech for "Platinum Edition"), a compilation of all four Gobliiins games on a single DVD, with the earlier games modified to run on newer Windows systems[^ref-41]. The original trilogy remained available through various digital storefronts including GOG.com and Steam, often bundled together, though Gobliiins 4 itself was not among them[^ref-43].

In May 2026 Gobliiins 4 returned in Red Art Games' Gobliiins Collection for PlayStation 5, Xbox Series and Switch, which bundles the first five games and describes it as the series' "first 3D instalment"[^ref-44].

### Sequels

Series creator Pierre Gilhodes developed Gobliiins 5 (titled "GOBLiiiNS5 - The Invasion of the Morglotons"), published by Schnibble Productions and released on Steam in July 2023[^ref-7]. The sequel received "Very Positive" user reviews on Steam (92% of 71 reviews)[^ref-7]. The game was also made available on itch.io for $11 USD[^ref-31].

### Related Publications

- **Online Walkthrough:** Comprehensive text walkthroughs available on GameFAQs, Neoseeker, and other community sites[^ref-11]

### Critical Perspective

Gobliiins 4 represents both a nostalgic revival and a cautionary tale about preserving classic gameplay without modernization. The game successfully recaptured the challenging, multi-character puzzle-solving that defined the original trilogy, satisfying longtime fans who appreciated its faithfulness to the source material. However, its refusal to incorporate modern conveniences—such as a proper save system, hint mechanism, or more flexible puzzle solutions—alienated potential new audiences and frustrated even sympathetic critics.

The game's reception illustrates the tension between preservation and evolution in adventure gaming. While publications like Adventure Gamers praised its puzzle design and charm, mainstream outlets found its archaic design choices inexcusable in 2009. The stark range of review scores—from 91/100 to 11/100—demonstrates how deeply personal the Gobliiins experience remained. As one reviewer aptly noted, "The characters and situations seem tailored to a child's demographic, whilst the game requires the iron will and stubbornness of an adult to solve"[^ref-15].

Gilhodes went on to make Gobliiins 5, released in 2023 under the Schnibble Productions label[^ref-7].
