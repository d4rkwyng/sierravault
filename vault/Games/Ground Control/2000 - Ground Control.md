# Ground Control

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Ground Control is a real-time tactics game developed by Swedish studio Massive Entertainment and published by Sierra Studios in 2000.[^ref-1] Set in the year 2419,[^ref-10] the game focuses on a conflict between two factions—the Crayven Corporation and the Order of the New Dawn—vying for control of a distant planet called Krig-7B and a series of mysterious alien artifacts.[^ref-2] The game was notable for dispensing with traditional RTS resource management, instead focusing on tactical combat: GameSpot noted that "you'll never have to worry about managing resources"[^ref-3] and one early player review describes it as an RTS "without base building and resource gathering."[^ref-17]

Ground Control represented a significant departure from the conventions of the real-time strategy genre: players win with the units they have available and the tactics they use, rather than by tank-rushing with an economy behind them.[^ref-2] The game was the debut title for Massive Entertainment, developed by a team of complete newcomers to the industry in Ronneby, Sweden.[^ref-5][^ref-6] Ola Strandh, who has been at Massive since the Ground Control days, calls it "one of the first games, if not the first, to take advantage of full 3D rendering for strategic gameplay."[^ref-6] Massive's own retrospective calls the result "a groundbreaking (no pun intended) game, mixing state of the art graphics with intense, action filled gameplay."[^ref-6]

The game received widespread critical acclaim upon release, earning a Metacritic score of 86/100[^ref-24] and being described as "simply one of the most beautiful games you are likely to see this year."[^ref-7] Ground Control was followed by an expansion pack, Dark Conspiracy,[^ref-41] and a sequel, Ground Control II: Operation Exodus.[^ref-45][^ref-8]

## Story Summary

The game is set in the 25th century, following a devastating "Sixteen-Minute War" that has left Earth scarred and changed humanity's relationship with warfare.[^ref-2] War has been outlawed on Earth itself, but mega-corporations have taken their conflicts to the stars, fighting over colonized planets in distant star systems.[^ref-29] The player's employer, the Crayven Corporation, has dispatched forces to the remote world of Krig-7B, ostensibly for another routine brush-fire war with their religious rivals, the Order of the New Dawn.[^ref-10]

The Order of the New Dawn has laid claim to the barren planetoid, and the Crayven Corporation wants to know why.[^ref-11] As Major Sarah Parker of the Crayven Corporation, or as Deacon Jarrod Stone of the Order, players command ground and air forces through a series of intense tactical engagements.[^ref-12] What begins as a straightforward territorial dispute gradually reveals a much deeper mystery involving ancient alien technology and a terrifying secret that both factions are racing to control.[^ref-11]

The narrative unfolds across a single 30-mission campaign divided in half, telling the story from the perspective of each of the two opposing factions in turn.[^ref-3] The story features espionage missions, dramatic betrayals, and the gradual uncovering of the alien artifacts' true nature and purpose.[^ref-11][^ref-2] The campaign is enhanced by fully voiced cutscenes featuring professional voice actors, with Jennifer Hale portraying Major Sarah Parker and Dee Bradley Baker voicing Deacon Jarrod Stone.[^ref-13]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Ground Control features what was described as "a remarkably easy-to-use interface" that wrapped intense tactical combat in one of the most impressive 3D engines ever seen in a computer game at the time.[^ref-7] Managing combat was streamlined by issuing commands to squads rather than individual troops, significantly reducing the micromanagement burden that plagued many RTS games.[^ref-7] The fully 3D engine gave players full control over the camera, letting them view the battlefield from any angle, from a bird's-eye view down to a single unit's ground-level perspective.[^ref-14]

The mouse rotates and tilts the view, while scrolling and zooming are on the keyboard, and squads' special abilities can be mapped to keyboard hotkeys.[^ref-3] Improved joystick support was added in a later patch.[^ref-15]

### Structure and Progression

Unlike traditional RTS games, Ground Control does not use mechanics of resource and economic management.[^ref-2] Before each mission, players configure their dropships with up to 12 squadrons of units, with three dropships carrying four squads each.[^ref-17] All forces available during a mission are the ones players start with—there is no way to produce additional units mid-battle.[^ref-17]

The single-player game is one 30-mission campaign, divided in half between the two factions:[^ref-3]

- **Crayven Corporation half:** following Major Sarah Parker as she leads corporate forces against the Order[^ref-12]
- **Order of the New Dawn half:** following Deacon Jarrod Stone and the Order's perspective on the conflict[^ref-12]

Each squad is drawn from one of four unit categories (vehicles, support, aircraft and infantry), and every deployment must include the Command APC.[^ref-2] Squads gain experience through combat and can earn medals, increasing their effectiveness in subsequent missions.[^ref-17] The dropship configuration system allows players to choose specific weapons loadouts and special equipment for their forces before deployment.[^ref-17]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Ground Control emphasizes tactical combat over strategic resource management. The core gameplay loop involves positioning units to take advantage of terrain, coordinating combined arms attacks, and preserving limited forces across missions.[^ref-16] The 3D terrain plays a crucial role, with hills and obstacles affecting line of sight, weapon effectiveness, and unit movement.[^ref-19]

The game features a friendly fire mechanic, requiring careful attention to unit positioning during firefights.[^ref-17] Units have a view range,[^ref-20] and because there is no fog of war, GameSpot found it difficult to tell when units are close enough to detect their enemies.[^ref-3] Special weapons, repair modules, and tactical abilities add layers of depth to combat encounters.[^ref-20]

One of the most controversial design decisions was the lack of mid-mission save capability.[^ref-7] As one reviewer noted, "There is no mid-mission save ability. That really sucks."[^ref-21] CNN/IDG's reviewer, however, noted that "most missions shouldn't take more than two or three tries to complete."[^ref-7]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Ground Control received overwhelmingly positive reviews upon release. GameSpot awarded the game 8.5/10, with Greg Kasavin praising it as "not only one of the best-looking tactical combat games to date, but also one that's suitably challenging and rewarding."[^ref-3] IGN gave the game 8.6/10 and awarded it an Editors' Choice designation, noting that it was "so pretty you'll bonk your head on the screen trying to get a closer look."[^ref-21]

Eurogamer awarded an impressive 9/10, declaring the game "a marvellous game" that "sparkles with polish, detail and originality."[^ref-22] GameSpy gave 90/100 and stated: "If you are a fan of action, strategy, RTS, or just like to see beautiful battles rendered in 3D, this one's for you."[^ref-23] PC Gameworld awarded 93/100 with the memorable proclamation that it was "the best thing to come out of Sweden since meatballs and tall, big busted, long legged blondes wearing high heels."[^ref-23]

Gaming Age awarded 91/100, calling it "currently the best real-time strategy title I have played since 'Homeworld.'"[^ref-23] FiringSquad gave 90/100, stating "as far as 3-D RTS games go, this is the best one we have ever played. With very few flaws this game pleases in almost every aspect."[^ref-23] Computer Games Strategy Plus gave it 4.5 out of 5 (reviewer Nick McElveen, 7 July 2000), calling it "an outstanding game, both for its graphics and its gameplay, but it is not for everyone," while noting that "there is no in-mission save, no speed control, no skirmish mode, and the AI is hit or miss."[^ref-51][^ref-24] CNET Gamecenter's Bill McClendon (15 June 2000) summed it up as "Easily the best, most accessible 3D real-time strategy game to date," listing "Occasional pathfinding problems; some scenarios are frustrating" among the drawbacks.[^ref-52]

### Modern Assessment

The game was nominated for Computer Games Magazine's 2000 "Real-time Strategy Game of the Year"[^ref-1] and was also a BAFTA Games Award nominee in the Games - PC category for 2000.[^ref-25]

## Development

### Origins

Massive Entertainment was founded in 1997[^ref-5][^ref-49] by Martin Walfisz,[^ref-6] who has said he started the company with Christian Pérez, whom he met after finishing his software engineering studies in 1996.[^ref-16] The small team behind Ground Control was made up of complete newcomers to the games industry, in their early 20s.[^ref-6] When Sierra Studios signed Ground Control in 1999, the studio was located in Ronneby, Sweden.[^ref-5] The studio's CEO, Martin Walfisz, set out to create something new in the RTS genre. "We changed that formula for Ground Control and wanted to focus more on the tactics of the battles, instead of the resource gathering and strategizing," Walfisz explained.[^ref-6]

The game was originally developed under the working title "Genesis."[^ref-16] Walfisz was an avid player of Warcraft II and Command & Conquer at the time, and his vision was to make classic strategy game features work in a 3D environment.[^ref-16] The team then shifted the focus to the tactics of the battles instead of resource gathering.[^ref-6] Sierra Studios signed on to publish the game, recognizing its potential to stand out in an increasingly crowded RTS market.[^ref-5]

### Production

The development team consisted of over 20 programmers, artists, and game designers,[^ref-5] most of whom were in their early twenties.[^ref-6] Massive's project page describes the studio's early work on the Ground Control saga as "fueled by the passion and creativity that has since become an integral part of the studio culture."[^ref-31] Ground Control combined "classic strategy elements with incredible 3D technology previously reserved for action games."[^ref-5]

The development goal was straightforward, according to Walfisz: "Our goal with Ground Control is to simply create a new game that makes players say, 'Yeah, this is cool.'"[^ref-5] The team focused heavily on visual presentation, creating what was at the time one of the most graphically impressive strategy games on the market.[^ref-7]

### Technical Achievements

Ola Strandh called Ground Control "one of the first games, if not the first, to take advantage of full 3D rendering for strategic gameplay."[^ref-6] The game's fully 3D engine rendered each unit with remarkable detail and realistic animations.[^ref-3] GameSpot noted realistic lighting effects that distinguished battles fought at different times of day,[^ref-3] terrain such as high ground carried real tactical weight, and the fully controllable camera let players view battles from any angle.[^ref-14]

The graphics were so impressive that CNN/IDG declared Ground Control "simply one of the most beautiful games you are likely to see this year."[^ref-7] The game featured support for both NVIDIA and 3dfx graphics cards, with GeForce Transform & Lighting acceleration for additional visual enhancement.[^ref-22] The engine could run in 32-bit color with hardware acceleration or fall back to software rendering for compatibility.[^ref-22]

### Technical Issues

The AI exhibited some limitations, with units tending to wander directly into the line of friendly fire.[^ref-7] Retail copies use SecuROM DRM.[^ref-9]

Modern compatibility issues include startup crashes on Windows 10, which can be resolved with community-created patches.[^ref-37] The game's online servers, hosted on the World Opponent Network (WON), were shut down on 1 November 2008;[^ref-27] the community-created NuWON master server still supports online play, alongside LAN play.[^ref-9]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- The character "Major Tom" is likely a reference to David Bowie's song "Space Oddity"[^ref-27]
- MobyGames describes Ground Control as the first real-time tactics game with "drop-in" multiplayer, letting players join a game already in progress[^ref-27]
- The game ships with an unsupported map editor, GenEd, which Massive used to build the game's single and multiplayer missions[^ref-27]
- The base game was released as a free download in June 2004 to promote the release of Ground Control II[^ref-27]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Ground Control was a commercial disappointment. In 2003, writer Mark H. Walker reported that the game "didn't sell", placing its sales at "a few tens of thousands of copies".[^ref-1] Walfisz later recalled that despite the reviews, "it hadn't made the kind of money we needed to sustain the company long term."[^ref-16] It still received an expansion pack,[^ref-53] and Massive went on to make World in Conflict.[^ref-49]

Ground Control was made available as a free download in June 2004 by Vivendi Universal to promote the release of Ground Control II: Operation Exodus.[^ref-27]

### Releases and Availability

GOG.com re-released the game as Ground Control Anthology in 2009, and Rebellion re-released it on Steam in 2015.[^ref-1] As of November 11, 2026, Steam lists Rebellion as the publisher at $4.99,[^ref-10] and GOG sells the DRM-free Windows version at $5.99.[^ref-29]

### Collections

The game was released in several compilation formats:

- **Ground Control Anthology (2001):** Bundled Ground Control with its expansion pack Dark Conspiracy[^ref-40]
- **Various budget re-releases:** Published by Sold Out Sales & Marketing Ltd., 1C Company, and Mindscape SA[^ref-27]

### Expansion and Sequel

**Ground Control: Dark Conspiracy (December 2000)**[^ref-41]

Sierra's announcement, as reported by GameSpot, named the developers as High Voltage Software and Massive Entertainment;[^ref-53] GameFAQs lists High Voltage Software alone,[^ref-41] and the expansion runs on Massive's engine.[^ref-35] This expansion pack continued the story of Major Sarah Parker.[^ref-42] After defeating both the Crayven Corporation and Order of the New Dawn forces on Krig-7B, Parker must enlist a group of mercenaries—the Phoenix Mercenaries—to help her escape the deserted planet.[^ref-42] The expansion included 15 new missions across 3 planets[^ref-41] and introduced the third playable faction.[^ref-42]

The expansion was released at an unusually low price point, with Sierra offering it to owners of the original for about five dollars; GameZone wrote that "Sierra is basically giving it away free."[^ref-43] IGN noted: "If you own the original Ground Control, and you don't send the five bucks in to get this game, you're seriously in danger of your children falling backwards on the evolutionary ladder."[^ref-43]

**Ground Control II: Operation Exodus (June 2004)**[^ref-44]

The sequel, also developed by Massive Entertainment, takes place 320 years after the original game.[^ref-45] The Crayven Corporation and Order of the New Dawn no longer exist, replaced by a new conflict between the Northern Star Alliance (NSA) and the Terran Empire.[^ref-44] MobyGames' description calls the NSA "democratic and freedom caring" and the Terran Empire "warmongering."[^ref-54] The game received an 80/100 on Metacritic.[^ref-45]

### Fan Projects

The modding community has created several notable projects for Ground Control:[^ref-46]

- **Ground Control: The Aftermath** – An 18-mission campaign that continues from where Dark Conspiracy left off, depicting events leading up to the theft of the CSS Astrid.[^ref-47] A re-release in November 2025 added AI voicing and moved the first six original missions to the custom campaign section.[^ref-47]
- **Ground Control Rebellion Mod** – A sequel to the "Sigma Incident" mod set years after the original story[^ref-46]
- **Ground Control Windows 10 Crash Fix** – Community patch enabling compatibility with modern operating systems[^ref-37]
- **Ground Control Remaster Fan Edition** – An in-development project updating the game to Full HD with English and Russian language support[^ref-46]

### Related Publications

- **Ground Control Manual:** Included with game, providing technical documentation about factions, units, and gameplay mechanics[^ref-20]
- **Ground Control: Dark Conspiracy Manual:** Expansion pack documentation[^ref-48]

### Critical Perspective

By stripping away the resource and economic management found in real-time strategy games, Ground Control is classed as a real-time tactics game rather than an RTS.[^ref-2]

Massive Entertainment went on to create World in Conflict, joined Ubisoft in 2008, and later took the lead on Tom Clancy's The Division.[^ref-49] As Walfisz reflected: "We were, in that sense at least, ahead of our time. We focused more on action, and less on strategic, long-term aspects of RTS games."[^ref-16]

In 2009, Rebellion acquired the Ground Control intellectual property from Vivendi following the Activision merger.[^ref-50] CEO Jason Kingsley stated: "We have complete ownership of the IP from the past and going forwards, so yes we're putting our plans together for new titles in each series."[^ref-50] However, no new Ground Control titles have been announced as of this writing.
