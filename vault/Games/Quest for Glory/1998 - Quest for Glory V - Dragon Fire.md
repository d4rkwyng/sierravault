# Quest for Glory V: Dragon Fire

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview
Quest for Glory V: Dragon Fire is the fifth and final game in the Quest for Glory series, developed by Yosemite Entertainment and published by Sierra FX for Windows and Macintosh in late 1998 (GameRankings and Metacritic list November 30, 1998)[^ref-1][^ref-21][^ref-24].

Designed primarily by [[Lori Ann Cole]] (with [[Corey Cole]] handling programming), the game marks a significant departure from its predecessors by being primarily an action role-playing game rather than a traditional adventure-RPG hybrid[^ref-1][^ref-6].

The game was always planned as part of the series (whereas Wages of War originally was not), but it would not have been produced if not for fans' enthusiasm and their pleas to Sierra On-Line[^ref-1][^ref-4][^ref-20].

Dragon Fire features real-time 3D characters over pre-rendered panoramic backgrounds[^ref-1][^ref-20].

Chance Thomas' soundtrack was released alongside a demo before the game's launch; Erik Bethke's *Game Development and Production* (2003, p. 349), as cited in the Wikipedia article, puts that product's sales at 50,000 copies and $500,000[^ref-1].

## Story Summary
The wizard Erasmus introduces the Hero to the Greece-like kingdom of Silmaria, whose king was recently assassinated[^ref-1][^ref-4]. The traditional Rites of Rulership are due to commence, and the victor will be crowned king[^ref-1]. The Hero enters the contest with the assistance of Erasmus, Rakeesh, and many old friends from previous entries in the series[^ref-1][^ref-4].

The Hero competes against several competitors, including the Silmarian guard Kokeeno Pookameeso, the warlord Magnum Opus, the hulking Gort, and the warrior Elsa Von Spielburg, who played a significant role in the first game[^ref-1][^ref-4]. As the Rites commence, an unknown assassin begins systematically picking off the contestants[^ref-1]. Each contestant is murdered by a poison dagger near Dragon Pillars, the objects used to keep the Dragon of Silmaria locked up[^ref-1][^ref-4].

After completing the second Rite, the Hero faces increasingly dangerous challenges as the conspiracy unfolds[^ref-1]. The conspiracy is eventually unraveled and the Dragon, having been released due to the destruction of the Dragon Pillars, is defeated[^ref-1]. The characters Katrina and Erana make a return in this installment as assistance for defeating the Dragon[^ref-1][^ref-4].

## Gameplay

Unlike the first four games, which were mostly adventure games incorporating role-playing elements, Dragon Fire is a role-playing game incorporating some adventure elements[^ref-1][^ref-4]. The game features a wide variety of weapons, armor, and magical items not present in earlier entries, and nearly every major mission consists of combat encounters[^ref-1][^ref-6].

### Interface and Controls
- Point-and-click graphical interface with new combat system[^ref-1]
- Real-time battles taking place in the main environment rather than isolated combat screens[^ref-1]
- 3D polygonal characters over 2D pre-rendered backgrounds[^ref-1]
- Character import from any previous Quest for Glory game; an imported character starts at a considerable advantage[^ref-20]

### Structure and Progression
- Seven Rites of Rulership serve as main quest progression[^ref-1][^ref-4]
- Many quests are optional, with numerous sub-quests for each class[^ref-20]
- Fighter, Wizard and Thief can be created new; the Paladin requires an imported character or the premade Paladin supplied with the game[^ref-20][^ref-33]
- Class affects puzzle solutions, NPC reactions and accessible areas, with class-specific side quests such as the Paladin's Ring of Truth, the Thief's Master Thief quest, the Fighter's Champion Gladiator title and the Wizard's mystical staff[^ref-20]

### Puzzles and Mechanics
- The Hero can woo and marry one of four women (Elsa, Katrina, Erana or Nawar); Elsa and Nawar can marry any class, while Erana is limited to the Paladin or Wizard and Katrina to the Fighter or Wizard[^ref-20][^ref-28]
- Branching story paths affecting character fates[^ref-1]
- Real-time action combat replacing turn-based or side-scrolling systems[^ref-1]
- Cut features due to deadline pressures: bow weapons, playable Elsa/Magnum Opus, multiplayer[^ref-1][^ref-4]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews
GameSpot's Elliott Chin scored the game 7.4/10 (Good), noting that "Quest for Glory V has a few quirks and problems that will try your patience, but series fans will want to play this game to see old friends and follow Hero's last adventure"[^ref-17]. He praised the game's continuity with the series, stating "The humor and style of the previous Quest games is wholly intact in this final chapter"[^ref-17]. IGN's Trent C. Ward gave the game 6.6/10, criticizing the combat system while acknowledging that "the graphics this time around are spectacular"[^ref-18].

Next Generation's December 24, 1998 review summarized the game as "A fine contribution to the genre that is accessible to novice and expert gamers alike"[^ref-33]. Joshua "Darien" Maciel of RPGamer scored the game 9 out of 10, praising its seamless combat and gameplay and concluding, "If there was ever an example to show the world of how to end a series, this game would be it"[^ref-19]. Dragon Fire was a nominee for CNET Gamecenter's 1998 "Adventure Game of the Year" award, whose editors wrote, "What Dragon Fire does really well is give traditional adventure gamers a place to call home"; the award went to Grim Fandango[^ref-34][^ref-36]. Adventure Classic Gaming's Greg Wallace gave it 4/5 stars, calling composer Chance Thomas's work "the finest musical scores ever found in an adventure game" while criticizing the combat as "an infuriating and confusing clicking frenzy"[^ref-20].

### Modern Assessment
Writing for Engadget in 2012, Rowan Kaiser said the final game "stands apart from the rest, and not necessarily in a good way," describing its switch to three-dimensional polygonal graphics as "not one that I find successful," though he noted "it certainly has its fans"[^ref-35]. In PC Gamer, Richard Cobbett wrote that "the fifth stumbles with its 3D world and more arcade style action"[^ref-5].

GameRankings averaged 72.57% from 21 reviews for the PC version and 62.50% for the Mac version; PC Gamer UK gave it 50/100[^ref-21]. Adventure Gamers rated it 3/5, concluding that it "has some questionable design issues, but it's still a decent title with enough entertaining elements to enjoy a Hero's final quest"[^ref-6][^ref-21]. Just Adventure gave it an A- grade[^ref-21], while Quandary rated it 3/5[^ref-21]. PC Zone UK gave it 68/100[^ref-21]. HowLongToBeat users report about 15½ hours for the main story and about 28½ hours with extras (small sample, as of October 2026)[^ref-7]. GameFAQs users rated it "Great" with 73 ratings (as of October 2026)[^ref-8]. On Speedrun.com the fastest Any% run is about 2 minutes (using a wrong warp); Any% without wrong warps is about 4½ minutes, and an All Rites run takes about 27 minutes[^ref-22].

## Development

### Production
The first four games in the series were intended to indicate the four elements and four wind directions: in the first game, the player is the Hero from the East, in the second from the North, etc[^ref-1][^ref-4]. Lori Cole was predominantly responsible for Dragon Fire's design, as Corey Cole was preoccupied with programming[^ref-1]. The game originally had a voxel engine, which was later changed to a 3D engine because of hardware limitations[^ref-1].

Because of deadline issues and financial pressures, several features were dropped before release, including the ability to use a bow, the ability to play as Elsa von Spielburg or Magnum Opus, and multiplayer capability[^ref-1][^ref-4]. A demo released in late 1997 contained a multiplayer game, but Sierra decided to remove it from the final product[^ref-1]. The entire Quest for Glory programming team was laid off when Yosemite Entertainment was closed on February 22, 1999[^ref-1][^ref-4].

### Music

Chance Thomas composed, orchestrated and produced the game's soundtrack[^ref-1][^ref-26]; Mark Seibert is credited on three tracks of the soundtrack album because the series' main theme appears in them as a motif[^ref-27]. The soundtrack was released alongside a demo prior to the game's launch; Erik Bethke's *Game Development and Production* (2003, p. 349), as cited in the Wikipedia article, puts that product's sales at 50,000 copies and $500,000[^ref-1]. Jenny Jordan provided vocals for "The Dance of Mystery and Intrigue" and "The Rite of Destiny," while Thomas himself performed the vocal chant for "The Rite of Peace"[^ref-26]. Reviewing the soundtrack album in 2009, RPGFan's Patrick Gann called Quest for Glory V "a half-decent game" but wrote that "most people who know anything about the game will agree that the soundtrack was what brought the game to life"[^ref-27].

### Technical Achievements
- First and only Quest for Glory game with 3D polygonal characters[^ref-1]
- Real-time combat in main game environment[^ref-1]
- Soundtrack by Chance Thomas, recorded mostly with live musicians, many of them members of the Utah Symphony[^ref-1][^ref-27]
- New graphics engine programmed by Eric Lengyel[^ref-1]
- Platform release: 1998, Windows and Macintosh[^ref-1][^ref-3]

## Legacy

Dragon Fire's reception as the series finale was mixed[^ref-35][^ref-5][^ref-19]. The game underwent a significant genre shift—while the first four Quest for Glory games were graphic adventures with RPG elements, Dragon Fire is an RPG with adventure elements[^ref-28]. This made combat a much larger part of the game, particularly notable since previous games allowed thieves and magic users to complete with minimal fighting[^ref-28]. RPGamer's reviewer saw it as a model of "how to end a series"[^ref-19], while Engadget's Rowan Kaiser found the move to 3D polygonal graphics unsuccessful and PC Gamer's Richard Cobbett said the game "stumbles with its 3D world and more arcade style action"[^ref-35][^ref-5].

The game's setting, inspired by ancient Greek myths and legends, introduces Silmaria as a land reminiscent of classical Greece with elements like the Rites of Rulership, Atlantis mythology, and creatures such as the Hydra and Cerberus[^ref-28].

Despite mixed reception, the game provided closure to the Hero's journey with numerous characters returning for the finale—including Elsa von Spielburg, Erasmus, Rakeesh, Katrina, Erana, and Bruno, who is revealed as the assassin[^ref-1].

Dragon Fire was a nominee for CNET Gamecenter's 1998 "Adventure Game of the Year" award, alongside Black Dahlia, King's Quest: Mask of Eternity and the winner, Grim Fandango[^ref-34][^ref-36].

The closure of Yosemite Entertainment on February 22, 1999 ended any possibility of future entries, making Dragon Fire the definitive conclusion to the Quest for Glory saga[^ref-1][^ref-4].
