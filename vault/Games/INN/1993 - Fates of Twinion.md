# Fates of Twinion

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

Fates of Twinion is a fantasy RPG dungeon crawler developed by Ybarra Productions, published by [[Sierra On-Line]] and released in 1993.[^ref-18][^ref-4] Originally designed as an online multiplayer game for The Sierra Network (later renamed ImagiNation Network), the game served as a successor to [[1991 - The Shadow of Yserbius|The Shadow of Yserbius]].[^ref-2] MobyGames describes the online original as "the graphical multi-user dungeon game on The Sierra Network".[^ref-18]

The CRPG Addict noted that Sierra "intended it not for authentic offline play but to whet the player's appetite for an online account".[^ref-4] Alongside the online game, Sierra sold "the solo version of the sequel/add-on" for offline play,[^ref-18] while the network itself was sold to AOL in 1996 and discontinued by 1998.[^ref-24][^ref-25] With the demise of INN the online games faded, though the HOTUD review hosted on MyAbandonware judges that, stripped of their multiplayer features, they remain decent RPGs.[^ref-14] Computer Gaming World recognized the game as a finalist for Online Game of the Year in June 1994.[^ref-2]

Reusing the original Yserbius engine, Sierra reportedly "tried to get the most story and diversity out of the engine and produced a somewhat more complex game, with a wider range of puzzles, traps, and turns".[^ref-18]

## Story Summary

The game's narrative centers around the realm of Twinion, where "once again brave warriors and wizards are needed to enter dangerous dungeons and uncover the secrets of the evils that plague the land".[^ref-18] Players must prove themselves worthy of being "Heroes who will solve the numerous quests and step through to Immortality".[^ref-9] The main quest involves finding four pieces of a map to challenge mighty beasts called Dralkarians.[^ref-7]

The player receives guidance through cryptic messages like "Find your fate in the portal east of here"[^ref-11] and must navigate complex dungeons filled with wraiths, illusions, golems, and vampire sorceresses.[^ref-11]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Fates of Twinion uses a first-person perspective.[^ref-18][^ref-27] The CRPG Addict found that it relies on the mouse to access inventory, the character sheet, spells and the combat menu, with few keyboard backups.[^ref-4] The game uses the same engine as The Shadow of Yserbius, with many graphics reused between the games.[^ref-12] Players explore new locations such as the Aqueduct, Twinion Falls and the Coliseum before working their way up to the Queen's Palace.[^ref-6]

Combat is fought in rounds against one or more groups of monsters; a lethal enough sword swipe can kill "two, three or four monsters with one round of combat".[^ref-27]

### Character Creation

Character creation offers a choice of race, guild (class) and alignment (Harmony or Chaos):[^ref-4]

**Races (8 total):**
- Human, Orc, Elf, Troll, Dwarf, Gnome, Halfling, Gremlin

**Guilds / classes (6 total):**
- Barbarian, Knight, Ranger, Thief, Cleric, Wizard

### Structure and Progression

The game features regenerating maps and fixed encounters, with unique gameplay mechanics including:[^ref-15]
- Protection spells that work on the NEXT round (important tactical consideration)[^ref-15]
- Teleportation systems between areas[^ref-15]
- Death serving as a travel mechanism to the top of the mountain rather than a traditional penalty[^ref-15]

Multiple dungeon levels must be explored, including Night Elf Ingress, The Enclave, aMAZEing, Aqueduct, Twinion Falls, and Coliseum.[^ref-7][^ref-9]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Music plays an important role in the game, with NPCs providing hints like "Music will open new doors for you".[^ref-11] The game features a "forcing" mechanic that allows players to equip items their guild originally cannot use.[^ref-17]

**Legendary Equipment:**[^ref-17]
| Item | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Sword of Ares | Legendary melee weapon |
| Bow of Eos | Legendary ranged weapon |
| Gaea's Flail | Legendary flail |
| Neptune's Trident | Legendary polearm |
| Jester's Cap | Best helmet, usable by all guilds |
| Skeleton Keys | Guild-specific keys for reaching inaccessible areas[^ref-7] |

The game has complex dungeon exploration with hidden doors, teleport squares, and doors marked for Harmony-only or Chaos-only characters.[^ref-7][^ref-11] Some areas are class-specific, requiring particular guilds to access.[^ref-7]

### Critical Assessment

Reviewing the boxed set, Bernie Yee of *Computer Gaming World* (February 1994) called it "a hollow shell of its vibrant on-line self", adding that "Playing Yserbius without fellow on-line gamers is like being in an amusement park after hours, one in which the rides aren't all that fun to begin with".[^ref-27]

Repeating his verdict on Yserbius, the CRPG Addict said his impression of Twinion was identical: "a competent enough dungeon crawler, not terribly far from Wizardry or The Bard's Tale in quality, except for the baffling decision not to allow the single player to create a full party".[^ref-4] However, they also noted it "feels like an empty, unfinished, single-player Wizardry".[^ref-4]

Their detailed critique elaborated: "Twinion offers too much breadth—too large a physical space—without enough depth or immersion. It's a line rather than a cube. The online version of Twinion must have at least been a square. Multiplayer interaction and the increase in combat tactics would have created greater depth."[^ref-21]

### Modern Assessment

Modern reviewers have been more positive about the game's qualities as a dungeon crawler. The HOTUD review hosted on MyAbandonware (its text also appears on ClassicReload) notes that "Stripped off its multiplayer features, they are still decent RPGs, with a good variety of monsters, spells, interesting sub-quests", calls them solid dungeon crawls "that are definitely better than average" and ends "Highly recommended for RPG beginners".[^ref-14]

The speedrunning community maintains interest, with one player noting that "Yserbius (in the Medievalands engine) can be beaten in under two hours from new characters."[^ref-22]

## Development

### Origins

Fates of Twinion was developed as part of Sierra's ambitious early online gaming initiative through The Sierra Network.[^ref-2] The game was designed to build upon the success of The Shadow of Yserbius, serving as its direct successor.[^ref-2] Reusing the original game engine, Sierra aimed to get the most story and diversity out of it.[^ref-18]

### Production Challenges

The development was troubled. Richard Aronson, who went on to create [[1995 - The Ruins of Cawdor|The Ruins of Cawdor]] on the same code base and fixed Twinion's system-code fatals, said that "Fates of Twinion was so buggy QA refused to test anymore".[^ref-6] Brian Thomson noted the technical challenges of early online gaming: "Ken [Williams] had no idea at the how hardcore gamers would eat his bandwidth".[^ref-6]

The game was produced by Joe Ybarra.[^ref-6] MobyGames' "Created By" credits include Joe Ybarra, Michael Moore, Scot Amos, Mark Dickenson, Fred Butts, Wendy Barlow and Sharon Chercowy, among others.[^ref-8] Russell Lieblich composed the music.[^ref-8]

**Playtesters:**[^ref-8]
Bob Blees, Carlos Flores, Paul Lavelle, Fuzz Lokajicek, Alisa Schaefbauer, Rich Waters

**Special Thanks:**[^ref-8]
Ken Williams, ImagiNation Network testers, ImagiNation Network friends, Sierra-Online friends

### Technical Achievements

Fates of Twinion was built on the same engine as **The Shadow of Yserbius**, which opened on The Sierra Network in 1992 (InterAction's Fall 1992 issue called its MedievaLand "the newest area on TSN")[^ref-28]; the network was renamed the ImagiNation Network after AT&T bought a 20 percent stake in July 1993, more than a year before its November 1994 buyout.[^ref-6][^ref-24] It did not stray far from Yserbius' model, offering a different dungeon to explore, and many graphics are reused between the two games.[^ref-6][^ref-12] Sound uses the AIL / Miles Sound System.[^ref-18]

Aronson also put an end to rampant cheat-code use: any player who attempted to use one automatically received a week's suspension from the game.[^ref-6]

## Legacy

### Historical Significance

The Shadow of Yserbius "was the first of three graphical MUDs for the online community",[^ref-2] and Fates of Twinion was the second; [[1995 - The Ruins of Cawdor|The Ruins of Cawdor]] became "the final of the INN's trilogy of graphical MUDs."[^ref-6] Yserbius and Twinion were also repackaged together in a retail "deluxe pack".[^ref-6]

### Network History

The Sierra Network (later ImagiNation Network) opened in 1991, and America Online bought it from AT&T on August 7, 1996, for an undisclosed amount;[^ref-24] Al Lowe later put the price at "10 million".[^ref-26] By 1998 the service was discontinued.[^ref-25] Fan-revived online play via the ImagiNation Network Revival Project has since ended; Sierra Help now says the project "is no longer active" and points to the INN Barn reimplementation.[^ref-29] (Wikipedia's date of early 2016 for the shutdown carries a "citation needed" tag, and no source for it has been found.)

### Modern Revival

The game has found new life through preservation efforts and modern remakes. ZaneDubya created MedievaLands, stating "MedievaLands is my remake of these favorite games from my childhood".[^ref-13] The game continues to be remembered fondly by early online gaming enthusiasts, with one player noting "The Shadow of Yserbius and the Fates of Twinion were my first experience with MUDs/Proto-MMOs".[^ref-23]

### Player Memories

GOG wishlist requests demonstrate ongoing interest, with users sharing memories of this pioneering online experience.[^ref-23] The game remains an important historical artifact of Sierra's experimental period in online gaming.

## Easter Eggs and Trivia

- Game allows players to play as gremlins in character creation[^ref-7]
- Skill and attribute points have caps (e.g., Agility max 8, skills max 12)[^ref-21]
- Players can level up to at least level 20[^ref-21]
- Some doors open only for characters of a given alignment, Harmony or Chaos[^ref-7][^ref-11]
- Guardian companions can be obtained as quest rewards[^ref-7]
