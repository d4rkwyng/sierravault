# A-10 Tank Killer v1.5

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

A-10 Tank Killer is a combat flight simulation that puts players in the cockpit of the A-10 Thunderbolt II, also known as the "Warthog"[^ref-1][^ref-2]. Dynamix first released the game in 1989 for DOS and in 1990 for Amiga[^ref-3]. Version 1.5, the subject of this page, was published by Dynamix and Sierra On-Line for DOS on July 1, 1991 and for Amiga on October 30, 1991[^ref-25]. According to its manual, the simulation was designed to be "as realistic as possible without bogging the game down with complicated controls"[^ref-4]. The game focuses on close air support, mainly hunting enemy tanks but also destroying roads and bridges[^ref-8].

Version 1.5 added seven new scenarios covering the Iraqi war and Operation Desert Storm[^ref-4], along with improved graphics and sounds[^ref-3]. Old-Games.com notes that the timing suited the game: "the early 1990's saw the flame up of the Persian Gulf War, a military action that was built for the A-10"[^ref-9]. Dynamix had been sold to Sierra On-Line in August 1990[^ref-8], and the updated version used the flight model from Dynamix's successful Red Baron simulator[^ref-9][^ref-27].

## Story Summary

A-10 Tank Killer places players in the role of an American A-10 Thunderbolt II pilot. Its missions are set in the Gulf War air campaign and in a fictional European scenario[^ref-3], which Old-Games.com describes as "somewhat contrived European WWIII fictional scenarios"[^ref-9]. The simulation features dynamic campaign elements where "actions in one mission affect another - something you failed to destroy in one mission can attack you later"[^ref-8]. The original version included six missions[^ref-4]. Version 1.5 expanded this to 21 missions, including seven new Iraq scenarios[^ref-4], which the box copy calls "7 all new Persian Gulf scenarios"[^ref-18].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Players could control the aircraft using keyboard, joystick, or mouse input[^ref-4]. On the VOGONS forum, one long-time Sierra collector suggested that the game's mouse behaviour may be linked to Dynamix often designing games of this era "to be playable without a mouse, where the keyboard cursor keys can move the cursor from button to button"[^ref-20].

### Structure and Progression

A-10 Tank Killer features campaign missions. The original release included 6 missions, expanded to 21 in version 1.5[^ref-4]. The game's dynamic campaign system means that mission outcomes affect subsequent scenarios, so something left undestroyed in one mission can attack the player later[^ref-8]. Players must master ground attack techniques, as the A-10's primary role involves close air support rather than air-to-air combat[^ref-9].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The simulation emphasizes ground attack missions using the A-10's 30mm Avenger cannon, which can fire around 70 times a second[^ref-8]. Players must learn low-altitude attack runs while avoiding enemy surface-to-air missiles and anti-aircraft fire[^ref-11]. Zzap!'s 1991 review of the original version noted that Dynamix were aware of its simplifications and intended the game "to be more of an entertainment package"[^ref-1].

### Modern Assessment

MyAbandonware users rate version 1.5 at 4.63/5 (54 votes)[^ref-16] and the original version at 4.44/5[^ref-17]. A 2008 review of version 1.5 noted that "it was relatively easy and it was painless to pick up and play in short bursts"[^ref-11], approving of the game's "fun over realism design choice"[^ref-11]. Old-Games.com described version 1.5 as "a forerunner of the highly successful flight simulation genre" and "a must-have for all fans of World War sims"[^ref-9].

## Development

### Origins

A-10 Tank Killer was developed by Dynamix, originally named Software Entertainment Company, founded by Jeff Tunnell and Damon Slye, both graduates from the University of Oregon[^ref-8]. The company was sold to Sierra On-Line in August 1990[^ref-8]. According to Damon Slye, the original game was initially planned as part of a larger project encompassing aircraft from different eras, but Ken Williams suggested making several games dedicated to single time periods each[^ref-19].

### Production

The development team included Lincoln Hutton, David McClurg, Mark Brenneman, Cyrus Kanga, Kobi Miller, Damon Slye, Alan McKean, Bryce Morcello, Piotr Lukaszuk, Jerry Luttrell, Randy Dersham, Christopher Reese, and Nels Bruckner[^ref-4]. Alan McKean also composed the game's music[^ref-3]. Zzap!'s 1991 review of the original noted that Dynamix used digitized photos of real actors in flight gear and a digitized cockpit from a genuine A-10[^ref-1].

### Technical Achievements

According to Damon Slye, as reported by MobyGames, the original A-10 Tank Killer was "among the first if not the first PC simulator to utilize 256 color VGA graphics for 3D environments"[^ref-19]. Version 1.5 supported Tandy/PCjr, CGA, EGA, MCGA, and VGA graphics, with a maximum resolution of 320 x 200[^ref-4]. Audio support included PC Speaker, Tandy/PCjr, PS/1 Audio Card, Ad Lib, Sound Blaster, and Roland MT-32/LAPC-I[^ref-4]. The game required 512 KB of RAM (640 KB for Tandy systems) and had an installed size of 2.7 MB[^ref-4]. The Amiga version required 1MB of RAM and came on three disks[^ref-15].

## Legacy

A-10 Tank Killer's success led to a sequel, Silent Thunder: A-10 Tank Killer II, released in March 1996[^ref-10]. Combined sales of A-10 Tank Killer and its sequel surpassed 250,000 units by the end of March 1996[^ref-3]. GameSpot's Chris Hudak praised Silent Thunder as "a wicked, clean, no-B.S. combat sim with enough options and realism to hold the serious gamer's interest," awarding it 8.5/10[^ref-10].

The game was included in Sierra compilations such as Sierra's Value Pack 3, Aces: The Complete Collector's Edition and the Aces Collection Series[^ref-21], and has been documented by fan communities[^ref-22][^ref-24]. VOGONS forum users have reported a mouse movement bug in version 1.5, where "when moving the mouse cursor down or right, it moves very slow" but "when moving it up or left, it moves at normal speed"[^ref-20]. DOSBox author ripsaw8080 posted a small fix program, A10FIX, in the same thread[^ref-20].

Due to its violence level, A-10 Tank Killer was placed on the "Index" in Germany[^ref-3].
