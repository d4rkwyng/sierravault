# Carcassonne

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

**Carcassonne** is a turn-based strategy video game for the Xbox 360's Xbox Live Arcade, developed by Sierra Online Seattle (formerly Secret Lair Studios) and published by Sierra Online, a division of Vivendi Games.[^ref-23][^ref-6][^ref-9][^ref-25] Released on June 27, 2007 for 800 Microsoft Points, it is a digital adaptation of Klaus-Jürgen Wrede's tile-laying board game of the same name, which won the Spiel des Jahres in 2001.[^ref-24][^ref-17][^ref-19]

The game was the second German-style designer board game on Xbox Live Arcade, following Catan, and one of a trio of board games Microsoft had announced for the service at the 2006 Games Convention in Leipzig.[^ref-1][^ref-11][^ref-25] Unlike Catan's initial release, which included only the base game, Carcassonne shipped with the board game's "The River" expansion included in the download.[^ref-1][^ref-11][^ref-6]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The Xbox 360 version features a controller-based interface for tile placement and follower deployment; the triggers rotate a tile, the bumpers zoom, and the right stick moves the camera.[^ref-13] Each player has independent camera controls for panning and zooming across the growing city landscape.[^ref-4] When zoomed out, the cursor automatically follows the active player by default.[^ref-4] The playing field cannot be rotated, only zoomed and panned.[^ref-27]

The game offers placement hints after a tile is drawn, and a step-by-step tutorial teaches the rules.[^ref-16][^ref-23] Eurogamer found the interface "nice and streamlined", though it noted that on-screen text was often too small to read and that a zoomed-out board was hard to make out on standard-definition TVs.[^ref-8]

### Core Mechanics

Carcassonne is set in a medieval landscape built from 72 tiles, the full tile set of the original board game.[^ref-23][^ref-24] Players begin from a single starting tile and take turns drawing from the pool of remaining tiles to build a growing landscape of cities, roads, monasteries, and fields.[^ref-28][^ref-2]

Each turn, players draw a random tile and must place it adjacent to existing tiles, matching edges appropriately (cities connect to cities, roads to roads, etc.). After placing a tile, players may optionally deploy one of their seven followers ("meeples") onto a feature of that tile to claim ownership and score points.[^ref-2][^ref-16]

### Scoring

Points are earned by completing features:
- **Cities** - Score based on the number of tiles and pennants
- **Roads** - Score based on length
- **Monasteries** - Score when completely surrounded by tiles
- **Fields** - Score at game end based on completed cities they touch

The game ends when all tiles have been placed. Players then score their remaining claimed features, and the player with the most points wins.[^ref-2] Both the American and the international rule sets are included, with the American rules as the default.[^ref-16]

### Multiplayer Features

Up to four players can play on a single Xbox 360, and up to five over Xbox Live.[^ref-23][^ref-24][^ref-6] Ranked matches use skill-based matchmaking and are limited to two players.[^ref-24][^ref-13] The game supports the Xbox Live Vision camera, letting players see each other during play, and offers custom AI settings for any skill level.[^ref-24][^ref-23]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Carcassonne received generally favorable reviews, with a Metacritic score of 79 based on 20 critic reviews.[^ref-5]

IGN awarded the game an 8/10 ("great"). Reviewer Daemon Hatfield wrote: "The question was whether or not an Xbox Live Arcade version would retain what made the real-world game fun, and the answer is 'indeed.'"[^ref-3][^ref-4] He criticised the thin single-player mode, saying that apart from a handful of achievements "there isn't any compelling reason to play by yourself."[^ref-4]

GameSpot's Alex Navarro gave it 7.5, calling it "a simple, yet effective translation of the German board game" and "an oddly addictive experience".[^ref-10] Eurogamer's Mathew Kumar gave it 7/10. He praised the offline multiplayer mode for up to four players, but judged that it "just isn't based on a board game quite as good as the one Catan is."[^ref-8]

## Development

### Origins

Carcassonne was developed by Sierra Online Seattle, a studio in Issaquah, Washington, formerly known as Secret Lair Studios. Vivendi Games bought the studio on September 28, 2006, together with Studio Ch'in in Shanghai.[^ref-25][^ref-26] At the time of the purchase, Sierra Online already had Carcassonne in development for Xbox Live Arcade, alongside Assault Heroes.[^ref-26] MobyGames credits Sierra Online – Shanghai with additional development and graphics, SomaTone Interactive Audio with music, and VMC Game Labs with testing.[^ref-6]

### Production

Sierra Online game designer Isaac Barry described the guiding principle at a May 2007 preview: "Because of the fact that there are so many loyal Carcassonne fans, the first and foremost ethic behind the design is that we don't change anything."[^ref-16] The cel-shaded visuals were meant to resemble the board game's hand-drawn pieces.[^ref-16]

### Technical Specifications

- **Platform:** Xbox 360 (Xbox Live Arcade)[^ref-23]
- **Price:** 800 Microsoft Points[^ref-24][^ref-23]
- **Players:** 1–4 local; 2–5 online; ranked matches 2 players[^ref-23][^ref-6][^ref-13]
- **Rating:** ESRB E (Everyone)[^ref-24][^ref-28]
- **Achievements:** 12 achievements, 200 Gamerscore[^ref-24]
- **Video/Audio:** HDTV 720p, Dolby Digital 5.1[^ref-23][^ref-6]
- **Vision Camera:** Supported[^ref-24]

### Downloadable Content

As with the board game, expansion packs were made available; each can be switched on or off in the options.[^ref-13]
- **"The River"** - 12 river tiles, included with the main download[^ref-23][^ref-8]
- **"The River II"** - released August 8, 2007 for 300 Microsoft Points; 12 more river tiles, including a fork[^ref-29][^ref-13]
- **"King & Baron"** - released October 3, 2007 for 300 Microsoft Points; 7 new tiles and King and Baron bonus scoring[^ref-30][^ref-13]

Major Nelson's marketplace listings noted that both paid packs were not available in Korea.[^ref-29][^ref-30] Before launch, Sierra Online said that further board game add-ons such as "Inns & Cathedrals" and "The Tower" would be released through Xbox Live.[^ref-27][^ref-1]

## Legacy

Carcassonne was one of several Xbox Live Arcade titles from Sierra Online, which positioned itself as "a key publisher of Xbox Live Arcade titles" after the 2006 studio acquisitions.[^ref-26][^ref-25] Its other XBLA projects included Assault Heroes, and an investor presentation also listed Ultra Mini Golf, The Incredible Machine and a Battlestar Galactica title.[^ref-25]

On February 29, 2016, Carcassonne joined the Xbox One backward compatibility program.[^ref-31][^ref-14]

### Availability

The game was added to Xbox One backward compatibility in 2016.[^ref-31] Whether it can still be bought today has not been verified for this page. Later Carcassonne video games for other platforms, including Nintendo DS (2009), iOS (2010) and Android (2011), are separate products.[^ref-6]
