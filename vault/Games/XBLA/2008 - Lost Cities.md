# Lost Cities

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

**Lost Cities** is a digital card game for Xbox Live Arcade, developed by Sierra Online Shanghai (formerly Studio Ch'in) and published by Sierra Online, the Vivendi Games division that published its Xbox Live Arcade titles.[^ref-8][^ref-25][^ref-28] Sierra Online announced it on March 24, 2008,[^ref-7][^ref-26] and it was released on April 23, 2008, for 800 Microsoft Points, worldwide except Japan and Korea.[^ref-21][^ref-15] It adapts Reiner Knizia's 1999 card game of the same name.[^ref-6][^ref-3][^ref-25]

Players mount expeditions to five lost cities, each represented by a colored suit of cards, and must manage risk and timing so that each expedition they start returns a profit rather than a loss.[^ref-22][^ref-23] The game was removed from the Xbox Live Arcade in early 2009, only the second game ever pulled from the service.[^ref-27][^ref-14]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

TeamXbox's reviewer called the controls "all but idiotproof" and judged that the developers had stayed faithful to the original card game.[^ref-23] A tutorial mode introduces the rules.[^ref-22]

The game supports the Xbox Live Vision camera, so players can see their opponents during matches.[^ref-22][^ref-24] Offline, players face computer opponents at three difficulty levels (Easy, Normal and Hard), one opponent per level.[^ref-22][^ref-24]

### Core Mechanics

Lost Cities is played with a 60-card deck in five colors, one per expedition.[^ref-22][^ref-23] Each color has:
- **3 Investment Cards**, which multiply the value of an expedition (and also multiply its losses)
- **9 Expedition Cards**, numbered 2 through 10[^ref-22][^ref-24]

Each player holds eight cards. On each turn a player either plays a card to an expedition or discards it, then draws from the deck or from a discard pile, so the hand always stays at eight.[^ref-22][^ref-15] Cards in an expedition must go up in value, though not necessarily in sequence. Discards can be picked up by the opponent.[^ref-22][^ref-24]

### Scoring System

The scoring creates tension between risk and reward:[^ref-22][^ref-24]

1. **Base Cost**: starting an expedition costs 20 points.
2. **Card Values**: the face value of each expedition card played is added.
3. **Investment Multipliers**: investment cards must be played before any numbered card. One investment card doubles the expedition's total, so the starting cost becomes −40. Two make it ×3 and −60.

A well-planned expedition can score well, but a poorly managed one ends in heavy losses, especially when investment cards are involved.[^ref-23][^ref-24]

### Match Structure

A round ends when the draw deck runs out.[^ref-22][^ref-24] Each game consists of three rounds, and the player with the highest total score wins.[^ref-24][^ref-1]

### Multiplayer Features

Lost Cities supports online play over Xbox Live for up to four players.[^ref-21][^ref-23] The original card game was designed for two players.[^ref-6] The Xbox Live Arcade version adds popular variant rules for four-player free-for-all games and two-on-two team games.[^ref-23][^ref-24] In team games, teammates can pass cards to each other.[^ref-15] The game also has leaderboards and achievements.[^ref-22]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Reviews were mixed to positive. Metacritic's 2009 listing gives a Metascore of 66, based on 17 critic reviews.[^ref-8]

TeamXbox scored it 85. Its review said "Simple gameplay, great presentation and a finely tuned balance of luck and skill are the elements that made Lost Cities a hit with card- and board-game fans, and this version just makes all of that even better."[^ref-23][^ref-8] Official Xbox Magazine also gave it 85 and called it "Live Arcade's latest gem". GameSpot gave it 80, writing "Card games don't get any more addictive than Lost Cities."[^ref-8]

Other critics were cooler. IGN's Ryan Geddes scored it 55 and concluded that it "makes for a pretty thin Xbox Live experience".[^ref-25][^ref-8] Eurogamer gave it 60, writing that the port "does little to justify the leap from deck to joypad".[^ref-8]

Lost Cities was nominated for Best Family Game in the official Xbox Live Arcade Community Awards for 2008.[^ref-27]

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **Metacritic (2009 snapshot):** 66/100 from 17 critic reviews. The average user rating was 6.8 from 10 votes.[^ref-8]
- **MobyGames:** critics' average 68% (12 reviews).[^ref-15]
- **GameFAQs (users):** rated "Good" (4 ratings).[^ref-9]
- **BoardGameGeek (the original 1999 card game):** about 7.3/10 community rating.[^ref-6]

## Development

### Origins

In September 2006 Vivendi Games bought two studios to build up Sierra Online's Xbox Live Arcade publishing: Secret Lair Studios in Issaquah, Washington, and Studio Ch'in in Shanghai. Sierra Online renamed them Sierra Online Seattle and Sierra Online Shanghai.[^ref-28] Jason Robar was head of studios for both.[^ref-28] IGN's review describes Lost Cities as "Adapted for Xbox Live by Sierra Online's Shanghai studio".[^ref-25]

### Production

Sierra Online announced the game on March 24, 2008, with six screenshots and an 800-point ($10) price.[^ref-26][^ref-7] Previews from TeamXbox (March 21, 2008) and IGN (March 28, 2008) described:
- a tutorial mode
- three AI difficulty levels
- four-player and two-on-two play over Xbox Live
- Xbox Live Vision camera support
- leaderboards, achievements and unlockable art[^ref-22][^ref-24]

### Technical Specifications

- **Platform:** Xbox 360 (Xbox Live Arcade)[^ref-21]
- **Price:** 800 Microsoft Points[^ref-21]
- **Rating:** ESRB E (Everyone)[^ref-21][^ref-8]
- **Players:** 1 against the computer, or 2–4 online, including two-on-two teams[^ref-8][^ref-23][^ref-24]
- **Vision Camera:** supported[^ref-22][^ref-24]
- **AI Difficulty Levels:** three (Easy, Normal, Hard)[^ref-22]

### Sierra Online on Xbox Live Arcade

Lost Cities was one of several Sierra Online Xbox Live Arcade releases. When Vivendi announced the studio purchases in 2006, it said its XBLA plans included:[^ref-28]
- *Assault Heroes*
- *Carcassonne*
- versions of *Ultra Mini Golf* and *The Incredible Machine*
- a *Battlestar Galactica* title

MobyGames counts roughly 15 Sierra Online releases between 2006 and 2008. It records that the Sierra Online division was dissolved after Activision and Vivendi merged in July 2008.[^ref-4]

## Legacy

### Delisting

In early 2009 Lost Cities disappeared from the Xbox Live Marketplace. 1UP reported it on April 16, 2009, as "just the second time since the Xbox Live Arcade was launched" that a game had been removed; the first was *Yaris*.[^ref-27] At the time 1UP called the reason "unclear" and pointed to Joystiq's theory of licensing problems after the Activision–Vivendi merger.[^ref-27]

On April 28, 2009, Joystiq reported, citing *Official Xbox Magazine* via GamerBytes, that "the Lost Cities license reverted back to the original licensor after the merger". Without that license, Activision could no longer sell the game.[^ref-29] Joystiq added that the trial version could still be re-downloaded but the full version could not.[^ref-29] Delisted Games likewise attributes the delisting to Sierra Online losing the license from Reiner Knizia. It records the game as last confirmed available on January 22, 2009, with its availability questioned by February 20, 2009.[^ref-14] TrueAchievements lists the game as removed from the Microsoft Store.[^ref-12]

### Related Games

Reiner Knizia's Lost Cities has been adapted digitally again since the XBLA release:
- BlueLine Games released a separate, officially licensed *Lost Cities* on Steam on May 19, 2018.[^ref-30][^ref-14] It is not the Sierra version.
