# Quest for Glory II VGA Remake

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

Quest for Glory II VGA Remake is a fan-made recreation of Sierra On-Line's 1990 adventure-RPG hybrid *Quest for Glory II: Trial by Fire*, developed and published by [[AGD Interactive]] and released on August 24, 2008[^ref-6][^ref-11]. The project replaced the original's 16-color EGA graphics and typed-parser interface with VGA-style artwork and point-and-click icons[^ref-21][^ref-6]. *Trial by Fire* was the only game in the series that Sierra never took beyond EGA graphics, so the remake gave it its first VGA presentation[^ref-11]. It was distributed as a free download[^ref-8][^ref-22].

Engadget reported that the remake had been "a labor of love in development off and on for nearly seven years"[^ref-4]. AGD Interactive, the team behind the King's Quest I and II remakes, kept the plot unchanged while adding interface options and a revamped combat system[^ref-5][^ref-6]. AGD Interactive's own site calls it an "award-winning Adventure/RPG game"[^ref-3].

## Story Summary

AGD Interactive's synopsis begins: "Far across the blazing desert lies the beautiful city of Shapeir, a peaceful place where folks are friendly and a Hero can relax for a while"[^ref-24]. The Hero soon receives a warning about Raseir, "once the mirror image of Shapeir, now a dark reflection ruled by evil," whose poison threatens to seep into Shapeir and doom the land and its people[^ref-24].

The Hero must navigate Shapeir's maze of alleyways, meet its inhabitants, and grow stronger through combat and skill development. Along the way players meet characters including Rakeesh, Uhura and Ad Avis, whom Engadget described as appearing "in their 256-color glory"[^ref-4][^ref-21]. AGD Interactive kept the plot, characters and locations the same as the original, adding only "small, additional features"[^ref-6].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The remake replaces the original's text parser with point-and-click icons for Walk, Look, Interact, Inventory, Special Actions and Spells[^ref-6]. For players who prefer the original style, AGD Interactive kept a text parser option for typing conversation topics. A combination mode allows point-and-click and the parser to be used at the same time[^ref-6]. PopMatters noted that players can "switch between parser mode and icon mode whenever you like"[^ref-17].

Dialog uses small character pictures in the style of Quest for Glory III, with point-and-click dialog trees in the style of Quest for Glory IV[^ref-6].

### Structure and Progression

The game keeps the original's adventure-RPG structure: players take on the role of a fighter, magic user or thief[^ref-5]. Stats work the same way as in the rest of the Quest for Glory series[^ref-6].

The alleyways have been redesigned from the original's dark, scrolling tunnels into spaces that are easier to navigate and do not scroll[^ref-6]. Players can choose the original alleyway layout or a simplified layout in which important locations link directly to one another with straight roads[^ref-6]. AGD Interactive says it is also "easier to maintain your orientation by looking at the skyline"[^ref-6]. The remake also adds a day-to-night fading cycle that closely resembles Quest for Glory III's[^ref-23].

### Character Import and Export

The remake supports the series' character transfer system[^ref-6][^ref-19]. Players can import a Hero from *Quest for Glory I: So You Want To Be a Hero* by copying its .sav file into the remake's directory, and can export the finished Hero for use in *Quest for Glory III: Wages of War*[^ref-6][^ref-23].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

PopMatters described the game's core as "a melding between RPG grinding and solving puzzles," noting that certain puzzles require a particular stat[^ref-17]. PC World observed that puzzles and opponents are tackled differently depending on the player's class[^ref-18].

According to AGD Interactive, the revamped combat system includes "many enhancements, new moves, new features, clever AI, and some auto-battle assistance" for players without sharp reflexes[^ref-6]. The AGS panel nevertheless warned that the high combat difficulty, "even after it is adjusted," may turn away some puzzle-oriented players[^ref-5].

AGD Interactive kept the ability to drop items that are needed later. This preserves deaths, jokes and Easter eggs that only happen that way, although it means players can still reach dead ends[^ref-23].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

PopMatters' L.B. Jeffries rated the remake 9/10 in September 2008. He wrote that AGD Interactive "has taken that classic, removed what few problems it had, and somehow made an even better game for fans to re-experience for no cost"[^ref-17].

The Adventure Game Studio panel gave it 4 cups, calling it "a solid remake of the original that tries to improve on its predecessor in nearly every way, often succeeding"[^ref-5].

### Later Assessment

In July 2012, PC World's Jim Norris rated the remake 4/5. He wrote that AGD Interactive "has rescued this fading star from obscurity with a slick remake, keeping the golden age experience intact while elevating the visuals and user interface to relevancy once again"[^ref-18].

AGS community reviewer Jester called it "a nearly flawless remake of a classic," adding that "the strengths of the original game are emphasized, and the weaknesses are eliminated. This game should not be missed by any fan of Sierra-style games"[^ref-5].

Another community member, Mandle, wrote: "This game is AMAZING once you get into it and start learning the lay-out and culture of the city. It begins to feel like you are in a real place living your character's life rather than playing an adventure game"[^ref-5].

## Development

### Origins

Quest for Glory II: Trial by Fire was originally released by Sierra On-Line in November 1990[^ref-21][^ref-7]. It was the only game in the series that Sierra never took beyond EGA graphics[^ref-11]. AGD Interactive notes that its 16-color graphics, parser-only interface and confusing street maze made it hard for new players to get into[^ref-21].

AGD Interactive says plans to remake the game were made before its first project, King's Quest I VGA, was released. A few early screenshots posted on its site suggested the remake "would be in high demand indeed"[^ref-21].

### Production

According to AGD Interactive's history page, the project's foundation was made in 2001: a set of Quest for Glory II screens connected without alleyways or much interaction. The project was not fully picked up again until 2003, shortly after the release of King's Quest II+[^ref-21]. Around 2004/2005, most animations had been created and a basic path from start to finish could be played. Years of refinement and bug-fixing followed[^ref-21]. Engadget summarised the effort as nearly seven years of on-and-off development[^ref-4].

The team kept the game faithful to the original, with "no plot changes"[^ref-6]. PC World highlighted "lovingly recreated full VGA backdrops, fresh music, and new backstory content left out of the original due to space and time constraints"[^ref-18]. Unlike AGD Interactive's King's Quest I and II remakes, it has no voice acting. AGD Interactive's FAQ says a voice pack add-on will not be made because the dialogue workload "would be immense"[^ref-23].

The credits list Lori Ann Cole and Corey Cole as writers and designers, alongside the AGD Interactive team[^ref-20].

### Technical Details

The remake was built with the Adventure Game Studio (AGS) engine[^ref-5][^ref-11]. AGD Interactive describes the result as "pseudo-VGA"[^ref-3].

### Technical Specifications

- **Resolution:** 320x200, 32-bit colour, as listed by AGS[^ref-5]. Engadget described the art as "256-color"[^ref-4]. PC World noted scaling options up to 1280x800[^ref-18].
- **Engine:** Adventure Game Studio[^ref-5]
- **Platforms:** Windows (XP through 11); Mac OS X 10.4.11–10.6, Intel only, X11 required[^ref-22]
- **Media:** Digital download (v2.0: Windows 71 MB; Mac 587 MB, though AGDI's download page labels it 142 MB)[^ref-22]

### Technical Issues

AGD Interactive's FAQ warns that its download mirrors "may go offline from time to time" and advises reloading the download page and trying again[^ref-6].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

AGD Interactive says players who explore will find "several fun extras on the side"[^ref-6]. The "controversial" X-ray glasses Easter egg from the original is included[^ref-23]. A bug that let players sell the Dervish's beard repeatedly at the Magic shop briefly appeared in the remake by accident and was then removed[^ref-23].

AGD Interactive remade Quest for Glory II rather than the first game because Sierra had already remade Quest for Glory I in VGA in 1992. AGD Interactive only remakes older parser-based Sierra games[^ref-23].

## Legacy

### Distribution and Reception

As a fan project, the remake was released free through AGD Interactive's website[^ref-8][^ref-22]. AGS lists it as freeware[^ref-5]. One fan in a 2008 NeoGAF release thread wrote that they had been "waiting for this for years"[^ref-7]. AGS chose it as Pick of the Month in March 2009. AGS explained that the game had won the blog poll for a game that "should really have had more […] nominations" at that year's AGS Awards[^ref-5].

### Collections

The remake is a standalone freeware release, downloadable from AGD Interactive's website[^ref-22]. PCGamingWiki lists it as a free unofficial remake on its page for the original game[^ref-19].

### Fan Projects

The remake is part of AGD Interactive's run of Sierra remakes, which also includes King's Quest I and King's Quest II. AGD Interactive reports that its King's Quest II remake has been downloaded over 451,400 times[^ref-9].

### Critical Perspective

Reviewers praised the remake's balance of preservation and modernisation. PopMatters argued that it improves the interface more than it overhauls the visuals[^ref-17]. PC World called it "a hand-polished classic you should be embarrassed to download for free"[^ref-18].
