# King's Quest IV Remake (Infamous Adventures)

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

King's Quest IV Remake is an unreleased fan remake of Sierra's 1988 classic *King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella*, associated with [[Infamous Adventures]]. Infamous Adventures previously released VGA-style remakes of [[2006 - King's Quest III Remake (Infamous Adventures)|King's Quest III]] (2006) and [[2011 - Space Quest II VGA Remake|Space Quest II]] (2011).[^ref-9]

After completing their Space Quest II remake in December 2011, the founders announced in July 2012 that Infamous Adventures would stop producing games, and moved to commercial development as [[Infamous Quests]], releasing *Quest for Infamy* in 2014.[^ref-9] Whether the KQ4 remake is still active cannot be publicly confirmed. The only source naming it in recent years is a January 2026 Infamous Quests Patreon update that only patrons can read.[^ref-1] A September 2025 post on the same Patreon, also patron-only, is said to describe renewed activity on the Infamous Adventures Discord, but it does not name King's Quest IV.[^ref-2] The public Patreon posts since January 2026 do not mention the remake.[^ref-21][^ref-22] No release date has been announced.[^ref-1]

**Note:** This project is distinct from other King's Quest IV fan remakes, including DrSlash's *King's Quest IV Retold* (2021)[^ref-5] and Magic Quest Entertainment's standalone *King's Quest IV Retold VGA + Talkie* (2025).[^ref-6]

## Development

### Origins

Infamous Adventures was founded in 2004 by Shawn Mills and Steven Alexander to make games in the classic Sierra and LucasArts adventure style. Its games were built in Adventure Game Studio (AGS).[^ref-9] Its King's Quest III remake, released on June 19, 2006, replaced the original's text parser with a point-and-click interface and updated VGA graphics. A voice pack followed on August 21, 2006.[^ref-9] Infamous Adventures confirmed its Space Quest II VGA remake on April 1, 2007, and released it on December 30, 2011.[^ref-9] That remake featured hand-drawn graphics meant to emulate VGA and a voice pack of 4,000 recorded lines.[^ref-17]

In July 2012 the founders announced that Infamous Adventures would no longer produce games and that they had started Infamous Quests to make original adventure games.[^ref-9] *Quest for Infamy*, an adventure RPG inspired by Sierra's Quest for Glory series, was released on July 7, 2014.[^ref-9][^ref-23] Infamous Adventures later updated its free Space Quest II remake to version 2.0 on January 1, 2020.[^ref-18]

When work on the KQ4 remake began is not documented publicly, and the team working on it has not been publicly named. A King's Quest IV VGA remake has been linked to Infamous Adventures since at least 2005. That April, an AGDI forum thread noted that the Sponge Factory's KQ4 VGA project was "on Infamous Adventures". The project's lead wrote that he had not decided "whether I'll ever find the time in my busy life to work on this anymore."[^ref-19] No public source connects that project to the current one.

### Original Game Significance

King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella represented a major technical milestone for Sierra when released in 1988.[^ref-4] It was Sierra's first game to use the SCI (Sierra Creative Interpreter) engine, doubling the resolution and adding support for MIDI music.[^ref-4] Sierra was uncertain whether players had hardware capable of running the new engine, so they released a parallel AGI version at lower resolution.[^ref-4]

The game was marketed with considerable fanfare. One Sierra advertisement asked "Can a Computer Game Make a Person Cry?", and the back of the box boasted of over 40 minutes of music by William Goldstein.[^ref-4] Hardcore Gaming 101 notes that Sierra hyped the game as "a true work of art on par with literature and cinema," and that what stirred up emotions at the time "is now positively quaint."[^ref-4]

The same review says the game "goes a little too far" by showing Roberta Williams' face whenever the player dies or tries to quit.[^ref-4]

### Current Status

The only recent source is a patron-only post, so the details below cannot be checked publicly. The post is titled "Small Development Update" and dated January 11, 2026.[^ref-1] SierraVault recorded its wording at the time, and the quotes below come from that copy. The post said: "The team has been really active again and hustling away to finish this up. They have been polishing some things up and doing just some amazing work." It added: "As usual, there's no release date or timeline for any of this."[^ref-1] It reportedly included promotional box and poster art made by two team members, a few in-game images, and examples of reworked backgrounds.[^ref-1]

In the comments, one backer said the shots "look like what Sierra would have released for a remake of the game."[^ref-1] Replying to another comment, the Infamous Quests account said the work made them want to finish their old King's Quest III "gold" remake, adding: "CONSIDERING THAT OUR KING'S QUEST III Remake is 20 years old this year!!"[^ref-1]

No public source confirms the remake's engine, interface, platforms, or whether it will include voice acting. Infamous Adventures' earlier games were made in Adventure Game Studio,[^ref-9] but that has not been stated for this project.

## Story Summary

The remake will retell the story of the original King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella (1988), designed by [[Roberta Williams]].[^ref-4][^ref-12] The story begins immediately following King's Quest III, with King Graham suffering a massive heart attack as he tosses his adventurer's cap to his children.[^ref-5] Princess Rosella must travel to the enchanted land of Tamir to find a magical fruit that can save her dying father.[^ref-4]

In Tamir, Rosella encounters the good fairy Genesta, who is weakened by her nemesis, the evil witch Lolotte.[^ref-4] Rosella must complete tasks for Lolotte: capturing a unicorn, stealing a golden egg-laying hen, and uncovering Pandora's box. She must also help Genesta and find the magical fruit before time runs out.[^ref-4]

The original was the first King's Quest game with a female protagonist.[^ref-10] Hardcore Gaming 101 calls Rosella "the first leading woman in adventure gaming."[^ref-4] The game ran on a real-time clock, and certain puzzles could only be completed at specific times.[^ref-4]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Infamous Adventures' King's Quest III remake replaced the original's text parser with a point-and-click interface.[^ref-9] Whether the King's Quest IV remake takes the same approach has not been publicly stated.

### Structure and Progression

No gameplay details of the remake have been published. In the original, Rosella "has exactly one in-game day to complete all of the tasks." The day is divided into two halves, day and night, and some puzzles can only be completed in one of them.[^ref-4][^ref-12]

### Publicly Known Details

The only details about the remake's content come from the patron-only January 2026 post. They are promotional box and poster art and reworked background artwork.[^ref-1] No feature list, engine, interface or voice cast has been announced publicly.

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The original King's Quest IV featured several gameplay elements that have been divisive among fans and present interesting challenges for a modern remake.

In the infamous whale tongue section, Rosella is swallowed and must climb the whale's tongue to tickle its uvula with a feather. Hardcore Gaming 101 says "it's impossible to tell what parts of the tongue are climbable and which aren't."[^ref-4] Sierra itself acknowledged the frustration: Leisure Suit Larry 3 includes a sequence where the characters watch Rosella repeatedly failing the climb.[^ref-4] DrSlash's King's Quest IV Retold kept the ability to fall off edges while adding quality-of-life features.[^ref-5]

The bridle needed to capture the unicorn is not even a single pixel. It is entirely invisible, hidden behind an innocuous piece of wreckage on the deserted island.[^ref-4]

The game's real-time clock meant certain events could only occur during specific periods, which "can naturally muck you up if you dawdle."[^ref-4] DrSlash's Retold version included an option to remove dead ends, addressing one of the original's most frustrating aspects.[^ref-5]

How Infamous Adventures balances preservation of the original experience with modern accessibility improvements will be a key consideration for the remake.

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

As an unreleased project, the Infamous Adventures King's Quest IV remake has no reviews. The original 1988 game sold 100,000 copies in its first two weeks and 800,000 within a year.[^ref-12] Reviews were generally positive. Critics praised the advances in graphics and animation over earlier adventure games, but were mixed on the gameplay, with some finding puzzles obtuse or tedious.[^ref-12] The game won "Best Adventure or Fantasy/Role-Playing Program" at the 1989 Software Publishers Association awards.[^ref-12] Rosella has been considered one of the first "major female protagonists" in a video game.[^ref-12]

### Modern Assessment

Hardcore Gaming 101's retrospective notes that what "stirred up tremendous emotions from gamers at the time is now positively quaint," concluding: "What remains is, more or less, another King's Quest game."[^ref-4]

**Aggregate Scores (Original 1988 Game):**
- **MobyGames:** Moby Score 7.7; critics average 77% from 25 reviews[^ref-8]

Infamous Adventures' earlier remakes are well rated on itch.io. King's Quest III averages 4.9/5 from 8 ratings,[^ref-3] and Space Quest II averages 4.8/5 from 22 ratings.[^ref-18] The only recorded public reaction to the KQ4 remake is a single backer comment on the patron-only January 2026 post.[^ref-1]

### Comparisons to Other KQ4 Remakes

Several other fan groups have attempted King's Quest IV remakes over the years:[^ref-19][^ref-20]

- **King's Quest IV Retold** by DrSlash (2021) is a point-and-click AGS remake. It was nominated for "Best Game Created with AGS" at the 2021 AGS Awards, and it stays faithful to the original's story and graphics while adding quality-of-life features.[^ref-5] The AGS database page shows over 9,000 downloads, and the game was selected as "Pick of the Month" in August 2022.[^ref-5]
- **King's Quest IV Retold VGA + Talkie** by Magic Quest Entertainment (released 29 November 2025) is a standalone version of DrSlash's Retold, made with his blessing, with 256-colour graphics and voices. Its VGA graphics draw on unfinished KQ4 projects, and the credits thank contributors including Steven Alexander of Infamous Quests and notid1 of the Sponge Factory.[^ref-6]
- **Unicorn Tales** announced a 3D remake of King's Quest IV. The same site reports that Magic Mirror Games stopped work on its own 2D remake.[^ref-20]

A 2009 VOGONS post by the user Malik preferred Infamous Adventures' remakes to AGD Interactive's. The post said AGDI's King's Quest II remake "strayed slightly away from the classic," while IA's King's Quest III remake "stayed quite close to the original."[^ref-7]

### Original Game Context

The original King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella (1988) remains available for purchase on GOG.com as part of the King's Quest 4+5+6 collection,[^ref-10] and on Steam in the King's Quest Collection.[^ref-13] GOG reviewers have described King's Quest IV as containing "great puzzles and a day/night cycle which was a big thing at the time." They also noted it was "the last in the series which used the typing interface, and the first to have a female protagonist."[^ref-10] Because the original is still sold, players can experience both the classic and the fan remakes.

## Legacy

### Infamous Adventures' Impact

Infamous Adventures has a notable place in Sierra fan remake history. Its King's Quest III remake (2006) had been downloaded more than 180,000 times by October 2008.[^ref-9] On itch.io, the King's Quest III remake is rated 4.9/5 (8 ratings)[^ref-3] and the Space Quest II remake (2011) 4.8/5 (22 ratings).[^ref-18] Space Quest II received a version 2.0 update in January 2020 and a Universal Binary Mac build in September 2024.[^ref-18]

If the remake is released, it would mark a return to fan remakes after the founders moved to commercial development with Infamous Quests.[^ref-9] In a reply on the January 2026 Patreon post, the Infamous Quests account noted that its King's Quest III remake was "20 years old this year."[^ref-1]

### The Challenge of Multiple KQ4 Remakes

Hardcore Gaming 101's coverage of the original King's Quest IV noted that "A fan-made VGA remake has been in the works for a few years, but nothing has been released outside of some demo footage," without naming the project.[^ref-4] Attempts have included the Sponge Factory's stalled VGA project,[^ref-19] Unicorn Tales' 3D version and Magic Mirror Games' abandoned project.[^ref-20] They reflect the game's beloved status and how hard such fan projects are to complete.

Whether the activity described in patron-only posts leads to a release is unknown. The public April 2026 Patreon update said others had been working "behind the scenes to make some amazing things that I hope we can get out this year," but did not name King's Quest IV.[^ref-22]
