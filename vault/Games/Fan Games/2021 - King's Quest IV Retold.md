# King's Quest IV Retold

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

King's Quest IV Retold is a fan-made point-and-click remake of Sierra On-Line's classic 1988 adventure game *King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella*[^ref-1]. Created by DrSlash using the Adventure Game Studio engine, this remake was released on May 5, 2021, and introduces modern mouse-driven controls while preserving the original's EGA graphics at 320x200 resolution[^ref-2]. DrSlash aimed it first and foremost at players who prefer point-and-click interfaces over text input, while keeping the original's story and puzzles unchanged[^ref-17].

The remake received a nomination for Best Game Created with AGS at the AGS Awards 2021 and was selected as Pick of the Month in August 2022[^ref-2]. As of October 2026 the Adventure Game Studio site records more than 9,600 downloads of the game[^ref-2].

A subsequent enhanced version, King's Quest IV Retold VGA Talkie, was released on November 29, 2025 by Magic Quest Entertainment with DrSlash's blessing, adding voice acting and VGA graphics[^ref-1][^ref-4]. This version drew upon assets from multiple unfinished King's Quest IV fan projects, and its makers describe it as a tribute to those fan teams[^ref-4].

## Story Summary

The game's narrative picks up immediately where *King's Quest III: To Heir Is Human* concluded[^ref-2]. As King Graham tosses his adventurer's cap to his children, he suffers a massive heart attack and is left on his death bed[^ref-2].

The devastated Princess Rosella is contacted via the Magic Mirror by the good fairy Genesta in the faraway land of Tamir, who tells her of a magic fruit that can heal King Graham, which Rosella can have in exchange for helping Genesta[^ref-6]. Rosella agrees and is teleported to Tamir[^ref-6].

Genesta reveals that her archenemy, the evil fairy Lolotte, has stolen her talisman, without which Genesta will die in twenty-four hours and lacks the power to return Rosella to Daventry[^ref-6]. Rosella agrees to undertake a quest for both the talisman and the magic fruit[^ref-6], saving the land of Tamir from Lolotte along the way[^ref-2].

The original game gives the player a time limit of 24 hours of game time, divided into a day period and a night period; some activities must be completed during the day, while other puzzles can be solved only at night[^ref-6]. Tamir draws on well-known fairy-tale tropes such as haunted houses, ogres, dwarves and unicorns[^ref-20].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

King's Quest IV Retold replaces the original game's text parser system with full point-and-click mouse support[^ref-7]. DrSlash describes this as the most prominent change from the original SCI game, along with a completely reworked interface similar to that of later Sierra titles[^ref-8][^ref-17]. AGS user Creamy wrote that the mouse controls and other quality-of-life improvements make it "probably the best way to experience the game nowadays"[^ref-2].

Despite the interface modernization, the remake keeps the original's 320x200 EGA graphics rather than upgrading to VGA[^ref-8]. DrSlash made this decision partly to halve the workload but largely out of dissatisfaction with how previous remakes of 320x200 SCI games had translated EGA graphics into VGA, often losing the charm of the original art[^ref-8].

### Structure and Progression

The game maintains the original's structure, taking place across the land of Tamir during a 24-hour period[^ref-6]. Choosing Retold's option to remove dead ends also removes the four-hour time limit[^ref-17]. Some activities must be completed during daylight hours, while other puzzles can only be solved at night[^ref-6].

Locations named in DrSlash's changelog include Genesta's island, Lolotte's castle, the Manor, the organ room, the crypt, the ogres' house, the dwarf mine, the troll caves, the swamp, Cupid's pool and the fisherman's shack[^ref-17].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The remake preserves the original's puzzles while adding an option to remove all dead ends that could prevent the player from progressing[^ref-17]. Players can alternatively keep the dead ends and the time limit to play the game as originally designed[^ref-17]. As Creamy noted, Rosella can still fall off the edges[^ref-2].

Other changes include an autosave system that saves every 10 minutes or when the player enters a dangerous area, and reworked troll caves with new backgrounds replacing the original's pitch-black screens[^ref-17]. DrSlash states that no changes were made to the game's story or puzzles[^ref-17].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

As a fan project, King's Quest IV Retold did not receive traditional publication reviews but drew community feedback on the Adventure Game Studio website, where it has 12 user votes[^ref-2]. Indie Retro News covered the release on June 1, 2021[^ref-21].

Community member Creamy praised the remake: "The mouse controls, option to remove the dead ends and other quality of life improvements make it probably the best way to experience the game nowadays"[^ref-2]. Another player, sabrex, wrote: "Absolutely adored the point and click system, the beautiful music, and the incredible effect sounds which I had not heard in the original"[^ref-2].

The subsequent VGA Talkie version received more mixed feedback. User irvstart wrote that "this is literally the same game that I have always cherished (the same as KQ4 Retold, which, in turn is a perfect adaptation of the original Perils of Rosella), but enriched with new graphics and speech"[^ref-1]. However, Isaac45 offered criticism: "This game honestly, is sort of a mess? The scripting/programming is a bit all over the place - the textboxes don't sync with the text, only some lines are voiced, and places like the Troll Cave are a lot worse than previously"[^ref-1].

### Modern Assessment

As of October 2026, the Adventure Game Studio database lists more than 9,600 all-time downloads of the original Retold version[^ref-2] and more than 2,700 of the VGA Talkie version[^ref-1].

The project was recognized in community awards, receiving an AGS Awards 2021 nomination for Best Game Created with AGS and being selected as Pick of the Month in August 2022[^ref-2].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **Adventure Game Studio (Original):** 12 user votes[^ref-2]
- **Adventure Game Studio (VGA Talkie):** 9 user votes[^ref-1]

The original 1988 Sierra game that this remake is based upon was generally well reviewed, earning scores including 91% from Computer + Video Games and 9/10 from Datormagazin, and winning "Best Adventure or Fantasy/Role-Playing Program" at the 1989 Software Publishers Association awards[^ref-6].

## Development

### Origins

DrSlash made the remake alone ("Just me, myself, and I") as a spare-time project[^ref-17]. It began in September 2018 as a one-room demo marking the 10th anniversary of his introduction to AGS; about 90% of the game was then finished over two months, and the rest came slowly over the following two years[^ref-17]. He chose King's Quest IV because it had major flaws he wanted to fix and was historically significant[^ref-17].

DrSlash wrote that the decision to keep the EGA graphics "was also largely motivated by my dissatisfaction with the way many previous attempts at remaking 320x200 SCI games translated EGA graphics into VGA"[^ref-17]. In many (though not all) cases, he wrote, the resulting art looked blurry and much of the original's charm was lost[^ref-17].

### Production

Retold was built with Adventure Game Studio[^ref-2]; ScummVM's AGS engine identifies it under the game ID `kq4retold`[^ref-18]. DrSlash designed it to run on Linux, MacOS and any version of Windows from Windows XP onward[^ref-17], and the AGS site offers Windows, Linux and MacOS downloads[^ref-2].

The subsequent VGA Talkie version was developed by Magic Quest Entertainment, a non-profit fan project made with DrSlash's blessing[^ref-1]. Its makers call it "basically the extension of his project," built on the same code with graphics drawn from unfinished King's Quest IV fan projects[^ref-1]. The VGA Talkie version serves as "a tribute to all the original teams of creative fans, their devotion and engagement to bring back this wonderful story of Rosella back to us"[^ref-4].

### Technical Achievements

DrSlash describes Retold as combining the best features of the game's official releases: Roland MT-32 music, sound effects from the Apple IIGS release, environmental sounds from the Amiga release, and extra animation frames and more detailed backgrounds found only in early PC versions[^ref-17]. The game runs in 32-bit colour while keeping the original 320x200 resolution[^ref-2].

The VGA Talkie's own release notes state that, because the code was not built for speech, characters' animations are often not in sync with their voices[^ref-1]. One AGS user, Isaac45, claimed that many of its backgrounds were AI-generated[^ref-1].

### Technical Issues

The VGA Talkie's release notes state that it is fully supported by ScummVM 2026.1.0, but freezes at one point under ScummVM 2.9.0 and 2.9.1[^ref-1]. The same notes say portraits appear only once for each speaking character[^ref-1].

The original 1988 Sierra game had copy protection requiring manual lookups ("what's the Xth word on page x?"), which a GOG forum user called tedious; another user suggested Retold as one way around it[^ref-11]. ScummVM does not remove copy protection unless the original interpreter had an official release with it disabled[^ref-11].

CrossOver testing indicated that the Retold version "Installs, Will Not Run" on Mac using CrossOver version 22.0.1, though this rating is considered outdated for current CrossOver versions[^ref-3].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

The 1988 original was one of the first games to support sound cards, with composer William Goldstein hired to write 40 minutes of music for it[^ref-6][^ref-20].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

The original 1988 *King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella* was a commercial success, selling 100,000 copies in its first two weeks and 800,000 copies within a year[^ref-6]. By November 2000, the game had sold between 300,000 and 400,000 units in the United States alone[^ref-6]. The game won "Best Adventure or Fantasy/Role-Playing Program" at the 1989 Software Publishers Association awards[^ref-6].

The Retold fan remake, while not commercially released, demonstrated continued interest in the King's Quest franchise, with more than 9,600 downloads of the original Retold version on the AGS site alone as of October 2026[^ref-2].

### Collections

The original 1988 game has appeared in numerous Sierra compilations and is available through digital distribution platforms. PCGamingWiki documents releases including the original DOS version (September 1988), a Windows 3.x version (1994), and a Windows ScummVM-compatible version (February 25, 2010)[^ref-12].

### Fan Projects

King's Quest IV Retold represents one of several fan efforts to preserve and modernize the King's Quest series:

- **King's Quest IV Retold (2021):** DrSlash's point-and-click remake preserving EGA graphics[^ref-2]
- **King's Quest IV Retold VGA Talkie (2025):** Magic Quest Entertainment's enhanced version with voice acting[^ref-1]
- **Multiple Unfinished Projects:** The VGA Talkie version incorporates assets from projects by Nightfable, Blackthorne, Lorenz Falkson, Sponge Factory, and Magic Mirror Games[^ref-1]

The community has embraced speedrunning the Retold version, with leaderboards tracking completion times. The current Any% record stands at 8 minutes 19 seconds by player Shawluck, with four total players having submitted 13 runs across categories[^ref-13].

### Related Publications

**Original Game Materials (1988, not Retold):**
- **Official Manual:** Used for the original game's copy protection lookups[^ref-11]
- **Physical DRM:** PCGamingWiki notes that all versions of the original game require the manual[^ref-12]

### Critical Perspective

King's Quest IV Retold demonstrates the enduring appeal of Sierra's classic adventure games. Rosella has been considered one of the first "major female protagonists" in a video game[^ref-6][^ref-20]. Designer Roberta Williams told *Computer Gaming World*: "I like the heroine, Rosella. I guess because she's a part of me that's coming out... Sometimes she's delicate, but she's strong, knows what she wants, she's not afraid to do what she has to do"[^ref-19].

The Retold version keeps the original narrative and puzzle design while adding point-and-click controls and an optional removal of dead ends[^ref-17]. In a 2014 IGN retrospective, Kosta Andreadis wrote that "The Perils of Rosella stands the test of time despite its shortcomings"[^ref-20].
