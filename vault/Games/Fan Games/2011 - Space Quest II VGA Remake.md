# Space Quest II VGA Remake

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Space Quest II VGA Remake (listed in game databases as *Space Quest II: Roger Wilco in Vohaul's Revenge*) is a fan-made graphical remake of the classic 1987 Sierra On-Line adventure game "Space Quest II: Vohaul's Revenge," originally created by [[Scott Murphy]] and [[Mark Crowe]], known collectively as "The Two Guys from Andromeda."[^ref-4][^ref-16][^ref-20] The remake was developed by Infamous Adventures and released on December 30, 2011, after more than five years of development.[^ref-2][^ref-20][^ref-24] Infamous Adventures' own release announcement called it "a labor of love and devotion to the original series," remade in a retro VGA style "like Sierra's later point and click adventures from the early 1990's."[^ref-2]

The remake lovingly recrafts the original game in simulated VGA colors, featuring hand-drawn backgrounds and hand-animated characters.[^ref-2][^ref-4] Players once again take on the role of Roger Wilco, the hapless janitor-turned-hero, as he embarks on an intergalactic adventure while being pursued by the agents of the sinister Sludge Vohaul.[^ref-4] The project includes a full voice pack with over 4,000 recorded lines of dialogue, transforming the text-based original into a fully voiced experience.[^ref-2]

Infamous Adventures distributes the game for free on a "name your own price" basis.[^ref-3] In the AGD Interactive forum's release thread, user DrJones wrote that "This game is of the same substance dreams are made of."[^ref-2] The game has continued to receive updates, including Version 2.0 released on January 1, 2020.[^ref-4][^ref-6]

## Story Summary

The remake follows the story of the 1987 original. Several months after saving his home world of Xenon from the Sariens, Roger Wilco has taken a position as Head Janitor aboard Xenon Orbital Station 4 as his fame fades into obscurity. Called to clean up a mess on a recently arrived shuttle, he is ambushed, knocked out and abducted, and wakes up a prisoner on an asteroid base orbiting the jungle planet Labion, run by Sludge Vohaul, a former Xenon scientist.[^ref-15]

Vohaul reveals that he created the Star Generator and had tried to use the Sariens to steal it, and that he is now building an army of cloned insurance salesmen to help him eradicate life on Xenon. Roger is sent to Vohaul's mines on Labion as a slave, but the transport crashes in the jungle, and Roger makes his way through the jungle and steals a shuttle, but Vohaul hijacks its controls and brings him back to the asteroid base, where Roger must stop the plan.[^ref-15]

The remake adds some new material along the way. A 2019 review on the Cola Powered Gamer blog notes a new intro scene in which Roger is interviewed by "David Letterdroid" (voiced, according to the reviewer, by Space Quest writer/designer Josh Mandel), the generic Hunter replaced by the Predator, and a Monolith Burger shop and other cameo appearances on Vohaul's asteroid in place of the original's series of hallways.[^ref-22]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The VGA Remake replaces the original's parser-based interface with an icon-based point-and-click system, similar to Sierra's later VGA-era adventure games, although the Cola Powered Gamer review notes that "some puzzles still require you to use the text parser."[^ref-9][^ref-20][^ref-22] The same review found that the new interface "makes the game easier, and you can find things on the screen more easily."[^ref-22]

### Structure and Progression

The game follows Roger Wilco's journey through the original's locations as he escapes captivity and works to stop Sludge Vohaul's plans.[^ref-15] According to the Cola Powered Gamer review, the puzzles in the second half of the game are "for the most part" unchanged, though the root monster puzzle "has a new twist."[^ref-22]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Like the original Space Quest II, the remake features inventory-based puzzles typical of Sierra adventure games. Some puzzles were simplified by the new interface: in the review's example, the cave sequence that once required putting the gem in Roger's mouth to light the way now only requires using the gem.[^ref-22]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The remake was greeted warmly in the AGD Interactive forum's release thread. Forum member Anonymous Game Creator 2 congratulated the team, writing that "It takes a lot of damn hard work and dedication to see projects of this magnitude through to the end," and DrJones wrote that "This game is of the same substance dreams are made of," adding "Superb job!!"[^ref-2] DrJones also reported a run of crash bugs in the same thread, and replies in the thread said they would be fixed in the next update.[^ref-2]

A 2019 retrospective on the Cola Powered Gamer blog described the fan remake as "clearly a labor of love, like any fan remake project."[^ref-22]

Before release, the project was followed on the VOGONS forums. In September 2009, VOGONS user Malik, announcing that Infamous Adventures was "nearing the completion" of the remake, compared the group's earlier King's Quest III remake with AGD Interactive's work: "Personally, I prefer the remakes made by Infamous Adventures, more than the other well known (re-)maker : AGD Interactive. King's Quest II remake (by AGDI) is good and professionally made but strayed slightly away from the classic. ... Whereas King's Quest III remake by Infamous Adventures stayed quite close to the original."[^ref-7]

Reactions to the voice acting were mixed in a VOGONS thread about the release. On December 31, 2011, VOGONS user Malik wrote that he was "gonna download right away!"[^ref-27] On January 7, 2012, SquallStrife wrote: "I'm hugely impressed! I don't like the voice acting, sorry, but the rest of the game is SPOT ON!" Later that day Malik agreed: "The voice acting leaves much to be desired," adding that he was playing "with only the text on," while praising the graphics and music as "really good." MusicallyInspired replied the same day: "We had very few actors. One guy did most of the voices, actually. The voice pack isn't ever going to be something really professionally well done in a fan game," and said he preferred text as well.[^ref-27]

## Development

### Origins

Infamous Adventures, an amateur team founded in 2004 by Shawn Mills and Steven Alexander,[^ref-28] had previously released a King's Quest III remake.[^ref-7] On January 25, 2007, the team had posted a static image of a Space Quest II scene without confirming a remake;[^ref-28] it officially announced the Space Quest II VGA remake on April 1, 2007, releasing screenshots "after some speculation in recent weeks that the game was indeed in production."[^ref-23] By release, the team described the game as the product of "more than five years of working on the game, bit by bit in their spare time."[^ref-2]

The Infamous Adventures version was not the first attempt at a VGA Space Quest II. In a March 2006 Sierra Gamers forum thread, one member recalled that "AGDI cancelled their (then secret) remake of Space Quest 2 because someone else named Kippesoep ... was working on it," adding that Kippesoep "eventually disappeared into obscurity."[^ref-8] Development updates on the Infamous Adventures project were shared periodically; on March 28, 2009, team member Broomie posted (as quoted on VOGONS): "Just a quick reminder, Space Quest II is on it's way soon! Not too soon, but quite soon. We've done a lot of work recently, hired some excellent new staff members and Klytos has finally got off his arse and started adding in the last few animations and sprites. Not much left to do now, and soon we'll be entering the beta stage of Space Quest II."[^ref-7]

### Production

The remake was produced by Steven Alexander and Shawn Mills, directed and scripted by Mills, with art direction by Elissa Ng and Alexander, animation direction by David Robert Smith, and music by Isaac Lundgren and Brandon Blume.[^ref-10] It features hand-drawn backgrounds and hand-animated characters in a style meant to recall Sierra's early 1990s VGA adventures.[^ref-2]

The remake is not a strict one-to-one copy of the original. The team's FAQ describes it as "a bit of both": "The story is the same, but we've fleshed out some parts more and a couple of puzzles have been changed due to the change to point and click interface."[^ref-25] Shacknews likewise noted that "a couple of puzzles have been altered slightly" to suit the mouse-driven interface, and that a few new cut-scenes were added.[^ref-26] The team's Roger Wilco sprite is based on the Roger of Space Quest IV, and the interface (icons and cursors) is also based on Space Quest IV, "but theres a couple of cool changes there too."[^ref-25]

In December 2011, shortly before the public release, Infamous Adventures showed the game at AdventureX, a convention in Oxfordshire, England. The team's news post said attendees would be able to play Space Quest II before it was released, while Broomie presented the design decisions on a big screen.[^ref-24][^ref-29] Infamous Adventures' own news post gives the dates as "the 17th and 18th of December," while the Adventure Game Studio wiki gives December 16th and 17th.[^ref-24][^ref-29]

A significant undertaking was the voice pack, which includes over 4,000 recorded lines of dialogue.[^ref-2] The narrator is also voiced; the Cola Powered Gamer reviewer felt he "does a decent Gary Owens impression."[^ref-22]

The team also created a PDF manual with custom content. Infamous Adventures team member Lambonius urged players in the release thread to "Be sure to check out the pdf manual included in the game directory," calling it "just as much a labor of love as the game itself!"[^ref-2]

### Technical Achievements

The remake recreates the original game "in simulated VGA colors," in the developers' own words.[^ref-4] It was built with Adventure Game Studio.[^ref-9] Shacknews described it as presented at 320x200 resolution with a 16-bit colour palette, and the Adventure Game Studio games database lists it as freeware with 320x200, 16-bit colour graphics.[^ref-26][^ref-30]

### Technical Issues

Players have reported some compatibility problems in the itch.io comments. One user reported that the Linux installation was missing the necessary libpng library, which they resolved by copying it from another AGS game, Heroine's Quest.[^ref-11]

On macOS, a user asked how to run the game in windowed mode, since editing acsetup.cfg seemed to be ignored. Another user explained that "windowed=1" must be set in the acsetup.cfg file in "~/Library/Application Support/Space Quest ][ Remake v2.0 [beta]", "not the one inside the application itself."[^ref-11] A third user, edenwaith, suggested revealing the window bar by holding the cursor at the top of the screen, but noted that this worked on macOS Mojave and not on Catalina.[^ref-11]

After the Version 2.0 release, a player reported a null-pointer crash in room31.asc. The developers traced it to a line that "checks to see if bgMusic is playing," rewrote the code, and said, "I will post an update that fixes this."[^ref-12]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

Version 2.0 added several hidden easter eggs to the game, including a Space Quest III Easter Egg that is not triggered simply by achieving the full 250 points.[^ref-12] According to the developers: "Getting full points isn't the trigger for the SQ3 Easter Egg. You have to discover how to do that by exploring the game. Though if you do something right, you'll hear something that lets you know you're on the right path."[^ref-12]

The remake also includes a secret bar easter egg. When a player reported that the old way of reaching it no longer worked in Version 2.0, the developers replied: "Yep, accessing the secret bar is a little different now," meaning the access method changed from the earlier version of the remake.[^ref-12]

- **SQ3 Easter Egg:** A hidden reference to Space Quest III that requires specific exploration to discover[^ref-12]
- **Secret Bar:** A hidden location whose access method changed in Version 2.0 compared with earlier versions of the remake[^ref-12]

## Legacy

### Unofficial Status

The remake was an unofficial fan project. Shacknews reported that Infamous Adventures had tried to reach the original design team for permission, and that, according to the group's FAQ, the only reply was an endorsement from Scott Murphy: "You and your team have my blessing. I can certainly appreciate that kind of spirit." The article also cited the project's About text, which explained the "unsanctioned nature" of the remake and encouraged players to buy Activision's re-released original games.[^ref-26]

### Commercial Impact

As a free fan project, the Space Quest II VGA Remake was never sold commercially. Instead, Infamous Adventures offers it as a "Name your own price" download, where "the developer accepts your support by letting you pay what you think is fair for the game."[^ref-3]

### Fan Projects

The Space Quest II VGA Remake is one of several Sierra fan remakes by Infamous Adventures, which had earlier released a King's Quest III remake that VOGONS user Malik said "stayed quite close to the original" and called "an absolute must for KQ fans" with its speech pack.[^ref-7] Infamous Adventures posted on July 27, 2012 that it was no longer producing games.[^ref-28]

### Related Publications

- **PDF Manual:** "SQ2 Manual - Space Piston by IA," a custom manual included with the game (2.7 MB)[^ref-2][^ref-3]

### Critical Perspective

Released 24 years after the original 1987 game,[^ref-2] the remake took its team more than five years of spare-time work.[^ref-2] It sits alongside other fan remakes from groups such as AGD Interactive; at least one fan, comparing the two groups' King's Quest remakes, preferred Infamous Adventures for staying closer to the original, while calling AGDI's King's Quest II remake "good and professionally made."[^ref-7]

The project has continued to be supported, with a Mac Universal Binary released as recently as September 2024.[^ref-4]
