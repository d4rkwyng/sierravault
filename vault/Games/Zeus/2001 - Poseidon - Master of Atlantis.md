# Poseidon: Master of Atlantis

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

Poseidon: Master of Atlantis is the official expansion pack for [[2000 - Zeus - Master of Olympus|Zeus: Master of Olympus]], developed by [[Impressions Games]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] in 2001[^ref-1]. The expansion invites players to "Return to ancient Greece and rule Atlantis as described by Plato!"[^ref-2], and its new adventures are based on Plato's stories of Atlantis[^ref-9]. It continues Impressions' city-building series, which grew out of the studio's Caesar games[^ref-3].

The expansion adds the Atlantis civilization to the mythological world of Zeus, with its own unique gods, monuments, heroes, monsters and industries[^ref-1]. Its box copy promises more than 45 episodes in six adventures, new gods such as Atlas and Hera, and the chance to "conquer or trade with Mayans, Egyptians, Phoenicians, and more"[^ref-2]. Missions are set around the ancient world, with new cities to build in Europe, Africa, Asia and the Americas[^ref-3].

Poseidon received "favorable" reviews according to Metacritic, where it holds a score of 84[^ref-9]. Critics agreed that it changed little of Zeus's underlying design; Computer Games Magazine wrote that "none of Poseidon's new features changes Zeus' core gameplay in any meaningful way, there is an awful lot of new content here"[^ref-25].

## Story Summary

The expansion draws on Plato's *Critias*, in which the gods "distributed the whole earth into portions" and Poseidon, "receiving for his lot the island of Atlantis," fell in love with the mortal Cleito, for whom he enclosed her mountain home in alternating rings of land and sea[^ref-29]. The game's first city is Atlantis itself, its landscape based on Plato's description of the city as a series of concentric rings[^ref-24].

According to IGN, the player takes the role of a son of Atlas and grandson of Poseidon ("the mythology gets a little loose here"), entrusted with building Atlantis into a great city[^ref-27]. The expansion features more than 45 episodes spread across six adventures[^ref-2]. From Atlantis, the campaigns turn west and then east to establish relations with the Mayans and Phoenicians; a plague later sweeps the Mediterranean, and the player founds a new city in remembrance of Atlantis' former glory[^ref-27]. In the later adventures the player switches sides and must fight against the Atlantean invasion of the eastern Mediterranean[^ref-26][^ref-27].

The Birth of Atlantis adventure's main challenge is managing housing and industry within the limited land of the ringed island, although Atlantis "has access to just about every resource imaginable"[^ref-8]. Heroes such as Theseus and Atalanta and gods such as Artemis and Poseidon figure in its episodes[^ref-8].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Poseidon plays as Zeus does; GameVortex wrote that "for the most part, Poseidon plays identically to Zeus"[^ref-11]. The game requires a mouse, with optional keyboard support, and offers both auto-save and manual saves[^ref-10]. It runs full screen at 800×600 or 1024×768[^ref-10].

### Structure and Progression

Poseidon offers "45 new scenarios spread over six challenging campaigns"[^ref-25]; IGN likewise counted "45 missions spread out across six campaigns"[^ref-27]. Smaller sandbox-style adventures let players build without campaign goals, or play through the destruction of Atlantis[^ref-25]. Steam's listing for the combined Zeus + Poseidon package counts "65 episodes in which you build and rule seven city-states and two powerful civilizations"[^ref-28].

The adventures progress through distinct episodes, each with specific goals:

- **Birth of Atlantis:** A fan walkthrough lists nine episodes, from "The Capital City" to "Atlas the God", with goals ranging from population targets and housing levels to slaying monsters, building pyramids and a 40-stade Hippodrome[^ref-8]
- **Additional adventures:** Take the player to the Mayans and Phoenicians and later against Atlantis itself[^ref-27]
- **Sandbox adventures:** Free-form city building without campaign goals[^ref-25]

Campaigns range "from easy to hard"[^ref-11]. Military conquest is largely absent: for most of the game a pact with the gods prevents the player from attacking other cities[^ref-24], and GameVortex noted that "battles have been almost completely eliminated"[^ref-11].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core gameplay remains city-building and resource management, with these additions:

**Resource Management:** Two new raw materials, orichalc and black marble, are mined and traded and are needed to build pyramids[^ref-24]. Players can also raise cattle and grow oranges[^ref-27].

**Science instead of entertainment:** Atlantis replaces Zeus's entertainment buildings with scientific ones, such as bibliothekes, observatories, laboratories and museums, which "play the same role as entertainment structures did in Zeus"[^ref-24]. The Hippodrome, a horse-racing track that brings in money and happiness, is the main new entertainment structure[^ref-24].

**Pyramids:** Pyramids of various sizes boost the appeal of nearby areas, and completing the Pyramid of the Pantheon ensures that no wrathful deity will attack the city[^ref-25].

**Gods, heroes and monsters:** Hera and Atlas join the pantheon; Hera is the only god able to stop Zeus when he invades a city[^ref-27]. Two new heroes, Bellerophon and Atalanta, face new monsters including the Sphinx, Chimera, Echidna and Harpies[^ref-27].

**Trade:** Players can conquer or trade with Mayans, Egyptians, Phoenicians and other cultures[^ref-2].

The game also includes an Adventure Editor that lets players design their own myths and scenarios[^ref-2], although an editor had already been made freely available on Impressions' website[^ref-9].

GameSpot's Ron Dulin (July 3, 2001) found that the expansion "doesn't include many new features, but it does include some significant ones," adding that "the fantastical setting has allowed the designers to take more creative license with the mission design"[^ref-24]. He welcomed the near-removal of combat, which "helps alleviate the problem of Zeus' subpar combat interface," but found building the Hippodrome "a chore"[^ref-24].

IGN's Steve Butts (July 3, 2001) wrote that "with 45 missions spread out across six campaigns, Poseidon rivals Zeus for depth," and concluded that "Poseidon is a great value"[^ref-27]. Computer Games Magazine's Dave Markell judged that "Poseidon is merely (much) more of the same, but you'll be having too much fun to care"[^ref-25].

GameSpy's William Harms (July 2001) put Poseidon among expansions that "simply offer more of the same," saying it "offers the same great gameplay that Zeus provided"[^ref-26]. GameVortex's Phil Bordelon wrote that "Poseidon keeps up the solid gameplay of the original release of Zeus as well"[^ref-11].

The base game had been reviewed separately; see [[2000 - Zeus - Master of Olympus]] for its reception.

## Development

### Origins

Poseidon was developed as the official expansion to Zeus: Master of Olympus, an installment in Impressions Games' City Building series[^ref-9]. That series traced its roots to the Caesar games[^ref-3]. The expansion moved the setting to Atlantis, with new adventures based on Plato's accounts of the island[^ref-9].

### Production

Impressions Games developed the expansion. MobyGames credits Tony Leier as designer, Ken Parker as producer, [[Chris Beatrice]] as director of design and development, Keith Zizza as audio director, Henry Beckett for music and Lisa Gagnon as writer[^ref-1].

**Development Credits:**[^ref-1]
- **Designer:** Tony Leier
- **Producer:** Ken Parker
- **Director of Design & Development:** Chris Beatrice
- **Audio Director:** Keith Zizza
- **Music:** Henry Beckett
- **Writer:** Lisa Gagnon

### Technical Achievements

GameVortex noted that Poseidon "looks a whole lot like Zeus," and that its music and sound effects "come wholesale from the original title," while new character types received new voice acting[^ref-11]. GameSpy found the graphics "just as stylistic and engaging as they were in Zeus," singling out details such as elephants dragging lumber and a librarian struggling under a pile of books[^ref-26].

### Technical Specifications

- **Resolution:** 800×600 or 1024×768
- **Video Memory:** 2 MB minimum
- **Media:** CD-ROM (4X speed minimum)

Steam's listing gives lower minimums for the same bundle: Windows XP through 10, 512 MB RAM, a DirectX 7 card and 2 GB of storage[^ref-28].

**Patch 2.1 Details:**[^ref-30]
The version 2.1 patch fixed, among other issues:
- Greek custom adventures showing up as Atlantean custom adventures
- Pyramids becoming corrupt after delete/undo
- The game pausing when certain structures were deleted
- Resource gatherers not finding the closest available resources
- Hera being slow to rebuff Zeus when he invades
- Pyramids destroyed by disasters not being rebuildable
- "Doubled" fishing points
- Dionysus not preventing unrest properly
- Armies getting stuck on the world level
- Disasters permanently killing heroes
- Heroes getting stuck on Hippodrome crosswalks
- Setting a "Slay" goal in the editor causing an exit to desktop

### Technical Issues

GameSpot rated the reviewed version's stability as "minor problems"[^ref-24]. The 2.1 patch later addressed construction problems with pyramids, pauses when deleting structures, divine interventions that did not work as intended, and Greek custom adventures appearing as Atlantean ones[^ref-30].

Combat remained the weakest area. GameSpy observed that "combat has always been the weakest part of Impressions' games," and wondered whether the reduced military role was a response to Zeus's problematic combat[^ref-26]. The 2.1 patch also fixed armies getting stuck on the world map[^ref-30].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- Although Zeus was translated and released in Sweden, the Poseidon expansion never received a Swedish release[^ref-31]
- Orichalc, the Atlantean resource in the game, comes from Plato's *Critias*, which describes orichalcum as "more precious in those days than anything except gold"[^ref-29]
- The game explains the reduced role of the military through a decree of the gods that forbids the Atlanteans from waging war[^ref-26]
- The new pyramids combine Egyptian, Mayan and Greek design elements[^ref-25]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Sales figures for Poseidon are not available. The original US retail price was $19.99[^ref-16]; IGN noted the "twenty drachmae asking price" as part of its value[^ref-27].

### Collections

The Acropolis collection bundles Zeus: Master of Olympus with the Poseidon expansion, and was is credited to publishers Sierra On-Line and Activision Publishing[^ref-13]. MobyGames also lists Poseidon in The Great Empires Collection II (2001) and The Biggest Creators of Empires (2003)[^ref-1].

The Zeus + Poseidon bundle is sold on GOG.com[^ref-7], where it was added to the catalog on May 10, 2012[^ref-20], and on Steam, where it was released on December 15, 2016 at $9.99[^ref-28].

### Fan Projects

The Adventure Editor included with Poseidon lets players design their own myths and scenarios[^ref-2]. IGN described it as a robust editor that is "less about scripting specific events than it is about creating a landscape, deciding what will and won't be available to the player and picking victory conditions"[^ref-27].

### Related Publications

- **Manual, reference card and adventure editor guide:** Included as goodies with the GOG release[^ref-7]

### Critical Perspective

Critics treated Poseidon as an evolutionary expansion rather than a reinvention. GameSpy placed it among expansions that "simply offer more of the same"[^ref-26], and Computer Games Magazine said that none of its new features changed Zeus's core gameplay in any meaningful way[^ref-25]. Reviewers still valued it for the sheer amount of new content and for setting the series in a fantastical Atlantis; GameSpot credited that setting with giving the designers "more creative license with the mission design"[^ref-24].
