# Gabriel Knight 4: Five Hearts

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Gabriel Knight 4: Five Hearts is an unreleased adventure game concept developed by [[Jane Jensen]], the original creator of the Gabriel Knight series.[^ref-1][^ref-9][^ref-13] Rather than a completed video game, Five Hearts exists as both a pitch for a potential fourth installment in the supernatural mystery franchise and as a published short story that Jensen has called "basically like the first chapter of a new Gabriel Knight story".[^ref-2][^ref-18] Jensen has talked about a fourth game since at least 2003, when she told GameSpy that "there was never a GK4 in development", but that its storyline "was going to be about ghosts and probably would have been set in England or Scotland."[^ref-21] According to Wikipedia, in a 2022 interview she instead described a possible fourth game about witches, set in Scotland.[^ref-1]

According to a ResetEra summary of Jensen's December 2025 Matt Chat interview, the pitch was made in cooperation with an indie team from Israel that reached out to Jensen and her husband, composer [[Robert Holmes]].[^ref-4] The Gabriel Knight intellectual property is now owned by Microsoft as part of the Activision Blizzard catalogue.[^ref-20] In July 2024 Jensen said she "would like to make it a full game", adding: "Now that the licenses are with Microsoft, I think there's at least a chance of that. Who knows."[^ref-18] By December 2025 she had submitted the idea to Microsoft several times, but the company had so far shown no interest, according to Multiplayer.it's report on the interview.[^ref-4][^ref-20]

**Working title:** "Five Hearts" is both the working title of the game pitch[^ref-4][^ref-20] and the title of an illustrated short story Jensen published in November 2024 on her husband Robert Holmes's website. It was first announced as a reward for backers of his "Son of Sequel" Kickstarter and is now a free download.[^ref-2][^ref-18][^ref-19]

The proposed game would continue the series' blend of supernatural horror and detective fiction. It takes Gabriel to Salzburg, Austria, where serial killings involving removed hearts are both a literal mystery and a metaphor for Gabriel's own heart.[^ref-4][^ref-20] In 1996 Computer Gaming World called Jensen "the interactive Ann Rice".[^ref-22]

## Story Summary

Adventure Game Hotspot describes the short story as "the first chapter of a brand new Gabriel Knight short story". It finds Gabriel "idly overdrinking and making no real progress on writing his book" at Schloss Ritter,[^ref-2] his ancestral home in Germany.[^ref-1] Grace Nakimura, Gabriel's research assistant, has disappeared without a trace. The only clue she left is an uncharacteristic letter to her parents, signed off with five hearts.[^ref-2]

In the short story, an American expatriate living in Salzburg believes she has been cursed by an ancient ceremonial dagger she bought from an antique shop. Gabriel agrees to travel there to retrieve it.[^ref-2]

The game pitch, as Jensen described it on Matt Chat, is also set in Salzburg. There, Gabriel investigates serial killings whose victims are found with their hearts removed.[^ref-4][^ref-20] Jensen describes a metaphorical layer to the plot, which deals with Gabriel's own heart.[^ref-4][^ref-20]

The proposed game would feature two paths: Gabriel is either ruthless or compassionate toward "the spirits" he encounters.[^ref-4][^ref-20]

## Development

### Origins

In a piece for The Adrenaline Vault published on October 4, 1999, shortly before Gabriel Knight 3: Blood of the Sacred, Blood of the Damned shipped, Jensen called that game "the last adventure game from Sierra in the immediate future".[^ref-3] She argued that while old ways of telling stories die, "story-telling is not" dead, and closed: "'Stories?'--not in a million years."[^ref-3]

In Gabriel Knight 3, players can search for "gk4" in the SIDNEY computer interface to find an entry about ghosts. Jensen said ghosts would have been the antagonists of a fourth game.[^ref-1][^ref-21]

### Production

The short story came first. In mid-2024 Jensen said she had dreamed "the complete plot for a new Gabriel Knight story" and wrote it down that morning as "basically like the first chapter of a new Gabriel Knight story".[^ref-18] It was published as "Five Hearts" in November 2024.[^ref-2][^ref-13]

The game pitch was revealed on Matt Chat in December 2025.[^ref-17] Jensen discussed the Salzburg setting, the heart-removal serial killings and the metaphorical side of the story.[^ref-4][^ref-17][^ref-20] According to ResetEra's summary, an indie team from Israel had approached Jensen and Holmes, and the pitch was made in cooperation with them.[^ref-4] Jensen would like to bring on a "younger designer" to help make the game.[^ref-4][^ref-20]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Gabriel Knight 4 has no sales data as an unreleased project. The series' most recent release was the 20th Anniversary remake of the original game. It was developed by Pinkerton Road Studio and Phoenix Online Studios on a budget of $215,269.50 and released on October 15, 2014.[^ref-7]

### Collections

- **Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers 20th Anniversary Edition** (2014): a remake built in Unity by Pinkerton Road Studio and Phoenix Online Studios[^ref-7]
- **Original Games:** see Downloads below

### Fan Projects

Fan sites such as gkpages.altervista.org preserve historical interviews and articles related to the franchise, including Jensen's 1999 "Love Letter".[^ref-3]

### Related Publications

**Short Story:**
- **"Five Hearts"** by Jane Jensen. The opening chapter of a new Gabriel Knight story, available from Robert Holmes's website.[^ref-2][^ref-19]

**Historical Documentation:**
- **"A Love Letter To: Sierra"** by Jane Jensen. Published by The Adrenaline Vault on October 4, 1999.[^ref-3]

### Critical Perspective

In 1996 Computer Gaming World called Gabriel Knight 2 "a haunting tale from Jane Jensen, the interactive Ann Rice".[^ref-22]

In 2013, USgamer's Pete Davison wrote that "Grace Nakimura remains one of the best, most realistic female characters in game history".[^ref-24] GameSpot wrote that she was "simply a more likeable character" than Gabriel, and "intelligent and resourceful".[^ref-23] Her disappearance is the central mystery of the Five Hearts short story.[^ref-2]
