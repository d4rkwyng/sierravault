# Adventure in Serenia

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

> [!note] Port stub
> This page covers only the 1982 IBM PC/PCjr release. The game itself (story, Apple II original, other ports, reception and legacy) is documented on **[[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 2 - The Wizard and the Princess]]**.

## Overview

*Adventure in Serenia* is the IBM Personal Computer version of On-Line Systems' second Hi-Res Adventure, [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 2 - The Wizard and the Princess|The Wizard and the Princess]], released in 1982 as a self-booting disk and published by IBM[^ref-6][^ref-12]. Its manual introduced it as "the first of On-Line Systems' pictorial adventures for the IBM Personal Computer"[^ref-15], and Wikipedia describes it as Sierra's first game for the PC platform[^ref-6].

The title screen credits the game to Ken and Roberta Williams, carries both "(c) Copyright IBM Corp. 1982" and "(c) Copyright On-Line Systems 1980", and states that it was "Implemented by Jeff Stephenson"[^ref-15]. Apart from the new title, a different 4-colour CGA palette, improved upper- and lower-case text, alternative inventory graphics and some reworded messages, there is little difference from the original game[^ref-14][^ref-21]. *PC Magazine* later listed *Serenia* as "a disk package for the PCjr" among Sierra's titles for IBM's new home computer[^ref-32].

## Story Summary

The IBM manual's introduction sets up the quest directly: "When you load this game on your computer, you are magically transported to the land of Serenia, where the evil Wizard Harlin has made off with the beautiful Princess Priscilla"[^ref-15]. The player must rescue Priscilla from Harlin's castle "beyond the mountains to the North" and return her to the village where the adventure began, starting out with a flask of water, a small knife, a loaf of bread and a blanket[^ref-15].

Some database descriptions say this version was framed as a sequel in which Harlin "uses the sands of time" to undo his defeat[^ref-12]. The 1982 manual does not contain that framing[^ref-15]; the "sands of time" prologue is part of a second introduction that, per the King's Quest Omnipedia, was added to the manuals of later releases[^ref-16].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Commands are typed as "one or two word sentences", usually a verb followed by a noun such as LOOK CHASM or GET STICK; directions can be abbreviated to their first letter[^ref-15]. The upper part of the screen shows a picture of the current location and the bottom four lines carry text[^ref-15]. When a message runs longer than four lines the game beeps and waits for the Scroll Lock key[^ref-15]; Gaming After 40 warned modern players that "access to the Scroll Lock key is absolutely essential"[^ref-9].

Function keys switch between the picture screen and a full text screen (F1), toggle between two colour sets (F2), and enter common commands such as save, restore, inventory and LOOK ROOM[^ref-15].

### Technical Specifications

| Item | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| Memory | "48K of user memory"[^ref-15] |
| Video | Color/Graphics Monitor Adapter (CGA)[^ref-15] |
| Drives | "one diskette drive"; two drives make saving more convenient[^ref-15] |
| Operating system | None needed (self-booting)[^ref-15] |
| Saves | Up to twenty games per save disk, which "must be formatted by the Adventure in Serenia program, and not by DOS"[^ref-15] |

PCGamingWiki lists CGA, CGA composite and Tandy/PCjr video modes for the PC booter release[^ref-19]. The King's Quest Omnipedia describes the two in-game colour schemes, and notes that a composite monitor yields additional blended colours[^ref-14].

## Reception

No contemporary review of the IBM release itself has been located; period reviews cited on the main page concern the Apple II and Atari versions. In a 2010 retrospective of the IBM version, Gaming After 40 noted that "the game runs in 4-color CGA mode, with very simple PC speaker sound effects", and judged that "the plotting is almost non-existent, few meaningful clues are provided, and many of the puzzles depend on trying random actions until something works"[^ref-9]. The reviewer still credited Roberta Williams as "a pioneer in the graphic adventure genre"[^ref-9]. Abandonware DOS users rate it 3.50/5 (40 votes)[^ref-1].

## Development

According to the King's Quest Omnipedia, this was reputedly Roberta Williams' least favourite version of the game, "as she thought the graphics looked atrocious"[^ref-14]; Wikipedia likewise says she "reputedly" called the IBM colours "atrocious"[^ref-6]. Gaming After 40 links IBM's relationship with On-Line Systems to the later development of the AGI engine and the original King's Quest for the PCjr[^ref-9].

The PCjr disk package was reported by *PC Magazine* in its 24 January 1984 PCjr games preview, which noted that it "was not on display"[^ref-32]. Wikipedia gives March 1984 as the PCjr release date[^ref-6].

## Legacy

When Sierra assembled the King's Quest Collection, it included the Apple II version of *The Wizard and the Princess* instead, explaining in the collection's READ2.WRI file: "That version will not work on today's fast computers, so we have included the Apple version instead"[^ref-14]. The game's wider legacy, including Serenia's return in King's Quest V, is covered on [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 2 - The Wizard and the Princess]].
