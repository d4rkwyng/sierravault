# NASCAR Racing 2 Grand National Series Expansion

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

NASCAR Racing 2 Grand National Series Expansion was an add-on pack for Sierra's NASCAR Racing 2, developed by Papyrus Design Group and released for DOS on November 3, 1997.[^ref-1] The pack added the top Busch Grand National Series drivers, 10 new official tracks, two fantasy tracks and several patches for the base game.[^ref-1] It required NASCAR Racing 2 to play.[^ref-1]

The base game NASCAR Racing 2 had used a new game engine that improved graphics, physics, sound, and multiplayer capabilities over its predecessor.[^ref-3] In the expansion, Elton Sawyer and Patty Moise, a husband-and-wife pair, gave advice on how to race at the new Grand National tracks.[^ref-24] Tim Soete's GameSpot review of the base game opened by insisting that "NASCAR 2 is not a toy."[^ref-4]

## Story Summary

As a racing simulation, NASCAR Racing 2 Grand National Series Expansion has no narrative. Electric Games described it as bringing "the whole 1997 Grand National series experience" to the game, with "10 new tracks, 30 new drivers, and two fantastic fantasy tracks."[^ref-21]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The expansion maintained NASCAR Racing 2's realistic control scheme, supporting keyboard, mouse, and racing wheel input devices.[^ref-8] The game featured advanced joystick and wheel support, with Joystick 1 configured for steering and Joystick 2 for pedals, though calibration was required for optimal performance.[^ref-8] A review of the base game reprinted on GameFabrique complained that keyboard and joystick control were "very touchy," adding that Sierra's own InterAction magazine admitted "the only satisfying way to play is with the $130 steering wheel module."[^ref-9]

The cockpit included working instruments (tachometer, oil pressure, water temperature and fuel gauges) and a three-way rearview mirror.[^ref-8]

### Car Setup and Tuning

The expansion retained NASCAR Racing 2's comprehensive car setup system, where "every track must be considered individually when tweaking the design of your car, where you need to know where and when to pit, all while staying aware of your competition's position on the road."[^ref-4] The base game's manual covers these garage adjustments:[^ref-8]

- Tire pressures (read alongside tire temperatures)
- Fuel levels
- Rear spoiler angle
- Camber
- Weight bias (cross weight)
- Shock absorber stiffness
- Steering lock ratio
- Gear ratios

### Structure and Progression

The expansion added the top Busch Grand National Series drivers to NASCAR Racing 2's existing structure.[^ref-1] As in the base game, players could run a Quick Race or a full Championship Season, and multiplayer was available over IPX and modem.[^ref-8][^ref-22] A MobyGames user review of the base game praised its multiplayer, noting "The best part was the multiplayability. Whenever my friends and I would get together to play multi-player games, this one always worked right the first time, everytime."[^ref-10]

Quick Races award no championship points; the Championship Season mode has players compete for points toward the series title.[^ref-8]

### Racing Features

The base game included a toggleable damage model (bodywork, engine and wheels), random or constant weather settings, and a Paint Shop for cars and uniforms.[^ref-8] NASCAR Racing 2 also introduced a radio spotter;[^ref-3] the spotter and crew chief were voiced by Ronald Gaines,[^ref-8] with lines such as "What did you do that for! What a bonehead move!"[^ref-11]

### Track Selection

The expansion added 10 official Grand National tracks plus two fantasy tracks, Bull Run Raceway and Red Rock International Raceway.[^ref-1][^ref-24] GameSpot's Papyrus history noted that it came without "serious, anticipated upgrades such as 3D acceleration support or pit crew animations."[^ref-17]

### Critical Assessment

Tim Soete's GameSpot review of the base game emphasized the simulation's depth: "NASCAR 2 is not a toy."[^ref-4] Tim Ponting's PC Zone review of the base game was more effusive: "It's fab. It really is."[^ref-26] He concluded that "If realistic saloon car-type racing is your bag, there is nothing else that comes close."[^ref-26]

Computer Gaming World gave the base game a perfect score, writing that it "continues down that near legendary road of glory."[^ref-12]

### Modern Assessment

The base game holds an 81% critic average on MobyGames,[^ref-10] and MyAbandonware users rate it 4.58/5 from 81 votes.[^ref-14] On MobyGames, the expansion itself has only two player ratings.[^ref-1]

## Development

### Origins and Context

The expansion was developed following the success of NASCAR Racing 2, which was released in December 1996 as a follow-up to the award-winning original NASCAR Racing from 1994.[^ref-6][^ref-7] Papyrus was founded in 1987 by [[Dave Kaemmer]] and Omar Khudari.[^ref-17][^ref-25] NASCAR Racing 2 went on to sell 800,000 units worldwide, which GameSpot called the second-highest total of any Papyrus game.[^ref-17]

### Production

MobyGames credits game design to Tom Faiano, David D. Flamburis, Matt Marsala and Ryan Smart, with Adam Levesque as executive producer, Matt Marsala as producer, Tom Faiano as associate producer and Ryan Smart as lead engineer.[^ref-23] MobyGames also notes that people credited on the pack had worked on NASCAR Racing and NASCAR Racing 2.[^ref-23] The expansion ran on NASCAR Racing 2's engine.[^ref-1]

Elton Sawyer and Patty Moise provided the advice for the new Grand National tracks.[^ref-24] Ronald Gaines is credited in the base game's manual as the spotter and crew chief voice.[^ref-8]

### Technical Achievements

MobyGames describes NASCAR Racing 2 as the first NASCAR game to have 3D acceleration, with 3Dfx under DOS and Rendition under Windows.[^ref-3] Sierra's 3Dfx executable for the base game came later, as a 1998 "3Dfx Beta Update" whose graphics options were set at the command prompt or in AUTOEXEC.BAT.[^ref-16] MobyGames lists 3Dfx support for the expansion,[^ref-22] while Computer Gaming World's review box listed Rendition as its 3D support and found that the pack "doesn't seem fully optimized for 3D."[^ref-27]

**Technical Specifications (expansion, per MobyGames):**[^ref-22]
- Platform: DOS, CD-ROM (2X drive minimum)
- Minimum: Intel i486, 16 MB RAM
- Video: VGA and SuperVGA
- Sound: Sound Blaster 16
- Input: keyboard, mouse, racing wheel, analog joystick
- Multiplayer: IPX and modem

### Version Compatibility

The expansion required NASCAR Racing 2 as a base game. MobyGames lists it as an add-on to NASCAR Racing 2, and it was later bundled in NASCAR Racing 2 + Track Pack (1998).[^ref-1]

## Legacy

### Commercial Success

The base game sold 800,000 units worldwide, making it the second-highest-selling Papyrus game according to GameSpot's 2004 Papyrus history.[^ref-17] No sales figure for the expansion pack has been found.

### Industry Recognition

In a 2022 article in The Athletic, NASCAR driver Dale Earnhardt Jr. said of Papyrus's NASCAR Racing games: "It was ahead of its time, just so ahead of its time."[^ref-18] His remarks concerned the NASCAR Racing series generally, not this expansion.[^ref-18]

### Papyrus Legacy

Papyrus continued developing NASCAR simulations after NASCAR Racing 2, including NASCAR Racing 3, NASCAR Racing 4 and NASCAR Racing 2003 Season.[^ref-25]

Vivendi Universal shut Papyrus down in 2004.[^ref-17] That year FIRST.net, LLC (later iRacing) acquired the NASCAR Racing 2003 Season code and copyrights from Vivendi and Papyrus, and went on to develop iRacing using that game's engine.[^ref-25]

### Preservation Status

The base game is no longer commercially available and is considered abandonware; a preserved installer runs it on modern Windows through DOSBox.[^ref-19] The expansion's original CD is preserved on the Internet Archive.[^ref-2] The wider NASCAR gaming history is covered by Daily Downforce.[^ref-20]
