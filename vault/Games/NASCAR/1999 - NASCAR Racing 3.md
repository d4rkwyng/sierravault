# NASCAR Racing 3

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

NASCAR Racing 3 is a racing simulation developed by Papyrus Design Group and published by Sierra Sports for Microsoft Windows, released in North America on September 16, 1999[^ref-22]. It is the third numbered installment in Papyrus's NASCAR Racing series, which Wikipedia describes as universally praised for its accuracy in terms of physics and track feel[^ref-2]. Built on a modified version of the NASCAR 2 engine rather than the Grand Prix Legends physics Papyrus had originally promised[^ref-7], it covers the 1999 NASCAR Winston Cup and Busch Series[^ref-22].

The game was part of Sierra Sports' portfolio, a label formed in February 1998 by Sierra On-Line as part of a company restructuring[^ref-3]. Reviewers counted 28 real-world Winston Cup and Busch tracks[^ref-8][^ref-17], though licensing issues kept Daytona and Pocono out of the game[^ref-7]. Online play ran through Sierra's WON.net matchmaking service built into the game[^ref-11]; IGN described support for up to 20 players over the Internet[^ref-8], while PC Gamer reported that 32 players could race for free on WON.NET[^ref-15].

## Story Summary

NASCAR Racing 3 is a pure racing simulation without a narrative storyline. Players compete in the 1999 NASCAR Winston Cup and Busch Series[^ref-22], either in single races or in a Championship Mode whose schedule includes all of the races from the real-life Winston Cup and Busch series[^ref-13]. Players can create custom paint jobs with the bundled paint kit[^ref-6].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

NASCAR Racing 3 offers a choice between an arcade or a simulation experience, with adjustable variables within each[^ref-8], and includes driver's aids for rookies[^ref-7]. Supported input devices include keyboard, mouse, racing wheel and analog joystick[^ref-4]. Reviewers singled out its force-feedback support, which Papyrus had previously declined to add[^ref-7][^ref-11]. The game supports Direct3D and Glide (3Dfx) at 800×600 and 1024×768[^ref-4].

The garage lets players adjust suspension, weight distribution and grille tape[^ref-9]; this release added front and rear sway bar adjustments and grille taping, the latter improving straightline speed at the expense of engine cooling[^ref-15]. Cars suffer realistic damage, and the physics model is biased so that a reckless driver hurts their own car rather than those around them[^ref-9].

### Structure and Progression

Race length, opponent ability, full-course yellows and restart style can all be customized[^ref-1], and opponent strength can be set from 80 to 125 percent[^ref-11]. Players can test on a track alone or race against as many as 42 other cars[^ref-9]. Preconfigured setups can be loaded for qualifying, test sessions or the race itself[^ref-13]. GameZone noted that Championship Mode differs from Single Race Mode mainly in following the full real-life schedule, with no economic model for upgrading cars[^ref-13].

Players can design their own cars with the built-in paint program or a third-party utility, and import their own image into a new driver[^ref-11]. Sierra's bundled WONswap utility let players trade car setups, paint schemes and other user-made files online[^ref-8][^ref-11]. A replay system lets races be viewed from multiple angles and saved to disk[^ref-1].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Rather than traditional puzzles, NASCAR Racing 3 challenges players with car setup and race strategy. Players act as both driver and crew chief, and pit stops can range from four tires and 22 gallons of gas to a splash-and-go[^ref-9]. Christian Spotlight's reviewer concluded that "racing clean (and smart) is the way to win"[^ref-9]. GameSpot rated its difficulty as hard, with a learning curve of about an hour[^ref-12].

Not every licence was in place. GameSpot noted that the game lacked the full lineup of NASCAR drivers and the licences to use the names "Winston Cup" and "Busch Grand Nationals"[^ref-11], and Christian Spotlight observed that no cars carried alcohol or tobacco sponsorship[^ref-9]. PC Gamer's lows included the aging physics engine, "20 percent fewer tracks than before", and an AI bug in which drivers queue up behind a car stopped on the racing line[^ref-15].

### Awards and Sales

NASCAR Racing 3 won GameSpot's 1999 Driving Game of the Year award, which called it "Papyrus' greatest achievement to date"[^ref-17]. It earned IGN's Editors' Choice[^ref-10] and was a nominee for CNET Gamecenter's Best Racing Game award[^ref-22]. Citing Edge, Wikipedia reports that the game sold at least 100,000 units in the US[^ref-22].

### Modern Assessment

On MobyGames the game holds a Moby Score of 7.3, with a player average of 4.1 out of 5[^ref-1]. It is not sold digitally, and has a GOG Dreamlist entry where users can vote for a re-release[^ref-16].

## Development

### Origins

NASCAR Racing 3 was originally to feature the physics that debuted in Grand Prix Legends and to ship in the fourth quarter of 1998, according to a teaser in the Busch Series expansion pack for NASCAR Racing 2. That version was cancelled in favour of one closer to its predecessor, and the Grand Prix Legends-style physics was pushed back to NASCAR Racing 4[^ref-22]. Project director Dave Matson told GameCenter, "It was a combination of factors, including the high system requirements, the hardware-only graphics engine, and, to some degree, the difficulty of the sim. We didn't want to leave the majority of our fans behind."[^ref-7]

### Production

MobyGames credits project director David Matson, lead engineer Charlie Heath, art director David D. Flamburis, designer Richard Yasi and producer Matt Marsala, among 140 credited people (107 developers and 33 thanks)[^ref-1]. CDMag described the physics model as a refinement of the system Papyrus had used for many years, though one that improved how players "feel" their way around the track[^ref-14].

### Technical Achievements

NASCAR Racing 3 runs on a modified version of the NASCAR 2 engine[^ref-7]. It supports Direct3D and Glide 3D acceleration[^ref-4], four-position audio[^ref-7], and A3D 2.0 or DirectSound 3D positional sound[^ref-15]. Minimum requirements are an Intel Pentium 166 MHz and 32 MB of RAM[^ref-26], with a 2X CD-ROM drive[^ref-4].

## Legacy

NASCAR Racing 3 was succeeded by NASCAR Racing 4, which received the Grand Prix Legends-style physics originally planned for this game[^ref-22]. A CD-ROM given away with the 2000 Daytona 500 official program included a demo featuring Daytona International Speedway, which made its official series debut in NASCAR Racing 4[^ref-22].

### Post-Launch Support and Expansion

The game received the Craftsman Truck Series Expansion Pack in 2000[^ref-25], featuring content from the 1999 NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series[^ref-22]. The final official patches were version 1.2.0.4 for the base game and version 1.2.1.7 for the expansion pack[^ref-26].

### Community Content

At launch, reviewers expected players to fill the licensing gaps themselves: GameSpot predicted user-made downloads would supply the whole NASCAR lineup[^ref-11], and Christian Spotlight noted that "real-life" carsets could be downloaded from many unofficial websites[^ref-9].
