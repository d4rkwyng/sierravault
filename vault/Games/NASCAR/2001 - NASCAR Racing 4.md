# NASCAR Racing 4

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

NASCAR Racing 4 is a stock car racing simulation that Sierra's own product description billed as "the next evolution of the best-selling, award-winning racing series," one that "fully immerses players into a truly realistic NASCAR experience."[^ref-1] Developed by Papyrus Design Group and published under the Sierra Sports label, it reached US stores in early February 2001: Metacritic lists February 6, while Wikipedia, citing an IGN news item, gives February 7, with a European release on April 6, 2001.[^ref-16][^ref-29][^ref-6] GameSpot called it "the glorious culmination of years of experience and expertise."[^ref-2] According to Wikipedia, the game came out 12 days before Dale Earnhardt's fatal crash at the 2001 Daytona 500.[^ref-6][^ref-28]

Retrospectives treat it as a turning point for the series. Daily Downforce wrote that "this was the game with which Papyrus really started to gain some traction" and that "when you start to think about NASCAR PC simulations by Papyrus, this is the game that started to look like a Papyrus NASCAR game."[^ref-3] Its physics engine was derived from Papyrus's Grand Prix Legends,[^ref-13] and GameRant notes it was "the first version that allowed cars to flip over, something that actually happens in auto racing."[^ref-5]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game supports keyboard and mouse, with joystick, gamepad or steering wheel optional, and force feedback.[^ref-7] Sierra's description says the arcade mode "includes antilock brakes, auto shifting, stability control and other great features."[^ref-1] Gamekult noted that damage can be switched off or reduced, and that players can choose arcade driving or pure simulation.[^ref-17] GameSpot described the simulation handling as "an exceptionally rigorous and exhilarating experience."[^ref-2] A French review on Jeuxvideo.com wrote that "Nascar Racing 4 est un véritable régal et l'usage du volant distillera à merveille toutes les sensations que peut procurer potentiellement ce titre" (NASCAR Racing 4 is a real delight, and using a wheel brings out every sensation the title can offer).[^ref-9]

### Structure and Progression

The game includes all 21 tracks of the 2000 Winston Cup schedule, among them Daytona, Pocono and Indianapolis,[^ref-7] and GameSpot noted that Daytona and Pocono had never before appeared in a Papyrus title.[^ref-2] Players choose from all four body styles of the 2001 season, including the newly introduced Dodge Intrepid R/T.[^ref-2] GameSpot counted "no fewer than 76 real-life drivers and cars,"[^ref-2] while Wikipedia lists 25 real drivers and teams from the 2001 season alongside 42 fictional ones.[^ref-6] It was the first game in the series to support a full 43-car field.[^ref-6]

Modes are testing, single race weekend, championship and online multiplayer,[^ref-6] and the championship includes qualifying sessions.[^ref-17] There is no career mode.[^ref-2] Sources disagree on the multiplayer limit. Computer Games Strategy Plus listed "2-42 players; Internet, LAN,"[^ref-22] Sierra's description and IGN say up to 43 players online,[^ref-1][^ref-29] and the description on MobyGames says 32.[^ref-4] During a race, a spotter and crew chief talk to the player over the radio, and a motion-captured pit crew services the car.[^ref-13] MobyGames' official description also mentions the "ability to tell pit crew what to do before you pit."[^ref-4]

### Physics and Mechanics

The physics engine is "derived from the GPL physics engine," with an aerodynamics package that IGN called "a major addition to the Legends engine."[^ref-13] The garage setup sheet is split into suspension, aerodynamics and weight sections.[^ref-13] One player review on MobyGames said "the physics can't get any better than this."[^ref-12] According to Gamekult, practice laps let players work out a strategy, such as how many pit stops to make given tire wear and fuel load.[^ref-17] Sierra's description says the computer-controlled cars "think and react according to various race conditions."[^ref-1] Reviewers were less impressed with the AI. IGN said "the AI needs some work before the game can truly be complete,"[^ref-13] and Computer Games Strategy Plus wrote that "the AI isn't improved much over previous Papyrus outings."[^ref-22]

### Modern Assessment

On Metacritic the game has a score of 89/100 from 14 critic reviews, all of them positive. The highest is Yahoo!'s 100.[^ref-14] Metacritic's archived page ranked it the #6 best PC game of 2001.[^ref-23] On MobyGames, critics average 80% across 19 ratings.[^ref-4] The game sold 260,000 units in the US and earned $10.4 million by August 2006, making it the country's 76th best-selling computer game between January 2000 and August 2006.[^ref-6] Amazon's product description calls it "the best NASCAR simulation ever created,"[^ref-18] and GameRant ranks it third among the best NASCAR games.[^ref-5]

## Development

### Origins

The game was published under Sierra Sports, the label Sierra On-Line formed in February 1998 as part of a company restructuring, which included Papyrus.[^ref-8] According to Wikipedia, it "addressed many requested features" from earlier games in the series.[^ref-6] Hasbro and Monster Games' NASCAR Heat had come out shortly before, and IGN's review weighed the two games against each other throughout.[^ref-13]

The game reached stores days before Dale Earnhardt died in a crash at Daytona International Speedway on February 18, 2001.[^ref-6][^ref-28] Computer Games Strategy Plus's reviewer wrote his review "one day after the tragedy that claimed the life of NASCAR's greatest driver, Dale Earnhardt."[^ref-22]

### Production

MobyGames credits Scott Stutsman as senior producer, David Kaemmer as technical director, Richard Yasi as senior designer and David D. Flamburis as art director. Randy Cassidy and Shawn Nash are credited as senior engineers.[^ref-21] According to GameSpot, Nash said the physics engine was similar to that of Grand Prix Legends, and "the developers did tweak certain features after making precise measurements based on actual specs gleaned from NASCAR garages."[^ref-2] Jon St. John voiced the crew chief and Ryan Drummond the spotter. Lani Minella and Pro-Motions Production Co. handled casting and direction, and Anthony "Tony" Trippi wrote the original intro music.[^ref-21] MobyGames credits 135 people in total: 118 in professional roles and 17 thanks.[^ref-21] Dale Earnhardt Jr. is among those thanked,[^ref-21] and the box carried his quote: "This is the sim we have been waiting for."[^ref-3]

### Technical Achievements

Computer Games Strategy Plus wrote that "for the first time since the original NASCAR Racing, the developers have implemented a much more realistic physics model—the same one that was previously used to power the seminal Grand Prix Legends."[^ref-22] Cars could now leave the ground. As that review put it, "previously the cars were unable to leave the ground, resulting in collisions that didn't always seem realistic."[^ref-22] It was the first game in the series to let cars flip.[^ref-5] GameSpot described "tried-and-true Papyrus damage modeling with new vertical physics."[^ref-2] The game moved to full 24-bit color, where earlier entries allowed only 256 colors.[^ref-6]

IGN noted that "NASCAR 4 is a Direct3D game, complete with TnL support,"[^ref-13] and Computer Hope lists DirectX 7 as a requirement.[^ref-7] Amazon's product description says that with the right hardware the game "can generate visuals that are difficult to distinguish from TV broadcasts,"[^ref-18] and Computer Games Strategy Plus wrote that its "graphics are so good you might think it's TV."[^ref-22]

## Legacy

NASCAR Racing 4 won Best Driving Game at GameSpot's Best and Worst of 2001 Awards. Computer Games Magazine named it the best racing game of 2001 at its 11th Annual Computer Games Awards and called it "the crown King of racing games."[^ref-6] It was also nominated for Best Simulation Game at Computer Gaming World's 2001 Premier Awards, which went to IL-2 Sturmovik.[^ref-6]

### Critical Assessment

GameRant wrote that the game "signified that the Papyrus series of games would be exactly what NASCAR fans were looking to see" and "signified a new age in racing games."[^ref-5] NASCAR Racing 2002 Season followed it.[^ref-6] Vivendi and Papyrus later sold the copyrights to NASCAR Racing 2003 Season to FIRST.net on May 28, 2004. FIRST.net went on to develop iRacing using that game's engine.[^ref-26]
