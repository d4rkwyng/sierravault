# NASCAR Racing

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

NASCAR Racing is a racing simulation developed by Papyrus Design Group and released for MS-DOS in late 1994.[^ref-1] Sources differ on the month: Wikipedia and The Cutting Room Floor give November 2, 1994,[^ref-1][^ref-17] while Next Generation reported in December 1995 that Papyrus had released it in October 1994.[^ref-29][^ref-15] Daily Downforce describes it as the first true simulation 3-D NASCAR video game.[^ref-2] As the third racing title from Papyrus, following Indianapolis 500: The Simulation and IndyCar Racing, the development team moved their focus from open-wheeled cars to closed-cockpit NASCAR stock cars.[^ref-3]

The simulation offered an unusual level of realism that appealed to both hardcore racing enthusiasts and casual sports fans. As Next Generation magazine noted, "it's the game's astounding ability to create a realistic feeling of speed that makes it an incredible hit. Flying around the tracks at 170 mph and up will make you respect those drivers who do this for a living."[^ref-1] The game was the first stock car racing simulator to include real sponsors on their respective cars, lending authenticity to the NASCAR experience.[^ref-4]

NASCAR Racing proved to be a commercial and critical success, selling over 1,000,000 units and winning numerous awards including PC Gamer's Best Sports Game of 1994 and Computer Gaming World's Simulation Game of the Year.[^ref-3] The game spawned a franchise that continued through NASCAR Racing 2003 Season, whose code Papyrus co-founder Dave Kaemmer and John Henry later used as the starting point for iRacing, a project they began in September 2004.[^ref-22][^ref-13]

## Story Summary

As a racing simulation, NASCAR Racing does not feature a traditional narrative storyline. Instead, players assume the role of a NASCAR Winston Cup driver competing across the 1994 season. The game provides authentic racing scenarios where players must navigate the challenges of professional stock car racing, including qualifying sessions, multi-lap races, and full championship seasons.[^ref-3]

The game features more than 25 of the 40 regular drivers from the 1994 NASCAR Winston Cup season, though notable absences include Dale Earnhardt (who won the Winston Cup that year), Dale Jarrett, Kyle Petty, and Darrell Waltrip.[^ref-1] Michael Waltrip was included despite his brother Darrell being absent from the roster.[^ref-1] Players could also create custom car designs using the included Paintkit program, allowing for personalized liveries and team identities.[^ref-4]

In the PlayStation version, released in 1996, the player races as a rookie in the number 96 Papyrus car, among 20 of the 39 regular drivers from the 1996 season.[^ref-1]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game supported keyboard and mouse as well as racing/steering wheels,[^ref-3] and joysticks could be used once calibrated.[^ref-19] Pressing F10 during play switches between camera angles.[^ref-12]

The game included various driving aids to accommodate different skill levels, including auto-shifting and auto-braking options that allowed newcomers to focus on racing lines while experienced players could take full manual control.[^ref-3] Races can be fine-tuned by setting the number of laps.[^ref-8]

### Structure and Progression

The game offered multiple racing modes that catered to different playstyles:

- **Championship Season:** NASCAR Racing was the first NASCAR video game to feature a Championship season mode.[^ref-2]
- **Practice/Testing:** Modes in which players test and optimize their car's setup for a particular track.[^ref-4]
- **Race weekends:** Practice, Qualifying, Warmup and Race sessions.[^ref-10]
- **Multiplayer:** Modem and null-modem play,[^ref-3] plus online racing through Papyrus's own service codenamed "Hawaii," reached over dial-up modems.[^ref-1][^ref-13]

Nine tracks are featured, with the Watkins Glen road course alongside the ovals.[^ref-3]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core mechanical challenge in NASCAR Racing centered on vehicle setup and racecraft. Players could set up their car by adjusting the airdam height, rear spoiler angle, gear ratios, shocks, tire pressures, cambers and more, and could also choose the car type (Chevrolet Lumina, Ford Thunderbird or Pontiac Grand Prix) and tire brand (Goodyear or Hoosier).[^ref-4] Setups could be saved to disk, and the game provided a few prefabricated setups for each track.[^ref-4]

One of the game's most notable features was its drafting system. As MobyGames describes it (a description mirrored on the Internet Archive listing), "you can position your car just inches off the rear of competitors in front of you. When in this aerodynamically optimal position, you gain acceleration, conserve fuel and place less stress on the mechanical parts. By timing right, you can also make a quick pass."[^ref-8]

The damage model added consequences to aggressive driving, with collisions affecting vehicle performance. The official documentation noted that "the damage level in version 1.2 strikes an appropriate balance between realism and playability."[^ref-10]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

NASCAR Racing received widespread critical acclaim upon release. Next Generation awarded the game 4 out of 5 stars in April 1995, praising its sense of speed and authenticity.[^ref-1] PC Gamer US editors declared it "the best racing game ever created," while Computer Gaming World editors noted that its "incredible graphics, sound and overall experience appeal to simulation enthusiasts and casual sports fans alike."[^ref-1]

GameSpot's Jeff Kitts gave the PlayStation version an 8.1 out of 10, describing it as "stock car racing at its most realistic."[^ref-1] Electronic Gaming Monthly's team review scored the PlayStation port 6.9 out of 10 in November 1996.[^ref-1]

The game earned numerous industry accolades including:
- **PC Gamer 1994:** Best Sports Game (winner); nominated for Best Simulation[^ref-1]
- **Computer Gaming World 1994:** Simulation Game of the Year (tied with Aces of the Deep)[^ref-3]
- **CODiE Awards 1995:** Best Sports Program[^ref-11]
- **Computer Game Review:** Golden Triad Award[^ref-3]

Computer Gaming World later ranked NASCAR Racing as the 31st best computer game ever released in their November 1996 retrospective of the 150 greatest games.[^ref-3]

### Modern Assessment

NASCAR Racing continues to be regarded as a landmark title in racing simulation history. User reviews on abandonware sites reflect enduring appreciation, with My Abandonware showing a rating of 4.51 out of 5 based on 148 votes.[^ref-12]

Retrospective analysis from outlets like Top Gear confirms the game's lasting significance, noting that "NASCAR Racing's simulation approach certainly proved to be hugely influential," with "handling that felt good even if you were pecking at the arrow keys on a keyboard."[^ref-13]

## Development

### Origins

David Kaemmer, founder of Papyrus Design Group, was inspired by his early experiences with flight simulation software on the TRS-80. As he recalled, "My favorite program for the TRS-80 was 'Flight Simulator' from subLogic. I always wanted to figure out how to make a program that could draw a 3D image like that."[^ref-5] This fascination with realistic simulation drove his vision: "Just as Flight Simulator let you climb into a Cessna and see what it was like to be a private pilot, I wanted to make a program that would show someone, as much as possible, what it was like to drive a racecar."[^ref-5]

Following the success of Indianapolis 500: The Simulation (1989) and IndyCar Racing (1993), Papyrus sought to expand into NASCAR. The company's philosophy was unwavering in its commitment to authenticity: "We never spent a lot of time thinking about what would make a fun game--only what would make the experience more like driving a real racecar."[^ref-5]

### Production

NASCAR drivers Dave Marcis and Joe Nemechek gave advice in the game's manual about how to race at certain tracks.[^ref-3] Papyrus would later bring drivers directly into testing: Kaemmer recalled that "real feedback started when we got Kevin Harvick and Dale Earnhardt, Jr. to beta-test NASCAR 4."[^ref-5]

### Technical Achievements

NASCAR Racing represented a significant technical achievement for its time. The CD-ROM version supported SVGA graphics, enabled from the command prompt, though this mode "was too demanding for many of the computers of its age."[^ref-1] The game also supported VGA at 320x200 for systems with less capable hardware.[^ref-6]

Tires are modeled in detail: the game tracks three temperatures for each tire, at the center and the inner and outer edges, influenced by factors such as inflation, camber, weight distribution and driver behavior.[^ref-4]

The game was notable for supporting early hardware acceleration. Special versions were bundled with the Matrox Millennium graphics card and Diamond Edge 3D video card.[^ref-1] PCGamingWiki notes that the game also supports Matrox Millennium's, Creative 3D Blaster's CGL, and nVidia NV1's NVLib APIs.[^ref-6]

### Cut Content

Several prominent NASCAR drivers were absent from the game, including Dale Earnhardt, Dale Jarrett, Kyle Petty, and Darrell Waltrip;[^ref-1] MobyGames suggests this was "presumably because of those drivers having their own license deals."[^ref-3] Advertisements for beer and tobacco products were also left off the cars; MobyGames notes the game "followed in the tradition of the toy companies,"[^ref-3] and fan-made car sets later restored many of these sponsors.[^ref-4]

European releases featured entirely fictional drivers instead of the licensed NASCAR roster, with 39 fictional drivers replacing the 25 licensed teams and 14 fictional drivers found in the US version.[^ref-17]

### Technical Issues

The official documentation explicitly warned users: "NASCAR Racing should not be installed, setup, or run while running Windows 95. You must restart your computer in MS-DOS mode."[^ref-10]

Known issues included:
- Palette setting problems on some video cards[^ref-10]
- Crashes between opponent cars could cause computer hang in early versions[^ref-10]
- Corrupted best lap times in modem races in earlier versions[^ref-10]
- Joystick calibration issues where the car would drift slightly left; this could be corrected by offsetting calibration during setup[^ref-19]
- The game's menu could "go haywire" if all joystick axes weren't calibrated the first time the game was launched[^ref-19]

A multiplayer exploit was discovered where players achieved faster lap times by deliberately hitting walls; vehicle sensitivity to crash impacts was increased in a patch to address this issue.[^ref-4]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- **CD Audio Tracks:** The game CD contains playable audio tracks including "Race Riff" by Rachel Bolan and Dave "Snake" Sabo of Skid Row (Track 2) and "The Fastest Sport" by The Fat Man and Team Fat (Track 3). The README warns: "WARNING do not play track 1 in your audio player, it contains the game."[^ref-10]
- **Driver Easter Egg:** Rachel Bolan of Skid Row appears as the driver of a green-and-purple No. 00 car in the game.[^ref-4]
- **Camera Views:** Pressing F10 during gameplay switches between available camera angles.[^ref-12]
- **High Resolution Mode:** Running the game with the command "nascar -h" enables the high-quality 640x480 SVGA mode.[^ref-12]
- **Daytona Museum Exclusive:** Papyrus produced a special Daytona track exclusively for the Daytona USA museum exhibit.[^ref-1]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

NASCAR Racing was a major commercial success, selling 350,000 units by December 1995 and 400,000 copies by February 1996.[^ref-1][^ref-29] The game and its sequel shipped above 2 million copies globally by March 1998, with the original game alone surpassing 1 million units sold by 2004.[^ref-1] MobyGames confirms sales "in excess of 1,000,000 units."[^ref-20]

At one point, NASCAR Racing 2 was ranked as the #6 selling game in the nation according to PC Data.[^ref-21] Sierra On-Line acquired Papyrus Design Group in late 1995,[^ref-22][^ref-29] with Ken Williams noting: "Papyrus has built a fantastic franchise with their NASCAR and Indy Car games. I'm especially excited about the addition of a talented development group to our already strong internal development resource."[^ref-29]

### Collections

NASCAR Racing appeared in several compilation packages:
- **NASCAR Racing with Track Pack:** Bundled version sold 225,737 units in 1998[^ref-1]
- **Hardware Bundle:** Special Win32 version packaged with Diamond Edge 3D video card[^ref-3]
- **Matrox Bundle:** Hardware-accelerated version included with Matrox Millennium graphics card[^ref-1]

### Fan Projects

The NASCAR Racing series developed a vibrant modding community, particularly around NASCAR Racing 2003 Season. Community members created custom tracks, car models, and paint schemes that extended the game's lifespan far beyond its commercial support period.[^ref-23] The r/NR2003 subreddit remains active with moderators stating: "We're dedicated to keeping this nearly 21-year-old sim alive and well, and our community is our best way to do this."[^ref-24]

Online leagues using NASCAR Racing games flourished throughout the late 1990s and early 2000s. The American Sim Racing community, for example, has documented championship results from 2002 through 2026 across multiple NASCAR simulation platforms.[^ref-25]

### Related Publications

- **Game Manual:** The original 1994 NASCAR Racing included a 176-page game manual,[^ref-26] with advice from Dave Marcis and Joe Nemechek on how to race at certain tracks[^ref-3]
- **Track Pack Documentation:** Additional documentation accompanied the NASCAR Track Pack expansion[^ref-12]

### Critical Perspective

NASCAR Racing stands as a watershed moment in racing game history. As Daily Downforce observed, "There were NASCAR games, but this was the first true simulation 3-D NASCAR video game... It was the first true simulation-style NASCAR video game, and that alone deserves recognition."[^ref-2]

The title's influence extended beyond its immediate successors. When Dave Kaemmer and John Henry began iRacing in September 2004, the team used NASCAR Racing 2003 Season's code as a starting point, "taking advantage of its greatest strengths, such as the net code, which allows full fields of drivers to race online in real time, and the replay system."[^ref-22]

That lineage resurfaced with NASCAR 25 in 2025, produced by iRacing, "known in a previous incarnation as Papyrus." iRacing's director of production Matt Lewis recalled playing Papyrus's NASCAR 2003 and wanting to combine "the multiplayer and the physics and the authenticity of what Papyrus did" with elements of other NASCAR games.[^ref-28]
