# NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series Racing

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series Racing is a racing simulation developed by Papyrus Design Group and published by Sierra On-Line for Windows, released in the United States in April 1999: database listings give April 1, and Sierra announced the release on April 5, 1999.[^ref-11][^ref-20][^ref-25] MobyGames describes it as "essentially one piece of NASCAR Racing 1999 Edition sold as standalone": players drive 700 hp trucks against the series' top truck racers, with full 3Dfx accelerator support and an included paint kit.[^ref-3]

The parent game, NASCAR Racing 1999 Edition, combined the Winston Cup, Busch Grand National and Craftsman Truck series in one box, and Sierra's announcement called its truck content "a first for any racing simulation".[^ref-4] Squakenet describes that collection as "all themed around the NASCAR 1998 season".[^ref-7]

## Story Summary

NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series Racing is a racing simulation with no narrative. Players race licensed NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series trucks against the series' drivers.[^ref-3] The credits list licences for real drivers and trucks of the era, among them Ron Hornaday, Jack Sprague, Greg Biffle, Joe Ruttman and Rick Carelli.[^ref-23] The real series began in 1995 as the NASCAR SuperTruck Series and took the Craftsman name in 1996.[^ref-17]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames classifies the game as having both 1st-person and 3rd-person perspectives, and its screenshots include a cockpit view and an instant-replay screen.[^ref-3][^ref-21] Graphics run at 640×480, and Glide (3Dfx) is the listed 3D API.[^ref-24] The one player review on MobyGames lists "No Force Feedback" and the lack of Direct3D acceleration among the game's weaknesses.[^ref-3]

### Trucks and Competition

Players drive 700 hp trucks against the best truck racers of the series.[^ref-3] Sierra's announcement of NASCAR Racing 1999 Edition said the truck content modelled "the accurate rules and vehicle dynamics" of the series: "A driver of a Craftsman Truck contends with more front drag, and less rear downforce than NASCAR cars, especially on faster tracks, and a Craftsman Truck operates with slightly less horsepower."[^ref-4] The same release promised "the most detailed set-up mode on the market" for customising cars and trucks in the pits.[^ref-4]

Squakenet calls the trucks "very different beasts from most racing games, and which take some getting used to."[^ref-7]

**Key features:**
- 3Dfx (Glide) accelerator support[^ref-3][^ref-24]
- Paint kit for custom truck designs[^ref-3]
- Championship mode, plus quick race and qualifying screens[^ref-3][^ref-21]
- Instant replay[^ref-21]

### Tracks

The 1999 Edition announcement listed its Craftsman Truck Series tracks as Evergreen, Flemington, Heartland Park Topeka, I-70, Las Vegas, Louisville, Memphis, Metro Dade/Homestead, Mesa Marin, Pikes Peak and Portland.[^ref-4] The standalone game's credits carry track licences for I-70, Mesa Marin, Flemington, Heartland Park Topeka, Portland, Louisville, Evergreen and Memphis, among others.[^ref-23]

### Compatibility with Other NASCAR Games

The MobyGames player review notes that owners of NASCAR Racing 2 with the Grand National expansion could use this game to add the truck racing they were missing. Owners of NASCAR Racing 1999 Edition already had the trucks and did not need it.[^ref-3]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

MobyGames records a critic average of 85% from three ratings and a player average of 4.6 out of 5 from two ratings.[^ref-3] No full contemporary review text was found for this page.

The Daily Downforce writes that the parent game, NASCAR Racing 1999 Edition, "was not as well received by critics as its predecessors".[^ref-10]

### Modern Assessment

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 85% critics (3 ratings); players 4.6/5 (2 ratings)[^ref-3]
- **MyAbandonware:** 5/5 (8 votes)[^ref-1]

In a modern retrospective, Squakenet says "this game is kind of a cheat", because it is one part of NASCAR Racing 1999 Edition "stripped out and given a release of its own". It still found that the game "does make for an entertaining time".[^ref-7] The reviewer praised "some impressive detail levels" and physics that are "well implemented". In its words, "The trucks handle realistically and although the usual sense of speed found in racers like Daytona isn't there, there's still a real thrill to take control of these monsters." The review calls the options "quite varied" and says the tracks "should keep even veterans challenged for some time". Its advice: "If you have got the original collection, then don't even think about this."[^ref-7]

## Development

### Origins

The game is the truck-racing portion of NASCAR Racing 1999 Edition, released on its own.[^ref-3][^ref-7] Sierra's April 1999 release called it "the first stand alone truck title available to consumers", sold on CD-ROM for Windows 95 at a suggested $19.99.[^ref-25] The Daily Downforce calls it "the first and thus far only NASCAR game to be centered around only a lower tier touring series."[^ref-10]

### Production

MobyGames credits 301 people. David Matson was Project Director, Matson, David D. Flamburis and Charlie Heath handled game design, and Heath was Lead Engineer. Steve Vandergriff wrote the manual.[^ref-23] The credits carry a 1998 Sierra On-Line copyright.[^ref-23]

Papyrus was founded in 1987 by David Kaemmer and Omar Khudari and was acquired by Sierra On-Line in late 1995.[^ref-5]

### Trivia

- A separate NASCAR Racing 3: Craftsman Truck Series Expansion Pack followed in 2000 for NASCAR Racing 3.[^ref-6]
- Vivendi Universal shut Papyrus down in 2004.[^ref-22] That May, Vivendi and Papyrus sold the NASCAR Racing 2003 Season copyrights to FIRST.net, which went on to build iRacing on its engine.[^ref-5]

## Legacy

### Series Context

Papyrus's NASCAR Racing 3, also released in 1999, won GameSpot's 1999 "Driving Game of the Year" award.[^ref-9] Truck racing returned in the 2000 NASCAR Racing 3 expansion pack.[^ref-6]

### The Real Series

Sears held the naming rights from 1995 through 2008. Camping World and Gander sponsorships followed, and Stanley Black & Decker revived the Craftsman name in 2023. From 2027 the series will be called the FedEx Freight Truck Series.[^ref-17]
