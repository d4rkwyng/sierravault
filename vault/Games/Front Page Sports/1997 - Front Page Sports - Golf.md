# Front Page Sports: Golf

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

Front Page Sports: Golf is a golf simulation video game developed by Headgate Studios and published by Sierra On-Line for Microsoft Windows, released in 1997 after nearly three years of development.[^ref-1] GameSpot, Sierra Chest and Metacritic list its release date as April 30, 1997.[^ref-16][^ref-8][^ref-23] Online Gaming Review's news page for May 16, 1997 reported that "Sierra On-Line has released Front Page Sports: Golf."[^ref-32] It was the first golf title under Sierra's sports label, and it entered a field led by Links LS, Jack Nicklaus 4 and PGA Tour '96.[^ref-20][^ref-3] Development was led by Vance Cook, who had been the director and lead programmer of Links and Links 386 Pro.[^ref-3]

The game's best-known feature was the TrueSwing control system, a mouse-based swing interface that let players control their golf swing directly through mouse movements.[^ref-1] Pulling back on the mouse made the 3D golfer begin the backswing, while pushing it forward carried out the downswing.[^ref-20] It was not the first mouse swing: Maxis's SimGolf came first, but critics considered TrueSwing the superior version.[^ref-1] Computer Gaming World's Scott A. May granted that "Maxis beat Sierra to the punch with its MouseSwing in SimGolf," yet still called TrueSwing "the game's biggest innovation."[^ref-30] GameSpot's Tasos Kaiafas wrote in his preview that TrueSwing "provides a greater sense of accomplishment when good shot is hit" than tri-click timing.[^ref-3] In his review for Computer Games Strategy Plus, Jeff Lackey wrote that it "just feels like golf in a way that no other golf sim duplicates."[^ref-5]

Front Page Sports: Golf set itself apart from competitors with a less formal approach to the genre, using humor and fun surprises to take "the stuffiness out of your typical golf sim."[^ref-6] Its golfers were fully rendered in 3D rather than the superimposed FMV golfers then typical of the genre.[^ref-6]

## Story Summary

As a sports simulation, Front Page Sports: Golf has no traditional narrative structure. Players instead create custom golfers and compete across its courses and game modes.[^ref-1] Character customization includes a "wild array of apparel" for dressing the golfer.[^ref-6]

Players can compete in formats including stroke play, match play, skins, four-ball, greensome, bloodsome, scramble, shootout, Ryder Cup and Stableford.[^ref-3] The game shipped with two default players, so Quick Start could be used the very first time the game was loaded.[^ref-8]

Professional golfer Tom Lehman appears as a playable character.[^ref-1] GameSpot's preview nonetheless noted the game's "lack of any professional golf endorsement" and its more "regular guy" feel compared with rivals.[^ref-3]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

TrueSwing replaced the meter-based swing that computer golf games traditionally used.[^ref-1] The player clicks the mouse button and drags it back to start the backswing; the further the player drags, the higher the onscreen golfer raises the club.[^ref-9] Gordon Goble of CNET Gamecenter found that the system "feels unrestrained, and yes, natural."[^ref-9]

For players uncomfortable with the new control method, the game also offered a traditional three-click swing method.[^ref-1] The game supports keyboard, mouse, and joystick/gamepad input.[^ref-20]

The interface includes a Quick Start option that lets players skip the setup windows and begin playing immediately, using the setup options from the last game played.[^ref-8]

### Structure and Progression

The game offers twelve game types for single-player and multiplayer sessions, including:[^ref-3][^ref-20]

- **Stroke Play:** Traditional scoring over 18 holes
- **Match Play:** Hole-by-hole competition
- **Skins:** Money-based hole competition
- **Four-Ball:** Team-based best ball format
- **Greensome:** Team alternate shot format
- **Bloodsome:** Competitive team format
- **Scramble:** Best shot team play
- **Shootout:** Elimination-style competition
- **Ryder Cup:** Team tournament format
- **Stableford:** Points-based scoring

Players can practice on a driving range, among other practice areas.[^ref-8] For multiplayer, the game supports Internet, modem and LAN play, with Sierra's free SIGS service providing online opponents, for 2-64+ players.[^ref-5]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core mechanic is a golf physics simulation. Cook and the development team spent hours studying golf ball dynamics to make the physics accurate.[^ref-1] Sierra advertised "advanced ball dynamics" that made the ball react accurately to different surfaces and weather conditions.[^ref-22] With TrueSwing, the speed of the forward stroke sets shot power, deviation from a straight path produces draws and hooks, and the length of the backswing controls punch shots.[^ref-9]

TrueSwing made consistency harder than with a meter: "There may be shots where you feel like you hit it perfectly only to see the ball sail into the trees with a lot of spin."[^ref-6] Kaiafas warned players they "might have a hell of a time hitting ten under par using the TrueSwing."[^ref-6]

Sierra described the courses as recreated "down to each clubhouse, tree and break in the green."[^ref-22] The putting system had a noted calibration problem: even at the lowest power setting, it did not take a very strong stroke to putt the ball too far.[^ref-6]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Front Page Sports: Golf was critically acclaimed on release.[^ref-1] GameSpot awarded it 8.1 out of 10, and reviewer Tasos Kaiafas wrote that it "certainly has some fun surprises, taking the stuffiness out of your typical golf sim."[^ref-6] The review also called TrueSwing "the one critical component" of the simulation.[^ref-6]

Game Revolution gave the game an A-, calling it "a gem of a golf sim" and "a strong title in every sense."[^ref-7] Reviewer Dr. Moo praised "a smooth interface, an excellent swing mechanism, beautiful graphics and crisp sounds."[^ref-7] CNET Gamecenter awarded it 8 out of 10.[^ref-1] Its reviewer, Gordon Goble, wrote that TrueSwing "feels unrestrained, and yes, natural--I can't say I've enjoyed computer golf more."[^ref-9] Scott A. May of Computer Gaming World gave it four out of five stars and wrote that the game had "been worth the wait."[^ref-30]

Jeff Lackey of Computer Games Strategy Plus headlined his review "A breakthrough in PC golf sims." He wrote that the game "has done what the like-minded SimGolf failed to do; revolutionize the swing interface."[^ref-5] John Zhaski of InterAction Magazine, Sierra's own publication, called it "the most playable and realistic golf game available for the PC."[^ref-1][^ref-28] Not every critic agreed: T. Liam McDonald of PC Magazine was disappointed with TrueSwing and called the game a "modest golf game."[^ref-1]

### Modern Assessment

The game keeps a small following among players. GOG.com wishlist commenters have called it "probably one of the few true golf games in the history of video games" and said it "continues to be an excellent golf game."[^ref-12] One MyAbandonware user wrote: "I've been addicted to it for about 10 years now."[^ref-13]

MobyGames user Overmann wrote that the game "Introduced mouse-swing and set the standard for controls in Golf games," though Maxis's SimGolf actually had a mouse swing first.[^ref-2][^ref-1] TrueSwing itself was later reused in PGA Championship Golf 1999 Edition, also by Headgate and Sierra.[^ref-1]

## Development

### Origins

Front Page Sports: Golf was in development for nearly three years.[^ref-1] The project was led by Vance Cook, the director and lead programmer of Links and Links 386 Pro, and it marked Cook's return to golf simulations.[^ref-3] Computer golf games had traditionally used a meter for the swing. Cook and his team experimented with other approaches, including a top-down perspective, before Cook settled on using the mouse to swing the club.[^ref-1]

Sierra On-Line purchased Headgate Studios in April 1996.[^ref-29] The purchase gave Headgate additional resources to perfect the game.[^ref-1] The game was once planned for release in November 1996.[^ref-1] It neared completion in April 1997 and was released for Windows shortly afterward.[^ref-1][^ref-3]

### Production

The team went to some lengths to recreate the real courses. Before each course was mapped, team members visited it to take video footage and hundreds of photos.[^ref-1] Planes were also flown over the courses to obtain aerial photographs.[^ref-1]

Multiplayer was not initially planned and was implemented late in development.[^ref-1] The game shipped with IPX and LAN networking, plus free Internet play through SIGS (Sierra's Internet Gaming Site).[^ref-7]

### Technical Achievements

The TrueSwing control system was the game's most praised feature.[^ref-1] By mapping mouse movements directly to the golfer's swing, it drew contemporary praise; Lackey wrote that it "has likely changed the face of all golf games to come."[^ref-5] Headgate later reused TrueSwing in PGA Championship Golf 1999 Edition.[^ref-1]

The golfers were fully rendered 3D models instead of the FMV golfers typical of the time. This let them respond in real time to the mouse swing and react to shots with numerous animations.[^ref-6] Dr. Moo of Game Revolution wrote: "At long last, the world of polygons has entered the fray."[^ref-7] The golfers are made of texture-mapped polygons.[^ref-1]

AllGame lists motion-capture animation among the game's features.[^ref-20] Sierra's 1997 product page advertised "3D stereo sound effects."[^ref-22]

### Technical Issues

Reviews identified several technical issues:

- **Putting Power Calibration:** Even at the lowest power setting, a fairly gentle stroke could putt the ball too far.[^ref-6]
- **Polygon Edges:** Sharp edges visible on some polygons, particularly in sand traps and walls.[^ref-6]
- **Terrain Blending:** Terrain did not blend as uniformly as in other golf sims.[^ref-6]
- **Bugs and AI:** Lackey reported a leaderboard bug, cart-path rulings charged as stroke penalties, and occasional poor AI decisions, noting that Sierra was working on a patch.[^ref-5]

Sierra later released the v1.0.0.5 and golfpt15 updates.[^ref-16][^ref-17]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

Front Page Sports: Golf contains several humorous touches:[^ref-6]

- **Idle Scream:** Leave the game untouched for a while and you'll hear someone scream at you from a distance
- **Character Physics:** Shake the mouse around during the TrueSwing and the golfer can lose balance and fall on his rear end
- **Apparel Options:** Game Revolution's reviewer said it was the first golf simulation to let players change their golfer's pants[^ref-7]

The preview also noted ambient sounds such as mowers, airplanes and lawn sprinklers, and idle golfers shouting lines like "Hey buddy, we're losing daylight!"[^ref-3]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures have been documented for Front Page Sports: Golf. Sierra listed it at $54.95.[^ref-22] For a limited time at launch, the game came with a free add-on course disk for the Coeur d'Alene Resort.[^ref-1][^ref-22]

The ESRB rated the game E for Everyone.[^ref-18][^ref-25]

### Alternate Titles and Compilations

MobyGames and MyAbandonware list the following alternate titles:[^ref-2][^ref-13]

- **DSF Golf:** German title
- **Sierra Sports Golf:** French title
- **FPS Golf:** Common title
- **Golf: All American Sports Series:** 1999 reissue[^ref-1]

Acer TWP Corp published the Taiwanese release.[^ref-2][^ref-13]

MobyGames lists the game in compilations including Le Meilleur de Sierra II (1998) and On-Line Games Collection Series (1997).[^ref-2]

### Fan Projects

No fan remake or modification projects have been documented for Front Page Sports: Golf. Fans have asked for a re-release on GOG.com's wishlist.[^ref-12][^ref-26]

### Related Publications

The game came with an instruction manual.[^ref-20]

The base game and its add-on disks covered these courses:
- The Prince Course on Kauai, Hawaii[^ref-3]
- Pete Dye Golf Club in Bridgeport, West Virginia[^ref-3]
- Coeur d'Alene Resort, Idaho (free add-on at launch)[^ref-1][^ref-30]
- Black Diamond Ranch (later add-on)[^ref-1][^ref-30]

### Critical Perspective

Front Page Sports: Golf did not invent the mouse swing; Maxis's SimGolf came first, but critics considered TrueSwing the superior version.[^ref-1] Lackey wrote at the time that it "has likely changed the face of all golf games to come."[^ref-5] The only documented direct legacy is Headgate's reuse of TrueSwing in PGA Championship Golf 1999 Edition.[^ref-1]

Headgate went on to develop golf games for Sierra, starting with Front Page Sports: Golf.[^ref-29] GameSpot's preview predicted the game would find its niche through TrueSwing, its non-FMV golfer and a more municipal-course feel, set against Links LS and other established rivals.[^ref-3]
