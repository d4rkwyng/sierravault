# Front Page Sports: Ski Racing '99

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

> [!note] German-edition stub
> This page covers the German release, **DSF Ski '99: Extreme Edition**. The game itself is documented in full at **[[1998 - Sierra Sports Skiing 1999 Edition|Sierra Sports: Skiing 1999 Edition]]**. Facts on this page are limited to what sources say about this edition and its parent game.

## Overview

*Front Page Sports: Ski Racing '99* is the title under which MobyGames lists a single 1999 Windows release from Germany, developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]], with the German title *DSF Ski '99: Extreme Edition*.[^ref-1] The German magazine PC Joker reviewed it under that name in its February 1999 issue.[^ref-18]

The parent game, *[[1998 - Sierra Sports Skiing 1999 Edition|Sierra Sports: Skiing 1999 Edition]]*, reached North American stores on November 10, 1998, according to a Computer Games Strategy Plus news item posted that day.[^ref-22][^ref-21] The "99" in the name marks the edition, not the year of release. In the UK the game was sold as *Ski Racing Extreme Edition*: PC Zone's January 1999 issue called it "forthcoming",[^ref-29] and PC Gaming World's February 1999 review listed it as "Out Now".[^ref-28] It followed *[[1997 - Front Page Sports - Ski Racing|Front Page Sports: Ski Racing]]* (1997), which is a separate game with its own page. PC Joker's review index lists the two separately: "DSF Ski" (5/98, 33%) and "DSF Ski '99 Extreme Edition" (2/99, 60%).[^ref-31]

## Gameplay

MobyGames describes the game as simulating five skiing variants (Slalom, Giant Slalom, Super Giant Slalom, Downhill and Alpine Combination) on 20 square kilometers of open terrain or on six World Cup slopes: Garmisch, Aspen, Mt. Bachelor, Val-d'Isère, Vail and Whistler.[^ref-1] Weather conditions and their effects on ski behavior are simulated, and players can race for the best time or against others in multiplayer.[^ref-1] MobyGames lists first-person and behind-view perspectives.[^ref-1]

PC Joker's review of the German edition counts five race types and up to eight players taking turns at one computer. Players can set snow density and wind speed on each slope and choose between skis that handle noticeably differently. US Olympian Picabo Street leads the training.[^ref-18]

Sierra's own magazine, InterAction, said the edition was "taking everything from Ski Racing and going to the extreme". It named Picabo Street as "design team inspiration", supplying pro video tips, and promised six international resort runs, seven aerial moves and "over 5,000 acres of manicured slopes on two mountains".[^ref-30] The new freestyle ("Hot Dog") mode let players practise tricks off the race courses on two invented mountains, Mt. Dynamix and Mt. Sierra.[^ref-22][^ref-23][^ref-25] PC Gaming World's UK review saw it differently: it said the Dynamix freestyle mountain had been in the first version, so "the only new experience in this release is the Sierra mountain".[^ref-28]

The UK review also lists the six real resorts as Aspen, Vail, Mt Bachelor, Whistler, Garmisch and Val d'Isère. Weather can be set to clear, overcast or snowing, wind from calm to strong, and the surface from slush to hard-packed snow. The game supports Force Feedback joysticks.[^ref-28] Sierra's announcement listed single-player seasons and single races, plus multiplayer through WON.net.[^ref-22] PC Gaming World gave the player count as 1–8, through a shared PC, IPX LAN or the Internet.[^ref-28]

Sean Gleason, Sierra Sports' director of marketing, said at launch: "We recognize that skiers want to explore open slopes, perform tricks and use extreme moves that make the sport fun."[^ref-22]

## Reception

PC Joker (issue 2/99, page 105) found little to like beyond Picabo Street. In translation from the German, its reviewer called the graphics "not pretty" with a 3D card and "downright ugly" without one, and said the sound effects never quite fit what was happening. The magazine credited the well-made controls and the many setup options with keeping it from being a total failure. Because the genre had practically no competition at the time, it said *DSF Ski '99* "can, or must, even be recommended." It scored the game 60%.[^ref-18] The same score appears in PC Joker's own review index the following month and in Kultboy's index of German magazine reviews.[^ref-31][^ref-19]

The English-language reviews of the parent game were mostly negative, and most reviewers saw little change from the 1998 game. PC Gamer US (December 1998) gave it 43% and called it "essentially a patched version of last year's game".[^ref-25] PC Accelerator (January 1999) rated it 3 out of 10 under the subheading "Hey, wasn't this game called Skiing 98?"[^ref-26] IGN's Tal Blevins gave it 2.5 out of 10 ("painful"), writing: "I'd rather lose a toe to frostbite than play this game again."[^ref-24][^ref-27] For Computer Games Strategy Plus, Benjamin E. Sones wrote that the game "could well be the long-lost twin of its 1998 predecessor".[^ref-23] GameRankings lists that review at 0.5 out of 5, and gave a 34.10% aggregate from five reviews.[^ref-27] In the UK, PC Gaming World's Mike Allen said it "looks extremely dated" and "might be fun for competitive skiers with Force Feedback sticks, but it will be unsatisfying for most others."[^ref-28] Gamecenter's Gordon Goble was kinder and gave it 7/10, calling it a "satisfactory Sierra skiing experience, but nothing new." He judged it "little more than a rerelease of Ski Racing."[^ref-20]

## Technical Specifications

- **Media:** CD-ROM[^ref-1]
- **Platform:** Windows 95/98[^ref-18]
- **System requirements (PC Joker):** Pentium 166 (P200 recommended), 32 MB RAM, 80 MB of hard disk space[^ref-18]
- **Minimum requirements (English-language reviews):** Pentium 90, 16 MB RAM, quad-speed CD-ROM; PC Accelerator gives 75 MB of hard disk space. These are lower than PC Joker's figures.[^ref-23][^ref-25][^ref-26]
- **Price at launch (Germany):** about 69 DM[^ref-18]
- **Price at launch (UK):** £34.99[^ref-28]
- **Price (US, Sierra direct):** $29.95, product #70576, Windows 95 CD[^ref-30]
