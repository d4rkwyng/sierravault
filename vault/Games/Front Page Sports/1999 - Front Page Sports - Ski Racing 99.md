# Front Page Sports: Ski Racing '99

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

> [!note] German-edition stub
> This page covers the German release, **DSF Ski '99: Extreme Edition**. The game itself is documented in full at **[[1998 - Sierra Sports Skiing 1999 Edition|Sierra Sports: Skiing 1999 Edition]]**. Facts on this page are limited to what sources say about this edition and its parent game.

## Overview

*Front Page Sports: Ski Racing '99* is the title under which MobyGames lists a single 1999 Windows release from Germany, developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]], with the German title *DSF Ski '99: Extreme Edition*.[^ref-1][^ref-16][^ref-4] The German magazine PC Joker reviewed it under that name in its February 1999 issue.[^ref-18]

The parent game, *[[1998 - Sierra Sports Skiing 1999 Edition|Sierra Sports: Skiing 1999 Edition]]*, was released in North America on November 10, 1998, and Wikipedia gives its European title as *Ski Racing: Extreme Edition*.[^ref-21] It followed *[[1997 - Front Page Sports - Ski Racing|Front Page Sports: Ski Racing]]* (1997), which is a separate game with its own page.[^ref-21]

## Gameplay

MobyGames describes the game as simulating five skiing variants (Slalom, Giant Slalom, Super Giant Slalom, Downhill and Alpine Combination) on 20 square kilometers of open terrain or on six World Cup slopes: Garmisch, Aspen, Mt. Bachelor, Val-d'Isère, Vail and Whistler.[^ref-1] Weather conditions and their effects on ski behavior are simulated, and players can race for the best time or against others in multiplayer.[^ref-1] MobyGames lists first-person and behind-view perspectives.[^ref-1]

PC Joker's review of the German edition counts five race types and up to eight players taking turns at one computer. Players can set snow density and wind speed on each slope and choose between skis that handle noticeably differently. US Olympian Picabo Street leads the training.[^ref-18]

## Reception

PC Joker (issue 2/99, page 105) found little to like beyond Picabo Street. In translation from the German, its reviewer called the graphics "not pretty" with a 3D card and "downright ugly" without one, and said the sound effects never quite fit what was happening. The magazine credited the well-made controls and the many setup options with keeping it from being a total failure. Because the genre had practically no competition at the time, it said *DSF Ski '99* "can, or must, even be recommended."[^ref-18] Kultboy's index of German magazine reviews records PC Joker's score as 60%.[^ref-19]

The English-language reviews of the parent game were mostly negative. Wikipedia lists IGN at 2.5/10, PC Gamer US at 43%, Computer Games Strategy Plus at 0.5/5 and a GameRankings aggregate of 34%.[^ref-21] Gamecenter's Gordon Goble was kinder and gave it 7/10, calling it a "satisfactory Sierra skiing experience, but nothing new." He judged it "little more than a rerelease of Ski Racing."[^ref-20]
