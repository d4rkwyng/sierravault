# Front Page Sports Football Pro 98

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

Front Page Sports: Football Pro '98 is a comprehensive American football simulation game that puts players in both the coach's headset and the quarterback's cleats[^ref-1]. Developed by Synergistic Software and published by Sierra On-Line, the game shipped for Windows 95 on November 3, 1997[^ref-5][^ref-8][^ref-9]. Sierra put Miami Dolphins quarterback Dan Marino on the box[^ref-3]. According to Sierra's launch press release, the Front Page Sports: Football series had by then "sold well over half a million units worldwide"[^ref-9].

Sierra's own pitch was that "Physics-based ball dynamics, motion-captured movement and individual player AI deliver the most accurate gameplay results of any football simulation"[^ref-5]. The game launched at a street price of $29.95, with a $5 rebate for registered owners of Football Pro '97[^ref-9].

Despite the box tagline "Don't play with imitations. This is real football,"[^ref-6] the game received mixed reviews, and GameSpot concluded it had been "pushed out the door before it was ready"[^ref-16]. GameSpot's Stephen Poole summed it up: "FPS: FP '98 turns in a Marino-like performance: There are definitely some high points, but not as many as you'd expect from a seasoned veteran"[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

As a sports simulation game, Front Page Sports: Football Pro '98 does not feature a traditional narrative storyline. Instead, it retains the series' simulation features, letting players guide a team across seasons through drafts, trades, and free agency[^ref-8]. Players can take the role of commissioner, general manager, coach, or player[^ref-6].

The career mode follows a complete NFL-style season structure, with players managing rosters, executing trades, conducting drafts, and building dynasties over multiple seasons. The game's long-term appeal came from watching player development, making strategic personnel decisions, and adapting game plans to maximize team potential.

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The biggest enhancement in the '98 version was a completely revamped interface. GameSpot noted that "The interface has been totally revamped, and the good news is that it's much easier to use than before"[^ref-16][^ref-11].

The game supported joystick, gamepad, and keyboard input[^ref-6], though GameSpot complained that it "still supports only two gamepad/joystick buttons," where Madden NFL '98 supported four[^ref-16]. A new option let players view the action from 16 different angles, and the game included artwork for all 30 NFL stadiums[^ref-16]. Sierra also added a default camera management system meant to simulate NFL television coverage[^ref-9].

### Game Modes

Front Page Sports: Football Pro '98 featured two main playing modes[^ref-2]:

**Simulation Mode**: The player takes the seat of the team manager and deals with drafting, match lineups, and tactics[^ref-2]. This mode emphasized the front-office aspects of football, including:
- Player scouting and evaluation
- Contract negotiations and salary cap management
- Trade proposals and roster construction
- Practice priorities and player development

**Action Mode**: Direct control of on-field gameplay with motion-captured animations. Sierra's press release billed an "Enhanced Arcade Mode" for the release[^ref-9].

The career mode allowed players to manage multiple seasons, with players improving as they gained experience and slowing down as they aged[^ref-2]. Computer Games Magazine judged that "The career play mode is still the best in the sports game business"[^ref-13].

### Physics and Mechanics

The box promised a "Physics-based 3-D environment and ball dynamics" and "Motion-captured player movement"[^ref-6]. Sierra's marketing emphasized that "Physics-based ball dynamics, motion-captured movement and individual player AI deliver the most accurate gameplay results of any football simulation"[^ref-5].

Key gameplay features included:
- Full motion-captured 3D player animations
- Individual AI for each player on the field
- Comprehensive playbook customization
- Realistic injury and fatigue systems

Sierra said the release would "address both the hardcore player who owns a previous version of the game and the brand new sports customer"[^ref-9].

### Multiplayer Features

The game supported modem, network, and Internet play[^ref-6]. Computer Games Magazine listed two-player Internet play via WON.net, LAN, modem, and play-by-email (PBEM)[^ref-13]. GameSpot identified Internet play as one area Sierra did improve, including a better chat interface[^ref-16].

### Critical Analysis

GameSpot's Stephen Poole delivered the definitive critical assessment: "For gamers who revel in the challenges of play design, draft and trade decisions, game plans, and even practice-camp priorities, FPS: FP '98 is still the only game in town. Whether or not a slightly improved version of 'the only game in town' is worth $25 or $30, though, is up to you"[^ref-8].

This analysis captured the game's core appeal while acknowledging its limitations. Computer Games Magazine was harsher, noting that "The in-game 3D engine itself is unchanged" and calling it a "massively buggy game"[^ref-13].

Poole also noted irony in the marketing: "It's more than a little ironic that Sierra On-Line chose Dan Marino over Barry Sanders to appear on the box," since Sanders, who had graced the two previous boxes, had just had his greatest season while Marino had the most mediocre campaign of his career[^ref-16].

### Modern Assessment

MobyGames shows a 62% critics aggregate score[^ref-2]. The enthusiast community at GMGames.org rates the game 8.9/10[^ref-15].

Community members express continued devotion to the title. One GOG Dreamlist contributor wrote: "Unfortunately, this was the last year the game was released and after that we stopped playing because nobody wanted to play anything else"[^ref-18].

## Development

### Origins

Front Page Sports: Football Pro '98 was developed by Synergistic Software and published by Sierra On-Line[^ref-5][^ref-8]. According to Sierra, its new features included play-by-play commentary from CBS Radio's Howard David, a redesigned default camera management system, and a wizard to guide players through league creation and customization[^ref-9].

### Production

MobyGames lists 108 people (107 professional roles, 1 thanks) in the game's credits[^ref-2]. Key personnel included:

- **Executive Producer:** Michael D. Branham
- **Lead Programmer:** Cheryl A. Makovsky
- **Art Director/Lead Artist:** Jeremy Jones
- **Original Concept:** Patrick Cook
- **Additional Design:** D'Andre Pritchett, Will Furrer, Jim Edmunds, Mark Madland[^ref-2]

The box notes that "Every NFL team's playbook is designed by the NFL's Will Furrer"[^ref-6]. Play-by-play commentary was provided by CBS Radio's Howard David[^ref-6][^ref-2]. MobyGames credits sound effects to Craig Utterback and Christopher Barker and lists no composer[^ref-2]; KHInsider catalogues a five-file game rip of the soundtrack, credited to Synergistic Software, without naming a composer[^ref-21].

### Technical Challenges

GameSpot wrote that "In the few weeks since FPS: FP '98 started shipping, three patches have already appeared," and counted more than three dozen problems addressed in the 1.04f patch[^ref-16]. Sierra later released a second official update bringing the game to version 1.08[^ref-22]. One mod description noted the core problems: "Out of the box, the ratings for the players are out of wack as well as stock plays not working very well"[^ref-23].

### Technical Specifications

**System Requirements**[^ref-5][^ref-13]:
- Processor: Pentium 75 MHz
- RAM: 16 MB
- CD-ROM: 2X drive

MobyGames classifies the game's perspective as 3rd-person[^ref-2].

## Legacy

### End of the Front Page Sports Brand

Football Pro '98 was the last football game released under the Front Page Sports banner, but not the last in the series. Computer Games Magazine's review already mentioned "the looming threat of an all-new Football 99"[^ref-13]. Sierra retired the Front Page Sports logo for *NFL Football Pro '99*, which was released on December 22, 1998 and recalled on January 20, 1999 because of its many bugs[^ref-27]. Wikipedia describes *NFL Football Pro '99* as "the seventh and final version of the Front Page Sports Football franchise"; a planned 2000 version was cancelled after the recall[^ref-28].

The community still remembers the '98 edition fondly. One GOG fan noted: "after that we stopped playing because nobody wanted to play anything else"[^ref-18]. Years later, a Steam community member wrote of "the Original FPS Football 98": "Incredible after all these years we still have nothing that can come close"[^ref-26].

### Community Preservation

The game continues to be supported by dedicated fan communities who have created mods addressing the original release's shortcomings. The VPNFL99 mod was described as "the most comprehensive and authentic NFL simulation yet for FB Pro users"[^ref-23], demonstrating the ongoing dedication of the game's fanbase.

These community efforts:
- Corrected player ratings
- Fixed problematic stock plays
- Added roster updates
- Improved game balance
