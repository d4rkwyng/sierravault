# Front Page Sports: Ski Racing

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

**Front Page Sports: Ski Racing** is a 1997 skiing simulation game developed by Dynamix and published by Sierra On-Line[^ref-1][^ref-5] for Windows 95[^ref-8][^ref-26]. GameSpot lists its first release as November 30, 1997[^ref-10], while the Sierra Chest gives December 10, 1997[^ref-6]. The game simulates the four World Cup skiing variants: Slalom, Giant Slalom, Super Giant Slalom and Downhill, focusing on realistic gameplay rather than arcade-style action[^ref-2]. Sierra enlisted Olympic medalist Picabo Street to help with the game[^ref-13]; she is credited as "The Star" of its video production and appears in video tips throughout[^ref-25][^ref-7].

Unlike the snowboarding games popular on console systems at the time, Front Page Sports: Ski Racing was designed as "a racing simulation through and through"[^ref-4]. The game features six courses based on real-world locations: Whistler, Aspen Mountain, Vail, Mt. Bachelor, Val d'Isère and Garmisch[^ref-7]. Players can compete in single races, multiple events at one resort, or an entire season on all six mountains[^ref-3].

## Story Summary

Front Page Sports: Ski Racing does not have a traditional narrative. Its career element is the season mode, in which points are awarded for the most successful skier, and whoever has the most at the end of the season wins the Sierra Cup[^ref-5][^ref-23]. Picabo Street appears in training videos "explaining the best and easiest way to make it down the hill alive"[^ref-7]. Gamezilla's reviewers opened their review by marvelling at the courage it takes "to put two boards on your feet and rush down a mountain at speeds of up to 90 mph"[^ref-7].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game supports mouse input[^ref-26] and was announced with support for Microsoft's Sidewinder and Force Feedback joysticks[^ref-21]. Gamezilla's reviewers listed four camera views: a first-person camera, an over-the-shoulder view, a view from 20 feet above the skier's shoulder, and a "TV cam" that bounces between angles[^ref-7]. A VCR function lets players record, save and replay runs[^ref-7]. Arrows placed between the gates show the line to ski, and a small overhead view looks one or two gates ahead[^ref-7][^ref-23].

Gamezilla's reviewer first found it easier to turn the force-feedback effects off, but once used to the game felt "the bumps and the air beneath my skis"[^ref-7]. GameSpot judged an analog joystick the best, "if not the only practical," way to play[^ref-4].

### Structure and Progression

Players can select from five skiing events: slalom, giant slalom, Super G, downhill, and all-around[^ref-8]. The game simulates "the four World Cup skiing variants: Slalom, Giant Slalom, Super Giant Slalom and Downhill," with realistic gameplay in which "there are more maneuvers necessary then just steering the skis in the right direction"[^ref-2]. Difficulty is shaped by the weather and snow conditions[^ref-7], and the setup screen includes a Course Difficulty setting alongside temperature, skies, wind and snow[^ref-6].

Players create a male or female skier and pick name-brand skis, boots, clothing, helmets and sponsors[^ref-7]. The "Ski Now" option lets players ski all mountains and events, as well as "Train with Picabo" for audio pro tips while running a course, but none of the results or statistics are recorded[^ref-6]. Single Mountain events cover one or all of the four alpine events at a single resort, while Season play runs across all six resorts[^ref-6].

Multiplayer is hotseat only[^ref-13]. Gamezilla described a "Hotseat" challenge in which "you can get up to seven of your friends on the same computer"[^ref-7], while Computer Gaming World and Computer Games Magazine listed hotseat support for two players[^ref-23][^ref-13].

### Racing Mechanics

The skiing mechanics require precise control and practice. Beyond steering, the player must edge turns (button one) and prepare for jumps (button two) while staying tucked as much as possible[^ref-4]. GameSpot noted that it "takes a lot of practice to ski a slalom course without missing a single gate and post a respectable time"[^ref-4]. One Gamezilla reviewer admitted that "of all the games I've played recently, this one was the hardest"[^ref-7]. The publisher's product description on CNET Download calls it "a fast-paced, adrenaline-pumping downhill ski simulation"[^ref-3].

The game also includes tricks such as daffies, back-scratchers, helicopters and back flips, but GameSpot wrote that "their use is a mystery," since none of the events warrants them[^ref-4]. MobyGames similarly notes there is "no playing mode which utilizes them"[^ref-5].

|-------------|-------|-------|

| GameStar | 28% | German magazine; "Kaufen Sie sich lieber einen Skipass"[^ref-1][^ref-27] |
| Computer Gaming World | 2/5 | "Moderately fun arcade racing game"[^ref-1][^ref-23] |
| Computer Games Magazine | 2/5 | "Mildly entertaining game"[^ref-1][^ref-13] |

Critical reception was mixed. GameSpot's Tasos Kaiafas called it "the best there's ever been on the PC" but concluded that "though the graphics are impressive and the sound effects are inspiring, FrontPage Sports: Ski Racing falls a split-second short of a gold medal run"[^ref-4]. Gamezilla's reviewers gave it 75, noting that "of all the games I've played recently, this one was the hardest"[^ref-7].

Computer Gaming World's Loyd Case and Computer Games Magazine's Benjamin E. Sones each gave it 2 out of 5[^ref-1]. Case praised the Picabo Street videos and the background graphics but called the controls "terrible, especially in slalom"[^ref-23]. Sones criticised the physics model, the six-trail limit and the hotseat-only multiplayer, calling it "a mildly entertaining game"[^ref-13]. GameStar (28%) and PC Joker (33%) scored it lower still, while PC Player gave 62%[^ref-1][^ref-27].

### Modern Assessment

The game holds an aggregate critics score of 56% on MobyGames, based on 13 ratings[^ref-5]. User ratings are thin. GameFAQs users rate it "Outstanding" from 4 ratings[^ref-11], CNET Download shows 3.8/5 from 10 votes[^ref-3], and MyAbandonware shows 5/5 from a single vote[^ref-12].

## Development

### Origins

GameSpot announced Ski Racing in May 1997 as a Dynamix title being added to Sierra On-Line's Front Page Sports series, "more simulation than arcade" like the other FPS titles[^ref-21]. Six world-class ski courses were to be "measured and re-created" for the game. The announced list included Park City[^ref-21], but the shipped resorts were Whistler, Aspen Mountain, Vail, Mt. Bachelor, Val d'Isère and Garmisch[^ref-7][^ref-25]. The announcement also promised modem, LAN and Internet multiplayer and an October 1997 release[^ref-21].

Scott Youngblood designed the game and Mark Crowe directed it. Kate Kloos produced it, and Loudmouth Inc. supplied the sound effects and original music[^ref-25]. Picabo Street, whom Gamezilla called "host, guide and teacher"[^ref-7], is credited as "The Star" of the video production[^ref-25]. As Computer Games Magazine noted, skiing games were "something of a rarity on the PC"[^ref-13].

### Production

The game was built on what GameSpot called "Dynamix's new 3-D engine," allowing first-person or third-person racing[^ref-21]. GameSpot's review said the team "had grander plans for this game," and speculated: "Probably due to the typical rush job of getting this game out for Christmas, a lot of what would have made this game complete was left on the cutting-room floor"[^ref-4]. GameSpot named the missing pieces as an arcade mode, "skiing aids" and a course editor[^ref-4]. The promised network play did not ship either. Computer Games Magazine reported hotseat-only multiplayer, with "a patch providing network, Internet, and modem play" promised for later[^ref-13].

Computer Games Magazine's preview said Ski Racing was headed to shelves "just in time for the Winter Olympics," with 3Dfx and force-feedback support[^ref-22].

### Technical Achievements

Gamezilla's reviewers said that, if you can get it to work on your system, it is "graphically one of the best sports sims available"[^ref-7], and GameSpot described the graphics as "impressive" and the sound effects as "inspiring"[^ref-4]. Critics noted weak spots too: GameSpot pointed to flat, texture-mapped trees with "some nasty clipping"[^ref-4], and Computer Gaming World found the skier figure "pretty polygon-riddled"[^ref-23].

The game was announced with support for Rendition and 3Dfx cards[^ref-21], and Computer Gaming World's spec box listed both[^ref-23]. Gamezilla reported that the advertised Rendition and Voodoo Rush support was missing at launch. Sierra's support team said a patch was in the works but would not comment on its contents or timing[^ref-7]. According to MobyGames, two later patches stated that "Sierra Screamin' 3D (Rendition V1000) support is not included in this update," and Rendition cards were never supported[^ref-5].

Gamezilla listed the system requirements as a Pentium 90, 16 MB RAM, 4X CD-ROM drive, a Windows-compatible sound card, 640x480 in 256 colors, and 75 MB of hard drive space plus 50 MB free after installation[^ref-7][^ref-26].

## Legacy

The game was later repackaged by publisher Sierra Sports as simply *Ski Racing*, and it was followed by *Skiing, 1999 Edition* ([[1999 - Front Page Sports - Ski Racing 99]])[^ref-1][^ref-24].

The game filled a niche in PC gaming, as "skiing games are something of a rarity on the PC"[^ref-13]. It is preserved on the Internet Archive[^ref-2] and catalogued in databases such as IGDB[^ref-18].
