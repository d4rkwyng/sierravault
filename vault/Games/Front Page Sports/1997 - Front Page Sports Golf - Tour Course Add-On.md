# Front Page Sports: Golf – Add-on Courses

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Front Page Sports: Golf add-on courses are three separate 18-hole course disks for Sierra On-Line's 1997 golf simulation [[1997 - Front Page Sports - Golf|Front Page Sports: Golf]]: Coeur d'Alene, Black Diamond Ranch and Pasatiempo Golf Club[^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20][^ref-25]. Each is listed by MobyGames as a 1997 Windows add-on developed by Headgate Studios and published by Sierra On-Line[^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20]. Each requires the base game[^ref-23][^ref-30][^ref-31]. Sierra's own catalogues called them "Add-On Course" products[^ref-30][^ref-31]; MobyGames titles them "Front Page Sports: Golf - Add on Course: ..." (for example *Black Diamond Ranch*)[^ref-18], and the Sierra Chest and the Dynamix wiki use "[Course] Add-on Course"[^ref-25][^ref-26][^ref-27][^ref-28].

The base game, developed by Headgate Studios and built around its mouse-driven TrueSwing control, shipped with two courses, the Pete Dye Golf Club in West Virginia and the Prince Course in Kauai, Hawaii[^ref-1][^ref-16]. The add-on disks gave owners further real-world courses to play on that engine; full coverage of the base game, its reviews and its credits is on the [[1997 - Front Page Sports - Golf]] page.

Sierra gave the first add-on, Coeur d'Alene, away free with early copies of the base game[^ref-16]. Computer Gaming World's September 1997 review reported that "So far, add-on courses include Coeur d'Alene and Black Diamond Ranch"[^ref-17], and Sierra announced Pasatiempo for December 1997[^ref-29]. A fourth course, Sahalee, was later offered as a free 45 MB download tied to the 1998 PGA Championship[^ref-23][^ref-24].

## Story Summary

The add-ons have no story. Each disk adds a single real-world 18-hole course to Front Page Sports: Golf[^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20]. Sierra's spring 1997 InterAction feature on the base game presented the courses as places to visit: "Experience the vicarious thrill of teeing off at a world-renowned resort from the comfort of your own computer."[^ref-33]

## The Courses

Each add-on adds one 18-hole course to Front Page Sports: Golf; the Sierra Chest describes each disk as "one of the three add-on courses for Front Page Sports: Golf"[^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20][^ref-26][^ref-27][^ref-28].

### Coeur d'Alene

MobyGames describes this disk as adding "an additional 18 holes of golf featuring Idaho's Coeur D' Alene golf course"[^ref-19]. Sierra's course page called the Coeur d'Alene Resort Golf Course "America's most beautiful resort golf course" and gave it as a 6,309-yard, par-71 layout on the shore of Lake Coeur d'Alene. Its signature feature is what Sierra called "the planet's only floating, moveable green". Sierra credited the design to Scott Miller and gave its opening year as 1991[^ref-21].

Before the base game shipped, Sierra promised "a free add-on course disk with each copy of Front Page Sports: Golf" while supplies lasted. The bonus course would be "the Coeur d'Alene Resort in Coeur d'Alene, ID". Sierra warned that "We only have a few thousand of these add-on disks"; once they ran out, the next batch would sell separately "for about 25 bucks each"[^ref-16]. Sierra's own advertising in the same CGW issue told players they would "tee up" on the Prince Course, the Pete Dye Golf Club, and "the course at Coeur d'Alene"[^ref-17]. The same spring 1997 InterAction feature called it "the immediately available add-on course, Coeur d'Alene Resort Golf Course in Idaho"[^ref-33]. The disk shipped with the base game, which Sierra had planned for late April 1997[^ref-16]; Online Gaming Review's news for 16 May 1997 reported that "Sierra On-Line has released Front Page Sports: Golf"[^ref-37]. That summer Sierra's catalogue offered the base game at $54.95 with "the beautiful Coeur d'Alene Resort Course Add-On (a $24.95 value) for no extra charge" for a limited time[^ref-34].

### Black Diamond Ranch

MobyGames describes this disk as adding "an additional 18 holes of golf featuring Florida's Black Diamond Ranch golf course"[^ref-18]. Sierra's course page explains that Black Diamond Ranch offers 27 holes, and that the add-on reproduces designer Tom Fazio's "famed 18-hole 'Quarry' course". Sierra described it as a 7,159-yard, par-72 course opened in 1987 and "carved out of a limestone quarry", with "five challenging quarry holes, 13 through 17"[^ref-22]. In spring 1997 Sierra described it as the "soon-to-be-released Black Diamond Ranch in Lecanto, Florida"[^ref-33]. By summer 1997 it was out: InterAction listed "83610 Black Diamond Ranch Add-On — for FPS: Golf (Win 95) $24.95" under New Releases and said "The Black Diamond Ranch add-on course is now available with other courses on the way."[^ref-34] A fall 1997 Sierra advertisement billed it as Florida's top-rated course and said "Five dramatic signature holes hug the rim of two retired quarries."[^ref-35] Sierra's later catalogues pitched it as "Tom Fazio's famed 'Quarry' course in this 18-hole add-on for Front Page Sports: Golf"[^ref-29][^ref-30].

### Pasatiempo Golf Club

MobyGames describes this disk as adding "an additional 18 holes of golf featuring California's Pasatiempo Golf Club"[^ref-20]. Sierra's Holiday 1997 InterAction told readers to "Watch for the next FPS: Golf add-on course, Pasatiempo. Coming in December."[^ref-29] By spring 1998, Sierra's catalogue described the disk as "the most realistic representation of the challenging golf found on California's rugged Monterey Peninsula", listing it as #69980 at $19.95; the summer 1998 catalogue repeated the entry at the same price[^ref-30][^ref-31]. MobyGames dates it to 1997[^ref-20].

### Sahalee (free download)

Sahalee was not a retail disk. Sierra's golf site offered it as a "Free Add-On Course", sahalee.exe, a 45 MB file that "requires the full version of Front Page Sports: Golf"[^ref-23]. Sierra's course page tied it to the 1998 PGA Championship: "Here's your chance to play Sahalee, the very course of the August, 1998 major PGA event"[^ref-24]. The same page calls the required base game "Golf Pro", the name Sierra was then selling it under[^ref-24][^ref-31].

## Gameplay

The add-ons supply new course data only. Every add-on states that it requires the base game[^ref-23][^ref-30][^ref-31].

### Interface and Controls

The disks add no new controls. Play uses the base game's TrueSwing mouse swing and its twelve types of play[^ref-1][^ref-16].

### Structure and Progression

Each disk adds one 18-hole course to the base game's course list; there is no campaign or progression of its own[^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20]. In 1998 the courses also hosted Sierra's organised online events (see below)[^ref-31].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

There are no puzzles. The challenge each disk adds is the course itself: Sierra singled out Coeur d'Alene's floating, moveable green and the five quarry holes, 13 through 17, at Black Diamond Ranch[^ref-21][^ref-22].

### Online Play

The add-on courses were used in Sierra's organized online play. Sierra's summer 1998 InterAction announced the Sierra Sports Golf Association's "inaugural season on the World Opponent Network". Its event schedule put the opening events on Coeur d'Alene and a June tournament on Black Diamond Ranch, and the season ended with an "SGA Championship, Live at Sahalee"[^ref-31]. Players who signed up would be "entered to win a trip to this year's PGA Championship at Sahalee"[^ref-31][^ref-24].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

No dedicated contemporary review of any of the three add-on disks has been located. Computer Gaming World's September 1997 review of the base game mentioned the add-ons only in passing ("So far, add-on courses include Coeur d'Alene and Black Diamond Ranch")[^ref-17]. MobyGames shows no Moby Score for any of the three disks[^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20]. Reviews of the base game are summarised on the [[1997 - Front Page Sports - Golf]] page.

### Modern Assessment

No modern retrospective of the add-on disks has been located. A collector writing in 2021 said he could not "find any info on any of the add on courses online"[^ref-3].

## Development

### Origins

Headgate Studios was founded by Vance Cook, who left Access Software in 1992 to form it, and was based in Salt Lake City. According to Sierra's own 1998 profile of the studio, Headgate developed TrueSwing for its golf engine, and Sierra acquired the company in 1996[^ref-39].

### Production

The add-ons were developed by Headgate Studios, the developer of the base game, and published by Sierra On-Line[^ref-1][^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20]. No team credits specific to the add-on disks are listed on MobyGames or the Sierra Chest[^ref-18][^ref-19][^ref-20][^ref-26][^ref-27][^ref-28], and the Sierra Chest lists each disk's engine as "Unknown/unnamed"[^ref-26][^ref-27][^ref-28].

### Technical Achievements

Before the base game shipped, Cook told InterAction that the courses in most other golf games "come in files of around 12MB", whereas "Files average 70MB for each of our courses…they're just full of accurate details."[^ref-33] The free Sahalee course was a 45 MB download[^ref-23].

### Disc Labelling

In 2021, a collector on the Vintage Computer Federation forums reported how the discs are labelled. The base game is marked "Vol.1 no.1", the Coeur d'Alene disk "Vol.1 no.3" and the Black Diamond Ranch disk "Vol.1 no.4". He then guessed that the Pasatiempo add-on was "no.2" ("I guess that's my 'no.2'")[^ref-3]. That Pasatiempo numbering is unconfirmed.

## Legacy

Sierra's catalogues and the Dynamix wiki list three retail add-on courses for Front Page Sports: Golf, and Sierra's download page added the free Sahalee course[^ref-25][^ref-23][^ref-30][^ref-31]. During 1998 Sierra marketed the base game as "Sierra Sports Golf 1.0" and "Golf Pro '98", and kept the add-on courses in its catalogue alongside it[^ref-30][^ref-31]. In fall 1998 Sierra bundled Golf Pro '98 with the Black Diamond Ranch and Pasatiempo add-ons, and announced that its next golf game, PGA Championship Golf 1999 Edition, would include Sahalee, "site of the 80th PGA Championship"[^ref-36]. A 2001 Sierra Sports course page for "PGA Golf -- Championship Edition" still offered course videos for Black Diamond Ranch, "Couer d'Alene Resort Golf Course", Pasatiempo and Sahalee alongside the Prince Course and the Pete Dye Golf Club[^ref-38]. A collector writing in 2021 said he had found almost no information on the add-ons online[^ref-3].
