# Front Page Sports Football Pro '97

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Front Page Sports Football Pro '97 is a professional American football simulation game developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] in late 1996 for Windows 95.[^ref-1][^ref-4] OGR's news page reported on December 2, 1996 that Sierra "has released" it, so it was on sale by early December.[^ref-29] The fifth version of the Front Page Sports Football series, it was the first released for Windows 95.[^ref-3] Its career mode, in which players aged and retired, was repeatedly singled out by period sources: Computer Gaming World's December 1996 preview promised "its stupendous career mode," and PC Games said the "Career play option is still unmatched."[^ref-30][^ref-32] As GameSpot reviewer Kevin Mical noted, the game was "more than just a run-of-the-mill football game for the PC."[^ref-4]

The game combined football management and on-field play: "You draft the players, you design the plays, and you make the calls on game day."[^ref-4] Sierra's box copy announced: "The best-selling football game on CD-ROM now sports modem and network play, allowing you to challenge friends head-to-head in a gridiron battle."[^ref-11] The same copy promoted "individual player Artificial Intelligence and motion-captured movements."[^ref-11]

## Story Summary

Front Page Sports Football Pro '97 does not feature a traditional narrative story, as it is a sports simulation game focused on managing and playing professional American football. Players can act as commissioner, general manager, coach, or individual player, managing everything from drafting players to designing plays and coaching games.[^ref-14][^ref-4]

Sierra's marketing promised that "Every pass, every run, every bone-shattering tackle looks, moves and feels authentic."[^ref-11] The game included every NFL team and every NFL player under licenses from the NFL and Players Inc.[^ref-14][^ref-26]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Football Pro '97 was a native Windows 95 product, and Computer Gaming World found the transition "seamless".[^ref-23] The move brought a new 32-bit play editor using pull-down menus, a Quick Plan feature that generates game plans from the number of play types chosen, and player "sets" that substitute a whole group of players in one action.[^ref-23][^ref-14] A new Play Wizard walks coaches through formation, pre-snap logic, pass routes, holes and blocking assignments.[^ref-23]

The game ran at 640x480, and each player's animation now used 16 angles of rotation rather than eight.[^ref-4] Computer Games Strategy Plus's October 1996 preview described it as "more of a Windows conversion than a complete rewrite," with jersey numbers and animation angles "up to 16 from eight" the most obvious improvements.[^ref-34] Sierra's FAQ called it "the first year we have had 16 angles", with an option to drop back to 8 angles and 11 kHz sound on slower machines.[^ref-27] Sierra also acknowledged that the joystick input routines had been rewritten for the Windows version and could feel different from '96.[^ref-27]

### Structure and Progression

The game offers both coaching and action modes of play.[^ref-4][^ref-23] Its career mode was a standout feature; Sierra billed it as "The only football sim to offer career play. Watch players mature, get injured, improve their skills and maybe even make the Hall of Fame."[^ref-26]

**Team Management Features:**
- 10,000 plays included, plus the Play Wizard for designing new ones[^ref-1]
- Custom leagues of 8 to 30 teams[^ref-1]
- Draft with real teams or a common draft pool; improved trading[^ref-4]
- Multiplayer trades of up to three players per side[^ref-23]
- Player aging and career play[^ref-26]
- League update tools to convert leagues from earlier versions of the series[^ref-28]

### Multiplayer Capabilities

Modem, network and Internet play was the edition's headline addition, for one or two players.[^ref-23][^ref-26] LAN play supported both IPX and TCP.[^ref-27]
- At launch, multiplayer was limited to coaching mode (and, per Computer Gaming World, exhibition games).[^ref-23][^ref-4]
- Sierra's FAQ explained that Internet latency made on-field calls such as audibles and time-outs impossible to keep in sync between machines.[^ref-27]
- GameSpot reported that a free upgrade from Sierra removed the coaching-only limitation.[^ref-4]

### Contemporary Reviews

Computer Gaming World's Dennis McCauley concluded, "it's still the best football sim on the market," while noting bugs in the initial version.[^ref-23] GameSpot's Mical summed it up as "good…though far from perfect."[^ref-4] TheComputerShow summarized PC Gamer's Jason Bates as finding that bugs, screen clutter, slow animation and multiplayer problems made it a downgrade from the DOS versions.[^ref-7][^ref-14] Next Generation was the harshest, calling it "three or four year old DOS code clumsily retrofitted into a Windows 95 product."[^ref-24]

Computer Gaming World's short review in its February 1997 issue judged it "far beyond the mere graphic upgrade of last year's version" and concluded, "It's a winner."[^ref-35] Computer Games Strategy Plus's March 1997 review was more critical: it said Football Pro '97 had "all of the hallmarks of a rushed release; fatal crash bugs, weird database glitches," yet remained "the most ambitious and feature-rich football game on the market" and "still the best all-around football game on the market."[^ref-33] PC Games' Christopher Tou warned that the game was "very buggy right out of the box" but called its gameplay controls "superb."[^ref-32]

### Modern Assessment

On MobyGames, the game holds a 79% critic average from five reviews.[^ref-1] Users on MyAbandonware rate it 3.5 out of 5 from two votes.[^ref-16] In Computer Gaming World's monthly poll of its subscribers, the game scored 8.25 in the April 1997 issue and 7.73 in the May 1997 issue.[^ref-36][^ref-37] A Hartford Courant chart of top-selling football games from January to October 1997 ranked Front Page Sports: Football '97 third, behind Madden NFL 97 and John Madden Limited Edition.[^ref-40]

Sierra's own product page listed the series' Computer Gaming World Sports Game of the Year awards for 1993, 1994 and 1995.[^ref-26] Computer Gaming World's December 1996 preview likewise called it the latest version of the "three-peat CGW Sports Game of the Year award ('93-'95)."[^ref-30] Computer Gaming World's review said the previous year's edition "had little to commend it" over the 1995 winner apart from improved graphics.[^ref-23]

## Development

### Origins

The series' original concept is credited to [[Patrick Cook]], and Computer Gaming World linked the series' focus on graphics to his earlier work on *TV Sports Football* for Cinemaware.[^ref-20][^ref-23] For '97, [[Scott Youngblood]] is credited with direction and design.[^ref-20][^ref-23] MobyGames also lists [[Doug Johnson]] and Scott Gilbert for Additional Design.[^ref-20] Sierra's own InterAction magazine (Holiday 1996) called Cook and Youngblood "Front Page Sports Developers."[^ref-31] Computer Gaming World judged that "the move to Windows 95 consumed the bulk of Sierra's efforts."[^ref-23]

### Production

MobyGames' Windows credits list 106 people.[^ref-20] Key roles include producer Kate Kloos, project manager Michael D. Branham, lead programmer Cheryl A. Makovsky, and art director Jay Dee Alley.[^ref-20]

**Music:** Sound and music are credited to Christopher Stevens and Timothy Steven Clarke.[^ref-20]

**Other credits:** MobyGames credits Football Statistics to STATS Inc., the manual to Kevin Lamb, the on-line manual to Linda Westerfield, and video production to James Carey and David Aughenbaugh.[^ref-20]

### Technical Achievements

TheComputerShow's review says Football Pro '97 was built on an updated Red Baron game engine with a Camera Angle Management System (CAMS).[^ref-14] It finally added player numbers to the jerseys.[^ref-14][^ref-23] Computer Gaming World's minimum requirements were a 486DX2/66 (P90 or better recommended), 8 MB RAM (16 MB recommended) and 30-100 MB of hard disk space.[^ref-23]

The game shipped with bugs. TheComputerShow reported that gameplans imported from the 1996 version could crash the game after the first kickoff, and that Sierra posted a patch on its website.[^ref-14] Computer Gaming World listed awards given in the wrong conference, category leaders based on career rather than season stats, black rain, and unrealistically frequent blocked kicks in advanced action mode.[^ref-23] PC Games' Tou likewise reported a crash after the kickoff with an imported '96 gameplan and about four blocked kicks per game, and said a patch was available on Sierra's Web site.[^ref-32] A Computer Gaming World patch roundup in April 1997 listed a Football Pro '97 patch that fixed a "Ready-Break!" button problem and added digitized sound support for AdLib and Pro Audio Spectrum cards.[^ref-36] The Sierra Help Pages archive several Football Pro '97 updates.[^ref-25]

## Legacy

Reviewers repeatedly singled out the career mode, in which players aged and retired; Computer Games Strategy Plus wrote that "the game's strength has always been the career mode,"[^ref-33] and Sierra billed Football Pro '97 as the "only football sim to offer career play".[^ref-26]

### Cover Athletes

The '97 edition featured running back Barry Sanders on the cover; Sierra's InterAction magazine described the game as the football sim "that carries Barry's picture on the cover,"[^ref-31] while the subsequent Pro '98 featured Dan Marino.[^ref-3]

### Series Decline

The series continued with Football Pro '98 and the rebranded Sierra Sports NFL Football Pro '99, but Sierra recalled the 1999 version in January 1999, offering refunds, and a planned 2000 version was cancelled.[^ref-38][^ref-2] In its 2008 article "Ten Sports Games That Need To Be Remade," IGN called the 1995 through 1997 editions the highlight of the series, "undoubtedly the best-looking and best-playing football game on the marketplace."[^ref-22]

### Collections

The game was included in *Front Page Sports: All Pro Collection - Collection Series* (Windows, 1997), which MobyGames lists as a collection of previously released games: Football Pro '97 alongside Baseball Pro '96, Golf, Trophy Bass and You Don't Know Jack: Sports.[^ref-39]
