# Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 4

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 4 is a bass fishing simulation developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by Sierra Sports for Windows. Sierra Sports announced on May 25, 2000 that the game had shipped[^ref-23][^ref-1][^ref-2]. The fourth game in the Trophy Bass series[^ref-1], it followed Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 3D and its Lake Expansion Pack with 25 tournament lakes, a new Team Play mode, saved tackle setups, customizable anglers and boats, and a multimedia "How-To" section with tips from professional anglers[^ref-23][^ref-8]. The game includes more than 30 species of game fish[^ref-8][^ref-4], and its tackle box offers thousands of rod, reel and lure combinations[^ref-5][^ref-23].

GameSpot's Stephen Poole awarded the game 8.2/10[^ref-8], while IGN gave it 8/10[^ref-5]. GameRankings listed a 74% average from 10 reviews[^ref-29]. The ESRB rated it E for Everyone[^ref-16][^ref-17].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Trophy Bass 4 renders its lakes in 3D, with graphics locked at 640x480[^ref-24][^ref-8]. Players can fish from the boat camera behind the angler, a new free-floating camera that can be zoomed and turned in any direction, a first-person view, or the "LureCam", which follows the lure underwater[^ref-8][^ref-25]. A mouse-and-keyboard control scheme handles casting, the depth finder, boat driving and reeling[^ref-5]; Eurogamer described reeling with the mouse while setting line tension and reel strength from the keyboard[^ref-28]. The only force-feedback controller the game supports is the Microsoft SideWinder Force Feedback Pro, which lets players feel a hooked fish fighting the line[^ref-8][^ref-25][^ref-24].

Built-in multimedia pro tips give pointers on where to find bass and how to make them strike[^ref-8]. Players can also customize the appearance of their angler and boat[^ref-23][^ref-8].

### Structure and Progression

The 25 lakes are the 22 from Trophy Bass 3D and its Lake Expansion Pack plus three new ones in the South and Southeast: Kentucky Lake (with Lake Barkley), Lake Marion in South Carolina and Toledo Bend in Louisiana[^ref-8]. Single fishing trips let players set temperature, rain, water conditions and wind[^ref-8]. Five difficulty levels range from instant strikes to a demanding search for fish[^ref-8][^ref-26]. Tournaments come in standard, elimination, "holes" and team-play formats, and can be fished in Eastern, Western or Central divisions; Career mode strings tournaments together and tracks cumulative earnings[^ref-8][^ref-24]. Up to 10 custom tackle setups can be saved for instant access[^ref-23][^ref-26].

Multiplayer runs over the Internet (through WON.net), modem and LAN[^ref-24][^ref-8][^ref-28]. Sources disagree on the player count: Computer Games Strategy Plus lists 1–8 players[^ref-24], while Eurogamer says up to six[^ref-28].

### Fishing Mechanics

Fish can be found with a fish finder that shows their location and depth, or by spotting their shadows in the water, an aid that can be made harder in the options[^ref-24]. Strategy Plus noted that Sierra had tweaked the strike model so that real techniques, such as teasing a fish with small twitches, work in the game[^ref-24]. Once a fish is hooked, an on-screen indicator shows line tension; giving line when the fish runs and reeling when it tires works as it does in real life, but the line breaks easily without the feel of a real rod[^ref-24].

Stephen Poole's GameSpot review praised the three new lakes, the angler and tackle customization and the pro tips, and concluded: "If you're in the market for a new fishing simulation, then Trophy Bass 4 is the game of choice." His main complaint was the 640x480 resolution limit[^ref-8]. Gordon Goble of CNET Gamecenter called it "a moderately enhanced version of an already good thing", but regretted that only one force-feedback joystick was supported[^ref-25]. GameZone's Michael Lafferty praised its "solid options package, solid sound and graphics, and thoughtful game play", but summed up: "Despite improvements over previous Sierra incarnations, this is the same program."[^ref-26] Jeff Lackey of Strategy Plus found the improvements "relatively minor", but called the game "a good, firm, and solid choice" for fans of the genre[^ref-24].

PC Zone's Martin Korda was the outlier. He scored it 42% and wrote that "Trophy Bass 4 isn't fun, although it's probably not aiming to appeal to the usual gamer", while allowing that avid fishermen might enjoy its equipment and features[^ref-27].

### Modern Assessment

On Amazon the game averages 3.8 out of 5 stars from customer ratings[^ref-3]. MyAbandonware users rate it 4.62/5 from 13 votes[^ref-9], GamePressure users rate it 7.8/10[^ref-4], and GameFAQs users rate it "Good" from 9 ratings[^ref-18].

## Development

### Origins and Team

Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 4 was developed by Dynamix and published by Sierra[^ref-2][^ref-24]. MobyGames' credits list Susan Folden as executive producer, Steven D. Letsom as director, Richard Rayl as technical director, Kurt Weber as designer and Damon Mitchell as art director. Randy Dersham and Michael Waite are credited with the original concept[^ref-22]. Four bass fishing pros appear in the credits: Denny Brauer, Larry Nixon, Kevin VanDam and Penny Berryman[^ref-22].

### Technical Details

The game runs at 640x480[^ref-8][^ref-24] and supports 3dfx hardware acceleration as well as software rendering[^ref-26]. PC Zone and Strategy Plus listed a Pentium 166 MHz and 32 MB of RAM as the minimum[^ref-27][^ref-24]. CNET Gamecenter specified a Pentium 166 with a 3D accelerator card, or a Pentium 200 without one[^ref-25]. Online play ran through the free WON.net service[^ref-25][^ref-8].

### Audio and Presentation

The music is credited to Christopher Stevens (www.fabmusic.com), and the sound effects to Christopher Stevens and Timothy Steven Clarke of Score! Music & Sound Design[^ref-22]. Gordon Goble called the soundtrack "just peachy", singling out its birds, frogs, jumping fish and whirring reels[^ref-25].

## Legacy

Trophy Bass 4 followed Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 3D (1999) in a series that began with the original Trophy Bass in 1995[^ref-2][^ref-11]. The Trophy Bass name returned in 2006 with Bass Pro Shops: Trophy Bass 2007 for Windows and Xbox, developed by Big John Games and published by Vivendi Games[^ref-30]. Gaming historian Jimmy Maher has noted that the original Trophy Bass "sold many more copies from the outdoor sections of Middle American Wal-Mart superstores than it did from computer and gaming stores", and called it Dynamix's most profitable game[^ref-6].

The game is preserved by MyAbandonware[^ref-9], and the Internet Archive holds Sierra's playable demo, which covers part of Lake Marion[^ref-31]. Sierra Chest catalogues the game's packaging and memorabilia[^ref-15].
