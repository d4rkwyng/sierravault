# Front Page Sports Trophy Bass 2

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

*Front Page Sports: Trophy Bass 2* is a bass fishing simulation developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] for Windows 3.1 and Windows 95, first released on September 30, 1996.[^ref-1][^ref-29] It was the sequel to Sierra's *Trophy Bass* and the newest addition to the [[Front Page Sports Series|Front Page Sports]] line, which Sierra's own *InterAction* magazine billed as a big expansion year for the label alongside *Front Page Sports: Golf*.[^ref-2][^ref-12]

Sierra's box copy called it "the most addictive fishing simulation ever."[^ref-3] The sequel brought the lake count to ten by adding five new tournament lakes, and its headline feature was head-to-head play by modem, LAN or the Internet, with Sierra promoting "on-line tournaments against as many as 100 anglers."[^ref-23][^ref-30] *Computer Gaming World* gave it four stars out of five and said it "further refines what's come to be the best fishing simulation available."[^ref-27]

Offline the game is single-player; MobyGames lists 2–8 players for online play.[^ref-12] Over 100 video tips from pro anglers Larry Nixon, Denny Brauer, Kevin VanDam and Penny Berryman were built in as a tutorial.[^ref-24][^ref-30]

## Story Summary

*Front Page Sports: Trophy Bass 2* is a fishing simulation with no narrative. The player is an angler who can take a single fishing trip, enter three-day tournaments, or pursue a career of tournaments that leads to a year-end final with cash prizes.[^ref-9] *InterAction* described the career path as competing in the *Trophy Bass Classic* tournaments "in hopes of one day fishing in the World Series of professional bass fishing."[^ref-23]

AllGame reviewer Steve Honeywell summed up the appeal: "It's all of the good stuff about fishing: catching fish and lying about the ones that slip the hook or snap the line without all of the nasty parts."[^ref-9]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

*Trophy Bass 2* shows the fishing from an overhead view and simulates reeling and reel drag for different classes of fish.[^ref-7][^ref-12][^ref-14] Sierra merged the lure, rod and cast windows into a single "tackle box" screen and offered over 200 lures.[^ref-30] *InterAction* listed 15 rod-and-reel combinations and a number of casting styles.[^ref-24] Players can cast manually, or place a cursor on the lake and let the computer cast for them.[^ref-28]

**Control Support:**[^ref-8]
- Keyboard
- Mouse
- Analog joystick (Windows 95 version only, per Sierra's technical notes)[^ref-32]

User-submitted tips on IGN's cheats wiki describe the landing technique: "After hooking into a fish, all you have to do to reel it in is: 1)Set your reel speed to 5. 2)Set your Drag to 3. 3)As you reel, keep pushing the mouse or joystick up and down or in circles."[^ref-11]

### Game Modes

**Fishing Trip:**
A single outing in which any species counts. AllGame noted that the 30-pound catfish you can catch here do not count in tournaments.[^ref-9] The same IGN user tips describe a "Quick Fish" option on the Fishing Trip screen that drops the player at Bull Shoals Lake.[^ref-11]

**Tournament:**
Tournaments are three-day events. Each day the angler brings in the five largest bass caught, and the total is ranked against the other anglers.[^ref-9]

**Career:**
A series of tournaments. Players who place well consistently qualify for a year-end tournament with large cash awards.[^ref-9] Tournament and career games track prize money as well as points.[^ref-30]

**Multiplayer:**[^ref-8][^ref-12]
- Internet, LAN and modem play (Windows 95 only)
- MobyGames lists 1 offline player and 2–8 online players
- Competitive tournaments against the clock, or relaxed "Cooperative" trips where players fish and chat[^ref-33]
- *InterAction* promoted Internet tournaments "against as many as 100 anglers", with the host choosing the length, the lake and the start time, plus pre-tournament chat[^ref-23]

Internet play ran through the Sierra Internet Gaming System (SIGS).[^ref-28] MobyGames credits a separate SIGS team alongside the game's multiplayer programmers.[^ref-34]

### Fishing Mechanics

**Bait and Lure Selection:**
Product copy describes video help files from top professionals with hints on "finding fish, which bait to use, and how to bring them in."[^ref-6] CGW counted hundreds of lure types and configurations, plus rod sizes, line weights and casting styles.[^ref-27]

**Location Finding:**
A fish radar helps players find good fishing spots.[^ref-12]

**Realism Settings:**
Sierra added a Custom Skill Level screen to fine-tune realism.[^ref-30] CGW described four online skill levels (novice, intermediate, advanced and guru), and noted that players could tweak nine fishing variables by hand if the three base skill levels were not challenging enough.[^ref-27]

### Realism Features

- **Fish AI:** *InterAction* wrote that "Realism is enhanced with second generation fish AI, true-to-life lake re-creations, and interactive lure interfaces."[^ref-23] Sierra's site promised "tougher realism, choosier fish, and snags (by request!)."[^ref-30]
- **Real Lakes:** Ten lakes, all actual tournament sites. The five new to TB2 are Bull Shoals, Lake Mead, Castaic Lake, Lake Kissimmee and Lake Eufaula, and trout were added at Lake Mead.[^ref-23][^ref-30]
- **Conditions:** CGW listed weather, water temperature, the season and time of day as factors in tackle choice.[^ref-26]

### Tutorial System

The how-to section includes over 100 full-motion video tips from bass pros Larry Nixon, Denny Brauer, Kevin VanDam and Penny Berryman.[^ref-24][^ref-30] *InterAction* noted that these are among the pros the player fishes against in Tournament and Career modes.[^ref-24]

### Critical Assessment

American reviewers mostly liked the game. In *Computer Gaming World*, Scott May wrote that "If you liked the original, you are going to love what they have done here," and praised the 10 accurately rendered lakes, the improved fish AI and the online tournaments.[^ref-27] His main complaint was the fixed overhead view: the angler stays on screen even on a long cast, which "gives the impression of fishing in a barrel." He also doubted the AI changes, writing that "you'd have to be a bona fide fishhead to notice."[^ref-27]

GameSpot's Tal Blevins found that "not much has changed in the sequel," apart from ten lakes instead of five and the new multiplayer. He still called it "by far the best bass fishing simulation on the market" for newcomers.[^ref-28] AllGame's Steve Honeywell, who admitted he had not been fishing since the mid-1970s, wrote that "this game is a blast to play" and singled out the tournaments and the pro videos.[^ref-9]

German magazines were harsher. *Power Play* (49%) wrote that the game offered far too few real innovations over the first game, and *PC Games* scored it 44%.[^ref-15]

### User Reception

MyAbandonware users rate it 4.44/5 from 9 votes.[^ref-4]

## Development

### Origins

*Trophy Bass 2* was developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]].[^ref-1] According to CGW, the first *Trophy Bass* (1995) had surprised Sierra's own marketing staff by remaining the company's top-selling title for more than 12 months, and the sequel followed under the growing *Front Page Sports* label.[^ref-27] *InterAction* framed the sequel around "the intense rivalry of true bass tournament competition."[^ref-23]

It joined a line whose flagship, *[[1996 - Front Page Sports - Football Pro '97|Front Page Sports: Football Pro]]*, was described in Sierra's own magazine and advertising as a three-time winner of *Computer Gaming World*'s "Sports Game of the Year" award.[^ref-25][^ref-26] For *Trophy Bass 2*, Sierra said it had "called on a whole team of pro bass anglers" and signed four pros to collaborate on the design.[^ref-2][^ref-24]

- **Music:** Loudmouth Productions (Loudmouth, Inc. in MobyGames' release data)[^ref-38]
- **Sound Effects:** Ken Rogers
- **Voice Recording:** Full Moon Studio

### Technical Notes

Sierra Chest lists the game's engine as unknown/unnamed.[^ref-1] According to Sierra's 1996 technical notes, the Windows 3.1 version needed Win32s 1.25 or later and WinG. The Windows 95 version needed DirectX 2.0 or later, and only it supported multiplayer.[^ref-32][^ref-8] Saved trips, tournaments and careers from the original *Trophy Bass* carried over.[^ref-30]

### Marketing and Promotion

Sierra ran a "Trophy Bass 2 Sweepstakes" whose grand prize was a three-day fishing trip on any of "the ten lakes featured in Trophy Bass 2," guided by a Pinnacle Pro Team angler.[^ref-24] *InterAction* also announced that Sierra planned to host "the first-ever virtual bass tournament" online in October 1996.[^ref-24] The game sold for $54.95 direct, and owners of the first game could upgrade for $29.95.[^ref-23][^ref-30]

The Northern Lakes expansion adds Lake Winnebago (Wisconsin), Lake Oneida (New York), Mille Lacs (Minnesota), Lake St. Clair (Michigan/Ontario) and Dale Hollow (Tennessee). Its new species are monster pike, sauger, rock bass, yellow perch, musky and tiger musky.[^ref-35]

## Legacy

### Series Continuation

The *Trophy Bass* series continued with *Field & Stream: Trophy Bass 3D* (1999).[^ref-12] MobyGames shows that much of the TB2 team carried over: 34 people credited on *Trophy Bass 2* were also credited on *Trophy Bass 3D*, and 32 on *Trophy Bass 4*.[^ref-34]

### Preservation

The game is not sold on GOG or Steam. It has a GOG Dreamlist entry, and the Windows and Windows 3.x versions are archived on MyAbandonware.[^ref-7][^ref-4] A 2022 user comment there reports that on Windows 7 and later the game "plays just way too fast."[^ref-4]
