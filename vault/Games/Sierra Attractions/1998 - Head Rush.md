# Head Rush

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Head Rush, also known as *You Don't Know Jack HeadRush*, is a trivia video game released on August 31, 1998 as a teen-oriented spin-off of the *You Don't Know Jack* series[^ref-1][^ref-14]. Developed by [[Berkeley Systems]] and Jellyvision, the game was published by Sierra On-Line under its Sierra Attractions label[^ref-2][^ref-4]. The game targeted a younger audience than its predecessors, and its box copy billed it as "The Twisted Trivia Game That Will Make You Pee in Your Pants!"[^ref-3]

The game keeps the basic format of the *You Don't Know Jack* series, the major difference being a shift from sexual humor to toilet humor and a change in art style to cartoons[^ref-4]. Head Rush came with a set of three stickers for players to place on their Q, P, and B keys, the keys used to buzz in and answer a question. What was on the stickers was simply the letters Q, P, and B[^ref-22].

The game features entirely new graphics and new questions while greatly resembling its predecessor, *You Don't Know Jack: The Ride*[^ref-6]. One to three players compete on the same screen[^ref-4]. The box warned that the product "contains immature content, loud body noises, a smattering of mildly saucy language and references to music, tv shows and movies that will definitely not be suitable for most geezers"[^ref-7].

## Story Summary

Head Rush does not have a traditional story, as it is structured as a trivia game show. As in a game show, a moderator and host named Bob gives the players their instructions and asks the questions[^ref-7]. Bob always explains why an answer is correct or incorrect[^ref-7].

The game's personality comes through its presentation rather than a plot. The WAP Journal's reviewer described the pace as "frantic enough" with "enough stupid jokes and wisecracking that it is entertaining"[^ref-7].

The box copy made clear that the content was aimed at teens and was not meant for older players[^ref-7]. Many questions dealt with the TV and movie culture of the 1990s[^ref-7], and the 2013 Steam release describes its content as "trivia about the teen-y topics of the day"[^ref-10].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Head Rush is played with the keyboard[^ref-4]. One player buzzes in with the B key, two players use P and Q, and three players use all three keys[^ref-7]. The box included stickers for these keys[^ref-22]. The game supports one to three players on the same screen[^ref-4].

At the start of each game, players are assigned colors and each chooses one of six icons to represent them on screen[^ref-6][^ref-7].

### Structure and Progression

Each round consists of eleven questions[^ref-6][^ref-7]. Questions are worth approximately ten times as much in Head Rush as in *You Don't Know Jack*[^ref-6].

The WAP Journal review lists the game's question types[^ref-7]:

- **Multiple choice:** The most common type of question
- **DisorDat™:** Players decide whether each item in a list of seven is one thing, another, or both
- **Fill in the blank**
- **HeadButt:** A timed word equation where two words combine to make another word or a phrase; the quicker the answer, the more money it earns
- **Trash Talkin' with Milan:** Questions about English grammar
- **Old Man's Moldy Memories:** A series of clues; players buzz in once they work out what the Old Man is trying to remember
- **HeadRush:** The final question of every game. Words and phrases move across the screen, and players buzz in when they see a matching pair

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Players compete for in-game money. In the final HeadRush question, a correct match earns $5,000 and a wrong one subtracts $5,000[^ref-7].

Once each round, a player who is stuck on a question can force an opponent to answer it, a move called "Bite Your Neighbor™". If the opponent gets it wrong, they lose money and the first player gets a shot at the question. If they get it right, they win the money and the first player loses the same amount[^ref-7].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

MobyGames records a critic average of 76% for the game[^ref-4][^ref-5]. In the January/February 2001 issue of the Washington Apple Pi Journal, reviewer Brian Mason gave the game six slices out of a pie of eight. He found the questions fairly simple apart from those on 1990s TV and movie culture, and said the game was entertaining[^ref-7].

In a 2001 player review on MobyGames, Adam Baratz was more critical: "Head Rush on the other hand assumes that teenagers have the maturity and intelligence of someone in Kindergarten. The humor takes a big shift towards the low-brow toilet end of the humor spectrum, assuming that teens think that gross-out humor is the name of the game"[^ref-5].

### Modern Assessment

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 76% (Critics Average)[^ref-4]
- **My Abandonware:** 5/5 (2 votes)[^ref-9]

Jackbox Games, Inc. re-released the game on Steam on November 5, 2013, as *YOU DON'T KNOW JACK HEADRUSH*[^ref-10]. Jackbox Games is the former Jellyvision Games, which rebranded in 2013[^ref-18]. My Abandonware no longer hosts the game because it is commercially available again[^ref-9].

## Development

### Origins

Berkeley Systems was best known for After Dark, a modular screensaver that included flying toasters. Its best-selling product was the trivia game *You Don't Know Jack*, developed by Jellyvision[^ref-19]. Berkeley Systems was acquired by the Sierra On-Line division of CUC International in 1997[^ref-19]. Head Rush, credited to both Berkeley Systems and Jellyvision[^ref-4], was a spin-off of the *You Don't Know Jack* series aimed exclusively at a teenage audience[^ref-1].

The game kept the series' basic format but used new graphics and questions[^ref-4][^ref-6].

### Production

Jellyvision was a Chicago-based studio[^ref-18]. MobyGames' credits list Michelle Gorchow as director and Andy Poland as co-director and host[^ref-21].

**Development Credits:**[^ref-21]
- **Director:** Michelle Gorchow
- **Co-Director / Host:** Andy Poland
- **Producers:** Christopher York, Michelle Gorchow
- **Executive Producer:** Terry Hackett
- **Technical Director:** Nathan Bamberger
- **Lead Writers:** Lena Chern and others
- **Contributing Writers:** Michelle Gorchow, Terry Hackett, Steve Heinrich, Shannon Miller, Andy Poland, Phil Ridarelli
- **Music:** Rob Warren (Sky Productions)

IMDb lists Gorchow and Poland as directors and Lina Chern (spelled "Lena" in the MobyGames credits), Terry Hackett and Steve Heinrich as writers[^ref-1].

The CD also contains three audio tracks that can be played in any audio CD player: "Lose Your Mind" by Motorbaby, "Don't Shake My World" by Swirl 360, and "The Whammy" by 2 Skinnee J's. These tracks cannot be played from within the game[^ref-7].

Sierra On-Line published the game under its Sierra Attractions label[^ref-2].

### Technical Achievements

Compared to the main *You Don't Know Jack* games, Head Rush changed its art style to cartoons[^ref-4]. Baratz's review also mentions interstitial cartoons between questions[^ref-5].

### Technical Issues

One retailer lists the original release as for 32-bit Windows systems and Mac OS 9 or below[^ref-6]. The 2013 Steam release supports Windows XP through Windows 8 and runs at a maximum resolution of 640x480. It is Windows-only[^ref-10].

Jackbox Games' own shop sells the game in a bundle with eight other *You Don't Know Jack* games. It cannot issue Steam codes to customers outside the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, the European Union, and Brazil[^ref-11].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

By the time of the WAP Journal review in early 2001, the game was no longer in Sierra On-Line's catalog, though it could still be found for sale online[^ref-7].

A Head Rush demo with a short set of sample questions was included on several other products[^ref-20]:
- After Dark Midnight Collection (PC/MAC CD-ROM)
- Hoyle Card Games 3 (1999)
- Hoyle Casino 3 (1998)
- Hoyle Word Games (1999)
- You Don't Know Jack: Volume 4: The Ride

Since November 5, 2013 the game has been sold on its own on Steam, for $2.99 at the time of writing[^ref-10]. It is also available in a bundle with eight other *You Don't Know Jack* games[^ref-11].

### Collections

The Sierra Chest hosts the playable demo, which contains a short set of questions of different types[^ref-20]. My Abandonware keeps a catalog entry but no longer offers the game, since it is commercially available[^ref-9].

### Critical Perspective

Head Rush adapted the *You Don't Know Jack* format for teenagers by swapping sexual humor for toilet humor[^ref-4]. Opinions differed. The WAP Journal's reviewer found it entertaining[^ref-7]. Baratz thought it insulted the intelligence of its teenage audience[^ref-5].

One of the game's two developers was already part of Sierra: Berkeley Systems had been acquired by the Sierra On-Line division of CUC International in 1997[^ref-19].

The Steam store pitches the game to players who were teens in the late 1990s, as a collection of "trivia about the teen-y topics of the day"[^ref-10].
