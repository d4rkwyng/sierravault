# Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science is an educational puzzle game developed by [[Jeff Tunnell Productions]] and released by [[Sierra On-Line]] in 1992 as part of the Sierra Discovery Series.[^ref-1][^ref-3] The box describes it as an educational science game for kids ages 9-14,[^ref-3] and its questions cover topics such as energy, chemistry, magnetism, and air and water pressure in a quiz-race format.[^ref-2][^ref-12] With its tagline "Look out! You're Surrounded By Science!!", the game aimed to make learning science entertaining through colorful characters and competitive gameplay.[^ref-3]

The game features two space "elfs" named Quarky (green) and Quaysoo (yellow) O'Ghandi, who, according to *Compute!*, "hail from New Delhi, India."[^ref-2][^ref-24] As members of the team Los Turbos, players compete against rival teams in a science race run against the clock: the opponents keep moving while the player researches and answers questions.[^ref-7][^ref-24] The objective is to win a series of races and climb the ranks, progressing from "Squid" to "Turbo Science Champ" by correctly answering science questions to earn money and advance through checkpoints.[^ref-6]

Old-Games.com called it "a pleasant surprise by master puzzler Jeff Tunnell" and "'edutainment' at its best."[^ref-7] The game included over 2,300 questions and experiments, along with a printed Research Guide that the box describes as a 150-page reference book.[^ref-3] *Compute!* called it "a delightful game in Sierra On-Line's Discovery series."[^ref-24]

## Story Summary

In the world of Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science, the title characters are space elves: MobyGames describes "the indian-originated space elf Quarky O'Ghandi and his sister Quaysoo," depicted in green and yellow respectively.[^ref-2][^ref-6] The siblings have organized a racing team called Los Turbos to compete in the Turbo Science Championship, an intergalactic competition that will once and for all resolve the dispute over the title of the best scientist in the universe.[^ref-8]

Los Turbos faces three rival teams: Das Liquidators, Cool City Maulers, and Valiant Cubed (spelled "Valiant Cube" in some descriptions).[^ref-5][^ref-12] Valiant Cubed is the reigning champion Odessa King.[^ref-10][^ref-24] Each race winds across a map of themed stops, such as a construction site for simple machines and a Clown College for chemistry, where players answer questions about what they see.[^ref-2][^ref-10]

At the end of each race, the television newscaster Paco de Suave interviews the winners or the losers.[^ref-10][^ref-24] (*Compute!* calls him "Paco Suave, a Turbonet newscaster".) The Research Guide uses comic characters to teach its topics, among them Sheik Oil Slick for energy and Hector Skullface for electromagnetism and static electricity.[^ref-2][^ref-25] Old-Games.com's reviewer felt the game's "really funny setting and story" made up "for the rather dry questioning style."[^ref-7]

As players progress through the championship, they earn money to buy faster transport between checkpoints, aiming to beat all three rival teams and claim the title of Turbo Science Champ.[^ref-6][^ref-12]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science uses a mouse-driven point-and-click interface, aimed at children ages 9-14.[^ref-3][^ref-6] It runs on the Dynamix Game Development System (DGDS) engine, with the "Dynamix bottom bar" icon interface also seen in later Sierra adventures.[^ref-7][^ref-11] An Eye icon gives pop-up information on objects, and a Tools icon opens a toolbox with a voltage meter, sound meter, tape measure, beaker and scale for measuring things on screen.[^ref-24]

Some questions are multiple choice, while others ask the player to click one of several objects that the game highlights in the scene.[^ref-6][^ref-3] For harder questions, the Research icon tells players which page of the printed Research Guide to read.[^ref-13][^ref-24] Sources give the guide's length differently: the box says 150 pages, *Compute!* says 145 and *PC Magazine* says 152.[^ref-3][^ref-24][^ref-25] *PC Magazine* noted that there is no penalty for using the guide and that the clock stops while kids measure and weigh objects.[^ref-25]

### Structure and Progression

Turbo Science is at heart a quiz-race game that combines educational content with competitive progression.[^ref-12] Players choose to play as either Quarky or Quaysoo at the start. The two play identically, and the speedrun guide's author believes they are male and female options "for inclusivity reasons."[^ref-12]

A full game is 21 races against three opponents: five against the easy opponent, Das Liquidators, seven against Cool City Maulers, and nine against the hard opponent, Valiant Cubed.[^ref-12] Losing a race means replaying it, and the game ends after 21 victories.[^ref-12] Difficulty is adjusted in two ways: players choose easy or hard questions, and the opposing teams get faster the more races the player wins.[^ref-7][^ref-25] At higher ranks the race route also passes through more locations.[^ref-24]

Players earn money by answering questions correctly: $700 for standard correct answers and $1,500 for bonus questions, while incorrect answers result in a $200 penalty.[^ref-12] The maximum money a player can accumulate is $9,999.[^ref-12][^ref-24] This money buys faster transport between checkpoints.

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core mechanic involves answering science questions to earn money for moving between checkpoints in each race.[^ref-5] Questions come in two difficulty levels and cover topics such as simple machines, chemistry, optics, energy, magnetism, and air and water pressure.[^ref-2][^ref-6] The game contains over 2,300 questions and experiments.[^ref-3]

Players can choose from five different transportation methods, each with varying prices and speeds.[^ref-5][^ref-9] Players do not have to answer questions at every checkpoint as long as they have money to continue, and earning more money early can decide the race.[^ref-6] Each stop has its own scientific theme, and players answer questions about that subject.[^ref-4]

The game's approach combines several educational strategies:

- **Questioning:** Multiple-choice and click-the-object science questions test knowledge[^ref-6]
- **Measurement:** In-game tools let players measure weight, volume, voltage, sound and more[^ref-24]
- **Reference Integration:** The printed Research Guide provides answers and explanations[^ref-24]
- **Adjustable Difficulty:** Easy or hard questions, and opponents that get faster as the player advances[^ref-7]
- **Reward Systems:** Monetary incentives encourage correct answers[^ref-12]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Upon release, Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science was reviewed positively in the computer press. David Sears reviewed it for *Compute!* in October 1993 and called it "a delightful game in Sierra On-Line's Discovery series." He wrote that it "manages to integrate scientific investigations, spirited competition, and some hilarious cartoon humor," and that "the research guide stands up fine on its own."[^ref-24]

In *PC Magazine*'s June 15, 1993 roundup of science software, Peter Scisco described a "frenetic, science-based race game for kids 9 to 14 years old." He praised the Research Guide as "straightforward, accurate, and amazingly thorough" and added that "this guide should be a textbook."[^ref-25] He also had reservations. Conservative parents might dislike the game's "frantic pace, blaring rock track, and slang-heavy text." The guide's section on famous scientists "lists no women," and the game lacks a two-player mode.[^ref-25]

The Oppenheim Toy Portfolio Guide Book, by Joanne and Stephanie Oppenheim (1993), described the game as a set of "fun science problems" focused on energy, chemistry, magnetism, and air and water pressure, and judged it best suited to children aged 9 and older.[^ref-2]

Old-Games.com's review called it "a pleasant surprise by master puzzler Jeff Tunnell" and "'edutainment' at its best."[^ref-7] It also noted that "Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science has a really funny setting and story which make up for the rather dry questioning style."[^ref-7]

A MobyGames user review is titled "Who knew that science could be so much fun?"[^ref-6]

### Modern Assessment

Modern retrospectives are generally positive. The Collection Chamber called the concept "exceptional" and the game "Highly recommended."[^ref-3] On MyAbandonware, user Pearlite commented that the game is "quirky, unexpected, and has a lot to explore."[^ref-13] The site's aggregate rating is 5/5 from 16 user votes.[^ref-13]

## Development

### Origins

Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science was made by [[Jeff Tunnell Productions]], a studio Jeff Tunnell founded in 1992. The studio is best known for The Incredible Machine series, and Tunnell later rejoined Dynamix.[^ref-16] The game belongs to the Sierra Discovery Series, a short-lived line of children's titles that Sierra published from 1991 to 1993.[^ref-17]

The box says the game was "created with the assistance of an award-winning team of educators."[^ref-3] The manual credits Sam Miller (PhD), Richard C. Adams and Marilyn Husser as educational consultants.[^ref-26]

### Production

The game was developed using the Dynamix Game Development System (DGDS).[^ref-11] Production involved creating over 2,300 questions and experiments covering multiple scientific disciplines.[^ref-3] The team also produced the printed Turbo Research Guide that came with the game. The box calls it 150 pages, while *Compute!* and *PC Magazine* gave 145 and 152 pages respectively.[^ref-3][^ref-24][^ref-25] Ken Embery edited the guide.[^ref-26]

**Development Credits:**[^ref-26]
- **Director / Designer:** Jeffrey Tunnell
- **Assistant Director / Writer:** Ken Embery
- **Senior Software Engineer:** Richard Rayl
- **Art Director:** Brian Hahn
- **Music:** Christopher Stevens, Timothy Steven Clarke, Ken Rogers, Robert Caracol
- **Educational Consultants (manual):** Sam Miller (PhD), Richard C. Adams, Marilyn Husser

### Technical Achievements

GamePressure notes the game's "colorful VGA graphics in low resolution, friendly characters and a friendly user interface."[^ref-8] A MobyGames user review compares its art style to [[1991 - The Adventures of Willy Beamish|The Adventures of Willy Beamish]], an earlier game Tunnell helped create at Dynamix.[^ref-6] *Compute!* singled out the digitized samples: "A flurry of grunts, mild insults, and random chatter from the cartoonish space elves adds considerably to the fun."[^ref-24]

With more than 2,300 questions, Old-Games.com noted, the game "can go on for hours before it becomes repetitive."[^ref-3][^ref-7]

### Technical Specifications

**DOS Version (as listed by *Compute!*, 1993):**[^ref-24]
- **Processor:** 16 MHz 80386 or faster
- **RAM:** 640K
- **Graphics:** 256-color VGA
- **Storage:** Hard drive with 3MB free (*PC Magazine* listed 8MB of hard disk space and DOS 3.0 or later)[^ref-25]
- **Media:** 3.5" high-density floppy disk
- **Input:** Mouse

MobyGames and PCGamingWiki both list an Intel i386 as the minimum CPU and 640 KB of RAM.[^ref-6][^ref-11] GamePressure lists a 286 instead.[^ref-8]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

The game features several notable design choices and quirky elements:

- **Character Origins:** MobyGames describes Quarky and Quaysoo O'Ghandi as "indian-originated" space elves.[^ref-6] *Compute!* says they "hail from New Delhi, India."[^ref-24]
- **Team Names:** The rival teams are Das Liquidators, Cool City Maulers, and Valiant Cubed.[^ref-12]
- **Character Selection:** Players can choose to race as either Quarky or Quaysoo.[^ref-12]
- **Educational Characters:** The Research Guide's Sheik Oil Slick teaches energy-related concepts, in keeping with the game's pun-based character names.[^ref-2]
- **Post-Race Interviews:** Paco de Suave, a TV newscaster, interviews the winners or losers after each race.[^ref-10][^ref-24]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Sales figures for Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science have not been published. The game retailed for $49.95.[^ref-24][^ref-25] It was part of Sierra's Discovery Series of educational software, which ran from 1991 to 1993.[^ref-17]

The game came with its Research Guide, a printed reference book, along with a manual.[^ref-3]

### Collections

Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science was released as part of the Sierra Discovery Series alongside other educational titles.[^ref-3] A MobyGames user review places it in Jeff Tunnell Productions' "short-lived Turbo Learning series," alongside Turbo Learning: Mega Math.[^ref-6][^ref-16]

The game is not sold on GOG, where it remains a wishlist request.[^ref-15]

### Fan Projects

The game maintains a presence in the retro gaming community primarily through preservation efforts. Multiple abandonware sites host the game for download.[^ref-1] The Internet Archive hosts a version that can be played in the browser.[^ref-5]

Speedrun.com tracks the game and hosts a beginner's guide with route and strategy tips.[^ref-12]

### Related Publications

- **Turbo Science Research Guide:** A printed reference book included with the game. Its length is given as 145 to 152 pages depending on the source, and it covers topics such as machines, chemistry, optics, and energy.[^ref-2][^ref-3][^ref-24][^ref-25]
- **Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science: Research Guide:** Listed on Amazon as a standalone book (ASIN: B000I1AV7S), with an item weight of 8 ounces[^ref-19]

### Critical Perspective

Quarky & Quaysoo's Turbo Science dates from the early 1990s, when Sierra was publishing a whole line of educational software. It combines quiz-based learning with a racing framework and comic characters, the "edutainment" idea of making learning feel like play.[^ref-7][^ref-17]

Old-Games.com's reviewer said it "shows off Jeff's versatility as an astute game designer."[^ref-7] Tunnell remains best known for The Incredible Machine series.[^ref-8][^ref-16]

Period reviewers praised the game and especially its Research Guide.[^ref-24][^ref-25] *PC Magazine* did criticise the guide's all-male list of famous scientists, along with the game's frantic pace.[^ref-25] The game is still hosted on abandonware sites and has a speedrunning community.[^ref-1][^ref-12]
