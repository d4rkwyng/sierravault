# Bouncers

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

Bouncers is an unconventional arcade basketball game developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by Sega of America exclusively for the Sega CD in December 1994.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-21] The game features a unique twist on basketball where players control the ball itself rather than a human athlete, bouncing off opponents to score goals.[^ref-1][^ref-3] Sega's promotional text for the game led with "Slam-Dunk Your Head" and told players: "Now you get to play basketball as the ball."[^ref-4]

Released exclusively in North America, Bouncers combines sports gameplay with fighting game mechanics in a slapstick atmosphere that Sega-16 compared to games like Earthworm Jim or ClayFighter.[^ref-3][^ref-5][^ref-21] The game features a voice cast of Mark Hamill (Star Wars), Michael Bell, and John Kassir.[^ref-2][^ref-6][^ref-22] Hardcore Gaming 101 observed that "Even for the Sega CD, a system with no shortage of strange, inscrutable games, the existence of Bouncers is nothing short of perplexing."[^ref-7]

## Story Summary

Bouncers features eight playable basketball characters (Ugh, Fu, Tank, Zap, Chip, Dash, Gog and Spike), each with a short CG-rendered introductory cartoon.[^ref-21] Sega-16 likened the cartoons' style to "classic 1960s space age cartoons".[^ref-3] The game takes place across fantasy courts ranging from a downtown city court to a tropical island where hoops pass through monkeys' arms, a haunted graveyard with skeleton rib cages as goals, an undersea shipwreck, and a low-gravity moon.[^ref-1][^ref-21]

The cartoons, written and animated by John Garvin, can be viewed from the main menu; Sega-16's reviewer called them "probably the better part of the game."[^ref-3][^ref-22] Garvin later recalled that "At Dynamix I had written the short cartoons for Bouncers."[^ref-25]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames classes Bouncers as a side-view game;[^ref-1] each court is one screen wide with no scrolling.[^ref-21] The game uses a two-button control scheme: A for jump and B for attack.[^ref-3] Players attempt to bounce off opponents to score or attack them to prevent scoring.[^ref-3] Sega's promotional text explained that players "can't jump high enough to reach them on your own. You have to bounce off your opponent or use the optional power-ups to score."[^ref-4]

The game options offer three physics settings: Arcade, Standard and Expert, ranging from unrealistic physics with a high degree of control to more realistic physics with no midair control.[^ref-21] Hardcore Gaming 101 described the premise as "each player controls an anthropomorphic cartoon basketball whose goal is to thrust their whole body into one of two or three basketball hoops around the stage," and judged that "the reality is that the game is practically unplayable," because Dynamix gave the characters realistic physics: slow acceleration into "a slippery run," and almost no control once airborne.[^ref-7]

### Structure and Progression

There are two modes: Challenge, a single match on any court, and Tournament, a best-of-five series on random courts.[^ref-21] Matches are split into four quarters; the options set quarter length to Short (1 minute) or Long (2 minutes).[^ref-21] Most baskets are worth two points, and a basket from a jump at mid-court or further is worth three.[^ref-21] All characters play identically, though different characters bring different difficulty levels when played by the AI.[^ref-7][^ref-21]

The game supports one to four players, using a Team Player or 4 Way Play adapter.[^ref-21] (MobyGames lists 1–2 players.[^ref-1])

**Gameplay Mechanics:**
- Score by bouncing off opponents and launching into hoops[^ref-1]
- Two or three hoops per court, depending on the arena[^ref-3][^ref-21]
- Power-ups including spring shoes for higher jumps, wing shoes for flying, track shoes, sticky shoes, a parachute and "Jam" slam dunks[^ref-3][^ref-21]
- Power-downs like lead boots that limit jump height[^ref-3][^ref-21]
- Bonus balls worth points, with more awarded for collecting matching colours[^ref-3][^ref-21]
- Players can kick opponents into the air[^ref-1]

Hardcore Gaming 101 saw the point-giving pickups as "less like a boon for the player, however, and more like a tacit admission from the developers that they released a broken game, so unwinnable by design they had to add some sort of concession."[^ref-7]
The game carries an ESRB rating of "Kids to Adults".[^ref-1]

Sega Retro lists seven courts, each with stage-specific gimmicks;[^ref-21] eBay's catalog description also says seven,[^ref-19] while Sega-16's review says eight.[^ref-3]

Hardcore Gaming 101 noted that many stages "have a hand-drawn quality to them similar to the backdrops in Dynamix adventure games such as The Adventures of Willy Beamish or Space Quest V: The Next Mutation."[^ref-7]

Doug Jackson's Sega-16 retrospective (May 2013) described the game as "shallow and rushed," criticizing its limited content while acknowledging the amusing intro cartoons for each character.[^ref-3] He called it "a mishmash of halfhearted ideas" and wrote that "the programmers at Dynamix came up with an idea that sounded pretty good for the most part but unfortunately, they never actually developed it into anything more than a rush job of a dull game."[^ref-3] His verdict was harsh: "Bouncers is just another game that ended up in discount bins real early on and makes the Sega CD look bad. It's a rush job and unneeded filler that never should've been released in the first place."[^ref-3] (The same review text also appears on GameFAQs under the name vgc2000.[^ref-11])

Used copies still trade on the collector market.[^ref-20] One reader commenting on the Sega-16 review disagreed with its score, finding that "playing with a friend/multiplayer really adds to the quality of gameplay."[^ref-3]

## Development

### Origins

Bouncers was developed by Dynamix, a Sierra subsidiary known for titles like The Incredible Machine and Betrayal at Krondor.[^ref-15] The game's conceptual origins can be traced back to earlier work by designers Rhett Anderson and Dave Hensley, who had previously created Basketball Sam & Ed, a one-on-one sports game, as a type-in program for Compute!'s Gazette magazine in July 1987.[^ref-2]

In 1988, Anderson and Hensley wrote the similar Arcade Volleyball for the Commodore 64, which Randy Thompson and Tim Midkiff subsequently ported to the Amiga.[^ref-2]

John Garvin, who created the game's art and cartoon cutscenes, described his career progression in an interview: "Then in 1990, I got a job as pixel jockey at Dynamix, working on graphic adventures like The Adventures of Willy Beamish, before going on to write and direct my own games like Bouncers for Sega CD."[^ref-9] Garvin later became lead designer and creative director on the Syphon Filter series, and wrote and directed Days Gone (2019).[^ref-2][^ref-9]

### Production

The development team was led by executive producer [[Jeff Tunnell]], a Dynamix co-founder,[^ref-15] with Randy Thompson serving as producer.[^ref-1][^ref-22] The original design was by Rhett Anderson and Dave Hensley; the Sega design credit went to Anderson, John Garvin, Randy Thompson and Tim Midkiff, and Anderson was credited as lead designer.[^ref-22] Thompson, Anderson and Midkiff programmed the game, and Thompson also wrote the manual.[^ref-22] Garvin was art director, handled art and animation, and wrote and animated the character cartoons.[^ref-22] Writing in 2012, he described his Dynamix experience as "art directing games like Sega CD's Bouncers."[^ref-24]

**Voice Cast:**[^ref-6][^ref-22]
| Actor | Characters |
|-------|------------|
| Mark Hamill | Ugh, Chip, Dash, Old Man |
| John Kassir | Snake, Little Kid, Fishes, Jake, Gog, Spike |
| Michael Bell | Fu, Master Fo, Tank, Zap, Mick |

Voice direction was handled by Ginny McSwain, with Sher Alltucker as talent coordinator.[^ref-1][^ref-22]

Music and sound effects are credited to Christopher Stevens, Timothy Steven Clarke, Jan Paul Moorhead, and Ken Rogers, with Joe Weber on guitar.[^ref-1][^ref-12][^ref-22] Stevens and Clarke left Dynamix shortly before the game's release to establish LoudMouth, Inc., a game music production company.[^ref-2] Tracks from Bouncers were included on the group's 1995 album *Get Loud! vol. 1*, which Stevens and Clarke produced and which was recorded and mastered at LoudMouth Studios in Springfield, Oregon.[^ref-2][^ref-23] (That album credits Bill Barrett for "Voice Characters"; he does not appear in the game's own credits.[^ref-22][^ref-23])

## Legacy

### Series Context

Bouncers was a standalone title. MobyGames and Sega Retro list no sequel or other-platform release.[^ref-1][^ref-21] It combined sports and fighting game mechanics in an unconventional package.[^ref-3][^ref-7]

### Notable Alumni

The game is perhaps most notable today for the career trajectories of its development team:

- **John Garvin** went from creating Bouncers' art and cutscenes to becoming lead designer and creative director on the Syphon Filter series, and later wrote and directed Days Gone (2019).[^ref-2][^ref-9][^ref-24]
- **Christopher Stevens and Timothy Clarke** founded LoudMouth, Inc.; their 1995 album *Get Loud! vol. 1* also drew on Dynamix and Sierra titles such as The Incredible Machine 2 and Football Pro '95.[^ref-2][^ref-23]
- **Randy Thompson** produced Bouncers, co-designed and co-programmed it, and wrote its manual.[^ref-22]

### Connection to Sierra

As a Dynamix production, Bouncers is part of Sierra's extended family of games. Dynamix was acquired by [[Sierra On-Line]] in 1990 and continued operating as a subsidiary until 2001.[^ref-15] The Sega-16 review notably credits Sierra as the developer, highlighting the corporate relationship.[^ref-3]

### Critical Perspective

Bouncers is a footnote in Sierra/Dynamix history and the Sega CD library. Reviewers praised its hand-drawn backgrounds and intro cartoons but faulted its physics and thin content.[^ref-3][^ref-7] eBay's catalog description says that despite Mark Hamill's voice work, "this is not enough to make the game anything more than a mere curiosity."[^ref-19]

## Easter Eggs and Trivia

- CG animatics for each of the eight characters are hidden on the main menu[^ref-7]
- The game's soundtrack can be played in a standard CD player by inserting the game disc[^ref-3]
- Bouncers features voice acting by Mark Hamill of Star Wars fame[^ref-1][^ref-2]
- Sega-16 likened the cartoons to "classic 1960s space age cartoons"[^ref-3]
- Rhett Anderson and Dave Hensley's 1987 type-in Basketball Sam & Ed was a forerunner of the game[^ref-2]
- John Garvin went on to create the Syphon Filter series after working on this game[^ref-2][^ref-9]
