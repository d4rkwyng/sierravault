# Hunter Hunted

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Hunter Hunted is a side-scrolling action computer game developed by [[Dynamix]] under its k.a.a. label and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] in November 1996[^ref-1]. The game takes players "out of reality and throws you into a side-scrolling, action-filled, gut-wrenching game" that offers a choice between playing as either a human named Jake or a beast named Garathe Den[^ref-2]. Set in a future where Earth has been conquered by brutal alien overlords, the game combines run-and-gun gameplay with platforming elements and a split-screen two-player mode[^ref-1].

Its levels consist of several parallel planes, and characters move between them through doors and windows[^ref-1]. One reviewer, writing for Home of the Underdogs, called this "an intriguing novel concept: the third dimention of 2D"[^ref-17][^ref-3]. Combined with split-screen cooperative and competitive modes, it made Hunter Hunted an appealing option at a time when, as Old PC Gaming later put it, "decent arcade games on PC are still hard to come by"[^ref-4]. *Next Generation* called it "a strong showing in the world of side-scrolling platform shooters"[^ref-24], and The Collection Chamber later wrote that it "bucked the trend and proved to be very decent" in a genre that had been "more miss than hit on computers"[^ref-8].

Sierra's InterAction magazine presented k.a.a. as a group of rebel developers who wanted to do something other than adventure games. Lead programmer Rhett Andersen said "k.a.a. stands for knot another adventure game... Sierra makes enough of those"[^ref-23]. The game's story was tied into the Metaltech: Earthsiege universe as "a prequel of sorts"[^ref-1]. With 65 single-player missions and 35 multiplayer missions across cooperative and head-to-head modes[^ref-1], the game offered "aimless but exciting thrills that you can play in small chunks at a time"[^ref-4].

## Story Summary

The year is 2015, and Earth has fallen to a technologically superior race of brutal alien warriors known as the Masters[^ref-6]. In a matter of days, five billion humans were exterminated, leaving humankind all but obliterated[^ref-7]. The Masters have invaded many planets throughout the galaxy, systematically exterminating or enslaving their populations[^ref-6]. Among their conquered worlds is Kullrathe, home to a minotaur-like species that has also been subjugated[^ref-1].

The surviving humans and the inhabitants of Kullrathe are forced to fight in the ruins of Earth's cities for the Masters' entertainment[^ref-1]. The box copy casts the player as the chosen champion: "You are the perfect specimen--the biggest, the baddest, the best of the best"[^ref-8]. The human champion is Jake, a "muscle-bound, chisel-jawed" warrior[^ref-4]. His opponent is Garathe Den, "the Minotaur-like warrior champion of the planet Kullrathe"[^ref-4].

The competitions take place in abandoned urban environments—dark alleys, office buildings, and even abandoned malls—that have become the Masters' gladiatorial arenas[^ref-2]. Players must navigate these hostile environments, battling not only each other but also the dangers the Masters have placed throughout the arenas. The ultimate goal is to collect all the car parts needed to build an escape vehicle called the SCURVe, which will allow the champion to escape from the Masters[^ref-9][^ref-10].

The manual's description of cooperative play warns, "If you've never owed your life to someone before, you will now," since in that mode each player can only reach certain items and the two must help each other[^ref-11].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Hunter Hunted is a side-scrolling action game that supports multiple input methods including keyboard, mouse, and gamepad/joystick[^ref-10]. It uses a side view, but most levels consist of several parallel planes, and characters can move into doors or windows to reach other rooms[^ref-1]; the Home of the Underdogs reviewer called this "the third dimention of 2D"[^ref-17]. Characters can also press against walls to avoid incoming projectiles[^ref-1].

Each playable character has distinct capabilities: Garathe Den possesses superior strength and stamina, while Jake can take advantage of more sophisticated weaponry[^ref-12].

### Structure and Progression

The game features a substantial amount of content spread across multiple game modes[^ref-1]:

- **Single Player Campaign:** 65 missions where players select either Jake or Garathe Den[^ref-7]
- **Two-Player Cooperative:** 15 missions designed for two players working together[^ref-1]
- **Two-Player Head-to-Head:** 20 missions where two players compete directly against each other in split-screen[^ref-1]

Each mission tasks players with navigating through the arena, defeating enemies, and reaching the stage exit alive[^ref-14]. There are three selectable difficulty levels: Easy, Medium, and Hard[^ref-11]. Head-to-head mode allows players to set timers and minimum kill requirements, adding competitive structure to the versus matches[^ref-11].

*Next Generation* singled out the split-screen two-player mode as giving "plenty of re-playability"[^ref-24]. An optional add-on mission pack extended the content with 15 new single-player missions, 2 new head-to-head missions, and 3 cooperative missions[^ref-1].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

While primarily an action game, Hunter Hunted incorporates puzzle elements into its level design. Players must "jump and run through a great amount of levels, solving different riddles and killing your enemies"[^ref-15]. *Next Generation* noted that "the levels are well built, with plenty of puzzles and secret passages"[^ref-24].

The two playable characters offer different gameplay experiences. Jake, the human champion, comes equipped with more sophisticated weaponry[^ref-12]. Garathe Den, in contrast, relies on greater strength and stamina[^ref-12]. In the two-player mode, Jake and Garathe Den work through the level simultaneously, "either helping or trying to kill each other depending on the rules of play"[^ref-4].

Players must collect parts throughout the game to ultimately build the SCURVe escape vehicle[^ref-10]. The game has been described as a "kill-em-all type game"[^ref-2].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Hunter Hunted received moderately positive reviews upon release[^ref-1]. GameSpot's Chris Hudak awarded the game a 7 out of 10[^ref-1]. He praised the ability to move between multiple planes, the sound effects, and the soundtrack, and found that the game did not quite revolutionize the side-scrolling shooter genre but was "a level-hopping step in the right direction"[^ref-1].

*Next Generation* gave it three stars out of five in its March 1997 issue, calling Hunter Hunted "a strong showing in the world of side-scrolling platform shooters"[^ref-24]. The reviewer warned that "some of the controls are a bit sticky" but concluded: "It may not be the best platform shooter for the PC, but it's definitely worth some attention"[^ref-24].

### Modern Assessment

Old PC Gaming's retrospective praised its "slick presentation (particularly the sumptuous soundtrack and oodles of character animation)" and concluded that "decent arcade games on PC are still hard to come by, so if you're looking for some aimless but exciting thrills that you can play in small chunks at a time, you could do a hell of a lot worse than this"[^ref-4].

MobyGames contributor Tomer Gabel called it "an innovative platform game with many twists" and "a completely underrated action game"[^ref-27]. The Home of the Underdogs review called it a "fun underdog", while warning that it "may prove too repetitive and easy in one-player mode"[^ref-17].

Players continue to discover and complete it decades after release. Online communities have shared their experiences finishing the game on modern hardware like the Steam Deck[^ref-9].

## Development

### Origins

Hunter Hunted was developed by Sierra's Dynamix subsidiary[^ref-8], and released under the k.a.a. label[^ref-1]. Sierra's InterAction magazine described k.a.a. as "a militant group of developers" who "decided it was time to shake Sierra out of a mid-life crisis"[^ref-23]. Sierra CEO Ken Williams recalled that his first reaction was to shut them down, but that he relented: "OK, k.a.a. Give it your best shot."[^ref-23] Hunter Hunted and CyberGladiators were the first two k.a.a. titles[^ref-23].

The story of Hunter Hunted was tied into the Metaltech: Earthsiege universe as a prequel of sorts[^ref-1]. While Hunter Hunted and the first two Earthsiege games make little reference to each other, the later Starsiege storyline retroactively makes Hunter Hunted a major part of the backstory, and characters in that universe reference a religious figure named "Jake Hunter" who, according to his followers, saved humanity from aliens[^ref-1].

### Production

Designer Chris Cole described the approach in InterAction: "We took a no-holds-barred death match approach to game play. Hunter/Hunted is a brutal game. Its theme is kill or be killed."[^ref-23] To make the action look as real as possible, the team motion-captured the moves for Jake, the Hunter Beast, and the other enemies[^ref-23]; MobyGames credits Biovision for the motion capture[^ref-22].

The Collection Chamber noted that Hunter Hunted "bucked the trend and proved to be very decent" as a PC platformer, a genre that had been "more miss than hit on computers especially when compared to any console's output"[^ref-8].

### Technical Achievements

A preview in InterAction's Summer 1996 issue, under the title "Hunter/Hunted", promoted its "Z-plane movement capabilities that let you move as many as 10 levels deep into the game"[^ref-25]. The Fall 1996 issue described a level that "might be five screens tall by seven screens wide, and 10 or 12 layers deep"[^ref-23]. According to the Home of the Underdogs review, when Jake or the Hunter reaches a door, pressing up sends the character "running headlong into the screen" into the next layer of the arena[^ref-17].

The game supports split-screen play for both cooperative and competitive modes, allowing two players on a single machine[^ref-1]. The Home of the Underdogs reviewer noted that thanks to the into-the-world ability, "you can sneak up on your opponent from behind, above or below"[^ref-17].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Sales figures for Hunter Hunted have not been publicly documented. The game was later reissued in Germany on the 1998 "Best of Sierra No. 06" budget disc alongside *Aces Over Europe*.[^ref-19]

The game was assigned a Teen rating by the ESRB[^ref-6].

### Collections

Hunter Hunted appeared in the following compilations:
- **Best of Sierra No. 06** (Germany, 1998) – with *Aces Over Europe*[^ref-19]
- **Arcade: 3 Thrilling Games of Action**[^ref-7]

The game has also been preserved through archival efforts, with copies available through the Internet Archive[^ref-13].

### Fan Projects

Hunter Hunted maintains a following in retro gaming communities. Discussions on platforms like Reddit show players continuing to revisit the game decades after release, with some completing it for the first time on the Steam Deck[^ref-9]. The game has a GOG.com Dreamlist entry where users vote to see it made available on modern digital distribution platforms[^ref-12].

### Related Publications

- **Hunter Hunted Survival Guide** – An on-disc guide installed with the game, covering game mechanics, enemies, and traps; per one MobyGames reviewer, "The only things missing are hints and tips on each level"[^ref-27]
- **Game Manual** – Standard documentation covering controls, characters, and game modes[^ref-11]

### Critical Perspective

Hunter Hunted was one of Sierra's attempts to move beyond its adventure game catalog: InterAction framed the k.a.a. label as a deliberate break from adventure games, with Rhett Andersen saying "we wanted to do something different"[^ref-23].

Critics gave the game moderately positive reviews[^ref-1], and it never achieved the distinctive status of Sierra's adventure games. Tomer Gabel's MobyGames verdict that it was "a completely underrated action game" is echoed by players who have rediscovered it[^ref-27][^ref-9].
