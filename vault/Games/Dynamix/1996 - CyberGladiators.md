# CyberGladiators

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

CyberGladiators is a one-on-one 3D fighting game for Windows 95, developed by [[Dynamix|Dynamix, Inc.]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]][^ref-1]. Online Gaming Review reported its release in its news for November 18, 1996[^ref-30]. It was one of two games in Dynamix's short-lived k.a.a. label of action games, alongside Hunter Hunted[^ref-3][^ref-17]. The game features eight metallic robotic fighters[^ref-27] in one-on-one combat where players can rip off limbs, throw spears, and do anything in their power to win[^ref-2].

The premise centers on a freak cosmic storm that turned fallen heroes and imprisoned villains alike into cyborg warriors known as CyberGladiators[^ref-7]. Players can choose to fight for either the Quaaflax Alliance or the terrorist organization Gy Djin[^ref-1]. Its 3D graphics, running through Microsoft DirectX, drew the most praise; GameSpot called it "one of the best fighter games the PC has ever enjoyed, mostly due to the outstanding 3-D graphics," but added that "as for the fighting, it's standard fare"[^ref-27]. Critics were divided overall, with scores collected on MobyGames ranging from 33% to 92%[^ref-31].

CyberGladiators evolved from a cancelled Sierra project called CRY.SYS[^ref-3]. It offers single-player play against the computer and two-player matches on the same PC; it has no LAN or online play[^ref-6].

## Story Summary

The game's background story, reproduced in its help file, begins on tiny Planet Id (Industrial Dump), once the home base of the Quaaflax Alliance's top special tactics team until it became their graveyard during a Gy Djin terrorist assault[^ref-7][^ref-9]. The fighters' bodies were never found, and Id became a radioactive wasteland and a dumping ground for the galaxy's military and industrial refuse[^ref-7].

Then a freak cosmic storm struck Planet Id, fusing toxic chemicals, military hardware, and radioactive waste together. As the lore describes: "out of the ooze crawled a strange, new life form: part military and computer hardware, part sentient life forms, these heavy-metal fighting machines came to be known as CyberGladiators"[^ref-9]. The new CyberGladiators looked "mean enough to rip the lungs out of the universe," but the Alliance was relieved to learn that they intended to use their powers only to serve and protect[^ref-7]. Strangely, they bore a startling resemblance to the Alliance heroes "killed long ago" in the Gy Djin assault[^ref-7].

The storm then slammed into DungeonStar, a deep-space prison whose inmates included the Gy Djin leader Master Pain, held in "Terminal Bio-Freeze." The prisoners were transformed into an evil band of CyberGladiators who escaped bent on revenge[^ref-7]. Led by Master Pain, they launched an assault on the galaxy and now face a showdown with the cybernetic enforcers from Planet Id, whose counter-assault is led by Sergeant Mayhem[^ref-7]. The backstory closes with the words "Welcome to Hell"[^ref-7].

Players choose their allegiance, fighting for the Alliance or the Gy Djin, as both sides clash across futuristic arenas[^ref-11].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

CyberGladiators can be played with the keyboard, and the game automatically detects a supported joystick, which must have at least four buttons[^ref-8]. Each fighter has four basic actions: power punch, power kick, speed punch, and speed kick[^ref-8]. Combat is real-time with direct control of the fighter[^ref-6].

The game runs full-screen by default; pressing Alt+Enter switches it to a window, which only works when the Windows desktop is set to high-color, 16-bit mode[^ref-7][^ref-8]. Players can also shrink the screen and lower the detail level to improve the frame rate[^ref-7].

### Structure and Progression

CyberGladiators features a roster of eight playable metallic robotic fighters, each with 50 moves, plus two bonus bosses[^ref-13][^ref-27].

The playable roster includes fighters from both the Alliance and Gy Djin factions[^ref-7]:

- **Psyclopps** – Alliance fighter
- **Plazma** – Alliance fighter
- **Nero Zero** – Alliance fighter
- **Sgt. Mayhem** – Alliance fighter
- **Death Row Jones** – Gy Djin fighter
- **Madam Discipline** – Gy Djin fighter
- **Brother Grimm** – Gy Djin fighter
- **Master Pain** – Gy Djin fighter

In the one-player "War," the player fights eight battles, two arenas on each of four Quaaflax galaxy planets, alternating between enemy fighters and clones of them, before facing the opposing side's boss on its home turf[^ref-7]. The two boss characters, **Carnigore** and **Shrapp**, can become playable: finishing the game without using a continue earns a special code for the boss just defeated[^ref-7]. The code is not given if the fighter strength is set to "Strong" in the Options screen[^ref-7].

The arenas are[^ref-7]:

- **Magnus Dungeons**
- **Vulca Swamp**
- **GloTech Loading Docks**
- **Magnus Power Facility**
- **GloTech Gneutonium Plant**
- **XuneDune Bliss Gardens**
- **Vulca Magma Plains**
- **XuneDune Holiday Oasis**
- **Dungeon Star**
- **Planet Id**

### Puzzles and Mechanics

As a fighting game, CyberGladiators focuses on combat rather than puzzle-solving[^ref-2]. Players can rip off opponents' limbs and throw spears[^ref-2], and fighters can pick up crates, barrels and spears from the ground to use as weapons[^ref-27]. GameSpot noted that the computer opponents log the player's fighting style, becoming progressively harder to beat[^ref-27]. The bosses' strongest attacks can destroy a player's hands, leaving only kicks[^ref-8].

The game supports single-player matches against the computer and two-player matches[^ref-8]. A solo player can also take the Player 2 controls if they prefer that keyboard layout[^ref-8].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Critics were split. The 14 critic scores collected by MobyGames average 62% and range from 92% (PC Multimedia & Entertainment, November 1996) down to 33% (Génération 4, December 1996)[^ref-31]. Hacker gave it 89% and the Danish PC Player 81%, while German magazines were cooler: Power Play 67%, PC Joker 66%, PC Games 54%, PC Action 50%, and PC Player 2 out of 5[^ref-31]. Computer Games Strategy Plus gave it 2 out of 5[^ref-31].

Gamecenter, CNET's games site, rated it 7 out of 10 in Shane Mooney's review of January 14, 1997[^ref-32]. MobyGames lists that same gamecenter.com review twice, once as Gamecenter 7/10 and once as CNET 3/5, so both entries count toward its 14 scores[^ref-31]. In Britain, Teletext's Digitiser gave it 68% overall on January 8, 1997, with 80% for visuals and 69% for gameplay[^ref-33].

PC Multimedia & Entertainment told owners of Rendition Vérité or 3Dfx Voodoo accelerators that it was "another MUST have game for your Christmas wish list"[^ref-31]. Gamecenter concluded that it "has enough unique features and killer graphics to make it appealing to fans of the genre"[^ref-32].

GameSpot's staff review scored it 8.3. It praised the graphics and sound: "CyberGladiators' graphics and sound alone make it worth checking out." It then added: "As for the fighting, it's standard fare"[^ref-27]. PC Zone gave the game 6.4 out of 10[^ref-4][^ref-31].

### Modern Assessment

User comments are sparse. On Classic Gaming Network, a 2014 comment says it "Feels like Rise of the Robots, but in 3D!"[^ref-5]. On MyAbandonware, one user called it a "VERY COOL GAME!" and the site's rating stands at 4.4 out of 5 from 5 votes[^ref-15].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames Critics Average:** 62% (14 ratings)[^ref-31]
- **GameSpot:** 8.3/10[^ref-27]
- **PC Zone:** 6.4/10[^ref-4]
- **Gamecenter:** 7/10[^ref-32]
- **Digitiser:** 68%[^ref-33]
- **Classic Gaming Network:** 76.3% (8 ratings)[^ref-5]
- **MyAbandonware:** 4.4/5 (5 votes)[^ref-15]

The game received an ESRB rating of Teen[^ref-1].

## Development

### Origins

CyberGladiators is what Sierra's cancelled project CRY.SYS evolved into[^ref-3]. It was one of two games released under k.a.a., a label of Dynamix, the other being Hunter Hunted[^ref-3][^ref-17]. MobyGames expands the name as "Knot another adventure game"[^ref-3]. Lead programmer Rhett Anderson[^ref-3] explained the joke to Sierra's InterAction magazine: "You could say that k.a.a. stands for 'knot another adventure game...Sierra makes enough of those..."[^ref-29].

### Production

Development was handled by Dynamix, Inc., with Randy Thompson serving as director and Ken Embery as producer[^ref-4]. The game was built for Windows 95 and used Microsoft's DirectX[^ref-27].

Voice recording was handled by Full Moon Studios, and the music was produced by Loudmouth, Inc.[^ref-18]

**Development Credits:**[^ref-4]
- **Director:** Randy Thompson
- **Producer:** Ken Embery
- **Music By:** Loudmouth, Inc.[^ref-18]
- **Voice Recording:** Full Moon Studios[^ref-18]

### Technical Achievements

CyberGladiators supports Direct3D 2 and 3dfx Glide 2.1.1 for 3D acceleration, and falls back to a DirectDraw software renderer when no accelerator is present[^ref-6]. Contemporary coverage pitched it at owners of Rendition Vérité and 3Dfx Voodoo cards[^ref-31]. GameSpot noted that the fighting scenes "move smoothly and have plenty of depth, even if you just have a run-of-the-mill graphics card"[^ref-27].

### Technical Issues

CyberGladiators has a timing bug that affects play on later systems. The game uses QueryPerformanceCounter() for timing calculations but fails to check QueryPerformanceFrequency(), resulting in incorrect conversion of performance counter cycles to milliseconds[^ref-20]. Bitpatch titles its patch a "Speed Fix"[^ref-20], and Sierra Chest notes that the demo may run at "lightning fast game speed"[^ref-28].

According to Bitpatch, ACT's EmulateSlowCpu shim "doesn't seem to work here," nor does it let the user adjust reported speeds[^ref-20]. Bitpatch calls substituting GetTickCount() for the faulty function "the easiest fix"; GetTickCount() returns milliseconds since Windows was started[^ref-20].

**Hex Edit Fix for cyber.exe v1.0:**[^ref-20]
- At file offset 00003116 write 0E 30 04 00
- At file offset 00003125 write FF 2F 04 00

**Demo Version Speed Fix:**[^ref-28]
- For lightning-fast demo gameplay, hex edits at file offsets 0000305F (69 30 04 00) and 0000306E (5A 30 04 00) can resolve the issue

Additional compatibility notes indicate the game likely requires a ddraw or glide wrapper for proper operation on modern computers[^ref-28]. The Sierra installer may also fail if the system test is performed during installation—users must answer "No" to bypass this check[^ref-10].

Users on MyAbandonware report that the best method for playing the game on modern systems is running Windows 95 inside DOSBox or a similar virtualization environment[^ref-15]. One user comment there reports a persistent "insert cd" message, and another asks whether the image file is corrupted[^ref-15].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for CyberGladiators have been found. The k.a.a. label it was released under was short-lived, with only two titles[^ref-3]. The game is not available digitally[^ref-6].

### Collections

No compilation releases of CyberGladiators have been documented.

### Fan Projects

No significant fan remakes or modifications have been documented for CyberGladiators. The technical compatibility issues with modern systems have inspired community-developed fixes and workarounds, particularly the hex-edit timing patches documented by various enthusiast sites[^ref-20].

### Critical Perspective

Reviewers mostly agreed on the game's strengths and weaknesses. Its 3D graphics won praise from GameSpot and from PC Multimedia & Entertainment[^ref-27][^ref-31]. GameSpot judged the fighting itself "standard fare"[^ref-27], and several German and French magazines scored it at or below 54%[^ref-31].
