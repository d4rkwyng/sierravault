# Ghostbusters II

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 19, 2026</small>

## Overview

Ghostbusters II is an action game for MS-DOS based on the 1989 film, developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Activision]]. MobyGames dates the US release to October 1989, with distribution by Mediagenic.[^ref-1][^ref-6] The game opens with the Ghostbusters fighting the Scoleri brothers' ghosts in a courtroom. From their headquarters the player then picks jobs: busting ghosts for money, collecting slime from beneath the city, testing it, and rescuing teammates from the Parkview asylum. With $55,000 earned, the team walks the Statue of Liberty to the museum for the battle with Vigo the Carpathian.[^ref-5][^ref-1] [[Jeff Tunnell]] is credited as director. *Computer Gaming World* named Doug Barnett as designer, and MobyGames credits the design to Barnett, Michael Latham, Piotr Lukaszuk and Tony Van.[^ref-2][^ref-5]

**Which Ghostbusters II this page covers.** Dynamix's game was made only for MS-DOS. Activision also published a different *Ghostbusters II*, developed in Britain by Foursfield, for the Commodore 64, Amiga, Atari ST, Amstrad CPC and ZX Spectrum, and converted to MSX by New Frontier. That version has three action levels based on the film.[^ref-6][^ref-3][^ref-12] The NES *Ghostbusters II* is a separate product again.[^ref-15] This page covers only the Dynamix MS-DOS game.

The game predates Sierra's March 1990 purchase of Dynamix.[^ref-17] In February 1989 Dynamix signed an "Affiliated Publisher" agreement with Mediagenic, and Jeff Tunnell said the firm planned "several development projects for Activision".[^ref-18] Ghostbusters II was a game Dynamix developed for Activision.[^ref-1][^ref-6] SierraVault covers it because the vault's inclusion criteria take in Dynamix's complete catalog, including work done before the acquisition. The real publisher was Activision, not Sierra.[^ref-1] Reviews were mixed. *Computer Gaming World* praised its design and humor but questioned its value for money, and the German magazine *Power Play* scored it 30%.[^ref-5][^ref-4]

## Story Summary

MobyGames summarizes the film's set-up. Five years after the first film, the Ghostbusters are bankrupt and retired. A river of slime has built up under New York, feeding on the city's negative energy, while the painting of Vigo the Carpathian waits in the Museum of Art to use it to escape.[^ref-1] *Computer Gaming World*'s David Wilson described Vigo, "scourge of Carpathia", gathering "his forces of evil in New York".[^ref-5]

The game begins in court. The judge fines the team $10,000, and his anger makes a beaker of slime explode, releasing the ghosts of the Scoleri brothers, whom he had sentenced to the electric chair.[^ref-5] After that the story advances through the player's choices of jobs. It ends at midnight at the museum, where the Ghostbusters slide down ropes for a final fight with Vigo and his "hypnotized lackey".[^ref-5]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

After the courtroom, the game is run from the Ghostbusters' lab. There the player moves an arrow to choose what to do next. A wrong choice, such as testing slime when you have none, brings up a portrait of Bill Murray with a comic thought balloon.[^ref-5] MobyGames lists keyboard, mouse and analog joystick input.[^ref-1] Wilson, siding with personal computers in the "Nintendo versus Personal Computer controversy", said he would rather not have seen "a Nintendo type game controller" used to guide the Statue of Liberty.[^ref-5]

### Structure and Progression

Wilson's review and MobyGames describe these sequences:[^ref-5][^ref-1]

- **Courtroom:** a side-scrolling fight against the Scoleri brothers' ghosts while dodging ectoplasm. Wilson advised trapping "the fat brother" first. Winning gets the sentence suspended, and losing leaves the team $10,000 in debt.[^ref-5][^ref-1]
- **Ghostbusting jobs:** taken by phone in three parts of town: Central Park, Northrone's department store and the Docks. Each pays $10,000 to $20,000, minus charges for damage.[^ref-5]
- **Slime collection:** climbing down a rope into an abandoned train tunnel while slimers attack and a green arm reaches up from the slime river.[^ref-5]
- **Slime testing:** playing tracks on a CD player to find three of nine songs that calm the slime.[^ref-5][^ref-6]
- **Parkview rescue:** a Ghostbuster who loses a job or the slime run is committed to the Parkview mental ward. A teammate can swing into its windows to rescue him.[^ref-5]
- **Statue of Liberty:** after $55,000 is earned and three positively charged slime samples are ready, the player walks "Libby" through New York between 11:00 p.m. and midnight. Stepping on cars drains her energy.[^ref-5]
- **Museum finale:** the battle with Vigo, in which a single fireball kills a Ghostbuster and the next one takes over.[^ref-5]

MobyGames notes that newspaper editorials sum up each victory or defeat against the ghosts.[^ref-1]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The game turns on money and slime. Egon needs $55,000 to build the slime blower, and the endgame requires three good slime samples, so the player has to balance paying jobs against trips to the tunnel.[^ref-5] Wilson thought it "advantageous" to get the whole crew out of Parkview before the final battle. He warned that the proton packs bounce back off Vigo's lackey, and that letting the slime blowers "get too hot allows Vigo to gain considerable advantage".[^ref-5]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

| Publication | Date | Verdict |
|-------------|------|---------|
| *Computer Gaming World* (David M. Wilson) | January 1990 | Mixed review, no numerical score[^ref-5] |
| *Power Play* | Not recorded | 30%[^ref-4] |
| *Computer Gaming World* readers' poll | May 1990 | 7.91, ranked 94th[^ref-10] |

David Wilson's *Computer Gaming World* review, "I Still Ain't 'Fraid of No Ghost!", listed the game for IBM PCs with 512K at $44.95, with a Commodore 64 version "soon".[^ref-5] He thought the graphics "nice, if not overly impressive", liked the "cutesy" details, such as a giant cockroach in a Central Park outhouse, and said the "digitized voice tracks add to the whimsical feeling that permeated the movie".[^ref-5] His reservation was about value: "Since the game can be played in less than an hour and players will probably play the game, at least, fifteen times before mastering it, some may feel that this is less than a bargain." All the same, he called it "well-designed and easily learned", and closed with "Bustin' will make you feel good!"[^ref-5]

*Power Play* was much harsher. Its 30% review noted that the PC game "hat nun gar nichts mehr mit dem gleichnamigen Programm für Amiga und ST zu tun" ("has nothing at all to do with the program of the same name for Amiga and ST"), and said its new graphics and action sequences could not hide "katastrophale Spielbarkeit" ("catastrophic playability").[^ref-4] *Computer Gaming World*'s May 1990 readers' poll rated the IBM game 7.91, 94th on its list of games.[^ref-10]

### Modern Assessment

The abandonware site Abandonia rated the game 80 in a later review. It called it "the secret alternative" to the better-known *Ghostbusters II*, which "even most Ghostbusters fans have no idea" existed.[^ref-4] On MobyGames the DOS game averages 52% from four critic ratings, and player reviews there are mixed.[^ref-7] Wikipedia's article on the computer *Ghostbusters II* games discusses the Dynamix version through Wilson's review. Its reception section otherwise covers the Foursfield versions, which drew praise for their graphics and criticism for loading times and disk swapping.[^ref-6]

## Development

### Origins

*Computer Gaming World*'s report from the summer 1989 Consumer Electronics Show noted that "Activision contracted for permission to digitize images from the film in their Die Hard and Ghostbusters II action games". It described both as having a "heavy action emphasis".[^ref-9] Wikipedia's Dynamix article names Die Hard and Ghostbusters II as the movie tie-ins where the studio's digitized graphics, animation and sound effects were used "most notably".[^ref-14] According to Wikipedia, the DOS version "includes digitized voice tracks and images from the film", while the Foursfield versions include only the images. It also reports that Activision planned the DOS, Commodore 64 and Amiga versions for October 1989 in the United States.[^ref-6] In December 1989, *Computer Gaming World* reported that Mediagenic was "optimistic about its entertainment products", naming Ghostbusters II first.[^ref-11]

### Production

MobyGames lists the team:[^ref-2][^ref-20]

- **Director:** [[Jeff Tunnell]]
- **Design:** Doug Barnett, Michael Latham, Piotr Lukaszuk, Tony Van
- **Programming:** Bob Arient, Gregg Hango, Piotr Lukaszuk, Richard Rayl
- **Graphics / Artwork:** Chuck Austen, Doug Barnett, Brian Hawn, Ron Winnick
- **Art Director:** Brian Hawn, Steve Snyder
- **Music:** Russell Lieblich; **Sound:** Bryce Morcello
- **Music / Sound Programming:** Glyn Anderson, Michael Latham
- **Producer:** Mark Johnson, Michael Latham
- **Project Manager; Writing / Dialogue / Story:** Kelly Flock
- **Documentation:** Hunter Cone, Mark Johnson, Leslie Roth
- **Packaging:** Robert Schonfisch
- **Playtesting:** Stewart Perkins

The credits mix Dynamix staff, including Tunnell, Rayl and Morcello, with names that also appear in Activision's own credits of the period. Michael Latham and Russell Lieblich, for example, are credited on Activision's 1990 MS-DOS *F-14 Tomcat*.[^ref-2][^ref-16] The sources consulted do not say how the work was divided between the two companies.

### Technical Specifications

- **Requirements:** an Intel 8088/8086 PC with 512 KB RAM.[^ref-1]
- **Media:** 3.5" and 5.25" floppy disks.[^ref-1]
- **Graphics:** CGA, EGA, Hercules and Tandy/PCjr.[^ref-1]
- **Sound:** AdLib, Roland MT-32, Sound Blaster, Tandy/PCjr, Tandy DAC and an enhanced ("tweaked") PC speaker mode.[^ref-1]
- **Input:** keyboard, mouse or analog joystick.[^ref-1]
- **Price:** $44.95 according to *Computer Gaming World*.[^ref-5][^ref-11]
- **Modern fix:** Steven Don's patches page offers a fix for a freeze in the intro of *Ghostbusters 2* when digitized sound is enabled.[^ref-13]

The Internet Archive's floppy-image preservation item for the Dynamix game is dated October 1989, matching MobyGames.[^ref-19][^ref-1]

**Not Dynamix versions:** Foursfield's *Ghostbusters II* for the Commodore 64, Amiga, Atari ST, Amstrad CPC and ZX Spectrum (1989), the MSX conversion, and a 1992 Atari 2600 release grouped with them by MobyGames.[^ref-3][^ref-6] Activision's player's guide for the Foursfield version lists its own programming team (Colin Reed, Stefan Ufnowski, Andrew and Phillip Oliver, and others) and its three sequences: Van Horne, Broadway and the museum.[^ref-12]

## Legacy

The game shared its approach with *Die Hard*, Dynamix's other 1989 film licence for Activision. Both used digitized imagery from their films.[^ref-9][^ref-14] The Foursfield version reached more platforms and accounts for most of the reviews Wikipedia collects for the computer games, and Abandonia's reviewer later called the Dynamix game "the secret alternative".[^ref-6][^ref-4] Sierra bought Dynamix on March 27, 1990.[^ref-17]
