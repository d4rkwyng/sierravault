# Caveman Ughlympics

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 19, 2026</small>

## Overview

Caveman Ughlympics, sold on the box as *Caveman Ugh-Lympics*, is a comic multi-event sports game set in the Stone Age. It was published by [[Electronic Arts]] for the Commodore 64 in 1988 (MobyGames dates the US release to November) and for MS-DOS in July 1989. Data East released a Nintendo Entertainment System version in October 1990 as *Caveman Games*.[^ref-1][^ref-15] Up to six players compete in six events: the Mate Toss, Fire Making, Clubbing, Saber Race, Dino Race and Dino Vault.[^ref-1][^ref-3]

The game was an Electronic Arts idea, designed by Greg Johnson of *Starflight* and programmed by [[Dynamix]] in Eugene, Oregon.[^ref-4][^ref-12] It predates Sierra's 1990 purchase of Dynamix, and Sierra never published it. SierraVault covers it because the vault's inclusion criteria take in Dynamix's complete catalog, and *Computer Gaming World*'s report on the Sierra deal named it among Dynamix's Electronic Arts titles.[^ref-5]

Reviewers liked it, mostly for its humour. *Computer Gaming World* called that humour "the most commendable aspect of this game", *Compute!* called it "a scream" in multiplayer, and Lemon64's average of seven Commodore 64 magazine reviews is 82%.[^ref-6][^ref-7][^ref-8]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game works much like Epyx's *Summer Games* and *Winter Games*, according to *Compute!'s Gazette*, with "similar joystick moves". Players can compete against each other or alone against the computer.[^ref-10] Gregg Keizer of *Compute!* summed it up as "lots of joystick moves, a few button presses", and thought "a sense of rhythm" was the most useful skill.[^ref-7] On the Commodore 64, odd-numbered players use one joystick and even-numbered players the other.[^ref-10] MobyGames lists keyboard and analog joystick support for MS-DOS, and same-screen play for one to six players.[^ref-1]

### Athletes

Six athletes are available: Vincent, Gronk, Glunk, Thag, Ugha and Crudla, the only cavewoman. Each is strong in two events except Vincent, "who's good at nothing", and Gronk, "who's good at everything". Picking one of those two lets players handicap a stronger or weaker participant.[^ref-7][^ref-6]

### Events

- **Mate Toss:** a hammer throw with a spouse. The player spins with the joystick and lets go; hanging on too long turns the athlete blue and ruins the throw. The mate then gives a thumbs up, a thumbs down or a shrug.[^ref-6][^ref-7]
- **Saber Race:** a race with a sabre-toothed tiger behind, dodging cacti and mud. Finishers climb a tree to taunt the cat; laggards are eaten.[^ref-6][^ref-7]
- **Fire Making:** rub sticks together, blow the sparks into a blaze, and club the opponent when he gets ahead.[^ref-6][^ref-7]
- **Clubbing:** two cavemen on a clifftop. An intimidation phase can push the opponent back toward the edge, then the fight uses face, knee and overhead blows. Pointing at the sky makes a gullible opponent look away, and a comet sometimes crosses the screen, letting a well-timed point knock the rival over.[^ref-6]
- **Dino Race:** ride a dinosaur over rough ground, clubbing it to speed up, but not so often that it becomes confused or knocked out.[^ref-6][^ref-7]
- **Dino Vault:** a pole vault over a *Tyrannosaurus rex*. Failure lands the athlete in a chasm or in the dinosaur's mouth.[^ref-6][^ref-3]

The game opens with a parody of an Olympic opening ceremony. A torch-bearer lights the flame and is flattened by the falling stone title, then replaced by "a comical caveman" as "the serious theme music is usurped by an offbeat overture".[^ref-6][^ref-18] Record holders are displayed in the "Caves of Fame".[^ref-7] *Computer Gaming World*'s David Wilson thought the Dino Vault "may be the hardest event to master".[^ref-6]

David Wilson's review in *Computer Gaming World*, "Neanderthal Nonsense and Cro-Magnon Comedy", called the game "a tongue-in-cheek prehistoric parody of our modern Olympics that manages to keep players snickering and chuckling throughout". After an evening with a group of teenagers, he found it "much more enjoyable with two to four players", though with more players "it tends to bog down somewhat".[^ref-6] He praised the documentation too, with its tips in "Caveman-like talk" and the *Ugh Zaminer* newspaper, and closed: "the next time you find yourself longing for those simpler times and looking for a way to sate the barbarian within, don't lose your cool — boot up Caveman Ugh-lympics instead."[^ref-6]

Gregg Keizer of *Compute!* wrote that it "is, without a doubt, a scream. Solo play is boring, but bring in another player and the room will echo with shouts and laughter." He compared its violence to Saturday-morning cartoons and *Three Stooges* slapstick, and praised the large cartoon characters, noting that only the Saber Race made it hard to tell athletes apart.[^ref-7] *Compute!'s Gazette* framed its review as an interview with a caveman critic, who ends: "Top-notch game. You buy."[^ref-10]

The NES version split *Electronic Gaming Monthly*'s Review Crew. One reviewer wrote "What a disappointment!", complaining that "The graphics are choppy" and "All events suffer from poor execution", while another found it "strange, silly and humorous".[^ref-17] In Britain, *Zzap!64*'s reviewers enjoyed the humour and the large, cartoony graphics. One warned that the jokes wore thin after a few games, and they complained about the multi-load, which would frustrate cassette owners.[^ref-11]

### Modern Assessment

Lemon64 lists seven Commodore 64 magazine scores averaging 82%, including a 79% from *Zzap!64* in February 1991 for a budget re-release on Electronic Arts' Star Performer label.[^ref-8] Designer Greg Johnson later described the game as "lots of crazy head bonking and running from sabre-toothed tigers and stuff", made as "a party game - it was a blast with four people who wanted to get silly".[^ref-4]

## Development

### Origins

Greg Johnson told Gamasutra in 2006 that the game "was actually EA's idea", and that he thought it came from Electronic Arts' Bing Gordon: "He came to me and said, 'Hey Greg, how would you feel about doing an Olympics game set in caveman times?', and I said, 'Cool!'"[^ref-4] Wikipedia's biography of Johnson places this in 1988, after his work on *Starflight* and art for other Electronic Arts titles.[^ref-12]

### Production

Johnson designed the game "from down in the Bay Area", and Dynamix programmed it in Eugene. "They could not possibly have been better," he said. "Jeff Tunnell was running Dynamix and he was awesome. Long distance designing can work when you have very motivated teams who are pretty self-sufficient."[^ref-4]

The archived MobyGames credits for the MS-DOS and Commodore 64 versions list:[^ref-9][^ref-13]

- **Programming:** Richard Rayl, Jon Leupp, Dariusz Lukaszuk
- **Graphics and animation:** Dariusz Lukaszuk, Greg Johnson, Brian Hahn
- **Sounds:** Bryce Morcello

- **Art direction:** Nancy L. Fong
- **Documentation:** Zina J. Yee

*Computer Gaming World*'s review box gave the designers as Greg Johnson, Richard Rayl Jr., Jon Leupp, Brian Hahn and Derek Lukaszuk.[^ref-6]

### Technical Specifications

- **Commodore 64:** two disks, although *Compute!'s Gazette* noted that the game refers to disks one to four.[^ref-10]
- **MS-DOS:** 8088 or better, DOS 2.11, 512 KB RAM; CGA, EGA, Hercules and Tandy graphics; PC speaker; keyboard or analog joystick; 5.25" disks.[^ref-1]
- **Players:** one to six on all three platforms.[^ref-1]
- **Prices:** *Computer Gaming World* listed $39.95, while *Compute!* and *Compute!'s Gazette* listed $29.95 for the Commodore 64 version. In the UK, £9.95 cassette and £14.95 disk.[^ref-6][^ref-7][^ref-10][^ref-11]

## Legacy

The NES version, retitled *Caveman Games*, took the game to a console audience in 1990. *Electronic Gaming Monthly*'s four reviewers gave it scores ranging from 4 to 8 out of 10.[^ref-15][^ref-3] *Computer Gaming World* listed it in May 1990 alongside *Arcticfox*, *Skyfox II* and *Project Firestart* as Dynamix's Electronic Arts work, before Sierra agreed to buy the studio.[^ref-5][^ref-14]

Johnson stayed with Electronic Arts long enough to complete *Starflight II*, then began work on *ToeJam & Earl*.[^ref-4] Wikipedia describes *ToeJam & Earl* as "an even quirkier title than *Caveman*".[^ref-12]
