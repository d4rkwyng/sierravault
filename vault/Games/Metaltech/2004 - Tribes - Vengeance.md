# Tribes: Vengeance

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

Tribes: Vengeance is a science fiction first-person shooter for Windows, developed by Irrational Games and released by Vivendi Universal Games under the [[Sierra On-Line|Sierra]] brand in October 2004.[^ref-1][^ref-2] It was the first PC Tribes game made without [[Dynamix]], the studio that created the series and had been disbanded after *Tribes 2*. Reviewers at the time noted that Vivendi had handed the series to Irrational, the studio behind *System Shock 2* and *Freedom Force*.[^ref-3][^ref-4][^ref-5]

The Sierra label is recorded in the game's own materials. The official Prima strategy guide carries the notice "© 2004 Sierra Entertainment, Inc. All rights reserved. Sierra, the Sierra logo, Tribes and the Tribes logo are trademarks or registered trademarks of Sierra Entertainment, Inc." It also states that the game was "Developed exclusively by Irrational Games".[^ref-6] The game's credits, as recorded by MobyGames, include a "Publishing by Vivendi Universal Games (Bellevue, Washington Team)" section with a "Sierra CPC Lead".[^ref-7] MobyGames now lists Sierra Entertainment as publisher, with Vivendi Universal Games subsidiaries as distributors. Wikipedia and The Sierra Chest name Vivendi Universal Games as publisher, and Wikipedia adds that it was "Released under the Sierra Entertainment brand name".[^ref-2][^ref-1][^ref-8]

Vengeance kept the series' jetpacks, "skiing" and large team-based multiplayer battles. It was also the first Tribes game with a story-driven single-player campaign.[^ref-3][^ref-2] Critics rated it highly. GameSpot gave it 8.8, and it won Best PC Game and Best Game at the 2004 Australian Game Developers Awards.[^ref-3][^ref-9] Even so, a 2006 GameSpot report said it "sold poorly", and Vivendi ended online support within months.[^ref-10]

## Story Summary

Vengeance is set hundreds of years before the original Tribes, when the Tribal War was first breaking out.[^ref-4][^ref-1] Its story centers on two generations of imperial princesses, Victoria and her daughter Julia. The missions jump back and forth in time, so the player sees events from several characters' points of view.[^ref-3][^ref-12]

In "The Past", the Phoenix tribe raids the imperial palace and abducts the young Princess Victoria. She is drawn, against her will, into the feud between the Phoenix and the Blood Eagle tribes.[^ref-2] Twenty years later, in "The Present", Julia sets out to find the person who killed her mother.[^ref-2][^ref-1] Beyond the two princesses, the player also controls two brothers of the Phoenix tribe and a mysterious cybrid assassin. Along the way Julia uncovers what GameSpot called "a vile conspiracy" and "a dark secret harbored by Victoria".[^ref-3]

GameSpot's pre-release interview with producer Chris Mahnken explained that the time-shifting structure was meant both to familiarize players with different armor and weapons for multiplayer and "to bring you closer to each character".[^ref-12] Wikipedia lists the playable characters as Victoria, Daniel, Julia, Mercury and Jericho across 18 missions. It names Tara Strong (Julia), Gabriel Olds (Daniel) and Steve Blum (Jericho) among the voice cast.[^ref-1]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

- **Perspective:** first-person by default, with a third-person view available at any time. Vehicles always use a third-person camera.[^ref-1]
- **Jetpack:** every suit of armor has one, powered by an energy meter that refills when the jetpack is not in use.[^ref-1][^ref-3]
- **Skiing:** a frictionless ground-movement mode that lets players slide down slopes at high speed. GameSpot noted that the indoor levels include rounded floors built for skiing.[^ref-1][^ref-3]
- **Grappling hook:** new to Vengeance. It can latch onto friendly vehicles or swing a player across terrain.[^ref-3][^ref-1]
- **Armor classes:** light, medium and heavy. Heavier armor trades speed for protection and ammunition.[^ref-3][^ref-1]

### Structure and Progression

GameSpot estimated the single-player campaign at 10 to 12 hours. It found that the campaign also works as a tutorial for multiplayer, introducing every weapon, armor type, vehicle and game type.[^ref-3] Missions include outdoor landscapes, an imperial palace and a large underground base. In one mission, the child Julia has to evade attackers in the palace before taking control of a fighter craft.[^ref-3]

Multiplayer supports up to 32 players on 15 maps at launch. Several reviewers regretted that there were no computer-controlled bots.[^ref-3][^ref-5] There are five modes:[^ref-3][^ref-5][^ref-1]

- **Capture the Flag:** the series' traditional mode, with bases, generators, turrets and inventory stations to defend.
- **Arena:** round-based team combat. GameSpy described it as basic team deathmatch.
- **Ball:** teams fight over a ball and throw it into the opposing goal.
- **Fuel:** teams collect fuel canisters to fill their depot. Each team death costs the team one unit of fuel.
- **Rabbit:** a free-for-all keep-away game built around a single flag.

The game shipped with an editor.[^ref-5]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Vengeance has no puzzles in the adventure-game sense. Its depth lies in team roles and equipment. GameSpot described matches in which "dozens of players are working on a variety of different tasks, such as base defense, repairs, driving a vehicle, or capturing the other team's flag".[^ref-3]

- **Weapons:** the spinfusor, chaingun, mortar and grenade launcher return. New additions include a rocket pod that fires six spiraling rockets with limited tracking, and a sniper rifle that now needs separate ammunition.[^ref-3]
- **Vehicles:** four in total. They are a fast buggy that doubles as a mobile inventory station, a jump tank, a one-man fighter and a three-man assault ship. Vehicles now spawn from vehicle pads rather than being bought at consoles.[^ref-3]

### Contemporary Reviews

| IGN (Steve Butts) | October 5, 2004 | "Irrational takes the beloved action series to new heights"[^ref-4] |
| GameSpy (Scott Osborne) | October 8, 2004 | Positive, with reservations about multiplayer depth[^ref-5] |

| Metacritic | (aggregate) | 83/100 from 44 critics[^ref-15] |

GameSpot's Bob Colayco called Irrational's handling of the licence "a resounding success". He wrote that the game "effectively captures the spirit of the original game, and adds a surprisingly good single-player campaign to a deep and addictive multiplayer component".[^ref-3] IGN's Steve Butts argued that Vivendi wanted "a really good single player experience", which made Irrational "the perfect choice to craft a compelling plot".[^ref-4]

Other reviewers were more divided about the multiplayer. GameSpy's Scott Osborne wrote that it "isn't as innovative or deep as the previous installments, but makes up for it with pulse-pounding online action and a rewarding solo campaign".[^ref-5] *X-Play*'s Todd White praised a "literate, compelling single-player game", but felt it "manages to eclipse what the Tribes series is fundamentally known for -- multiplayer".[^ref-14] *The Sydney Morning Herald* called it "a shining example of why the Tribes universe is so popular, although fans aren't happy with many of the refinements".[^ref-13]

### Awards

At the 2004 Australian Game Developers Awards, Irrational Games won Best PC Game and Best Game of 2004 for Tribes: Vengeance. Dean Tate won Best Level Design and Edward Orman won Best Game Design.[^ref-9] Wikipedia adds that the game was a runner-up for *Computer Games Magazine*'s ten best computer games of 2004.[^ref-1]

### Modern Assessment

Metacritic's archived page shows a Metascore of 83, "Generally favorable reviews", based on 44 critic reviews.[^ref-15] Its Moby Score on MobyGames is 7.9 out of 10.[^ref-2]

## Development

### Origins

GameSpot reported in March 2004 that "Vivendi Universal Games and members of the original Tribes team, along with Irrational Games" were working on the game, and that it would add a single-player story built around three factions: the Imperials, the Blood Eagles and the Phoenix.[^ref-12] Ken Levine, Irrational's co-founder, is credited as writer.[^ref-7][^ref-10] MobyGames credits the development to Irrational's Canberra, Australia studio, with lead designer Edward Orman, lead programmer Rowan Wyborn, lead artist Andrew James, art director Ben Lee and producer Tony Oakden.[^ref-7]

### Production

The game runs on a modified Unreal engine. Wikipedia says Irrational called its enhanced Unreal Engine 2.5 the "Vengeance engine", and The Sierra Chest lists both "Vengeance Engine" and "Unreal Engine".[^ref-1][^ref-8] GameSpot praised its detailed models and the physics-driven explosions.[^ref-3] The Prima guide's legal notice credits Havok and software technology licensed from GameSpy Industries.[^ref-6] The credits list a "Publishing by Vivendi Universal Games (Bellevue, Washington Team)" led by producer Chris Mahnken and executive producer Mike Jacob, plus a second Vivendi Universal Games team in Los Angeles.[^ref-7]

Eric Brosius wrote the music and sound.[^ref-7]

### Release and Support

Eurogamer reported on October 5, 2004 that the official 483 MB multiplayer demo had been released, with four maps and all of the full game's weapons, armor, vehicles and game types. A single-player demo was on Sierra's site. The full game was then expected in Europe on October 22.[^ref-11] MobyGames lists the US release on October 5, 2004 and releases in France, Germany and Italy on October 22, 2004.[^ref-2]

Support ended early. Wikipedia reports that on March 23, 2005 Vivendi Universal Games announced it was ending support, starting with the cancellation of the 1.1 update.[^ref-1] When GameSpot asked Ken Levine in January 2006 about a supposed "falling out" with VU Games over the game, he answered: "With Tribes, we did a patch, and for whatever reason they decided not to release it."[^ref-10]

## Legacy

In 2015 Hi-Rez Studios made Vengeance free, alongside Earthsiege, Earthsiege 2, Starsiege: Tribes, Tribes 2 and Tribes: Aerial Assault. Hi-Rez's own *Tribes Ascend* was already free-to-play.[^ref-16][^ref-2]

Its commercial failure overshadowed its reviews. GameSpot's 2006 report described a game that "sold poorly" despite "widespread acclaim", which led VU to end online support "within a few months". By then Irrational was working on *BioShock*.[^ref-10]

The Prima guide records an Irrational in-joke: in the cutscene before the palace missions, young Julia's toys are characters from Irrational's earlier *Freedom Force*.[^ref-6]
