# Metaltech Earthsiege Expansion Pack

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

The *Metaltech Earthsiege Expansion Pack* was an add-on expansion for the original *[[1994 - Metaltech - Earthsiege|Metaltech: Earthsiege]]*, developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] in 1995.[^ref-1][^ref-24][^ref-49] The expansion continued the struggle between humanity and the Cybrid war machines, picking up the narrative three months after the events of the base game.[^ref-1][^ref-17] Sierra's own *InterAction* preview puts the nearest Cybrid ships 90 days from Earth's orbit.[^ref-58] It adds three new campaigns, several new landscapes, a new HERC (the Apocalypse) and several new Cybrid weapons, and it requires the original game to play.[^ref-1][^ref-24]

The setting is the 25th or 26th century,[^ref-27] and the expansion keeps the game's core premise of piloting HERCs (Humaniform-Emulation Roboticized Combat Unit with Leg-Articulated Navigation) against automated Cybrid enemies.[^ref-3] The game's official description sets it up this way: "The attack on Earth by the Cybrids has been repelled and humanity is starting to rebuild. But a second wave is already incoming, and the battle starts again."[^ref-1]

Dynamix created the Metaltech universe as its own setting after losing the Battletech licence to Activision,[^ref-52] and the *Tribes* series of multiplayer shooters later grew out of it as a spin-off.[^ref-53]

## Story Summary

### Setting and Premise

The base game's opening text reads: "Five hundred years in the future, mankind is fighting a desperate battle for survival. Sentient cybrid war machines have rebelled against their human masters and crushed the great military powers of the world."[^ref-11] In the series backstory, the Cybrids (cybernetic-hybrid machines) were mass-produced and used for war, to the point of a nuclear war that devastated Earth's population, before they turned against their human creators.[^ref-27]

### The First Wave Repelled

The original *Metaltech: Earthsiege* contains 45 missions across 8 campaigns.[^ref-27] By the end of it the Cybrid attack on Earth has been repelled and humanity is starting to rebuild.[^ref-1]

### The Second Wave

The expansion pits humanity against a second wave of Cybrid attackers three months after the end of the original game.[^ref-17][^ref-27] It opens with a full-motion video introduction,[^ref-17][^ref-27] and players return to their HERC cockpits across new landscapes, with new hardware on both sides.[^ref-1] Sierra's own magazine, *InterAction*, previewed the setup in a Mark Crowe feature in Spring 1995: after twenty years of war mankind has won back its homeworld, but a massive Cybrid re-invasion fleet of 10,000 ships is on its way from the outer colony worlds, the nearest only 90 days from Earth's orbit, which fits the three-month interval of the premise.[^ref-58] The preview is framed as a personal log dated March 2, 2553, by Maj-Gen J. Gierling, commander-in-chief of the Earth Defense Force.[^ref-58] *The Encyclopedia of Science Fiction* describes the expansion as one that "largely repeats the structure of the first game, centred on an attack on Earth by Cybrids who have been rebuilding their strength in space colonies," and notes that *Earthsiege 2* continues the story from the expansion pack.[^ref-52]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The *Earthsiege Expansion Pack* keeps the first-person mech simulation gameplay of the base game, placing players in the cockpit of HERC war machines.[^ref-3][^ref-6] The expansion requires the original *Metaltech: Earthsiege* to play.[^ref-24]

MobyGames' official description of the base game reads: "Command heavily-armed HERCs against relentless automated Cybrids (Cyborg Hybrids; intelligent creatures designed by mankind, who rebelled against us) in this MechWarrior-ish first-person simulation."[^ref-6] Each HERC has its own HUD layout, but all of them show radar, weapons and damage information.[^ref-3]

### Control Systems

The base game, which the expansion runs on, supports these input devices:[^ref-15]

**Supported Input Devices:**
- Keyboard
- Mouse
- Analog Joystick
- Thrustmaster WCS (Weapons Control System)
- Thrustmaster FCS (Flight Control System) with rudders
- CH Products Flightstick Pro

Each HERC has three areas to control: movement, the upper torso and the thrust.[^ref-3] Collection Chamber's retrospective found the controls "a little daunting at first" and the wealth of HUD information "quite daunting at first", but added that players eventually grow comfortable with both.[^ref-3]

### Structure and Progression

The expansion's new campaigns plug into the original game's three modes of play:[^ref-3][^ref-17]

**Instant Action:** Drops players straight into a combat scenario. According to Collection Chamber's retrospective, "This is the most frenetic option, but you never know what you're gonna get."[^ref-3]

**Single Mission:** Lets players choose from a number of levels for a single playthrough.[^ref-3]

**Career Mode:** Links missions together into an ongoing story, with the added bonus of scavenging for parts and upgrading your HERC.[^ref-3] The expansion is switched on with a toggle in the options menu, which resets the game with the new career mode replacing the old. Save files are separate, so salvaged weapons cannot be carried over.[^ref-3]

### Expansion Content

According to MobyGames' description, the expansion adds:[^ref-1]

**New Campaigns:**
- Three new campaigns[^ref-1][^ref-17]
- Several new landscapes[^ref-1]

**New Equipment:**
- The Apocalypse, a powerful new HERC[^ref-1]
- Several new Cybrid weapons[^ref-1]
- According to Sierra's *InterAction* preview, the new Cybrid machines include a Diablo and a Headhunter, the player gains a new Plasma Launcher, and the Particle Beam, Electron Flux and Electromagnetic Pulse weapons can be upgraded with captured Cybrid technology; fighting moves to urban wreckage and black ash terrains[^ref-58]

Collection Chamber found the expansion missions "far more diverse" than the original's, though still structured the same way: go to a waypoint, destroy enemies, and repeat.[^ref-3]

### Combat System

The Cybrids have location-based damage: limbs, weapons and torso can each be destroyed independently. Where you hit also affects salvage, since a thoroughly destroyed enemy leaves little to recover for you or your three squadmates.[^ref-3] One MobyGames player review of the base game noted that the best strategy was to "blast out their legs from under them, thus preserving the rest of the machine for salvage," and called the atmosphere "incredible, and in my opinion it outclassed Mechwarrior 2."[^ref-6]

Enemy AI circles around to get into the best position or hides behind structures to escape fire.[^ref-3] Computer-controlled squadmates are "more help than a hindrance," though Collection Chamber called them "useless at ground to air combat."[^ref-3]

### German Localization

*PC Games* praised the expansion's German localization: "All screen texts are available in the German version, and even the voice output has been properly synchronized." (translated from German, via the Dynamix Wiki)[^ref-17]

### Modern Assessment

| Source | Score |
|--------|-------|

Collection Chamber's retrospective of the base game declared that "EarthSiege is one of the most technically impressive games released in 1994 and is still very playable today."[^ref-3] Its chief complaint is the amount of walking: travelling to the next checkpoint "is a lesson in tedium."[^ref-3]

Player comments on the base game remain fond. One MobyGames reviewer wrote, "The graphics were amazing for its time, as was the sound on the CD-rom version."[^ref-6] A MyAbandonware commenter, rejecting "poor man's mechwarrior" comparisons, wrote that "Earthsiege is very much its own beast, with a lot to recommend it."[^ref-46]

## Development

### Origins

Dynamix had developed the first *MechWarrior* game in 1989, when it still had a publishing agreement with Activision.[^ref-11] After that, Dynamix lost the Battletech licence to Activision and responded by creating its own Metaltech continuity with its own HERCULAN war machines.[^ref-52] *Earthsiege* is not part of FASA's BattleTech universe.[^ref-6]

### Technical Development

The original *Earthsiege* initially suffered from many problems, including a badly implemented career mode, but these subsided once updates arrived.[^ref-3] An Earthsiege Expansion patch fixes a crash in Campaign 4, Mission 8. Collection Chamber applied it in its January 2023 package update.[^ref-3]

### Release Context

The expansion was out by mid-1995: *PC Games* reviewed it in July 1995.[^ref-17] Activision's *MechWarrior 2: 31st Century Combat* shipped for DOS on July 24, 1995.[^ref-54] Earlier, the UK magazine *PC Games* (issue 10, catalogued by the Internet Archive as May 1995; not the German *PC Games*) reported in its news pages that "an Earthsiege expansion disk will be available from next month," with new enemy HERCs, upgradeable versions of existing weapons and approximately 20 new missions interspersed with previously unseen cinematic sequences.[^ref-59] The preceding issue (#9, catalogued as April 1995), reviewing the CD-ROM version of the base game, had already told readers to "look out for the inevitable expansion disk and then EarthSiege 2 later this year."[^ref-62]

## Legacy

### Evolution to Tribes

The Metaltech universe that *Earthsiege* began went on to include *[[1996 - Earthsiege 2|Earthsiege 2]]* (1996), *Metaltech: Battledrome* (1994), the strategy games *MissionForce: CyberStorm* (1996) and *Cyberstorm 2: Corporate Wars* (1998), and the direct sequel *Starsiege* (1999). It also produced the first-person shooters *Starsiege: Tribes* (1998), *Tribes 2* (2001), *Tribes: Aerial Assault* (2002), *Tribes: Vengeance* (2004) and *Tribes: Ascend* (2012).[^ref-27] The *Tribes* series is a spin-off from the Metaltech mecha simulation series.[^ref-53]

### Preservation Status

MyAbandonware lists the expansion as abandonware.[^ref-24] On October 23, 2010, Hi-Rez Studios announced that it had bought the *Tribes* IP from InstantAction.[^ref-53] In 2015, for the franchise's 21st anniversary, Hi-Rez made every game in the franchise free to download, starting with 1994's *Metaltech: Earthsiege*.[^ref-53][^ref-27] *PC Gamer*'s list of the newly free games names *Earthsiege* and *Earthsiege 2* but not the expansion pack.[^ref-55]

The Collection Chamber maintains a pre-configured DOSBox package that includes the Expansion Pack and the Campaign 4, Mission 8 fix.[^ref-3]
