# Metaltech: Earthsiege

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

Metaltech: Earthsiege is a mech combat simulation game developed by Dynamix and published by Sierra On-Line for DOS, released in North America on October 25, 1994.[^ref-1][^ref-4] Set five hundred years in the future, the game depicts mankind's desperate battle for survival against sentient cybrid war machines that have rebelled against their human creators and crushed the great military powers of the world.[^ref-2] Players command giant bipedal war machines called HERCs (Humaniform-Emulation Roboticized Combat Unit with Leg-Articulated Navigation) in first-person combat against these relentless automated enemies.[^ref-4]

Earthsiege is described by Wikipedia and The Collection Chamber as the first game in the Metaltech series, a universe that grew to include sequels, strategy games and the Tribes series,[^ref-4][^ref-6] though the Sierra Wiki's Dynamix history names Metaltech: Battledrome as the first game in the series.[^ref-5] Next Generation rated it four stars and called it "Without a doubt, the finest mech simulator available."[^ref-27] Computer Gaming World was cooler, giving it two and a half stars; its reviewer called it "the best attempt yet at creating a futuristic simulation of giant armored combat," but added, "of course, it's also the only game of its kind to come out in the last few years."[^ref-26] The Collection Chamber's reviewer called it "one of the most technically impressive games released in 1994," adding that it "is still very playable today."[^ref-6] The Digital Antiquarian counts Metaltech: EarthSiege among the "solid performers" in non-adventure games delivered that year by Dynamix, "the most consistent of Sierra's subsidiary studios."[^ref-7]

The Collection Chamber's reviewer noted that MechWarrior and the BattleTech series "got there first five years earlier," but found Dynamix's Metaltech universe ("an obvious rip-off name if ever there was one") "to be far superior."[^ref-6]

## Story Summary

The game's narrative centers on the rebellion of artificial-intelligence war machines called Cybrids against their human creators.[^ref-4] Prometheus, a prototype, was the first cybernetic-hybrid machine, or Cybrid.[^ref-4] As the backstory explains, "We created Cybrids with breakthrough AI to perform dangerous tasks with lightning precision. But we should never have taught them to fight."[^ref-9] Project: Prometheus was activated by Sentinel Cybertronix on November 29, 2471.[^ref-12] After a nuclear war fought for control of Cybrid production, the Cybrids rose against humanity in a strike known as the Overthrow, seizing every military base, space port and city.[^ref-11] The game opens twenty years after the Overthrow, as a human Resistance using hit-and-run tactics grows in strength.[^ref-11]

The central premise warns that "whoever controlled the Cybrids would control the world"—but the Cybrids now control themselves.[^ref-11] The machines fought both as individuals and as coordinated teams, all united in their goal of human annihilation.[^ref-12]

Players take the role of HERCULAN warriors who must navigate this target-rich wasteland to deliver heavily mechanized payback against the Cybrid forces.[^ref-9] Full-motion video clips from the player's commander detail each mission and keep the player in touch with the wider course of the war.[^ref-1]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Earthsiege is played from a first-person perspective inside the cockpit of the HERCs.[^ref-1][^ref-4] The mechs, known as HERCULANs or HERCs, are gigantic imposing metal beasts, and the in-game HUD definitely reflects their massive scale.[^ref-6] Each of the different HERCs comes with its own HUD, with different layouts, but ultimately they all consist of the same essential information: radar, weapons displays, damage indicators, and navigation data.[^ref-6]

The game supports various input devices including keyboard, mouse, analog joystick, and specialized flight controllers like the Thrustmaster WCS, FCS and rudders, and Flightstick Pro.[^ref-13][^ref-26] The game is single-player only.[^ref-1][^ref-4] The wealth of information given to the player at once can be quite daunting at first, but players eventually become comfortable with interpreting the complex cockpit displays.[^ref-6]

### Mech Control Systems

These hunks of metal have three different areas to control: the movement, the upper torso, and the thrust.[^ref-6] In the heat of battle, it's easy to get discombobulated by the controls, but with practice they can be mastered.[^ref-6]

The keyboard-based aiming can be imprecise, which is particularly noticeable given that enemies feature location-based damage.[^ref-6] This makes targeting specific components on enemy mechs a skill that requires practice to master.

### Structure and Progression

One MyAbandonware user praised its campaign, "with persistent squad mates, mechs, salvaging, mech construction, and damage."[^ref-15] Right from the off, there are three modes of play: Instant Action lands players straight in the middle of a warzone; Single Mission allows selection from a number of levels for a single playthrough; while Career mode pieces missions together into an ongoing story with the added bonus of scavenging for parts and upgrading mechs.[^ref-6]

Computer Gaming World stressed that matching the correct HERC to the mission, and "equipping the right HERC with the correct weaponry," were vital to success.[^ref-26] The combat takes place across multiple locations including North America, South America, Antarctica, and Asia.[^ref-11] The game has at least 5 campaigns of about 7 missions each; success or failure can trigger secondary missions and campaigns, for 45 missions in 8 campaigns in all.[^ref-4][^ref-26]

### Combat and Tactics

The most impressive aspect of Earthsiege was its AI system, described as allowing players to "imagine tactical thoughts going through your opponent's minds" despite being from 1994.[^ref-6] The Cybrid enemies will circle around players to get into the best position or hide around structures to escape fire.[^ref-6]

Enemy limbs, weapons and torso sections can each be destroyed independently, but depending on where hits land, there may not be much left to salvage for the player or their squad mates.[^ref-6]

Player squad mates are also more help than hindrance. Players can quite possibly survive a mission by letting their team do all the shooting, though they're noted as being useless at ground-to-air combat.[^ref-6] Computer Gaming World noted that a brief list of commands lets the player order comrades to patrol waypoints, attack targets or provide cover, but found that most "charge blindly into combat regardless of the pilot's skill level."[^ref-26]

One criticism of the combat is the significant amount of walking between combat encounters. The speed of mechs is quite slow, which is good when aiming shots but not so good for dodging them.[^ref-6] Traveling to checkpoints can be tedious, with players sometimes finding nothing but a single building at a waypoint, meaning another hike before seeing combat again. The recommendation for more action is to start on higher difficulty settings.[^ref-6]

### Salvage and Customization

Career mode includes a salvaging mechanic where players can recover components from destroyed enemies to upgrade their own mechs.[^ref-6] Next Generation noted that the game "forces players to carefully target their powerful weaponry... to cripple their opponents without blowing them up," since "only by collecting salvage and new technology from the husks of their foes' bots can an outclassed human army stand against inevitable defeat."[^ref-27] Computer Gaming World added that salvage is used "to build new HERCs and repair damaged ones."[^ref-26]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

| Publication | Score | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| Next Generation | 4/5 | Called it "Without a doubt, the finest mech simulator available"[^ref-27][^ref-4] |
| Computer Gaming World | 2.5/5 | Praised the "stunning graphics and animation"; criticized "overly difficult career missions" and "tiny, but irritating, glitches"[^ref-26][^ref-4] |
| Critics Average | 74% | MobyGames average of 13 critic ratings (January 2026 snapshot)[^ref-28] |

Initial reviews at the time were somewhat mixed, with the game suffering from many problems including a badly implemented career mode.[^ref-6][^ref-26] However, once updates arrived, these issues subsided. A later expansion pack that added extra environments, missions, and mechs beefed up the game even more, addressing many of the initial criticisms.[^ref-6]

### Modern Assessment

MyAbandonware users rate it 4.5/5 from 39 votes.[^ref-15] MobyGames players gave it 3.7 out of 5 from 14 ratings in a January 2026 snapshot.[^ref-28] GameFAQs users rate it "Good" based on 8 ratings.[^ref-9]

Player opinions remain divided on how Earthsiege compares to its primary competitor. Some users felt it "looked, played and felt like a poor man's Mechwarrior,"[^ref-15] while a MobyGames player review praised that "the atmosphere in this game was incredible, and in my opinion it outclassed Mechwarrior 2."[^ref-1] One Vogons forum poster noted that "Earthsiege is the game that made me upgrade from a 486 DX4-100 to a Pentium 90 and it was well worth it."[^ref-23]

The Collection Chamber retrospective concludes that "EarthSiege is one of the most technically impressive games released in 1994 and is still very playable today."[^ref-6]

## Development

### Origins

Dynamix had developed MechWarrior (1989) under its earlier publishing agreement with Activision.[^ref-2] The author of Remember PC DOS Games found no details about the decision to build a BattleTech competitor and guesses that Activision still held the BattleTech rights; on that reading, Dynamix created the Metaltech Universe as a setting it could develop over multiple games.[^ref-2] MobyGames notes that Earthsiege is not part of FASA's BattleTech universe, "though the resemblances are too close for legal comfort."[^ref-1] Sources differ on series order: Wikipedia calls Earthsiege the first Metaltech game,[^ref-4] while the Sierra Wiki names Metaltech: Battledrome as the first, released the same year.[^ref-5]

Game design is credited to Mark Crowe and David Selle, with Crowe also producing; Paul Bowman was lead programmer and Robert Caracol art director, and Tim Gift is credited for the HERC animation technology.[^ref-1] Crowe, best known for creating the Space Quest series with his fellow "Guy from Andromeda" Scott Murphy, had moved to Dynamix after Space Quest IV and worked on the Earthsiege series there.[^ref-25]

### Production

The game featured voice acting from several performers, including Andre Rosey Brown as the Service Bay Technician, John Rice as Base Commander #1, Gary Bullock as Base Commander #2, Celeste Yarnall as Base Commander #5, and Ericka Klein as Pilot #1.[^ref-20]

The original music was composed by Timothy Steven Clarke and Christopher Stevens, with sound effects and digitized speech by Ken Rogers.[^ref-1][^ref-4] A rip of the DOS soundtrack is hosted on KHInsider.[^ref-21]

In 1995, an expansion pack was released featuring a full-motion video introduction, new vehicles and weapons.[^ref-11] It added three new campaigns, new landscapes and the Apocalypse HERC, with original music by LoudMouth.[^ref-22] The expansion missions were far more diverse than the original campaign, though they were structured similarly with the standard waypoint-destroy-repeat pattern.[^ref-6] Access to the expansion content required toggling an option in the menu, which would basically reset the game with the new career mode replacing the old, with separate save files meaning salvaged weapons could not be carried over.[^ref-6]

### Technical Achievements

Earthsiege was built on Dynamix's 3-Space engine.[^ref-12] A MobyGames player review called the graphics "amazing for its time, as was the sound on the CD-rom version."[^ref-1]

The Sierra Help Pages' wiki lists the minimum requirements as an Intel i386 DX33 processor, 4 MB RAM and MS-DOS 5.0, matching Computer Gaming World's spec box (386-33 MHz, 4 MB RAM, MS-DOS 5.0),[^ref-13][^ref-26] while The Sierra Help Pages give a 386SX minimum.[^ref-14] Supported sound hardware included Sound Blaster, Pro Audio Spectrum, AdLib, Gravis Ultra Sound, Ensoniq SoundScape, and General MIDI.[^ref-14]

### Version History and Patches

The game received updates that addressed initial problems with the career mode.[^ref-6] A lockup around Campaign 4, Mission 8 after installing the Expansion Pack is fixed by a patch listed on The Sierra Help Pages;[^ref-14] a Collection Chamber commenter noted that the fix means copying its DBSIM.EXE file into the MTECH folder.[^ref-6] Another commenter suggested setting DOSBox to 74000 cycles (Pentium 120) because the game otherwise runs too fast.[^ref-6]

## Legacy

### Franchise Expansion

Earthsiege established the foundation for an extensive game universe. The immediate sequel, EarthSiege II, was "HUGE when it was released in 1996, garnering mostly scores in the top ten percentile."[^ref-24] The Metaltech universe eventually expanded to include:[^ref-4]

- **Metaltech: Battledrome** (1994) - Arena combat game in the same universe
- **Earthsiege 2** (1996) - Direct sequel with enhanced graphics and largely unchanged gameplay[^ref-24]
- **Cyberstorm** (1996) - Turn-based strategy spinoff
- **Cyberstorm 2: Corporate Wars** (1998) - Strategy sequel
- **Starsiege** (1999) - Mech combat continuation
- **Tribes** series - First-person shooters; the universe eventually became known as the Tribes Universe, after the successful series[^ref-2]

### Preservation Status

In 2015, Hi-Rez Studios released Earthsiege as freeware.[^ref-4] The game has been preserved and made playable on modern systems through the efforts of the retro gaming community. The Collection Chamber has created a custom installer using DOSBox DAUM that brings the game to modern systems, with the manual, reference card, and expansion pack included.[^ref-6] Version 2 of this preservation package (released January 27, 2023) fixed the Campaign 4, Mission 8 crash issue and improved the installer package.[^ref-6]
