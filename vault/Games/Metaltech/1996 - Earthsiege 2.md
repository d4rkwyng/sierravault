# Earthsiege 2

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

Earthsiege 2 is a mech simulation game developed by Dynamix and published by Sierra On-Line in March 1996 for Windows[^ref-1][^ref-5]. Set in 2624, the game places players in control of massive bipedal war machines called HERCs (Humaniform-Emulation Roboticized Combat Unit with Leg-Articulated Navigation) in humanity's struggle against the Cybrids[^ref-2]. The terrain and buildings are rendered with the Sierra/Dynamix 3Space engine[^ref-18], and the sequel raised the maximum resolution over its predecessor to 640x480 with 3D rolling terrain[^ref-2].

As part of the larger Metaltech universe, Earthsiege 2 continues the story of the original Metaltech: Earthsiege, in which the Cybrid HERCs "turned on their creators and initiated a 20-year guerrilla battle for control of the earth and its distant colonies"[^ref-4]. Computer Gaming World called it the best-looking giant robot game on the market, but found "nothing that will leave a lasting impression or hunger for EarthSiege 3"[^ref-27][^ref-2]. Next Generation gave it four stars out of five, concluding that "if you want fast action, good pacing, and incredible gameplay and graphics, EarthSiege 2 shines in all the right places"[^ref-28][^ref-2].

## Story Summary

The game is set in the year 2624[^ref-2]. In the series backstory, "Cybrid HERCs, created by man to do men's bidding, turned on their creators and initiated a 20-year guerrilla battle for control of the earth and its distant colonies"[^ref-4]. After the remnants of humanity destroyed the Cybrid army and regained control of Earth, a second Cybrid force arrived from the space colonies; the humans prevented the initial landings, but the Cybrids began building their strength on the Moon[^ref-2][^ref-1].

Players assume the role of a HERC pilot in humanity's defence, where "the future of human existence rests squarely on your shoulders and the metal shoulders on your fighting HERCs"[^ref-4]. As the game's own description puts it, "for them it is victory or death, and death has little meaning to a machine"[^ref-4][^ref-8]. The campaign ends with a final sequence of missions on the Moon against Prometheus[^ref-2].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Earthsiege 2 features first-person cockpit-based gameplay[^ref-9], with the option of switching between first-person and external views[^ref-2]. The player controls a HERC with the keyboard and/or joystick, while the mouse is used to interact with cockpit buttons[^ref-2]; one reviewer singled out the lack of mouse support for turning the turret during missions as a limitation[^ref-10]. Eight HERCs are available for piloting, each with its own armor, shield output, speed and number of hardpoints for mounting weapons and equipment[^ref-2].

### Structure and Progression

The campaign offers "over 50 missions in total," and "terrain variety is comprised of lava flows, snow, hills, desert and even lunar craters"[^ref-19]. Missions follow a branching structure: failing a mission does not always end the game, missions can have varying degrees of success, and the outcome can determine which mission comes next[^ref-2][^ref-1].

The player may take up to three squadmates on each mission, assigning and outfitting their HERCs, and squadmates gain experience as missions are completed[^ref-2]. Destroyed HERCs and buildings leave salvage metal, weapons and equipment, and players "must continually recycle his/her machinery and collect salvageable material from the battlefield conquests, so as not to run out of metal"[^ref-19][^ref-2]. Video briefings and debriefings frame each mission[^ref-2]; not every player warmed to them, with one MobyGames reviewer calling the briefings "really boring... sitting there listening to General Gierling going on and on"[^ref-10].

### Combat and Mechanics

As one review noted, "EarthSiege 2 demands a fine balance between action, tactical finesse, and skill"[^ref-15]. Combat takes place across 3D rolling terrain[^ref-2], and one player review praised the destruction effects: "HERCs burst into flames, which looks much more impressive and realistic than the shattering polygons of other sims"[^ref-16].

The game adds an air-capable HERC called the "Razor," which GameSpot's Chris Hudak jokingly likened to an "A-10"; he found that missions involving Razor air strikes were "tantamount to pretty decent little flight sims in their own right"[^ref-18]. Hudak summarized the game's appeal: "If I were forced at gunpoint to point newbies toward one battle-mech game, EarthSiege 2 would probably be the one"[^ref-18].

### Modern Assessment

MobyGames lists a critics' average of 81%, based on 18 ratings[^ref-9]. A Collection Chamber retrospective recalled that "EarthSiege II was HUGE when it was released in 1996, garnering mostly scores in the top ten percentile"[^ref-14]. Its stability has drawn criticism, however, with one GameFAQs reviewer writing that "Earthsiege 2 also crashes to the desktop more than any other game that was released probably ever"[^ref-20].

## Development

### Origins

Earthsiege 2 was developed by Dynamix and published by Sierra On-Line as the sequel to Metaltech: Earthsiege[^ref-1]. MyAbandonware describes it as Dynamix's "third entrant into the giant robots genre," after Battledrome and Earthsiege[^ref-3]. The game was dedicated "to the memory of Dariusz Lukaszuk (1964-1995)"[^ref-1].

### Production

The game was directed by [[Mark Crowe]], with Jay Balakrishnan as executive producer and Frank Evers as producer[^ref-1]. [[David Selle]] and Blake Hutchins are credited as designers[^ref-1].

The lead programmer was Paul Bowman, with Robert Caracol handling art direction[^ref-1].

The credited voice actors are Sam A. Mowry as narrator, Tammie Andreas as the computer voice, and John Rice[^ref-1].

The original score is credited to Loudmouth[^ref-1].

### Technical Achievements

Earthsiege 2 uses the 3Space engine, with graphics that GameSpot's reviewer found "quite a bit cleaner than those in, say, MechWarrior 2"[^ref-18], and a maximum resolution of 640x480[^ref-2]. Next Generation highlighted its SVGA graphics and texture-mapped HERCs as improvements over the first Earthsiege[^ref-28].

The game's video files use an unusual mix of codecs: Indeo 3, Indeo 4, MS RLE Video, MS Video 1 (with an Autodesk RLE stream), and Cinepak[^ref-24]. The RBXIT fan project noted that "this game probably sets the world record for most number of different codecs used in its video collection"[^ref-24].

## Legacy

Earthsiege 2 is the precursor to Starsiege, which set the stage for Starsiege: Tribes and the rest of the Tribes universe[^ref-25]. As the Sierra Wiki notes, "Metaltech: Battledrome, the first game in the new MetalTech series, was released within that same year. A giant robot combat game with similarities to the BattleTech universe and games, the MetalTech series later spawned the Earthsiege, Cyberstorm, and Tribes series"[^ref-21].

In 2015, Hi-Rez Studios (who had acquired the rights to the Tribes franchise) released the game as freeware, making it freely available to modern audiences[^ref-26]. PC Gamer reported that "Hi-Rez has made all the Tribes games available for free, as well as the Earthsiege series that predated it"[^ref-26]. One MobyGames player review summed the game up as having "challenged the mighty MechWarrior 2 series for the giant robot sim crown"[^ref-9].

### Modern Compatibility

Running Earthsiege 2 on modern systems presents challenges. PCGamingWiki documents "VSHELL.EXE - Entry Point Not Found" and "You don't have enough virtual memory to run Earthsiege II" errors on launch, and notes that the game refuses to run under Windows 10 because of broken DirectDraw support[^ref-5]. Community advice includes wrappers such as dgVoodoo 2 or DXwnd[^ref-29].

Stability problems were also reported on period hardware. A GameFAQs reviewer wrote that "Mission objectives randomly don't get completed" and that "In one hour I might've had as many as 10 crashes"[^ref-20].
