# Crazy Nick's Software Picks: King Graham's Board Game Challenge

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

**Crazy Nick's Software Picks: King Graham's Board Game Challenge** is a small board-game package that Sierra On-Line released for DOS PCs on June 5, 1992.[^ref-6] It offers two tabletop classics, Checkers and Backgammon, and casts King Graham of the King's Quest series as the player's only opponent.[^ref-2][^ref-1] It is one of five Crazy Nick's Software Picks packs, budget compilations that reused mini-games and casino or board games from Sierra's adventure and Hoyle titles.[^ref-6][^ref-4]

Both games were lifted from *Hoyle Official Book of Games Volume 3: Great Board Games* (Hoyle 3).[^ref-6] The pack runs on Sierra's SCI1.1 interpreter.[^ref-7]

## Story Summary

There is no story as such. King Graham, hero of the King's Quest series, sits across the board from the player, and the two try to see who can win more games.[^ref-1] When the player makes a mistake, Graham offers tips, and he points out when a special move is available.[^ref-1]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The games fill the whole screen and the scores carry over from game to game, as a 2011 guest post on the Super Adventures in Gaming blog noted.[^ref-9] MobyGames lists mouse input as supported or optional.[^ref-1] MobyGames' screenshots of the DOS version are 320x200.[^ref-8]

### Structure and Progression

The package contains just two games, Checkers and Backgammon.[^ref-2] PCGamingWiki lists it as singleplayer: the human plays against the computer-controlled Graham.[^ref-7] MobyGames says both games are faithful to their traditional rules and offer three skill levels.[^ref-1]

**Backgammon**

Sierra Chest summarises the rules this way. Each side races its pieces around the board in opposite directions, moving by the roll of the dice, and the first to bring every piece home wins. A piece left alone on a point can be hit, which sends it to the bar. Its owner then has to bring it back onto the board before doing anything else.[^ref-6] Doubling the stake is available, and the computer takes its turns briskly.[^ref-9]

**Checkers**

The goal is to take all of the opponent's pieces or leave them with no legal move. Each side has twelve pieces that step one square diagonally forward, or jump an adjacent enemy piece to capture it. A piece that reaches the far row is crowned a King and may then move backwards too.[^ref-6]

### Game Origins

Sierra Chest records that both games came from Hoyle 3, which also counts this title among its collections.[^ref-6] The MobyGames reviewer Katakis found that the games, interface and mechanics match Hoyle 3.[^ref-1]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

No reviews from 1992 have been found so far.

### Later Assessments

| Source | Year | Verdict | Notes |
|--------|------|---------|-------|
| MobyGames user review (Katakis) | 2015 | Negative overall | Headlined "Basically a cut-down version of Hoyle 3"; MobyGames' player average is 4.4/5 from only 2 ratings[^ref-1] |
| Super Adventures in Gaming (guest post by mecha-neko) | 2011 | Mildly positive | Praised full-screen play and persistent scoring[^ref-9] |

Katakis's 2015 review on MobyGames calls the series "overlooked." He notes that the pack holds only two games, both the same as in Hoyle 3 with a similar interface. His bottom line is "So if you played Hoyle 3, then don't bother with this."[^ref-1]

The Super Adventures in Gaming guest post from March 2011 was kinder. It doubted the "crazy" label ("These games don't sound particularly crazy to me") but liked the full-screen boards, persistent scores, doubling and quick turns. It ended: "If you want to play a couple of board games in DOS, this is certainly one way to do it."[^ref-9] The writer also called this pack an improvement on the set the blog had covered just before it, where Nine Men's Morris was, in his words, the only one that was "actually a game and not a broken mess."[^ref-9]

## Development

### Origins

Sierra Chest says that in the summer of 1992, Sierra gathered mini arcade sequences and casino and board games from earlier adventure and Hoyle titles. It released them as budget compilations in blister cards.[^ref-6] Five packs came out under the Crazy Nick's Software Picks name:[^ref-4][^ref-6]

1. **King Graham's Board Game Challenge**
2. **Leisure Suit Larry's Casino**
3. **Parlor Games with Laura Bow**
4. **Robin Hood's Games of Skill and Chance**
5. **Roger Wilco's Spaced Out Game Pack**

### Production

MobyGames credits a separate team for this pack. Bill Davis was director, Tammy Dargan and Tony Caudill were producers, J. Mark Hood was software supervisor and Thaddeus M. Pritchard was lead programmer.[^ref-21] The same credits list an "Original Board Game Challenge Team" from Hoyle 3, including game designers Marti and Bridget McKenna, lead programmer Warren Schwader and composer Robert Atesalp.[^ref-21]

Each pack came on a single 3.5" floppy disk on a blister card, and the packs were released for the PC only.[^ref-6]
