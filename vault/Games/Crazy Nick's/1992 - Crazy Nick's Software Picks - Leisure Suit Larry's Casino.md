# Crazy Nick's Software Picks: Leisure Suit Larry's Casino

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

*Not to be confused with [[1998 - Leisure Suit Larry's Casino]], Sierra's separate 1998 Windows casino game.*[^ref-16][^ref-5]

## Overview

Crazy Nick's Software Picks: Leisure Suit Larry's Casino is a budget gambling title released by [[Sierra On-Line]] on June 5, 1992 for MS-DOS.[^ref-1][^ref-15] It was one of five "Crazy Nick's Software Picks" packs, which Sierra released in the summer of 1992 as budget compilations of mini arcade sequences, casino games and board games originally included in its adventure and Hoyle games, sold in blister cards on a single 3.5" floppy.[^ref-2] This pack takes its Slot Machine and Blackjack from the 1991 VGA remake of Leisure Suit Larry 1 and its Poker from Leisure Suit Larry 5.[^ref-2]

Sierra's own InterAction magazine announced the line in its Summer 1992 issue, listing "Leisure Suit Larry's Casino: With blackjack, Poker, and Slot Machine, all rendered in the incomparable Al Lowe style," and saying the packs would come in blister packs and retail for around $10.[^ref-19] The player starts with 100.00 "Larry Dollars" and can take out a loan after losing everything.[^ref-1] The pack was later included in the Leisure Suit Larry: Collection Series (1997) and the Leisure Suit Larry: Ultimate Pleasure Pack (1999).[^ref-1]

## Story Summary

The pack has no story. It is a set of three casino games (Slot Machine, Blackjack and Poker), and the player gambles with Larry Dollars, playing with Larry's hand on screen to operate the buttons.[^ref-1][^ref-15] InterAction described the Nick's Picks packs as "favorite arcade and strategy sequences extracted from best-selling Sierra games and featuring Sierra characters as opponents."[^ref-19]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames classifies the interface as point and select. The player operates the games' buttons with Larry's hand, and a text window gives information about the bets and odds during each game.[^ref-1] No source documents the pack's keyboard controls.

### Structure and Progression

The pack contains three games:[^ref-1][^ref-2]

- **Slot Machine** (from the LSL1 VGA remake): pull the handle of the one-armed bandit; winning combinations are one or more cherries, two or more bells or sevens, or three Bars for the jackpot.[^ref-2]
- **Blackjack** (from the LSL1 VGA remake): beat the dealer without going over 21; face cards count 10 and aces 1 or 11; the player can Hit until Standing or Busting.[^ref-2]
- **Video Poker** (from Leisure Suit Larry 5): winning hands run from a pair of Jacks or Better up to a Straight Flush; after the deal the player chooses which cards to keep and draws replacements.[^ref-2]

Players begin with 100.00 Larry Dollars, and a loan is available to a player who loses everything.[^ref-1] Money won in one game carries over to the others.[^ref-11] The game has no save feature.[^ref-5]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

There are no puzzles. Each game is a straightforward casino game, played against the house for Larry Dollars.[^ref-1][^ref-2]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

No period reviews of the pack have been found. The Sierra Chest lists "No reviews yet," MobyGames has no critic reviews on file, and the only 1992 publication the Sierra Chest indexes for the pack is InterAction's own announcement.[^ref-23][^ref-22][^ref-25][^ref-19]

### Modern Assessment

The only user review found is a 2012 comment by "paperbrain" on MyAbandonware: "Just as it's name suggests, this is a collection of all the video poker and slots games from Larry 1 and 5. Recommended if you are a fan of both Leisure Suit Larry and video gambling. However, If you were bored with the gambling sequences in the Larry games, don't bother."[^ref-3]

A 2012 editor note on the Universal Videogame List says the pack "was also included in the Leisure Suit Larry Collectors Edition CD-ROM compilation" and that its three casino games "are not really 'new' games however. They appeared in the Leisure Suit Larry 1 remake as well."[^ref-4]

**User ratings:**
- **MobyGames:** 3.0 out of 5 from a single player rating, with no written reviews[^ref-22]
- **MyAbandonware:** 3/5 from 4 votes on one listing[^ref-6], and 4.33/5 from 12 votes on a duplicate listing[^ref-3]

## Development

### Origins

InterAction introduced Crazy Nick as "the hack 'n' slash hero of discount software buyers everywhere" and Nick's Picks as "a line of low-priced (but high-quality) VGA games" due in software stores in May or June 1992. The magazine said all five packs were "contained on a single disk for quick bursts of computer entertainment" and featured "VGA graphics and major sound card support."[^ref-19]

### Production

**Development Credits:**[^ref-1]
- **Director:** [[Bill Davis]]
- **Producers:** [[Tammy Dargan]], Tony Caudill
- **Software Supervisor:** J. Mark Hood
- **Lead Programmer:** Thaddeus M. Pritchard
- **Quality Assurance:** Michael Brosius
- **Special Thanks:** Randy MacNeill

MobyGames credits the same seven-person team on the other Crazy Nick's packs, including King Graham's Board Game Challenge, Roger Wilco's Spaced Out Game Pack and Parlor Games with Laura Bow.[^ref-1]

The music also comes from the LSL1 VGA remake: the Sierra Chest says "the songs are also taken from the soundtrack of Leisure Suit Larry 1 VGA, although the main theme is a shortened version." It lists two pieces, the game-selection music (the Larry Theme) and a Casino Theme, each in Roland MT-32 and Sound Blaster versions.[^ref-20]

### Technical Issues

The game has no savegame feature.[^ref-5] ScummVM supports the game.[^ref-5][^ref-24]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

The Larry Theme heard in the pack was first written for the 1987 original Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards. Al Lowe writes that he composed it on May 11, 1987, in about 20 minutes, inspired by Irving Berlin's "Alexander's Ragtime Band".[^ref-12]

## Series Continuity

MobyGames places the pack in the Leisure Suit Larry series between [[1991 - Leisure Suit Larry 1 - In the Land of the Lounge Lizards (VGA)]], which supplied its slots, blackjack and music, and [[1993 - Leisure Suit Larry 6 - Shape Up or Slip Out!]].[^ref-1][^ref-18] Its poker game comes from [[1991 - Leisure Suit Larry 5 - Passionate Patti Does a Little Undercover Work]].[^ref-2] The later [[1998 - Leisure Suit Larry's Casino]] is a different game for Windows, with its own credits and reviews.[^ref-16]
