# Aces of the Deep

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

Aces of the Deep is a World War II submarine simulator developed by Dynamix for MS-DOS and published by Sierra On-Line in 1994[^ref-1][^ref-14]. It was the last installment of Dynamix's Aces series, after the flight simulators Red Baron, Aces of the Pacific and Aces Over Europe[^ref-14]. The game puts players in command of German U-boats in the period between September 1939 and May 1945, when "Germany launched over 1,000 U-boats into combat against the Allies"[^ref-2]. Computer Gaming World said that "Aces of the Deep sets the standard for submarine simulations, perhaps even simulations in general"[^ref-2], and in 1996 ranked it the 71st best game of all time[^ref-14].

Players take the role of a U-boat commander, stalking enemy convoys in what the game describes as "a battle where the hunter quickly becomes the hunted"[^ref-2]. As a GOG Dreamlist summary puts it, players "take command of one of seven German U-Boats during WWII and hunt down and destroy allied convoys," with campaigns that can span the entire war[^ref-4]. The game is single-player only[^ref-8][^ref-14].

## Story Summary

Aces of the Deep has no single narrative. It places players in the U-boat campaign of the Battle of the Atlantic[^ref-3], where "submersible war machines prowled the stormy seas, striking without warning as deadly phantoms of the deep"[^ref-2]. Players "Attempt to strangle shipping lanes and outmaneuver convoy escorts" and "React as technological advances, such as sonar and radar, wreak havoc with your mission"[^ref-2].

The campaign can start in October 1939. Reviewer Richard Agnew describes a strategic map "stretching from the eastern coast of N.America to Greenland in the north, Africa and S. America down south, and encompassing all of Europe and part of western USSR"[^ref-7]. The operational area runs from the North Sea to the Gulf of Mexico, and the Expansion Disk later added the Mediterranean[^ref-3][^ref-10]. Missions are not scripted, because the engine "rolls up" convoys and targets according to the historical date and the submarine in use[^ref-4]. One GOG user notes that radio messages report real events, and that actions such as the hunt for the Bismarck and the loss of HMS Ark Royal appear in the ocean areas where they happened[^ref-4].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The Control Room is "the command center of your U-boat". It gives point-and-click access to the Torpedo Data Computer, Bridge, Torpedo Room, Radio Room, Damage Report and Captain's Log, and the Tactical and Strategic Charts are reached from it as well[^ref-9]. Quick keys include Periscope Depth [P] and Crash Dive [C][^ref-9]. The most important functions, including the sea map used to set waypoints, can be brought up at any time, and the crew can take over tasks such as identifying ships[^ref-4]. The game supports VGA and SVGA video modes[^ref-8].

### Structure and Progression

The game offers single missions against a convoy, a warship task force or a carrier task force, along with historical missions that recreate the patrols of famous U-boat captains[^ref-14]. In Career Mode the player picks a starting year and earns promotions, medals and newer U-boats[^ref-14]. Three U-boat types (II, VII and IX) are available in several variants as they historically appeared[^ref-3][^ref-14]. Realism and difficulty can be tuned with ten options[^ref-4].

Time compression reaches 4096x, though Agnew noted that "quite often the computer will slow you down to a max of 150 or so. This can go on for 5 or 10 minutes"[^ref-7].

The Expansion Disk (1995) works with either the floppy or CD versions. It adds "six new historical missions", access to the Mediterranean Sea, and the "prototype Type XXI U-boat, which includes sonar and radar abilities as well as two kinds of torpedo types for its exclusive use"[^ref-10]. Mike Jones recalled that the Expansion Pack "was conceived of prior to the release of AOD"[^ref-11].

### Mechanics

The game includes merchant ships, troop ships, escorts such as corvettes, frigates and destroyers, larger warships and anti-submarine aircraft, all appearing in line with their historical service[^ref-14]. Escorts adopt new technologies such as Asdic and new tactics such as convoying as the war goes on, while the player gains the snorkel, radar and guided torpedoes[^ref-3]. Agnew praised the "outstanding gameplay, ocean graphics, and sounds. The asdic 'pings' will raise the hair on the back of your neck! As will the ever increasing whirr of the escort ships' propellers as they pass over your u-boat"[^ref-13].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

SUBSIM Review wrote that "Aces was received with critical acclaim upon its release"[^ref-11]. Computer Gaming World ranked it 71st in its 1996 list of the best games of all time, calling it "still unrivaled for creating an authentic atmosphere"[^ref-14].

### Modern Assessment

The German Videospiele Wiki calls it "spielerisch der Vorläufer aller moderneren U-Boot-Simulationen mit historischem Szenario, vor allem der Silent-Hunter-Reihe" (in gameplay terms, the forerunner of all modern submarine simulations with a historical setting, above all the Silent Hunter series)[^ref-3]. A 2007 GameSpot user review, rated 9.8 by its author, was titled "The Best Submarine Simulation before Silent Hunter!"[^ref-12]. GOG Dreamlist users call it "likely the best uboat simulator released, the attention to details is stunning and realistic" and "the grandfather of sub sims"[^ref-4]. Richard Agnew's MobyGames review concludes: "I can't think of a game that makes me feel more like I am actually there, participating and sharing the fate of my submarine"[^ref-7].

## Development

### Origins

Mike Jones was producer and lead designer, and one of the programmers[^ref-11]. He told SUBSIM that a submarine simulation was one of the projects he proposed when he joined Dynamix in 1992, and that the team chose the Battle of the Atlantic because "I didn't think that the few titles out that modeled U-boats had come close to doing justice to that side of the war"[^ref-11]. He said: "Working on a product like AOD was labor of love. Our entire development team was immersed in the history of the U-boat campaign for two years"[^ref-11].

For the CD version the team interviewed U-boat veterans. MobyGames credits list the interviewees as Admiral Erich Topp, Reinhard Hardegen, Admiral Otto Kretschmer, Gerd Thäter, Hans-Georg Hess, Jürgen Oesten, Otto Westphalen and Wilhelm Grap, with translation by Barbara Beyer, Kai Brinkmann and Mireille Wheeldon[^ref-1]. Jones described going to Germany to interview Topp, Kretschmer and others as "an amazing experience for the team members that went"[^ref-11].

### Production

The game had two art directors. Kyle Miller came first, from a cartoon-animation background, and Jarrett Jester finished the project[^ref-11]. The rolling waves began as a proof-of-concept demo by Tim Gift, made by "introducing sine wave motion to a grid and texturing it"[^ref-11]. Programmer Peter Lukaszuk then developed the waves seen in the game, which "hardly take any processor time"[^ref-11]. Jones recalled: "People would come up to me at shows and asked how we did the waves, and I could honestly reply, 'I don't know'"[^ref-11]. Audio used the "Human Machine Interfaces Inc., Sound Operating System", and the original music is by Jan Paul Moorhead[^ref-1].

### Technical Achievements

The Videospiele Wiki describes its 3D graphics engine as "für ihre Zeit revolutionär" (revolutionary for its time). It showed ships and aircraft as textured 3D objects in place of the "holprige Rastergrafiken" (choppy raster graphics) of earlier games such as Silent Service and 688 Attack Sub. Explosions and coastlines were still 2D bitmaps[^ref-3]. The game's music plays from the boat's gramophone and only while cruising, not during combat[^ref-3]. The MobyGames credits list "3Space Shape Modeling" by Damon Mitchell and Vance Naegle[^ref-1].

PCGamingWiki lists a minimum of DOS 5.0, an Intel 386DX 33 MHz and 4 MB RAM[^ref-8]. The Videospiele Wiki gives "MS-DOS ab Version 5.0" and hard disk space of "min 200 KByte, normal 4 MByte, max 11 MByte"[^ref-3].

## Legacy

SUBSIM Review's 1998 introduction to its Mike Jones interview said: "In the area of submarine simulations, it was Aces of the Deep. Aces was a huge leap forward in every facet of the submarine sim; graphics, music, gameplay, and most importantly, realism and historical accuracy," and "No other subsim before or after Aces of the Deep has captured the ambiance and tension quite so well"[^ref-11].

Jones gave the Leader Board as an example of the team's research: "So when you see your name up there with Prien or Kretschmer, know that you are tracking with their actual tonnage sunk at that moment in the war." He added that convoys sail "when and where they should be"[^ref-11]. He said there was no collaboration with Silent Hunter, though he was "flattered that Silent Hunter has some elements of the AOD interface in it"[^ref-11].

The series did not continue. Wikipedia describes Aces of the Deep as "the last installment of Dynamix's Aces series"[^ref-14]. Jones said: "I would have liked to see the AOD line continue, but it didn't"[^ref-11].
