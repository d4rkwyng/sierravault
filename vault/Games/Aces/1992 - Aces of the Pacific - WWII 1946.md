# Aces of the Pacific: WWII: 1946

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

*WWII: 1946* is an expansion disk for [[1992 - Aces of the Pacific|Aces of the Pacific]], developed by [[Dynamix]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] for MS-DOS in late 1992.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-3] Sierra's own magazine called it "the first expansion disk for Aces of the Pacific".[^ref-4] It asks what would have happened if the atomic bomb had never been used and the United States had been forced to invade Japan. To fly that war, it adds seven aircraft that were still prototypes or barely in service in 1945, including the first American and Japanese jet fighters.[^ref-4][^ref-5] Aces of the Pacific is required to play it.[^ref-5][^ref-6]

[[Damon Slye]] is credited as designer and director.[^ref-5][^ref-2] The add-on was sold as part of Dynamix's "Great War Planes" line, which Sierra advertised alongside the *Red Baron Mission Builder* expansion.[^ref-7][^ref-6]

*Computer Gaming World*'s Doug Fick enjoyed flying the new machines but wished the disk had more in it. He judged it "a nice add-on for devoted Aces fans, but ... not essential to enjoying a game that is already very good".[^ref-5][^ref-8]

## Story Summary

The manual opens by acknowledging that the atomic bombings "remain a source of unending controversy". It then sets out two parallel histories. "WWII: 1945 — As It Happened" covers the real end of the war, and "WWII: 1946 — What If" is what the manual calls "a historian's fictional interpretation" written by "the Dynamix historical research staff".[^ref-9]

Bob Lindstrom's article in Sierra's *InterAction* magazine sums up the team's premise in three changes to history.[^ref-4]

1. The Soviet Union stays out of the Pacific war.
2. The atomic bomb project stalls, and its scientists begin to have moral doubts about their work.
3. Japanese militarists regain control of the government and resolve to fight to the end.

In the manual's version, the army and its allies murder Prime Minister Suzuki and his peace faction at the Imperial Palace on August 20, 1945. Admiral Onishi, the creator of the kamikaze corps, is installed as premier. President Truman, without a working bomb, approves Operation Olympic, the invasion of Kyushu.[^ref-9][^ref-4] The invasion of Kyushu goes badly. Lindstrom's summary has Marine units on the western beaches thrown back into the sea. After that, Operation Coronet lands on either side of Tokyo Bay on March 20, 1946, later than planned.[^ref-4][^ref-9] The war ends in January 1947, when Japan's leaders sign a surrender aboard the USS *Iowa*.[^ref-9]

The manual gives American losses of 295,125 killed in action. In its version, Truman loses the 1948 Democratic primaries and Thomas Dewey wins the presidency.[^ref-9] Wikipedia says the game itself calls the extended campaign Operation Coronet.[^ref-8] A retail listing in *Computer Gaming World* described the disk as including "a fictional scenario of Operation Olympic".[^ref-10]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

*WWII: 1946* adds content to Aces of the Pacific and does not change its controls or cockpit interface. Fick's review describes it as adding aircraft, aces, a campaign and scenarios to the existing game, which "is required to play 1946".[^ref-5] Like the base game, it runs on Dynamix's 3-Space engine.[^ref-3]

### Structure and Progression

According to Fick's review, the expansion adds:[^ref-5]

- **A new campaign:** it starts in late 1945 and runs "until the hypothetical land invasion is resolved".
- **Veteran pilots:** pilots who retired at the end of an Aces of the Pacific career can be recalled for the new war.
- **Career missions:** the mission types are the same as in Aces of the Pacific, with new dates, aircraft and aces.
- **New historical scenarios:** several, written for the 1946 setting.
- **Single missions:** all the new aircraft and aces can be flown in single-mission mode.

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The expansion's main draw is its aircraft. Sierra and *Computer Gaming World* list the same seven:[^ref-4][^ref-5][^ref-1]

| Aircraft | Side | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|
| Grumman F7F Tigercat | United States | Twin-engine carrier fighter[^ref-4] |
| Grumman F8F Bearcat | United States | *InterAction* called it and the Tigercat "two of the finest prop-driven planes ever built"[^ref-6] |
| Goodyear F2G-2 Corsair | United States | A "souped-up, 3,000 hp engine version of the Corsair"[^ref-6] |
| Lockheed P-80 Shooting Star | United States | Early jet fighter[^ref-5] |
| Kyushu J7W Shinden | Japan | Canard design that, in Fick's words, "seems to be flying backward"[^ref-5] |
| Mitsubishi Ki-83 | Japan | Twin-engine fighter. Fick: "truly a killer"[^ref-5] |
| Nakajima Kikka | Japan | Jet fighter that Fick compared to the German Me-262[^ref-5] |

Fick found that the jets changed the style of combat. With top speeds nearly 100 miles an hour faster than piston aircraft, they suit "a more high speed style of hit and run". He found the Kikka so slow in roll response that the only way to fight in it was to keep the speed up and slash through the enemy. At low speed, he wrote, "propellor driven aircraft once again reign supreme".[^ref-5] Sierra's own advertising made the same point: "You'll have to adapt to a considerably faster combat environment, where mastery of hit-and-run tactics separates active pilots from late, great ones."[^ref-6]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

| Publication | Date | Verdict |
|-------------|------|---------|
| *Computer Gaming World* #104 (Doug Fick) | March 1993 | Positive, but "not essential"[^ref-5][^ref-8] |

Fick's review, "A Flight Into the Hypothetical", opened with the decision to end the war "with a nuclear punctuation point" and called the disk "the ultimate 'What if?'".[^ref-5] He enjoyed flying the new aircraft, but compared it unfavorably with *Red Baron Mission Builder*, which had added "a very powerful mission builder that brought new life to the program". Without one, he concluded, "the Aces add-on should be considered more of an aircraft expansion disk, like those for the LucasArts SWOTL system, than any sort of program addition".[^ref-5]

### Modern Assessment

Little retrospective criticism of the add-on was found. It does appear in the *PC Research* "Hits List of Top-Selling Software" for December 1992, reprinted in *Computer Gaming World*. Under MS-DOS games it is listed as "WWII 1946: Aces Mission Disk (Sierra On-Line)", alongside Aces of the Pacific itself.[^ref-11] MobyGames lists no critic score for it.[^ref-1]

## Development

### Origins

Sierra's *InterAction* magazine previewed *WWII: 1946* in its Summer 1992 issue with the question: "Have you ever wondered what would have happened if President Truman had decided against dropping the bomb?"[^ref-12] By the Fall 1992 issue, Sierra's "Coming up from Sierra and Dynamix" update reported the disk "about three-quarters" finished. The research, aircraft shapes and artwork were all done, and release was scheduled for early November.[^ref-6] The Winter 1992 issue still listed it as "Scheduled for release in November".[^ref-4]

### Production

Lindstrom's article describes how the team built the scenario. Slye and his team looked at the aircraft still in prototype in August 1945 and chose seven. They then "brainstormed for several weeks" on how the war might have gone after August 1945.[^ref-4] The team's historians used the official Marine Corps and Army histories to learn where and when the invasions were to take place and which units would take part. They also read speculative fiction about the invasion, notably the novel *The Burning Mountain*.[^ref-4] The manual's bibliography adds published histories, including Toshikazu Kase's *Journey to the Missouri* and E. Bartlett Kerr's *Flames Over Tokyo*.[^ref-9]

## Legacy

The expansion was later sold bundled with the base game as *Aces of the Pacific with WWII: 1946*.[^ref-14] Wikipedia and the Dynamix fan wiki both describe the 1946 campaign as an alternate-history continuation available once the expansion is installed.[^ref-8][^ref-15]

The Dynamix team itself was proud of the alternate history. Lindstrom concluded that "Dynamix however, found it can be a rewarding activity and can often add insight into the real implications of authentic historical situations".[^ref-4] The manual's introduction strikes a more sober note. It says its speculative version "joins the opinions of many historians in concluding that alternative resolutions to WWII may have held even more dire consequences than the historical reality".[^ref-9]
