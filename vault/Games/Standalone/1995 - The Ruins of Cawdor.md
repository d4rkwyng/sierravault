# The Ruins of Cawdor

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 14, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Ruins of Cawdor was the last of three graphical MUDs made for [[Sierra On-Line]]'s online service The Sierra Network, which later became The ImagiNation Network (INN).[^ref-1] It was released online in 1995, developed by Ybarra Productions and published by Sierra On-Line.[^ref-2] Richard Aronson designed it and co-programmed it.[^ref-3] It followed [[1991 - The Shadow of Yserbius|The Shadow of Yserbius]] and [[1993 - Fates of Twinion|The Fates of Twinion]] (1993).[^ref-7] It used the same engine as its predecessors, but it had a completely new plot based on William Shakespeare's *Macbeth*.[^ref-3]

The game ran on the same engine as the earlier games. Players created a character from one of six guilds (thief, barbarian, cleric, knight, wizard or ranger) and explored the six floors of Castle Cawdor. Along the way they fought battles, solved puzzles, and found secret doors and magic items.[^ref-3][^ref-5] The French website Abandonware France described the game as "bourré de pièges, d'énigmes et de téléporteurs" (packed with traps, puzzles and teleporters). It judged Cawdor "nettement moins basique que les deux autres" (clearly less basic than the other two).[^ref-6]

Cawdor came out only about a year before INN shut down in 1996.[^ref-4] Yserbius and Twinion were sold as offline boxed versions, but Cawdor never got an offline release.[^ref-1][^ref-6] It could not be played offline until a patch appeared in 2006.[^ref-6]

## Story Summary

The Ruins of Cawdor took its story from Shakespeare's *Macbeth*. Massively OP called it "essentially Macbeth redone for an online gaming audience."[^ref-3][^ref-4] One player who finished the earlier games described Cawdor as a "Macbeth-inspired place of madness". He said its NPCs talk in Early Modern English and its encounter descriptions "constantly break the 4th wall".[^ref-12]

Its setting was separate from the earlier games: Yserbius and Twinion "are fantasy games set on the same island", while Cawdor moved the action to its own Macbeth-themed location.[^ref-12] Players faced an army of enemies and NPCs across the six floors of the castle.[^ref-4]

There is a real Cawdor Castle in Scotland. Abandonware France pointed out that, unlike the game's castle, it is not a ruin.[^ref-6]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The Ruins of Cawdor reused the engine of The Shadow of Yserbius.[^ref-3] That engine used a first-person view with the character statistics on screen and a point-and-click interface. *Computer Gaming World* compared the setup to the original *Bard's Tale*.[^ref-27] MobyGames lists the game's perspective as first-person.[^ref-3]

In the original online games, each player ran one character. Up to three other players could join that character to form a party of four.[^ref-26]

### Structure and Progression

The game took place on the six floors of Castle Cawdor.[^ref-4][^ref-5] Each player created a character from one of six guilds: thief, barbarian, cleric, knight, wizard or ranger.[^ref-5]

The game's own sources do not describe a floor-by-floor progression or a set role for each guild.

An RPG Codex forum player gave some notes on how the trilogy played:[^ref-12]
- Maps usually regenerate when you enter them again.
- In Cawdor, fixed encounters regenerate when you leave a map, and some quests depend on winning every fixed encounter on a map.
- Training points from levelling up go into skills and spells.

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Abandonware France singled out the game's traps, puzzles and teleporters.[^ref-6] The same RPG Codex player offered a different view. He found the dungeons of Yserbius and Twinion very complex, with backdoors and teleports. By contrast, he said "Cawdor maps are easy and don't have this kind of complexity."[^ref-12]

Combat was turn-based and party-based.[^ref-5][^ref-12] The forum notes describe some details of the system. Protection spells take effect on the next round. The "Control" spell is recommended as the best opening spell because it lets the enemies "take care of themselves".[^ref-12]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

No contemporary review of The Ruins of Cawdor has been found. MobyGames lists no critic reviews.[^ref-3] We also searched the period *Computer Gaming World* issues held in our archive, and none of them mention Cawdor. The coverage that does survive is about its predecessors, which ran on the same engine.

- **Shadow of Yserbius (May 1993):** Samuel Baker reviewed the online version in *Computer Gaming World*. He concluded that "those who possess the money would be well served to try out TSN and *Yserbius*."[^ref-26]
- **The 1994 offline boxed sets (February 1994):** Bernie Yee reviewed the boxed versions of *Yserbius* and *Twinion* for *CGW*. He was much cooler about them: "Playing *Yserbius* without fellow on-line gamers is like being in an amusement park after hours, one in which the rides aren't all that fun to begin with."[^ref-27]
- **Online Game of the Year (June 1994):** *CGW* named its finalists for On-Line Game of the Year. The ImagiNation Network's entry was "a new version of *Shadow of Yserbius: Fates of Twinion*". The award went to *Multi-Player BattleTech*.[^ref-25] This was more than a year before Cawdor was released, so Cawdor itself was not part of that nomination.

### Modern Assessment

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** player average 4.7 out of 5, from 2 ratings. There is no critic MobyScore.[^ref-3]
- **MyAbandonware:** 5/5 user rating, from 8 votes.[^ref-16]

On RPG Codex, a player who played the trilogy offline (Cawdor needs the patch) noted that solo play means a "one man party". Because of that, he said, the games "are hard, but not really hard".[^ref-12] Other posters in the same thread regretted that the offline versions do not allow full party creation.[^ref-12]

## Development

### Origins

Massively OP describes the background. *The Shadow of Yserbius* was a big hit for Sierra on The Sierra Network. After AT&T took over the service and renamed it The ImagiNation Network, the new owner greenlit two sequels on the same engine: *The Fates of Twinion* and *The Ruins of Cawdor*.[^ref-4]

Richard Aronson posted his own account on Wikipedia in 2009: "I designed "The Ruins of Cawdor" for TSN (when I started on it) and INN (as it was named when the dungeon game shipped)." He also said he worked at TSN/INN/WorldPlay "as the Bridge/RPG designer/programmer from April 1993 until November 1997". He named Stuart Moulder as his producer "until he left for Microsoft in 1994 or so".[^ref-10]

Aronson also told Massively OP how he fixed the Twinion code: "I fixed all the system code fatals in *Twinion* … since [*Ruins of Cawdor*] was on the same code base, but I seriously redesigned everything for more balance."[^ref-4]

The decision to base the plot on *Macbeth* gave Cawdor a different literary source from the earlier games.[^ref-3]

### Production

MobyGames credits the game to Ybarra Productions.[^ref-3] Joe Ybarra had been an Electronic Arts producer. In 1992 Sierra's *InterAction* magazine described him as "the product leader on the original Bard's Tale games" and on Konami's *Spellcaster*.[^ref-28] *Computer Gaming World* later noted that "Joe Ybarra, a *Bard's Tale* implementor, contributed to the *Yserbius* design."[^ref-27]

Cawdor's development spanned the change from The Sierra Network to the AT&T-owned ImagiNation Network.[^ref-4][^ref-10] Aronson said he named INN's newsletter, *Imaginings*, and that it carried a preview interview with him about Cawdor.[^ref-10]

Aronson had also been working on systems that would be useful in online RPGs, including chat features, a quest generator and behavioural AI. Massively OP's account does not say whether these systems shipped in Cawdor.[^ref-4]

### Technical Issues

The Ruins of Cawdor depended on The ImagiNation Network. Unlike its two predecessors, it never had a solo or offline edition. It could not be played offline until it was patched in 2006.[^ref-1][^ref-6]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- Cawdor Castle is a real castle in Scotland, and it is not a ruin.[^ref-6]
- Massively OP and ClassicReload both describe designer Richard Aronson as the author of the tabletop RPG humour story "Eric and the Gazebo".[^ref-1][^ref-4]
- The game's NPCs speak in Early Modern English.[^ref-12]
- Its encounter descriptions often break the fourth wall.[^ref-12]
- Richard Aronson voiced Cedric the Owl in *King's Quest V*.[^ref-4][^ref-29]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Cawdor was released only about a year before The ImagiNation Network shut down in 1996. Partly because of this, it "isn't as well remembered or discussed today" as its predecessors.[^ref-4] No sales figures are known.

### Collections

Unlike *Yserbius* and *Twinion*, Cawdor never got an offline boxed edition.[^ref-1] It survives mainly through abandonware preservation sites.[^ref-16][^ref-18]

### Fan Projects

A patch released in 2006 made the game playable offline.[^ref-6]

The game has been preserved at MyAbandonware, ClassicReload and the Internet Archive.[^ref-16][^ref-1][^ref-18]

### Related Publications

No hint book or strategy guide for The Ruins of Cawdor has been found. Aronson recalled that INN's *Imaginings* newsletter ran a preview interview with him about the game.[^ref-10]

### Critical Perspective

Cawdor was the last of the three graphical MUDs from Sierra's online network.[^ref-1] Massively OP covered Twinion and Cawdor in its "Game Archaeologist" series as an example of online RPGs that came long before *World of Warcraft*.[^ref-4] Cawdor's *Macbeth* source gave it a different identity from the two fantasy-island games that came before it.[^ref-3][^ref-12]
