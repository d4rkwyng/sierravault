# Stay Tooned!

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 7, 2026</small>

## Overview

Stay Tooned! is a 1996 point-and-click cartoon adventure game developed by Funnybone Interactive and published by Sierra On-Line on a single CD-ROM for Windows 95, Windows 3.1 and Macintosh[^ref-16][^ref-21][^ref-3][^ref-12]. The box cover subtitles it "Unsweetened Multi-Game Experience!"[^ref-21][^ref-2]. Cartoon characters escape from the player's television, steal the remote control and turn the player's apartment building into a cartoon playground; the player must find the remote and zap the toons back into TV land[^ref-16][^ref-21].

Sierra's launch press release promised "over 30 zany games" built into the adventure, and the building has 20 apartments plus a roof and a basement[^ref-16][^ref-13]. PC Magazine's December 17, 1996 holiday gift guide likewise described "over 30 games," five of them in 3-D, across "over 50 intriguing environments," for $40[^ref-27]. Rooms and games are shuffled between playthroughs, which reviewers in 1996 and later noted added to its replay value[^ref-16][^ref-17][^ref-13]. The box included clear-cellophane "ToonVision" 3-D glasses with ChromaDepth lenses for a handful of 3-D games[^ref-16][^ref-21].

Stay Tooned! was built with Macromedia Director[^ref-3][^ref-15]. Funnybone Interactive, based in Canton, Connecticut, had been founded in 1985 as The Cute Company and became a division of Davidson & Associates in February 1995[^ref-16][^ref-18].

## Story Summary

The game opens in an ordinary apartment building. The player relaxes in an easy chair, remote in hand, and channel-surfs through cartoons, commercials and spoofs such as "Whinefeld", "Frauds" and "Schlepardy"[^ref-16]. Pressing a mysterious red button on the remote releases the toons from their Saturday-morning shows. They steal the remote and transform the building into a "loony playground"[^ref-16][^ref-2]. The manual describes "five wacky cartoon characters" who have run off and hidden the remote somewhere in the building[^ref-21].

The five main toons are Fiddle, Schmooze, Scoops and the twins Pixel and Chisel[^ref-16][^ref-23]. Wikipedia's character list describes Pixel as the leader of the group and Chisel as her twin brother and second-in-command[^ref-2]. Other characters include the strongman Frank, the mad scientist Dr. Pickles and Al Extra Bucks, host of the "Schleopardy!" game show[^ref-26][^ref-16][^ref-19][^ref-2]. Early on, Frank warns the player: "Watch out, 'cause us toons are gonna make your life miserable!" (as transcribed on TV Tropes)[^ref-4]

To restore order the player must find the missing remote and zap the toons back into TV land[^ref-16]. Along the way the toons try to hinder the player. As Creative Director Susan Decker put it in the press release, "the characters will steal your cursor, or throw a cream pie at the screen to get in your way"[^ref-16]. In the ending, as described on Wikipedia and TV Tropes, the toons take their revenge after being captured. Pixel says, "Chisel, we can't let them get away with this!" and Chisel answers, "That's right, buddy, you're coming with us," before pulling the player into TV land[^ref-4][^ref-2].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Stay Tooned! is played from a first-person perspective with fixed, flip-screen scenes, using point-and-select mouse controls[^ref-3][^ref-5]. The Electric Playground found "the controls are easy to use" but advised a responsive mouse for some of the mini-games[^ref-17]. Holding down the Control key and clicking skips an animation[^ref-21][^ref-13].

### Structure and Progression

Players explore a five-storey building with 20 apartments, plus the roof and basement[^ref-16]. The Collection Chamber counts 22 areas in all: 20 apartment rooms, a cellar and a rooftop[^ref-13]. Most apartments start locked. Players obtain keys "by uncovering their secret hiding places or by winning them in games," and a map in each hallway shows which apartments the player has yet to visit[^ref-16][^ref-3][^ref-21]:

- **20 Apartments:** each "tooned" into a different landscape, "from prehistoric to futuristic"[^ref-21][^ref-16]
- **Basement:** part of the explorable building; one of the manual's hints mentions bribing the rats there[^ref-16][^ref-21]
- **Roof:** the top of the explorable building[^ref-16][^ref-13]

The press release promises "over 30 actual games," including arcade games, shoot-'em-ups, puzzles and brain teasers[^ref-16]. Games are randomly placed so that each playthrough differs, and the apartments shuffle when a new game starts[^ref-16][^ref-17]. The Collection Chamber notes that rooms tied to a story joke or item, such as the Shakespeare room "2B", stay in place[^ref-13].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Many puzzles are what lead programmer and co-designer Ben Howard called adventure-game "switches," which require "manipulating one environment in order to affect another." His example was a giant doughnut named Duncan who blocks a doorway until the player phones 911 for a police officer[^ref-16][^ref-24]. Named games include "Kartoon Kombat" (a *Mortal Kombat* spoof), "Fridge Fight," "Schlepardy!" (a trivia show spoofing *Jeopardy!*, spelled "Schleopardy!" in some later write-ups) and "The Quick and the Tooned"[^ref-16][^ref-13]. Channel-surfing also turns up "Whinefeld," a *Seinfeld* spoof[^ref-16][^ref-17].

The manual marks five games as ToonVision 3-D: "Ask Frank", "Ham-a-roids", "Make Way for the Highway", "Bubble Trouble" and "D-Ding Dong" (3-D ping pong), the last three located in Apartment 3D[^ref-21][^ref-13]. The manual notes that the glasses are not strictly required: "You don't necessarily need the TOONVISION glasses to play the 3D games, they just make everything look much cooler!"[^ref-21]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

MobyGames lists an average critic score of 76% from five ratings: Gamezilla 83%, HonestGamers 80%, World Village (Gamer's Zone) 80%, The Electric Playground 75% (Windows and Mac) and Gamecenter 60%[^ref-20][^ref-3].

Bonnie James of The Electric Playground gave the game 7.5 out of 10 on December 5, 1996. She called it "Itchy and Scratchy meets Tiny Toons" and praised its humour as "truly unique." She concluded: "If you find yourself getting up Saturday mornings to watch cartoons in your pajamas and eat sugar cereal on the couch, then I think you'll like Stay Tooned"[^ref-17].

MacAddict's March 1997 issue (#7) had a panel of ten-year-old Brisbane Elementary School students review it among its kids' CD-ROMs. The magazine listed it for grades "5 to Adult" and its final report read: "Wise-cracking game 'toons' kids in to real quality programming." Holly liked the opening TV channels such as "Whinefeld" and "Frauds," Hilary praised the "very, very good graphics and colors," and Max's one complaint was "This program has a lot of bad words"[^ref-28].

World Village's Gamer's Zone praised the graphics ("It is almost like watching Saturday morning cartoons") and "the high level of randomization." Its reviewer was less impressed by the ToonVision glasses and wrote that "this attempt still leaves a lot to be desired"[^ref-24].

### Modern Assessment

PC Computing's March 1998 software roundup gave it three of five circles, calling it "a game oscillating somewhere between a children's game and an adult satire." The unsigned review found that the adults-oriented satires "don't last long" and advised: "Turn this one over to the kids, turn down the volume on the PC, and take two aspirin"[^ref-29].

In a January 2006 staff review for HonestGamers (4/5), Zack Little called it "a decade-old graphic adventure that gets wrongly marked as edutainment"[^ref-25]. The Collection Chamber's 2018 retrospective by Austin Brewer suggested it is "probably one of Sierra's least remembered titles." Brewer enjoyed the "Schleopardy!" mini-game but called the mini-games in general "shameless clones of other better known games"[^ref-13].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames Critics:** 76% (5 ratings)[^ref-3][^ref-20]
- **MyAbandonware Users:** 4.44/5 (43 votes, as of October 2026)[^ref-5]
- **The Electric Playground:** 7.5/10[^ref-17]

MobyGames has one player review, by Katakis (2019), titled "This interactive Saturday morning cartoon show will tickle your funny bone." It concludes that "Stay Tooned! is the type of game that most people would go back and play, due to its randomness"[^ref-3].

## Development

### Origins

Funnybone Interactive was founded in 1985 as The Cute Company by Joel Fried and Susan Swanson (Decker). It became a division of Davidson & Associates in February 1995[^ref-18]. By the time of Stay Tooned! it was Davidson's kids' entertainment software division in Canton, Connecticut, and the press release credits it with "over 100 titles as a third-party developer"[^ref-16]. The 2019 MobyGames player review says the name changed in 1996, but this contradicts MobyGames' own company record, which dates The Cute Company name to 1985–1995[^ref-3][^ref-18]. Sierra On-Line, which had recently merged with CUC International, published the game[^ref-16]. Animation World Magazine's December 1996 issue noted that CUC had also bought Davidson & Associates, and that Stay Tooned!, "created by Funnybone, which was acquired by Davidson & Associates not too long ago," was being released under the Sierra label[^ref-30].

### Production

The manual's credits list Susan Decker, Ben Howard, Kevin O'Neill and Christopher Lewis under Design & Concept. Joel Fried was executive producer, Susan Decker creative director and Ben Howard lead programmer[^ref-21][^ref-19]. MobyGames credits Decker as Susan Swanson-Decker[^ref-19].

Funnybone's MobyGames credits run from 1994 to 2006. Later titles include *Fisher-Price: Time to Play – Pet Shop* (1998), *Fisher-Price Big Action Construction* (2002) and several *JumpStart* titles[^ref-18]. According to MobyGames, Vivendi Universal Games closed the studio in 2003[^ref-18].

### Technical Achievements

The game was built with Macromedia Director, which the MobyGames player review calls "an animation tool quite popular in the Nineties"[^ref-3][^ref-15]. The press release lists 50,000 frames of hand-drawn animation, "over 50 different worlds," and 20 original musical numbers that can also be played on an audio CD player[^ref-16].

The 3-D effects came from clear-cellophane ToonVision glasses. In the press release's words, "The ChromaDepth™ lenses in these glasses are licensed from and manufactured by Chromatek Inc. Unlike the traditional red-and-blue filters," the method lets players without glasses see the same images "in crisp 2-D without any distortion"[^ref-16]. The Collection Chamber reviewer, who did not own the glasses, found the 3-D colour scheme did not work with ordinary red-and-cyan glasses[^ref-13].

### Technical Issues

Stay Tooned! was made for Windows 3.1, Windows 95 and classic Mac OS (System 7.1 or higher)[^ref-16]. The Collection Chamber's installer gets it running on modern systems by using "DOSBox running Microsoft Windows 3.1"[^ref-13]. The MobyGames player review reports that in some versions the audio for an earlier video clip does not play at first and then corrects itself[^ref-3].

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

Stay Tooned! contains several creator cameos and fourth-wall jokes:

- **Ben Howard:** the lead programmer appears as himself and offers playing tips on one of the TV channels[^ref-21][^ref-17][^ref-2]
- **Joel Fried:** Funnybone's president voices Chisel[^ref-21][^ref-19]
- **Fake errors:** error boxes such as "Punchline unfulfilled" pop up, followed by a giggling toon[^ref-17]

Additional trivia:
- The box was designed to look like a cereal box, "with the nutritional information and ingredients listing down the side"[^ref-17][^ref-13]
- The official website (stay-tooned.com) offered Shockwave mini-games, including a game in which players could "beat the snot out of Frank"[^ref-26][^ref-22]
- The TV spoofs include "Whinefeld" (*Seinfeld*), "Frauds" and "Schlepardy" (*Jeopardy!*)[^ref-16]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for Stay Tooned! have been published. The Collection Chamber's reviewer noted there is "not much information out there on its reception or earnings"[^ref-13]. Sierra released it on CD-ROM in autumn 1996 at about $40 through "most major computer, software, and mass-merchant chains"[^ref-16]. Funnybone kept developing children's software, including Fisher-Price and JumpStart titles, until its 2003 closure (MobyGames still lists later credits up to 2006)[^ref-18].

### Collections

No compilation re-release is documented in the sources consulted. The game survives through disc-image preservation on the Internet Archive and through abandonware and emulation sites[^ref-7][^ref-5][^ref-13].

### Fan Projects

The sources consulted document no fan remakes. Preservation has taken the form of disc images and pre-configured emulation installers[^ref-7][^ref-13].

### Related Publications

The manual includes a page of hints, and Sierra's press release said the official website offered hints, downloadable demos and screen captures[^ref-21][^ref-16]. No separate hint book or strategy guide is documented in the sources consulted.

### Critical Perspective

Stay Tooned! arrived in 1996, the same year as Burst Studio's *Toonstruck*. The Collection Chamber suggests this competition helps explain why it is little remembered[^ref-13]. HonestGamers argues that it is often wrongly filed as edutainment when it is really a graphic adventure[^ref-25]. Reviewers in 1996–97 and later retrospectives agree on its strengths: the cartoon humour aimed at the player, the shuffled building that rewards replaying, and a large set of mini-games that vary in quality[^ref-17][^ref-24][^ref-13]. MyAbandonware users remember it fondly. One calls it "one of the greatest memories" of their childhood, and another was "so happy" to share it with a daughter[^ref-5].
