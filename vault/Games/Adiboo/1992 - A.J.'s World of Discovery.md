# A.J.'s World of Discovery

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

A.J.'s World of Discovery is an educational game for young children developed and published by Coktel Vision, released in 1991 for Atari ST and 1992 for DOS.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-3][^ref-10][^ref-11][^ref-18] The game marks the debut of A.J. (known as Adibou in France), a young alien who became the most popular character of Coktel's Adi franchise.[^ref-5][^ref-22] More than a dozen Adibou titles were published until the series went dormant in 2009, and Wiloki revived it in 2022.[^ref-4]

Designed for children ages 4-7 (the Home of the Underdogs review describes it as aimed at toddlers), the game offers a collection of mini-games and activities in colorful VGA graphics, similar in concept to The Treehouse but targeted at a younger audience.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-23] A.J. serves as the player's guide throughout, providing help with digitized speech that reviewers noted was "appropriate and never annoying."[^ref-1]

Roland Oskian and Manuelle Mauger are credited as the series' co-creators.[^ref-24] French Wikipedia's article on the game names a four-person core team: Roland Oskian (CEO of Coktel Vision, originator of the concept), Manuelle Mauger (content development), Arnaud Delrue (technical development) and Joseph Kluytmans (visual design).[^ref-23]

## Story Summary

A.J.'s World of Discovery features A.J., an alien character who invites young players into his world of fun and learning.[^ref-2] While the game has no traditional narrative, A.J. accompanies players through various educational activities, providing assistance and encouragement throughout their exploration.[^ref-1] Most stories in the wider Adibou series are set in the world of Celesta;[^ref-4] the sources consulted do not say whether this first game names it.

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game features a user-friendly point-and-click interface designed for very young children, with VGA graphics and intuitive controls.[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-6] Many hidden hotspots throughout the game trigger funny animations when clicked, encouraging exploration.[^ref-1]

### Activities and Mini-Games

A.J.'s World of Discovery offers a diverse collection of activities:[^ref-2][^ref-5][^ref-6]

**Creative Activities:**
- **Drawing Program:** A surprisingly robust graphics program with color palette and paint tools for creating and saving pictures[^ref-1][^ref-2]
- **Funny Face Maker:** Design cartoon faces from different facial components or randomly generate faces[^ref-2]
- **Sticker Book:** Collect and arrange stickers[^ref-2]

**Educational Games:**
- **Keyboard Typing:** Learn to type using a keyboard interface[^ref-2]
- **English Practice:** Alphabet and reading/writing activities[^ref-1][^ref-2]
- **Math/Logic Games:** Educational mini-games teaching basic mathematics, with sweets as rewards for completion[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-5]

**Puzzles and Games:**
- **Jigsaw Puzzles:** Piece together puzzles to unlock animated picture rewards[^ref-2][^ref-6]
- **Sliding Tile Puzzle:** Classic tile-sliding puzzle mechanics[^ref-1]
- **Breakout Clone:** Arkanoid-style ball and paddle game, which would become an iconic recurring feature in the series[^ref-2][^ref-5]

**Other Activities:**
- **Matchbox Car Races:** Build custom tracks and race against another player or computer[^ref-2][^ref-6]
- **Garden Growing:** Plant seeds and watch flowers grow over time, another iconic series feature[^ref-2][^ref-5]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Wikipedia summarizes reviews of the game and of the wider Adibou series as follows (wording below is Wikipedia's paraphrase, not the critics' own):

**Critical Assessments:**[^ref-4]
- **Feibel:** Referring to *AJ's World of Discovery*, felt there were better run and jump games available
- **Jeuxvideo:** Argued that *Adibou* games were a good option for children but too short
- **The Argus:** Praised the series for its music but criticised its "daft" title

The Home of the Underdogs review praised the game's "vibrant graphics, user-friendly interface, and a number of fun activities," calling it "one excellent package that toddlers are sure to enjoy." The review noted that "more activities would have extended the program's longevity" but still marked it as "Recommended."[^ref-1]

### Modern Assessment

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MyAbandonware:** 4.1/5 (29 votes)[^ref-1]
- **Glitchwave:** 1.09/5.0 (4 ratings), ranked #646 for 1991[^ref-7]

The game has been preserved through abandonware archives.[^ref-8] In July 2023 ScummVM announced that its Gob engine support for the game was ready for public testing, covering all releases from the original 16-color floppy versions to the 1994 CD versions with richer graphics and sound.[^ref-5]

## Development

### Origins

A.J.'s World of Discovery was the first game in the Adibou series.[^ref-4][^ref-5] Coktel created its ADI pedagogical concept in 1990 for older children and adapted it in 1992 into Adibou, for ages 4-7.[^ref-22][^ref-25] By 1993 the Adibou series covered 65% of the French edutainment market.[^ref-21] French Wikipedia credits the game to four core team members:[^ref-23]

- **Roland Oskian** - CEO of Coktel Vision, conceived the Adibou character and coordinated development
- **Manuelle Mauger** - Managed content development
- **Arnaud Delrue** - Responsible for technical development
- **Joseph Kluytmans** - Responsible for visual quality

### Production

Coktel Vision handled both development and publishing for A.J.'s World of Discovery.[^ref-1][^ref-13] Atari Legend credits the Atari ST release's developer as MDO.[^ref-3]

The French version was titled "Adibou - Accompagnement Scolaire 4-7 Ans," with the name Adibou derived from the French acronym ADI meaning "Accompagnement Didacticiel Intelligent" (Intelligent Accompaniment Tutorial).[^ref-3][^ref-4][^ref-22][^ref-25]

### Technical Achievements

French Wikipedia lists the 1992 Adibou release for DOS, Mac OS, Atari ST and Amiga,[^ref-22] while MobyGames lists only Atari ST and DOS.[^ref-10] By 1997 the Adibou series had sold over 1.5 million copies,[^ref-4] and the wider Adi franchise eventually passed 2.5 million.[^ref-22]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- A.J. is the English name of Adibou, who became the most popular character of the Adi franchise[^ref-4][^ref-22]
- The French name "Adibou" builds on ADI (Accompagnement Didacticiel Intelligent)[^ref-4][^ref-22]; Konbini adds that the "-bou" referred to co-creator Manuelle Mauger's baby, then nicknamed "boubou"[^ref-24]
- In early floppy versions, A.J. spoke the same "strange gibberish" as characters in the Goblins series[^ref-5]
- Hidden hotspots throughout the game trigger silly animations[^ref-1]
- Original releases included a promotion where children who completed their virtual sweets collection could mail a floppy disk to Coktel Vision for actual candy rewards[^ref-5]
- Sierra On-Line acquired Coktel Vision in 1992, with the deal finalised on 29 October 1993[^ref-21][^ref-19]
- Similar in concept to The Treehouse, but for younger audiences[^ref-1]
- The series was divided into age categories: Adi (10-14), Adibou (4-7), and Adiboud'chou (18 months-3 years)[^ref-4]

## Legacy

### Series Context

A.J.'s World of Discovery represents the beginning of Coktel Vision's Adibou educational franchise, which by 1993 covered 65% of the French edutainment market and 35% of the European market.[^ref-21] By 1997, the series had sold over 1.5 million copies.[^ref-4]

The series expanded beyond games into comic books, music, and a television series (Adiboo Adventure, 2009).[^ref-4] Following various corporate changes, the IP rights eventually passed to Ubisoft, and in 2020, French company Wiloki (founded by Roland Oskian's children) partnered with Ubisoft to revive the character with new titles in 2022.[^ref-4]

**Adibou Series Timeline:**
- Adibou (A.J.'s World of Discovery) (1991) - Series debut[^ref-4]
- Adiboo: Magical Playland (1996)[^ref-4]
- Adiboo: Discover series (2000)[^ref-4]
- Adibou & Le Secret de Paziral (2003)[^ref-4]
- Adibou et les Saisons Magiques (2009) - Wii[^ref-4]
- Adibou: La Chanson d'Adibou (2022) - Revival[^ref-4]

### Critical Perspective

Many of the Adibou series' iconic activities were already present in this first game: planting flowers, playing Breakout, and earning sweets for completing reading and counting exercises.[^ref-5]
