# Police Quest: In Pursuit of the Death Angel (VGA Remake)

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 28, 2026</small>

## Overview

Police Quest: In Pursuit of the Death Angel VGA Remake is a 1992 enhanced remake of Sierra On-Line's 1987 police procedural adventure game, published in the summer of 1992.[^ref-1][^ref-45] The remake runs on Sierra's SCI 1.1 interpreter. It replaced the original's EGA graphics and text parser with 256-color VGA graphics, support for sound cards such as the AdLib, Sound Blaster and Roland MT-32, and an icon-driven mouse interface.[^ref-29][^ref-1] The story was kept essentially unchanged, but the remake made police procedure far less punishing. Just Games Retro found that routines such as the patrol-car inspection "almost always just give bonus points, instead of being mandatory and punished by death."[^ref-46]

The game places players in the role of Sonny Bonds, a patrol officer in the fictional city of Lytton, California. Sonny works his way up from routine patrol to an undercover operation against a drug lord known as the Death Angel.[^ref-4][^ref-45] The series was designed by former California Highway Patrol officer [[Jim Walls]]. In his 1987 original, failing to follow proper procedure typically cost the player points or got Sonny killed.[^ref-22] Producer Tammy Dargan stated that "it was [our] intention to create a real life simulation, to explore the feelings that are part of the police experience."[^ref-6]

Sierra rebuilt several of its older adventures with the same SCI technology, including Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards, King's Quest: Quest for the Crown and Space Quest: The Sarien Encounter.[^ref-2] Jim Walls had left Sierra after finishing Police Quest III, so the remake was made by producer Tammy Dargan and her team without him.[^ref-7]

## Story Summary

The game begins with Officer Sonny Bonds arriving at the Lytton Police Department for his patrol shift.[^ref-4] As a patrolman he hands out speeding tickets, arrests drunk drivers and deals with bikers troubling a local bar.[^ref-2][^ref-22] Lytton's teenagers are falling into drug use, and at the shift briefing Sergeant Dooley tells the officers: "We need to find the scumbag that's supplying them."[^ref-6]

Sonny's first lead is a car crash whose driver, drug dealer Lonny West, turns out to have been shot in the head.[^ref-6] Along the way he meets "Sweet Cheeks" Marie, a prostitute who was once his high-school crush and who supplies him with information on the drug ring.[^ref-22] Marie later helps set up the card game at the Hotel Delphoria where the drug lord can be found.[^ref-6]

After proving himself on patrol, Sonny is promoted to the Narcotics division.[^ref-7] His investigation leads to the Death Angel, "a notorious criminal with ties to the underworld".[^ref-12] The Death Angel is Jesse Bains, who runs an illegal gambling operation at the Hotel Delphoria.[^ref-22] The trail ultimately leads to a confrontation with the Death Angel himself.[^ref-15]

The climax has Sonny go undercover at the hotel's poker game. If he remembers to radio in from the hotel room, backup arrives and shoots Bains.[^ref-5] Bains survives and is convicted, and Sonny is awarded the Key to the City.[^ref-22]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The 1992 VGA remake replaced the original game's text parser with Sierra's point-and-click interface.[^ref-1] Players interact with the game world using icon-based commands corresponding to Walk, Look, Touch, and Talk actions.[^ref-1] Just Games Retro noted that "a click now represents hundreds of possible actions and the game picks the right one". In its reviewer's words, "You click on a suspect to cuff him. You click on the suspect again to search him."[^ref-46]

Driving was simplified in the remake. The player no longer steers: the car drives automatically, and the player only chooses turns and brakes at stop signs.[^ref-1] Just Games Retro called it "an erratic minigame foolproof", though the car is slower and auto-brakes "to 30mph for every turn".[^ref-46] In the 1987 original, driving was notoriously hard: "your tiny game car does not have the maneuverability of your full-sized real one".[^ref-18]

### Structure and Progression

The game follows Sonny through several days of police work, beginning with routine patrol duties and progressing to detective work and undercover operations.[^ref-4] The structure reflects a law enforcement career progression:

- **Patrol Duty:** Sonny attends briefings and hands out tickets while cruising Lytton in his black-and-white[^ref-45]
- **Narcotics Division:** After proving himself, Sonny is promoted and begins investigating the drug trade threatening Lytton[^ref-7]
- **Undercover Operation:** A good cop may be asked to "go undercover" to infiltrate the Death Angel's gang[^ref-45]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The remake shipped with the *Lytton Police Gazette*, a newspaper-style document that merged the original's manual and map. Players need to refer to it during the game.[^ref-45][^ref-7] Its codes serve as copy protection: Sonny's locker combination is printed there, and suspects have to be booked using its codes.[^ref-43] Just Games Retro called booking "the main unique feature in the remake". In its words, "One successful code will do, but the majority of your extra points come from entering in all the applicable charges you can."[^ref-46] (The 1987 original instead came with an "Indoctrination Guide" manual. RetroFreak, reviewing that version, wrote that its procedures and codes are "so highly detailed in the manual that it probably can pass off as an official police academy manual."[^ref-19])

Procedure is much less punishing than in 1987. Some routines remain, but Just Games Retro found that they "almost always just give bonus points, instead of being mandatory and punished by death". It added that players "can also no longer forget items or get stuck in unwinnable situations."[^ref-46] Sierra's wiki lists further relaxations:[^ref-43]

- The routine inspection of the car is no longer required
- Sonny does not need to load his gun
- He does not have to read Barnum his rights, nor read the drunk driver or the car thief their Miranda rights
- When he turns on the siren to pursue a car, the pullover is automatic

Mistakes can still be fatal. Katakis describes the "death screen" and its "evil laugh", and found it "necessary to go 'by the book' when it comes to police procedure".[^ref-7] Sierra's own InterAction magazine warned that "One careless mistake" could let "a dangerous suspect get the upper hand - with fatal results."[^ref-45]

The original's mandatory poker mini-game was made optional in the remake.[^ref-1] MobyGames notes that "a few puzzles have different solutions", though not all of them simpler: some became more complex in the remake.[^ref-1]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews (1992–1993)

MobyGames lists eleven critic scores for the remake.[^ref-7] Finland's *Pelit* (July 1992, 83%) called it the most successful of Sierra's VGA conversions so far, the others being Larry 1 and Space Quest 1. It recommended the remake both to players who had finished the AGI version and to newcomers.[^ref-7] The UK's *PC Format* (January 1993, 73%) said that "the graphics are excellent, and even a little gruesome in places, but the adventure gets bogged down in the details of police procedures."[^ref-7]

Germany's *PC Games* (February 1993, 77%) praised a solid background story, witty Sierra-style comments and high-quality graphics and sound effects. It docked points for the sometimes very long waits for new assignments, which can only be filled by driving the streets.[^ref-7] *Play Time* (February 1993) gave 78%.[^ref-7] Poland's *Secret Service* (November 1993, 90%) called it probably the best-made game in the run of remakes. It said point-and-click let players concentrate on content and action instead of thinking up commands, and that tedious sequences such as driving had been improved.[^ref-7]

### Retrospective Reviews

Adventure Classic Gaming (1998, 4/5) wrote that "The VGA remake boasts better graphics and enhances the player's immersion into the daily danger of police work". It also found control of the patrol car "very sluggish" and felt that constant hints about forgotten procedures "may have ruined the atmosphere."[^ref-6][^ref-7] AllGame's Alec Norands (2/5, two of five stars) called the remake "unarguably superior in the level of graphics, sound, and control", but also "considerably less fun to play on almost every level."[^ref-48][^ref-7]

Just Games Retro (2006, 4.5/5) praised the remake's "Amazingly improved presentation, with vastly enhanced graphics, music, and writing". Its criticism was that "it becomes more of a story about police officers instead of a simulation of being one."[^ref-46] Other retrospective scores include Adventure Gamers (2007, 3.5/5), Aventura y Cía (3/5) and A Force for Good (2.5/5).[^ref-7]

MobyGames user reviewer Katakis called it "an excellent Sierra remake well worth playing, even if you never played the original", with hand-painted backgrounds that "look stunning".[^ref-7] Alex Bevilacqua played the SCI remake in 2022 and scored it 67%. He found it "exponentially more playable than the original AGI version", but felt that Sierra's push for an accurate procedural simulator "results in long segments just being boring" and that "it was not as much fun as I remember it being."[^ref-5]

### The 1987 Original

Reviews of the 1987 AGI original are covered on [[1987 - Police Quest - In Pursuit of the Death Angel|its own page]]. In brief, *Computer Gaming World*'s Michael S. Chaut praised some of its graphics as "the most terrific this reviewer has ever seen", while *Macworld*'s Keith McCandless said it "plays like a long version of a routine cop TV show, and you can't lose if you just follow the manual."[^ref-22] *Antic*'s Rick Teverbaugh wrote that "there is a strong sense of actually becoming the cop on the beat."[^ref-23] Nostalgia Trigger, looking back at the 1987 version, called it "one of the toughest and frustrating games out there".[^ref-27]

- **IMDb:** 7.7/10 (48 ratings, as of October 2026)[^ref-2]

## Development

### Origins

The Police Quest series grew out of a 1985 meeting between Sierra's Ken Williams and California Highway Patrol officer Jim Walls.[^ref-22] Walls was then on administrative leave to treat trauma from a shooting a year earlier. After Walls retired from the CHP in 1986, Williams asked him to write a short story about his experiences, which became the basis of the game's plot.[^ref-22]

The Police Quest wiki's page on the original describes Walls as having "15 years on the police force".[^ref-11] Many incidents in the game were based on situations Walls encountered in the CHP.[^ref-22] The protagonist, Sonny Bonds, was named after Walls' son,[^ref-19] and the game drew on Walls' personal experiences as a patrol officer.[^ref-30]

### Production

The original 1987 version was built on Sierra's Adventure Game Interpreter (AGI). Because Walls was initially unfamiliar with computers, other Sierra developers assisted him.[^ref-22] Al Lowe, Greg Rowland, Ken Williams and Scott Murphy programmed the original, with art by Mark Crowe and Gerald Moore.[^ref-22]

The 1992 VGA remake was produced after Jim Walls had left Sierra following the completion of Police Quest III.[^ref-7] As Walls stated on his personal website, "after PQ 3, circumstances developed causing me to decide it was time that I leave."[^ref-31] Katakis credits Tammy Dargan "and her crew" with making the remake "authentic as the original game".[^ref-7] Just Games Retro found Walls' story "identical to the original, with no added scenes, no cuts", though the dialogue was reworked and strengthened.[^ref-46] MobyGames lists Sierra On-Line and U.S. Gold as publishers.[^ref-1]

Sierra's InterAction magazine introduced the new edition with "superb, full-color VGA graphics and video-captured human actors for life-like action sequences", a stereo soundtrack and an icon interface. It also noted the included *Lytton Police Gazette*, which "Every rookie should read it cover-to-cover."[^ref-45] For the remake, Sierra merged the original's various game documents (map, poker guide, etc.) into this newspaper feature.[^ref-1]

### Technical Achievements

The original used low-resolution (160x200) EGA graphics, while the remake has 256-color VGA graphics.[^ref-6][^ref-1] Its hand-painted backgrounds were singled out by reviewers.[^ref-7] Just Games Retro described "stylized, hand-drawn background paintings; scanned into the computer and beautifully reproduced in 256-color VGA."[^ref-46] The remake replaced the original's "cartoon like characters with video captured human actors".[^ref-6]

The remake also added sound effects and sound-card music;[^ref-2][^ref-29] Just Games Retro called the music "far and away" the greatest addition to the series.[^ref-46] MobyGames reviewer Katakis, who usually prefers the Roland MT-32, found this game's music sounds better through the Sound Blaster.[^ref-7]

### Cut Content

The remake made several changes to the original content that created continuity issues with the series. Most notably, the male hotel bartender Woody Roberts from the original was replaced with a female bartender named Alexandra Parker.[^ref-2] Woody Roberts appears in Police Quest II: The Vengeance (1988), so his absence from the remake creates a continuity gap.[^ref-2]

Katakis also noted that the remake's ending "skips a vital scene that makes it follow into the Police Quest II" and called the remake "non-canonical".[^ref-7]

### Technical Issues

On faster PCs, the VGA remake can fail with the message "Unable to initialize your audio hardware". DOS Days explains that the audio-hardware detection does not get enough time to complete. Its suggested remedies are slowdown utilities or the GOSiERRA driver patch.[^ref-29] Just Games Retro found that the remake "does seem a little rushed in programming", with five crashes in one playthrough against none in the original.[^ref-46] Sierra's wiki lists a patch for the CD collection version that fixes a lockup when leaving Bert's Park on newer systems.[^ref-43]

DOS Days also notes that the game is speed-sensitive and can run too fast on newer computers.[^ref-29]

Reviewing the 1987 original, Just Games Retro wrote that "the vast majority of deaths I had in the entire game were in the driving section", including game overs for running red lights or making a legal right turn on red.[^ref-26]

One walkthrough author noted that players will be "confronted with what is probably the worst parsing problem in any Sierra game" when trying to interact with certain characters. This applies only to the AGI text-parser version.[^ref-18]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

The game contains numerous hidden interactions and easter eggs that reward curious players. These come from the 1987 AGI original; text-parser eggs are not possible in the point-and-click remake:

- Typing "Get Naked" while in police uniform produces the comedic response "What's a naked?" before causing problems[^ref-34]
- Entering programmers' names (Al Lowe, Ken Williams, Jim Walls) in the crime computer produces amusing results[^ref-17]
- A newspaper in the briefing room contains an article about Daventry (from King's Quest) being under siege by a three-headed dragon[^ref-17]
- When chicken feathers fall on the ground, typing "pick up feather" results in the reply "Do that in King's Quest III"[^ref-17]
- If Sonny trades his integrity for sex with Helen Hots, a driver he pulls over, she gives him a phone number; calling it may reach the Police Commissioner's wife, and "It doesn't end well for his career"[^ref-17][^ref-34]

In the remake, the stolen Cadillac became a Mercedes-Benz. The undercover "pimp suit" from the original was renamed a "leisure suit", a nod to Leisure Suit Larry.[^ref-43]

The 1987 original was the first Sierra DOS release to ship without copy protection.[^ref-29] The remake, by contrast, uses codes from its manual for the locker combination and for booking suspects.[^ref-43] Katakis also mentions "the copy protection that kicks in while you are in a certain place".[^ref-7] The original was also one of the first PC games to use the word "Crap" (with profanity symbols), and it had unique typing shortcuts (o = open, c = close, d = door).[^ref-11]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

The Police Quest series achieved significant commercial success for Sierra. The first four Police Quest adventure games totaled 850,000 sales by late 1995.[^ref-22] Combined sales of the entire Police Quest series (including the SWAT spin-offs) surpassed 1.2 million units by the end of March 1996.[^ref-22]

### Police Training Applications

Police Quest was reported to have been used as a training tool for police officers,[^ref-22] and Sierra promoted the remake on that reputation. Its InterAction feature on the new edition (Winter 1992, pp. 34–35) said that, since the game's initial release, this "fun little computer game" "has proven to be a practical, effective training tool officers enjoy using. It safely demonstrates to rookies the consequences of failing to observe proper procedures and can serve as a valuable refresher course for experienced officers."[^ref-45]

The Police Quest wiki's page on the 1987 original quotes *Law and Order* magazine calling the game "a serious training tool... The simulation is based on skill and knowledge... The depth of training is enormous."[^ref-11] The original was also apparently used for officer training at the Allegan City Police Department in Michigan.[^ref-25]

One MobyGames user who became a police officer wrote of the 1987 original: "As the game that made me interested in law enforcement, and event[u]ally led me to a career in the field, this game holds a very special place for me. [...] The attention to actual police procedure, while not perfect down to the details, is very accur[at]e and to my knowledge, the most accur[at]e to be presented in a computer game to date."[^ref-37]

### Collections

The VGA remake has been included in several compilations:

- **Daryl F. Gates' Police Quest Collection: The 4 Most Wanted** (1995), with both versions of PQ1 (the original and the VGA remake) plus PQ2 to PQ4[^ref-1][^ref-29]
- **SWAT Career Pack** (2000), which also carries both versions of PQ1, alongside PQ2 to PQ4 and the first two SWAT games[^ref-1][^ref-50]
- **Police Quest Collection** (Vivendi, 2006), whose Windows menu lists it as "Police Quest 1 VGA"[^ref-29][^ref-43]. Sierra's wiki adds that owners of the AGI original can copy its files in and set it up with the collection through a later DOSBox update, which suggests the 2006 collection shipped only the remake.[^ref-43] MobyGames, by contrast, links this collection only to its entry for the 1987 original.[^ref-51]
- **Police Quest Collection** on GOG.com and Steam, which includes both the original EGA release and the 1992 VGA remake[^ref-9][^ref-16]

### Preservation

Archivist Jason Scott uploaded the source code of Police Quest to GitHub between October 25 and 27, 2022, as part of a video game preservation effort.[^ref-22]

### Related Publications

- **The Lytton Police Gazette:** The remake's newspaper-style manual, merging the original's map and manual and needed during play[^ref-45][^ref-7]
- **LPD Indoctrination Guide (1987 original):** The original's manual, the "Lytton Police Department Policeman's Indoctrination Guide"[^ref-29]
- **The Police Quest Casebook** (Peter Scisco, Osborne McGraw-Hill; advertised by Sierra as "Available 10/92", copyright page dated 1993): Sierra-authorized guide to the first three games, covering both the new and classic editions of Police Quest 1[^ref-44][^ref-45]
- **Novels:** *Police Quest: In Pursuit of the Death Angel* and *Death Angel, Sweet Angel*[^ref-40]

### Critical Perspective

Just Games Retro, reviewing the original, notes that Sierra and others had previously built adventures around cartoonish, comedic properties, and that "Police Quest represents an interesting experiment in changing what adventure games were all about."[^ref-26]

The same reviewer argued that "the game has about as much use in preparing you to be a policeman as The Oregon Trail has in preparing you to set off from Independence, Missouri in a wagon".[^ref-26] Sierra, by contrast, promoted the game's use in real police training.[^ref-45] For the remake, Just Games Retro concluded that "In terms of personal enjoyment, both versions balanced out to be about the same."[^ref-46]

Jim Walls had no involvement in the remake, but his story was carried over intact.[^ref-7][^ref-46] John Edward Betancourt replayed the first game from the Police Quest Collection in a 2018 retrospective and summed up its appeal: "There was something supremely cool about stepping behind the badge and actually digging into police work."[^ref-13]
