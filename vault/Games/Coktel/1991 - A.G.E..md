# A.G.E. (Advanced Galactic Empire)

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

A.G.E. (Advanced Galactic Empire) is a first-person space exploration game developed and published by [[Coktel Vision]] in 1991 for DOS, Amiga, and Atari ST platforms[^ref-1]. The game is a follow-up to *[[1990 - Galactic Empire|Galactic Empire]]* (1990) and, like its predecessor, is a first-person space exploration game rendered in 3D[^ref-1]. A later Home of the Underdogs write-up, reproduced on Old-Games.com, credits both games with "a fast 3D engine with beautiful graphics - as one could expect from Tomahawk/Coktel Vision"[^ref-3]. Many databases list the developer as Tomahawk, which was Coktel Vision's own publishing label[^ref-17][^ref-19].

The game draws frequent comparisons to *Elite* in that players are "relatively free to travel to wherever you please," piloting their spacecraft around planets and stars while engaging in space combat and conversation with other characters to unravel the plot[^ref-1]. However, A.G.E. features a more linear narrative structure than *Elite*, with the plot advancing through triggered events rather than purely emergent gameplay[^ref-3]. The game updated its predecessor's engine with VGA graphics and included various in-game help options, making it more accessible to players who lacked the manual[^ref-4].

Modern retrospective assessments have been mixed, with one reviewer noting, "I don't think A.G.E. is a great game, but neither is a bad one," while observing that "its graphics have aged badly, I found new value in them since they remind me of the most minimalist part of the origins of internet culture, a sort of vaporwave without its characteristic colors"[^ref-5]. The game has been preserved through various abandonware archives and remains playable through emulation[^ref-6].

## Story Summary

A.G.E. places players in the role of a secret agent on a covert mission to the planet Kaiser[^ref-7]. Upon arrival, the player quickly becomes embroiled in a web of terrible conflicts that threaten to destabilize the entire region. The narrative involves multiple interconnected crises: the kidnapping of the religious Rachnouist leader, a technorobots uprising, and the possibility of war between different sections of the army[^ref-7].

Adding to the complexity of the situation, a fellow agent named Dale has disappeared under mysterious circumstances[^ref-7]. The player must navigate these various threats while uncovering the truth behind what the game describes as a "Conquer the Universe plot"[^ref-1]. In the original *Galactic Empire*, players likewise took the role of a secret agent of the empire, sent to a planet in the grip of civil war[^ref-8].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

A.G.E. presents its gameplay through a first-person perspective rendered in 3D[^ref-1]. MobyGames lists keyboard and mouse input for the game[^ref-1], and Atarimania lists the Atari ST version's controls as mouse[^ref-9]. One Lemon Amiga user commented in 2024: "Still figuring out all the controls on my ship. Figuring out my recently acquired laser and blowing up an evading creature brought a smile to my face"[^ref-10].

Players pilot their spacecraft around the planets and stars, engaging in space combat and conversation with other characters[^ref-1]. The game provides various in-game help options that compensate for the lack of a manual[^ref-4].

### Structure and Progression

The game follows a semi-open structure similar to *Elite*, allowing players freedom to travel between planets and stars[^ref-1]. However, unlike the purely sandbox approach of *Elite*, A.G.E. features a more linear plot progression where players cannot advance significantly "without running into the next event that trigger plot advance"[^ref-3].

- **Space Travel:** Piloting the spacecraft around planets and stars[^ref-1]
- **Combat:** Space combat[^ref-1]
- **Conversation:** Talking with other characters to unravel the plot[^ref-1]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Old-Games.com's write-up calls A.G.E. "an early first-person space/adventure hybrid"[^ref-3]. Weapons are acquired during play; one Lemon Amiga user mentions a "recently acquired laser"[^ref-10]. Detailed descriptions of the game's puzzles and equipment were not found in the sources consulted.

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

The magazine scores found in sources date from 1992. *ACAR* magazine gave the Amiga version a score of 60%, with reviewer Peter Christopher providing the assessment in November 1992[^ref-10]. Swedish publication *Datormagazin* was more favorable, awarding the game 79% in issue No. 6 from March 1992, with the review written by Pia Wester[^ref-10].

German magazine *Aktueller Software Markt* rated the DOS version 7/12 in May 1992[^ref-12].

### Modern Assessment

Modern retrospectives have approached A.G.E. with appreciation for its historical context while acknowledging its limitations. A Home of the Underdogs write-up, credited "Review By HOTUD" on MyAbandonware and reproduced on Old-Games.com, concluded of the two Galactic Empire games: "Not great games, but worth a look if you like games such as Cybercon III"[^ref-4][^ref-3]. Games Nostalgia's own review scored the game 79/100[^ref-5]. User communities have been more divided, with MyAbandonware users rating the game 4.21/5 based on 28 votes[^ref-4].

Retrospective criticism has noted that "among retro games, the first adventures to experiment with 3D are those in which the magnifying glass is held with a more critical eye"[^ref-5]. Atarimania users rate the Atari ST version 8.6/10, from 5 votes[^ref-9].

**Aggregate Scores:**

- **Atarimania:** 8.6/10 (5 votes)[^ref-9]

## Development

### Origins

A.G.E. was developed by Coktel Vision as a follow-up to its 1990 title *Galactic Empire*[^ref-1].

The project was conceived by François Nédélec (credited as F.X. Nedelec) and Frédéric Chauvelot (credited as F. Chauvelot), who shared conception duties[^ref-1]. French Wikipedia classifies it as a video game developed in France[^ref-12]. The game updated its predecessor's engine with VGA graphics[^ref-4].

### Production

Programming was handled by Mathieu Marciacq under the M.D.O. designation[^ref-1][^ref-10]; Lemon Amiga lists the development tool as C Code[^ref-10]. The 3D graphics were created by Frédéric Chauvelot, while bitmap graphics were produced by Pascal Pautrot (P. Pautrot) and Yannick Chosse (Y. Chosse)[^ref-1].

MobyGames lists 1991 releases for DOS, Amiga, and Atari ST[^ref-1]. The Amiga version runs on OCS and ECS hardware and shipped on 2 disks[^ref-10][^ref-18]; the Atari ST version ran on ST and STe machines with 0.5MB and shipped on 2 double-sided disks[^ref-9].

**Development Credits:**[^ref-1]
- **Conception:** François Nédélec (F.X. Nedelec), Frédéric Chauvelot (F. Chauvelot)
- **Programming (M.D.O.):** Mathieu Marciacq
- **3D Graphics:** Frédéric Chauvelot (F. Chauvelot)
- **Bitmap Graphics:** Pascal Pautrot (P. Pautrot), Yannick Chosse (Y. Chosse)
- **Music & Sound Effects:** Frédéric Motte (Moby)
- **Production:** C.V.S.

### Technical Achievements

A.G.E. is rendered in 3D[^ref-1]. The Home of the Underdogs write-up described the engine as "fast" with "beautiful graphics"[^ref-3]. The DOS version supported VGA graphics, representing a significant visual upgrade from the predecessor[^ref-4].

Old-Games.com calls it "an early first-person space/adventure hybrid"[^ref-3]. A Games Nostalgia reviewer wrote that "in a sense, its graphics have aged badly"[^ref-5].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Sales figures for A.G.E. have not been publicly documented. The game was published by both Coktel Vision and Tomahawk (a Coktel Vision label) across different regions[^ref-1]. In some markets, Bomico also served as a publisher for the Amiga version[^ref-14]. Electronic Zoo handled publishing for the original *Galactic Empire* in certain territories[^ref-8].

The game was released commercially on DOS, Amiga, and Atari ST in 1991[^ref-1]. No awards or special recognition have been documented for the title.

### Collections

A.G.E. has not been re-released in any modern game collections or digital storefronts. A Steam store search returns no listing for it[^ref-15], and it has no GOG listing. It has been preserved primarily through abandonware sites and the Internet Archive[^ref-6].

### Fan Projects

No significant fan projects, remakes, or modifications have been documented for A.G.E.

### Related Publications

No specific hint books or strategy guides have been documented for A.G.E. in available sources. The game notably "features various in-game help options which make it much easier to overlook the lack of the manual"[^ref-4].

### Critical Perspective

Old-Games.com calls A.G.E. "an early first-person space/adventure hybrid"[^ref-3]. MobyGames compares it to *Elite* in its freedom to travel[^ref-1], while the Home of the Underdogs write-up notes that its plot is "much more linear"[^ref-3]. That write-up judged the two Galactic Empire games "Not great games, but worth a look if you like games such as Cybercon III"[^ref-3].
