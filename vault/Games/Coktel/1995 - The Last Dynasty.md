# The Last Dynasty

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 27, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Last Dynasty is a hybrid action-adventure game developed by [[Coktel Vision]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] in 1995.[^ref-6] The game ambitiously combines space combat simulation sequences reminiscent of Wing Commander with first-person puzzle-solving adventure segments in the style of Myst.[^ref-2] Players assume the role of Mel Raauq, an intergalactic hero tasked with preserving the "Ultimate Knowledge" while battling the villainous Lord Iron across the galaxy.[^ref-1][^ref-9]

Coming Soon Magazine called it "probably the largest production from Sierra On-Line to this day with a budget of over two million dollars," featuring several hours of dialogue, SVGA graphics throughout, and extensive video sequences.[^ref-4] The demo's marketing promised players could "Play the adventure and combat phases simultaneously and experience total gaming freedom," with all action in real time.[^ref-5] Reviews were split: Coming Soon Magazine scored it 91%, while Computer Gaming World gave it 40% and found its endless video clips and two-CD structure wearing.[^ref-4][^ref-1][^ref-6]

## Story Summary

The game opens on Earth, with Mel and his friend Dok "engaged in some sort of satellite launch, from what looks like their Hamburg apartment," as Computer Gaming World put it; Mel then receives an alien message, and an alien starship arrives on Earth.[^ref-1] The Collection Chamber describes the same opening as the pair's attempt to blast a miniature rocket ship into outer space.[^ref-10] Mel is secretly an alien prince with valuable data locked within his mind.[^ref-10] Players take on the role of Mel Raauq, a space pilot who receives what is described as "the mission of a lifetime"—to locate and ultimately destroy Lord Iron, whose minions have spread across space in an attempt to subjugate the inhabitants of the galaxy.[^ref-2] Lord Iron's dark forces threaten "peace and tranquility in the galaxy."[^ref-9]

Throughout the adventure, Mel is accompanied by his companion Dok, who is revealed to be an Imperial Squire sent to protect him on Earth.[^ref-1] The Collection Chamber blog's 2017 retrospective asked of the pair, "Are they the first representation of a gay couple in videogames? It's not explicit but it looks commendably likely."[^ref-10] The adventure phase takes place on a large space station.[^ref-10]

Other characters include Vanna, the Colonel, Lord Yannis, Malchus, Malinka, Vaid, Ike and the Hoja.[^ref-11] Coming Soon Magazine's reviewer deliberately left the scenario undescribed, noting only that the game "features several hours of dialogue" and "lots of video sequences."[^ref-4]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The Last Dynasty uses a first-person perspective.[^ref-2] In the adventure sequences the player examines rooms with the cursor to find and take objects, uses them from an inventory, and follows on-screen arrows showing which directions are open.[^ref-4] Movement is not a slideshow: Computer Gaming World said it "is handled very much like in 7th Guest," with scenes that "flow past in a cinematic way,"[^ref-1] and The Collection Chamber notes each step is "animated from point to point," unlike "the slide-show photographs of titles like Myst."[^ref-10]

Combat sequences switch to a space combat simulator with a customizable cockpit: windows for speed, shields, nearby friends and enemies and more can be configured or removed, and a 3D map can be rotated and zoomed.[^ref-4] An on-board computer provides permanent help in the cockpit.[^ref-4] Computer Gaming World liked the HUD but found the enemy ships to be "2D cutouts" and complained that combat is repeatedly interrupted by short animated replays.[^ref-1]

### Structure and Progression

The game is divided into a space combat phase and a first-person adventure phase, with full-motion video sequences advancing the plot:[^ref-2]

- **Adventure Phase:** First-person exploration and puzzle-solving where players search for clues, interact with characters, and collect items necessary for progression
- **Combat Phase:** Space combat simulation where players pilot their customized cockpit against Lord Iron's fleet, featuring over 20 different types of vessels including both allies and enemies[^ref-4]

Although the demo's marketing spoke of playing both phases "simultaneously,"[^ref-5] the first seven combat missions must be completed before the adventure phase opens;[^ref-9] after that, the player can return to the space action at any time.[^ref-10] The demo copy also warns players to "Move decisively" in the adventure phase, since "All of the action is in real time."[^ref-5]

Reviewers disagreed about the switches between modes. Coming Soon Magazine wrote that "the transitions are so fast that you don't have time to realize you are not shooting anymore at the enemy vessel."[^ref-4] Computer Gaming World countered that the game ships on two CDs, one for each mode, so that "'Seamless' is not a word to be used here," and listed among its cons: "Having to swap CDs whenever the game shifts to its alternate mode is a nuisance."[^ref-1]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The adventure portions involve collecting items and solving puzzles.[^ref-2] Computer Gaming World found the puzzles "generally straightforward," requiring "only cleverness" rather than clairvoyance, with weapons used only to solve particular situations.[^ref-1] The Collection Chamber felt the mix of action and adventure "sits uneasily for both action gamers and adventurers."[^ref-10]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Contemporary reviews of The Last Dynasty were sharply split.

**Coming Soon Magazine** awarded the game an exceptional **91%** score, writing that it "blends action and adventure into a fabulous story throughout space that will rave you from the beginning to the end."[^ref-4]

**Computer Gaming World** (November 1995) was far less enthusiastic, giving the game **40%**.[^ref-6] Reviewer Martin E. Cirulis treated it as an odd Euro-import, writing: "If this game was a big hit in Europe, then I guess it all comes back to the mystery of different sensibilities and expectations of a game. For all the glitter, The Last Dynasty doesn't offer anything more than you could have found on an Atari ST ten years ago, and the endless video clips leave the player feeling like flotsam carried away on the raging current of unchecked multimedia."[^ref-1][^ref-6]

### Modern Assessment

The Collection Chamber blog's 2017 retrospective concluded that "The Last Dynasty ultimately ends up being a rather schizophrenic game meant for everyone but caters to no-one."[^ref-10]

## Development

### Origins

Coktel Vision, a French developer that had been acquired by Sierra On-Line in 1993, was already known for innovative adventure games including the Goblins series, the Inca series, Lost in Time, and Ween.[^ref-15] Sierra's 1996 annual report dates the acquisition to October 29, 1993.[^ref-21]

### Production

Coming Soon Magazine reported a budget of over two million dollars, calling the game "probably the largest production from Sierra On-Line to this day."[^ref-4] MobyGames likewise records a $2 million budget.[^ref-2] The German magazine MAN!AC reported that Sierra hired professional actors: nearly a year of planning was followed by 30 days of filming in front of a blue screen, after which the backgrounds were replaced with rendered sets, sound was added and the dialogue was dubbed into all languages.[^ref-3]

**Development Credits:**[^ref-2]
- **Project Supervisor:** Philippe Lamarque
- **Scenario:** Frédéric Chauvelot, Philippe Lamarque
- **Dialogues and Adaptation:** Silvan Boris Schmid
- **Live-Action Director; Casting & Voice Casting:** Silvan Boris Schmid
- **Director of Photography:** Alain Youcef Khoudja

### Technical Achievements

The Last Dynasty runs on the Gob engine, Coktel Vision's in-house engine.[^ref-7][^ref-20] It requires 640x480 graphics in 256 colors.[^ref-7] Coming Soon Magazine praised the combat graphics as fast despite running under Windows, and a soundtrack that "changes according the action" instead of looping one track.[^ref-4] The game ships on two CD-ROMs, one for the space-combat mode and one for the adventure, and Computer Gaming World found the disc swapping a nuisance.[^ref-1]

### Technical Specifications

**CD-ROM Version:**[^ref-4][^ref-7][^ref-1]
- **Media:** Two CD-ROMs
- **Price:** $59.99 (US, per Computer Gaming World)
- **Resolution:** 640x480, 256 colors (SVGA)
- **Operating System:** Windows 3.1 (Coming Soon also lists MS-DOS 5.0 or later)
- **Processor:** 486 DX-33 MHz minimum
- **RAM:** 8 MB
- **Hard Drive Space:** 4 MB free
- **Copy Protection:** None (CD must be in drive)
- **CD-ROM:** Double-speed or faster
- **Input:** Microsoft Mouse or compatible
- **Audio:** Sound card compatible

### Cut Content

In October 1996 MAN!AC previewed Saturn and PlayStation conversions as Sierra's first console title, listing a German release for November.[^ref-3] The Sega Saturn version was "all but complete" but never released.[^ref-17] According to Sega Saturn Shiro, the port was "basically finished and ready to go (minus a few bug fixes here and there)" but was pulled at the last minute, which the site speculates was "most likely" due to the Saturn's poor market performance.[^ref-17] Prototype CD-Rs had been auctioned off some years earlier, and the game was finally dumped publicly in March 2021.[^ref-17][^ref-18]

### Technical Issues

The original Windows 3.x release does not function on 64-bit versions of Windows without emulation or compatibility layers.[^ref-7] PCGamingWiki lists winevdm as a workaround,[^ref-7] and The Collection Chamber's installer runs Windows 3.1 inside DOSBox.[^ref-10] The Sega Saturn prototype, despite being nearly complete, contained some minor bugs that would have required fixes prior to commercial release.[^ref-17]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

Hidden debug functionality remains embedded within the game's executable files:[^ref-19]

- **Cheat Code:** TCRF suggests typing "_CHEAT_" (with underscores) during gameplay; its effect is unconfirmed
- **Debug Functions:** Hidden French-language debug text in WLOADER.EXE lists functions for killing all enemies, becoming immortal, high player speed and winning the game, plus debug statistics
- **Configuration Mystery:** The game checks for a file called "WMDO.CFG" but doesn't appear to actually utilize it for any purpose

Additional trivia:
- The game featured over 20 different types of vessels, encompassing both allied and enemy craft[^ref-4]
- A limited edition release shipped with a baseball cap[^ref-2]
- Coktel Vision was acquired by Sierra in 1993, making The Last Dynasty part of the Sierra family of games[^ref-15]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for The Last Dynasty have been found. Computer Gaming World's review mentions European success only conditionally ("If this game was a big hit in Europe..."), so claims of a European hit remain unconfirmed.[^ref-1]

### Collections

The game was re-released in Germany under the Sierra Originals budget label.[^ref-12]

### Fan Projects

In March 2021, a preservation milestone was achieved when the previously unreleased Sega Saturn prototype was publicly released by user "olsaturndev" on Hidden Palace.[^ref-18] The prototype has a build date of November 12, 1996,[^ref-18] and represents a nearly complete port that was pulled at the last minute.[^ref-17] Saturn collector "Saturn Guru" had possessed official pre-production silver discs of the game for several years before the public dump occurred.[^ref-17]

### Related Publications

- **Game Manual:** Shipped with the retail release; copies still circulate with secondhand boxes and in The Collection Chamber's package[^ref-9][^ref-10]

### Critical Perspective

Computer Gaming World saw the game as a product of mid-1990s multimedia excess, saying its "endless video clips leave the player feeling like flotsam carried away on the raging current of unchecked multimedia," and likened it to Coktel's Inca.[^ref-1]

The Collection Chamber argued that the game's "'action-packed' and 'adventure' elements are mutually exclusive," leaving a game that "has something of an identity crisis."[^ref-10]

## Purchase

The Last Dynasty is not currently sold digitally on GOG or Steam.
