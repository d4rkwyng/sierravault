# Galactic Empire

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

> [!note] Not the 1979 Brøderbund game
> This page covers the 1990 Coktel Vision/Tomahawk game. Doug Carlston's unrelated 1979 strategy game of the same name, published by Brøderbund, is a different title.[^ref-19]

## Overview

*Galactic Empire* is a first-person 3D action-adventure released in 1990 for MS-DOS, Amiga and Atari ST. MobyGames credits its development to [[Coktel Vision]] and MDO, with publishing by [[Tomahawk]] and, in North America, Electronic Zoo.[^ref-1] Tomahawk was not a separate company. In 1990 the Spanish magazine *Micromanía* explained that Coktel Vision published under two labels, Tomahawk for games only and Coktel Vision for educational software.[^ref-2] This explains why some databases list Tomahawk as the developer and Coktel Vision as the publisher, the reverse of MobyGames' arrangement.[^ref-3]

The player is an imperial secret agent sent to the planet Ether, where a civil war has run for three years. Travelling under the cover identity of Ted Fost, a reporter, the agent has to find out what caused the uprising.[^ref-1] The game was adapted from *Empire Galactique*, the French tabletop role-playing game that François Nédélec created for the publisher Robert Laffont in 1984.[^ref-4][^ref-5][^ref-2] MobyGames credits Nédélec and Frédéric Chauvelot with the game's conception and scenario.[^ref-1]

Contemporary reviews admired the filled-polygon 3D world but split on the game itself. France's *Tilt* gave the PC version 15 out of 20 and called it "un bon jeu très original et bien réalisé" ("a good, very original and well-made game").[^ref-6] Germany's *ST-Magazin* gave the Atari ST version 2 out of 6 because of its slow frame rate.[^ref-7] Coktel followed it in 1991 with a sequel, [[1991 - A.G.E.|A.G.E. (Advanced Galactic Empire)]].[^ref-1][^ref-8]

## Story Summary

*Amiga Action*'s review summarised the setup. In the year 11590, Ether is a small planet on the border of the Galactic Empire, and it has been at civil war for about three years. An adventurer called Voltar has seized most of the power, declared Ether's secession and is hunting down those still loyal to the Empire.[^ref-10] MobyGames' description says Voltar leads the "Scarlet Militia", and that Ether's harsh climate makes the planet dependent on imports. Ether is inhabited only because of its medically and militarily useful wildlife.[^ref-1]

The player is an officer of Sersec, an imperial service, chosen for a confidential mission to investigate the war and report back.[^ref-10] *Tilt* described the hero as a SERSEC agent who lands under a false identity to understand why Ether's inhabitants have turned against the Empire.[^ref-6] MobyGames names that identity: Ted Fost, a reporter for the "homeo-newpaper" *Cosmopolis*. The previous agent sent to Ether vanished, leaving a single clue: "It's the Malkouts fault."[^ref-1] According to *Amiga Action*, some groups stayed loyal and can help: the Teknos, the Merchants and "a certain type of extra terrestrial". The player must first find them and hope to be recognised.[^ref-10]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Humans cannot survive Ether's atmosphere and gravity unaided, so the agent travels in a small vehicle.[^ref-6] *Amiga Action* named it the Fornec, an anti-gravity craft that also supplies food, water and oxygen. The game is played from inside it, with a full-3D view of the outside world.[^ref-10] On the Amiga:[^ref-10]

- Moving the mouse left and right rotates the craft, and the left button thrusts it forward.
- The right button brings up a cursor for the control panel.
- The panel includes a comms window, armour control, get and discard object, weapon and object inventories, an ammo counter, energy, heat and oxygen counters, a compass, and a radar with zoom.
- A camera mounted on the rear of the craft lets the player watch the action from outside.

*ST-Magazin* criticised the "konfuse Kombination aus Tastatur- und Maussteuerung" ("confused combination of keyboard and mouse control") during fights.[^ref-7] MobyGames lists mouse support and a single player for the DOS version.[^ref-1]

### Structure and Progression

According to *Tilt*, the agent's first jobs are to stock up on oxygen at dispensers and to register at customs. Then the exploration begins, and the agent has to watch for zones where he is not tolerated.[^ref-6] *ST-Magazin* described the hero starting out unarmed and bringing his weapon systems online as he explores and tracks down clues to his enemies' whereabouts.[^ref-7]

Home of the Underdogs compared the game to *Elite* in its freedom of movement, but noted that "the plot is much more linear, and you can't do much without running into the next event that trigger plot advance."[^ref-11] The Amiga version supports saved games.[^ref-9]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Conversation runs through what MobyGames describes as "an emotion dialog system", offering five options when talking to the game's many non-player characters.[^ref-1] *Tilt* explained that the five attitudes range from a silly grin, which avoids a lot of trouble, through a more "commercial" manner to outright aggression.[^ref-6] *ST-Magazin* noted that choosing the right facial expression mattered as much as choosing the right words.[^ref-7] MobyGames notes that the player is "not an one-man army" and has to choose "when to apply violence" and when to talk instead.[^ref-1]

Other mechanics, as described by contemporary reviewers:

- **Object handling.** *Amiga Action* called object manipulation "the key to the game". The player picks up and uses weapons, radars and armour, and can talk to people in other Fornecs through a go-between.[^ref-10]
- **Living wildlife.** The planet holds "no less than 80 species of animals" that are born, grow, age and die. Some can be tamed to help, while others are aggressive.[^ref-10]
- **Hostile zones and copy protection.** *Tilt* warned that the difficulty was "un peu élevée" (a bit high) and that it was too easy to die.[^ref-6] The Amiga manual scans held by Hall of Light include a sheet of copy-protection codes.[^ref-9]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

| Publication | Platform | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Tilt* No. 87 (February 1991) | PC | 15/20 (interest) | Four stars for graphics and animation, two for sound; reviewer Jean-Loup Jovanovic[^ref-6] |
| *Amiga Joker* (March 1991) | Amiga | 59% | Reviewer Joachim Nettelbeck[^ref-3][^ref-9] |
| *ST-Magazin* 05/1991 | Atari ST | 2 of 6 | Price about 90 DM[^ref-7] |
| *Amiga Action* No. 22 (July 1991) | Amiga | 69% | Review on page 44[^ref-3][^ref-9] |

*Tilt*'s Jean-Loup Jovanovic praised a "décor 3D surfaces pleines splendide (en VGA)" ("splendid filled-surface 3D scenery, in VGA") and an original communication system. He also found the world coherent and full of surprises.[^ref-6]

*ST-Magazin* liked the idea of a 3D action-adventure with role-playing interludes and dialogue. It concluded that the game "scheitert ... an der technischen Umsetzung" ("fails ... on its technical execution"), citing eight frames per second, which it called far too slow for vector graphics. It also complained that polygon objects looked too alike and that the built-in mini-parser was more a long-winded dialogue system than artificial intelligence.[^ref-7]

*Amiga Action* called it "a complex game and one that you'll have to invest time getting into". It faulted a manual that "tends to waffle on about minor details" and added: "Although the graphics are fairly smooth, they update slowly and only appear visible when you're literally on top of them."[^ref-10] By January 1993 the magazine's buyer's guide dismissed it as "A futuristic adventure featuring some appalling vector graphics and equally poor sound effects."[^ref-12]

*Tilt* looked back on the game twice while previewing and reviewing its sequel. In September 1991 it called *Galactic Empire* "techniquement très réussi" ("technically very successful") but said it suffered from a limited scenario and an overly linear story.[^ref-8] In December 1991 it recalled "la superbe 3D mais au scénario mince comme du papier à cigarettes" ("the superb 3D but a scenario as thin as cigarette paper"). In the same review, Tilt said the original's dialogue system had been "bien plus élaborée" ("much more elaborate") than the sequel's.[^ref-13]

### Modern Assessment

Home of the Underdogs grouped the game with its sequel as "early first-person space/adventure hybrid[s]" with "a fast 3D engine with beautiful graphics". Its verdict was "Not great games, but worth a look if you like games such as *Cybercon III*." The reviewer warned that neither game "works well on Pentium II but run[s] fine on 486 or slower computers."[^ref-11] MyAbandonware reuses that text in its own entry.[^ref-14]

**Aggregate and user ratings:**

- **MobyGames:** 63% critic average from 5 ratings; players 4.0/5 from 3 ratings[^ref-1]
- **Lemon Amiga:** average magazine rating based on 2 reviews (69% and 59%)[^ref-3]
- **Home of the Underdogs:** 8.27 from 11 votes[^ref-11]
- **MyAbandonware:** 3.75/5 from 16 votes[^ref-14]

## Development

### Origins

*Galactic Empire* grew out of a tabletop game. *Empire Galactique* is a French space-opera role-playing game that François Nédélec created and Robert Laffont published in November 1984. It came as a single 246-page book with a booklet of play aids, illustrated almost entirely by Manchu.[^ref-4] In 1990 *Micromanía* reported that the computer game had been "convertido desde el juego de tablero del mismo nombre, basado a su vez en cinco libros publicados por Robert Laffont" ("converted from the tabletop game of the same name, itself based on five books published by Robert Laffont").[^ref-2] *Tilt* confirmed the link twice. In its review it said the game "se veut inspiré de l'Empire galactique" ("is meant to be inspired by Empire Galactique"), though it found that only the science-fiction universe carried over into what was a pure adventure game.[^ref-6] In its September 1991 preview of the sequel, it called *A.G.E.* the second 3D game drawn from the *Empire Galactique* role-playing game, written by the tabletop game's own creator.[^ref-8]

### Production

MobyGames' DOS credits list:[^ref-1]

- **Conception and scenario:** François Nédélec, Frédéric Chauvelot
- **Project manager:** François Nédélec
- **Graphics:** Frédéric Chauvelot, Yannick Chosse
- **Programming (M.D.O.):** Mathieu Marciacq
- **Music and sound:** Frédéric Motte (Moby)

Hall of Light describes the game as "Jointly-developed by MDO and Coktel Vision", and Atari Legend credits MDO with programming.[^ref-9][^ref-15] Lemon Amiga's Amiga credits add box art by Philippe Bouchet and record that the game was written in C.[^ref-3]

Coktel showed the game to the press in Paris in 1990 alongside *[[1990 - Cougar Force|Cougar Force]]*, *[[1990 - Geisha|Geisha]]* and *No Exit*. *Power Play* described *Galactic Empire* as Coktel's excursion into the role-playing genre. It reported that all of Coktel's new games would appear on MS-DOS first, with Atari ST and Amiga conversions in preparation.[^ref-16] *Micromanía*, reporting on a separate visit by Coktel's commercial director Jérôme Gastaldi and Muriel Tramis to its Spanish distributor System 4, listed the game for Atari, Amiga and PC.[^ref-2]

### Technical Achievements

*Tilt* later described *Galactic Empire* as "le premier jeu 3D surfaces pleines de Tomahawk" ("Tomahawk's first filled-surface 3D game").[^ref-8] Hall of Light classifies its view as cockpit and first-person filled 3D, with into-screen scrolling.[^ref-9]

### Technical Specifications

**MS-DOS:** CGA, EGA and MCGA/VGA graphics modes[^ref-9]; 3.5" and 5.25" floppy media; mouse supported[^ref-1]

**Amiga:** OCS chipset, one floppy disk, single player[^ref-3]

**Atari ST:** ST low resolution[^ref-17]

**Languages:** English, French, German, Italian and Spanish, for both the game and the manual on the Amiga releases[^ref-9][^ref-3]

Hall of Light notes that Bomico distributed the game in Germany.[^ref-9] A Bomico advertisement in *Aktueller Software Markt* (January 1991) promoted it as "Eine 3D-Science-Fiction Simulation einer futuristischen Welt, in der Sie mit den außerirdischen Leben kommunizieren können", with a German manual.[^ref-18]

## Legacy

### Sequel

Coktel released *[[1991 - A.G.E.|A.G.E. (Advanced Galactic Empire)]]* in 1991. It is the next game in MobyGames' Galactic Empire series, and Hall of Light records *Galactic Empire* as its precursor.[^ref-1][^ref-9] *Tilt*'s December 1991 review said the sequel fixed the original's thin scenario and greatly improved its graphics, but dropped its more elaborate dialogue system.[^ref-13]

### Name Confusion

The title is shared with Doug Carlston's 1979 strategy game, published by Brøderbund. MobyGames keeps separate entries for the two, and Wikipedia gives the 1990 game its own article.[^ref-1][^ref-19]

### Collections

No re-release in a compilation or on a digital storefront was found during research for this page.
