# E.S.S. Mega

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

E.S.S. Mega (European Space Simulator Mega) is a space shuttle simulation game developed by [[Coktel Vision]] and published by Tomahawk in 1991[^ref-1]. Unlike most simulations based around NASA's projects, it recreates the European Space Agency's space vehicle concepts of the early 1990s, especially the Hermes shuttle[^ref-9]. In its November 1991 issue, French magazine *Joystick* reviewed the PC floppy and CD-ROM versions side by side and declared "Le premier jeu disponible sur CD ROM est français" ("the first game available on CD-ROM is French")[^ref-21].

The game uses 3D-modelled graphics and features one long campaign that emphasizes micro-level decisions over macro-level space program policies[^ref-5]. Players equip the space shuttle with cargo, crew and energy, then launch, pilot and land it, while sending up and maintaining satellites and building and managing a space station[^ref-5]. According to the game's manual, the simulated timeline runs from January 2010 to December 2013[^ref-2].

E.S.S. Mega is an enhanced version of the original E.S.S. (European Space Simulator), released in 1989[^ref-6][^ref-14]. The back of the box advertises "video sequences (200 MB) and HI-FI music (300 MB)" for the CD versions only[^ref-7], and *Joystick* counted "plus de vingt minutes de film" (more than twenty minutes of film) on the CD-ROM[^ref-21]. Home of the Underdogs calls it "probably the most obscure game ever made by quirky French developer Coktel Vision" and "one of the rarest space simulations"[^ref-8].

## Story Summary

E.S.S. Mega places players in charge of shuttle operations for a European space program in the early 2010s[^ref-2]. The game recreates the European Space Agency's space vehicle concepts of the early 1990s, with particular emphasis on the Hermes shuttle[^ref-9].

The campaign spans the period from January 2010 to December 2013, during which players must earn money by placing satellites into orbit, building a space station to run experiments and performing successful shuttle landings[^ref-2]. *Joystick* noted that each mission also has to be completed within the deadlines set by the contract, and that the contract with the client countries includes on-site maintenance of their satellites[^ref-21].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

E.S.S. Mega features a mouse-driven interface and both first-person and third-person perspectives[^ref-1][^ref-15]. One online description compares it with the NASA-focused Space Shuttle and MAX, saying "this game is much more friendly to the player (that does not negate its overall difficulty and complexity of interface)"[^ref-10]. *Joystick* was less kind about the controls, complaining that the mouse cursor moves slowly and jerkily ("au ralenti et par à-coups")[^ref-21].

### Structure and Progression

The game features one long campaign, with emphasis on micro-level decisions (such as how much fuel to carry) rather than macro-level space program policies[^ref-5].

- **Mission Preparation:** Players equip the space shuttle with cargo, crew and energy[^ref-5]
- **Launch Operations:** Launch the shuttle and pilot it into orbit[^ref-5]
- **Orbital Activities:** Send up and maintain satellites[^ref-5]; the box back advertises plotting "in full 3D"[^ref-7], and a cosmonaut on a space "scooter" can repair bases and satellites[^ref-21]
- **Space Station Management:** Build and manage a space station[^ref-5]
- **Return and Landing:** Land the shuttle on the Corporation's shuttle-carrier[^ref-7]

The same online description says the strategic element "often prevails over 'simulator'"[^ref-10].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The game's challenges center on resource management and technical decisions rather than traditional puzzle-solving[^ref-5]. *Joystick* warned players not to exceed the shuttle's weight limit and to pack enough food for the technicians, and noted that the main piloting difficulty is steering precisely in three dimensions without burning too much fuel[^ref-21]. An in-game space database provides reference information[^ref-5].

Money is central: *Joystick* explained that launching a shuttle is very expensive, so each flight must be managed carefully by launching satellites that bring in enough revenue[^ref-21]. A flight recorder lets players keep a trace of a flight in order to correct their mistakes later[^ref-21].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

*Joystick* magazine (issue 21, November 1991, reviewer Moulinex) gave the PC floppy version 89% and the CD-ROM version 91%[^ref-11][^ref-21]. The magazine explained that the floppy version's score drops a little because it lacks the CD's technological showpiece, yet called both versions "un logiciel hors du commun" (an out-of-the-ordinary piece of software)[^ref-21]. Its overall verdict on the CD version was mixed: "un logiciel révolutionnaire pour tout ce qui concerne les inter-écrans mais bien sans plus en terme de jeu" (revolutionary in its interludes, but nothing special as a game)[^ref-21]. The 77% from *Génération 4* (issue 18, January 1990) that is sometimes attributed to this game was a review of the original E.S.S. on PC and Atari ST, not of Mega[^ref-11].

The CDTV version received a more modest assessment. *Amiga Joker* reviewed the CDTV release in October 1992, with reviewer Joachim Nettelbeck awarding it 3 out of 5[^ref-11][^ref-12]. The original E.S.S. on Amiga had received 76% from *Amiga Joker* (reviewer Michael Labiner, March 1990) and 68% from *CU Amiga* (reviewer Mark Patterson, February 1990), an average magazine rating of 72%[^ref-6].

### Modern Assessment

Home of the Underdogs' own review is negative. It calls the game "a lackluster space shuttle simulation" and, although it praises the eye-catching 3D-modelled graphics, it says the whole "comes across as a repetitive, 'dry' simulation that won't hold anyone's interest for long"[^ref-8]. It also notes the "lack of random elements that make Buzz Aldrin challenging", and concludes that the game is "worth a look for space buffs, but only for historical interest"[^ref-8]. The site's readers rate it more generously, with an average of 8.69 from 49 votes[^ref-8][^ref-13]. Another online description is warmer about the visuals, saying that they are "not just all bright and beautiful, but often there is a serious hint of three-dimensionality"[^ref-10].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **Home of the Underdogs (reader average):** 8.69 from 49 votes[^ref-8]
- **MobyGames:** 83% critic average (one review)[^ref-1]
- **Lemon Amiga (E.S.S. original):** 72% average[^ref-6]

## Development

### Origins

Coktel Vision was founded by Roland Oskian, an engineer at Matra Espace, the space division of Matra[^ref-14]. Writing about the original 1989 E.S.S., Abandonware France said that the subject "was pretty obvious - remember Roland Oskian worked in a space company before - and it was the first step towards a new technology"[^ref-14]. The same history says Coktel then invested in a Getris graphics station and CD-ROM tools, and "in late 1991" released E.S.S. Mega, "an enhanced version of the game", whose CD-ROM version contained digitized pictures and footage from space exploration archives and a CD audio soundtrack[^ref-14].

The original E.S.S. (European Space Simulator) was coded by Inférence and released in 1989, with Amiga reviews of 76% and 68%[^ref-6].

### Production

MobyGames credits five staff from MATRA Space, the ESA and the CNES under the game's thanks[^ref-1], and the Amiga version carries a note of special thanks to Matra and ESA[^ref-12].

### Technical Achievements

E.S.S. Mega uses real-time 3D graphics[^ref-2], with UVList listing its display as "raster, flat polygons"[^ref-3]. *Joystick* said that some scenes reached "un réalisme encore jamais vu sur micro" (a realism never before seen on a home computer), but that the graphics of the game itself were "un peu décevant" (a little disappointing), with screens lacking personality and sometimes unclear[^ref-21]. One online description praised "a serious hint of three-dimensionality"[^ref-10].

*Joystick* presented the CD-ROM version as the first game available on CD-ROM and said it "éclaire aussi d'un jour nouveau l'avenir du jeu sur PC" (sheds new light on the future of PC gaming)[^ref-21]. The CD's extra capacity carried digitized pictures and footage from space exploration archives[^ref-14], which *Joystick* described as 20 minutes of black-and-white and colour archive film covering Sputnik, the shuttle and the first steps on the Moon[^ref-21].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for E.S.S. Mega were found in the sources consulted. Abandonware France notes that Coktel Vision invested in CD-ROM tools around this time, and "I think they were the first French game company to do so"[^ref-14].

### Critical Perspective

E.S.S. Mega stands apart from most space simulations of its time by recreating ESA concepts rather than NASA projects[^ref-9]. Home of the Underdogs places it alongside Coktel Vision's better-known games such as *Gobliiins* and *Lost in Time*, while calling it the company's most obscure title[^ref-8]. The same site describes Coktel Vision as "famous for quirky, wacky games that can only come from France"[^ref-13].

*Joystick*'s 1991 verdict sums up its place: impressive as an early CD-ROM showcase, with a reviewer who wrote "Pas facile d'être un pionnier" (it's not easy being a pioneer), but "bien sans plus en terme de jeu" (nothing special as a game)[^ref-21].

### Related Publications

The game included an in-game space database[^ref-5]. The CD-ROM version added digitized pictures and footage from space exploration archives[^ref-14].
