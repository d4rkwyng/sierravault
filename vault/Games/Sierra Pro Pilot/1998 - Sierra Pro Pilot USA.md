# Sierra Pro Pilot USA

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

Sierra Pro Pilot USA is the second title in Sierra's Pro Pilot series of civilian flight simulators, announced as available nationwide on October 1, 1998 for Windows 95[^ref-17][^ref-24]. Developed by [[Dynamix]], the studio Sierra acquired on March 27, 1990 for $1.5 million[^ref-21], Pro Pilot USA was one of two follow-ups to Pro Pilot '98 released in 1998[^ref-3]. Sierra's press release described it as featuring "the full functionality of Pro Pilot '98 while offering enhanced beginner assistance for novice users," sold at a price of $19.95 through mass merchandisers and software retailers[^ref-17]. IGN's news brief summed it up as "a modified version of Sierra Pro Pilot, with extra help for novices added to the package"[^ref-13].

Compared with Pro Pilot '98, USA omitted the European terrain and focused solely on North America, while adding a "Virtual Co-pilot" to help novices with radio communications and with the altitude and course changes required in controlled airspace[^ref-24]. The original Pro Pilot had been Sierra's attempt to challenge Microsoft Flight Simulator's "virtual lock on the civilian flight sim market"[^ref-2], building on Sierra's late-1995 acquisition of Sublogic[^ref-22], whose staff brought a huge geographical database "massaged" from USGS maps to the project[^ref-2]. GameSpot's preview of that original described the effort as "a full-blown, private and commercial flight training system"[^ref-2].

Dynamix's launch press release for the original had billed it as "The Most Authentic and Accurate Flight Simulator for Home PCs"[^ref-10]. The series continued with Pro Pilot '99, also released in 1998[^ref-3][^ref-14].

## Story Summary

As a civilian flight simulator, Pro Pilot USA does not feature a traditional narrative storyline. Instead, it offers open-ended flying across the United States; Sierra said users "can fly to just about any location across the continental United States"[^ref-17]. Players assume the role of a civilian pilot learning to master various aircraft types and flight procedures.

Following Pro Pilot '98, the game lets PC users become familiar with aviation procedures, flight maneuvers, cross-country flight planning and Air Traffic Control (ATC) communications, under both Visual Flight Rules (VFR) and Instrument Flight Rules (IFR)[^ref-17]. Like the rest of the series, it puts its focus on communication with air traffic controllers and other computer-controlled pilots[^ref-24].

The game's "narrative" unfolds through the player's own progress: an eighty-page "Pilot Guide" takes users step-by-step through their first flight and basic flight proficiency, and thirty training videos cover a range of flight basics[^ref-17].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Sierra stated that Pro Pilot USA kept the full functionality of Pro Pilot '98[^ref-17], whose cockpit simulation included dual COMM/NAV radios and a built-in GPS[^ref-3]. Navigational aids listed for USA include the Instrument Landing System (ILS), Global Positioning System (GPS), Non-Directional Beacons (NDB) and radio navigation aids[^ref-17]; MobyGames also lists ATC, VOR and Victor routes for IFR flight planning[^ref-24].

A "simple select-and-click" Flight Planning Wizard lets players choose a departure airport, arrival airport, waypoints, flight elevation and external flight conditions[^ref-17]. The new "Virtual Co-pilot" assists novice pilots with radio communications and with tracking the altitude and course changes needed to pass through controlled airspace[^ref-24].

### Structure and Progression

There are six aircraft in Pro Pilot USA, "each one selected to assist in the understanding of basic aviation to advanced piloting"[^ref-17]:

- **Cessna 172R Skyhawk (1997):** New in this title[^ref-17]
- **Cessna 172P Skyhawk:** The classic trainer[^ref-17]
- **Beechcraft Bonanza V35**[^ref-17]
- **Beechcraft Baron B58**[^ref-17]
- **Beechcraft King Air B200**[^ref-17]
- **Cessna 525 CitationJet**[^ref-17]

For newcomers, a "Scenic Flights" option offers ten easy-to-begin flights over popular US destinations, including Washington DC, the Grand Canyon, the Statue of Liberty, Las Vegas, Cape Canaveral, the Golden Gate Bridge, the Rocky Mountains, Seattle's Space Needle, Hollywood and the St. Louis Arch[^ref-17].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Rather than traditional puzzles, Pro Pilot USA challenges players with flight planning, navigation and ATC communication[^ref-17]. The scenery uses over 27 million elevation points from the U.S. Geological Survey to model landmarks, mountains, valleys and rivers for visual navigation[^ref-17].

The simulation includes 29 highly detailed major metropolitan areas covering over 100,000 square miles and over 2,500 airports[^ref-17]. (Pro Pilot '98, with its North American and European scenery, is credited with over 3,000 airports across the U.S. and parts of Canada[^ref-3].)

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

No contemporary review scored specifically for Pro Pilot USA has been found; its MobyGames entry carries no critic score[^ref-24]. IGN reported its release in an October 1998 news brief, noting that Sierra had released it "for only $20"[^ref-13]. Because USA was a modified version of the original[^ref-13], reviews of Pro Pilot '98 are the closest guide to how the underlying simulator was received.

GameSpot awarded Pro Pilot '98 a score of 6.1 out of 10, with reviewer Denny Atkin concluding that "with the glaring omissions fixed and a graphics overhaul, Pro Pilot could be contender. As it stands now, it's primarily of interest to potential student pilots looking for a good instrument flying simulator"[^ref-3][^ref-11].

Computer Gaming World gave Pro Pilot '98 2.5 out of 5 stars in its April 1998 issue[^ref-3][^ref-15]. PC Joker, a German gaming publication, awarded Pro Pilot '98 58% in its September 1998 issue[^ref-3][^ref-16].

### Modern Assessment

In a 2001 Airliners.net thread titled "Pro Pilot USA: Need Help!", a user who had just bought the game reported that "when I try to fly in full screen mode, the image quality is really low"[^ref-6]. Another poster replied that he kept Pro Pilot around "just so I can fly the Citation," adding that the scenery was "the best I've ever gotten it to do"[^ref-6].

The game has been preserved on the Internet Archive as part of the clearancebin_pc collection[^ref-1].

## Development

### Origins

The Pro Pilot series emerged from Sierra's strategic acquisitions in the flight simulation space. Sublogic had begun a new flight simulator when Sierra acquired it in late 1995; Sierra completed the program and released it as Pro Pilot in 1997[^ref-22]. Sublogic's personnel moved from Illinois to Eugene, Oregon, and joined Dynamix, bringing no code, "just a huge geographical database 'massaged' from USGS maps, along with their solid flight sim expertise"[^ref-2]. The target was Microsoft's Flight Simulator, which had "a virtual lock on the civilian flight sim market"[^ref-2].

Dynamix, the development studio behind Pro Pilot, was founded in 1984 in Eugene, Oregon by Jeff Tunnell and Damon Slye[^ref-23]. Sierra acquired Dynamix on March 27, 1990 for $1.5 million[^ref-21]. By the time of Pro Pilot, Dynamix was known as the creator of air combat simulators such as Red Baron II[^ref-2].

### Production

Pro Pilot USA was conceived as a cheaper, beginner-oriented repackaging of Pro Pilot '98. "We want to offer our realistic, civilian flight simulator to a larger user base by taking our successful product and adding a few new 'getting started' features to take users off the runways faster," said Kate Kloos, marketing director for Pro Pilot[^ref-17]. The press release added that USA "builds on our successful Pro Pilot '98 product, which sold over 275,000 units," and that the price had been dropped "to introduce a new audience of general home PC users to the adventure of flight"[^ref-17].

During development of the original Pro Pilot, Sierra hoped flight sim enthusiasts, student pilots and would-be pilots would switch because of its "dramatic" improvements in accuracy[^ref-2]. "We wanted to combine Dynamix's proprietary 3-D tool set with the sharp minds and massive data of Sub-Logic," producer Graeme Bayless said, adding that "Pro Pilot is not based on the old Sub-Logic technology"[^ref-2]. Beyond Microsoft, Sierra was also looking at high-end instrument trainers such as X-Plane and FS-200; "They are superlative products," Bayless said, "but they don't bother with visuals"[^ref-2].

The Pro Pilot '98 CD-ROM included two separate map installation discs, one for the US and one for Europe, which used the same registry keys and so could not be installed simultaneously[^ref-4]. Pro Pilot USA dropped the European terrain[^ref-24].

### Technical Achievements

The original Pro Pilot was built with Dynamix's proprietary 3-D tool set rather than old Sub-Logic code[^ref-2], and ran at a low-res 640x480 setting[^ref-3]. GameSpot's review of Pro Pilot '98 noted "some nice effects, such as vibration upon starting the engine," while judging the visuals "far behind the state of the art"[^ref-11].

### Technical Issues

In 2001, an Airliners.net user reported that in full screen mode "the image quality is really low," with resolution and colors "way below what it's supposed to be," and that he could not take screenshots in full screen mode[^ref-6]. After another user said the scenery he got was similar, the original poster concluded "maybe this resolution is the way it is supposed to be"[^ref-6].

A known installation conflict exists in the original Pro Pilot '98: the two regional map installations (US and Europe) cannot be installed simultaneously because they share the same registry keys[^ref-4].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

The original Pro Pilot '98 sold more than 275,000 units[^ref-3][^ref-17]. Sierra's launch release for Pro Pilot USA cited that figure, saying USA "builds on our successful Pro Pilot '98 product"[^ref-17]. No sales figures for Pro Pilot USA itself have been found.

The Pro Pilot series represented Sierra's attempt to compete in the civilian flight simulation market dominated by Microsoft Flight Simulator[^ref-2].

### Collections

The game belongs to Sierra's Pro Pilot series alongside Pro Pilot '98 (1997) and Pro Pilot '99 (1998)[^ref-3]. All three titles were released between 1997 and 1998[^ref-3].

The Redump wiki's Sierra/Vivendi USA publisher list includes a "Sierra Pro Pilot (USA)" disc with barcode 0 20626 70849 4 and serial S7048910; the list does not say which Pro Pilot edition that disc is[^ref-7].

### Fan Projects

A community wishlist on GOG.COM has accumulated 71 votes (as of October 2026) requesting the digital release of Pro Pilot '98[^ref-5]. The series is not currently available on Steam or GOG[^ref-8][^ref-9].

### Related Publications

Pro Pilot USA shipped with an eighty-page "Pilot Guide" covering simulator setup, flight planning, air traffic control procedures, how planes work, and proficiency tips[^ref-17]. No separate hint books or strategy guides have been found.

### Critical Perspective

The Pro Pilot series occupies an interesting position in flight simulation history as Sierra's serious attempt to challenge Microsoft's dominance in civilian aviation simulation[^ref-2]. GameSpot later wrote that Pro Pilot '98 "was far from the Microsoft Flight Simulator killer it was touted as," released "in a buggy, unfinished state"[^ref-12]. Critics such as GameSpot saw the original's main value as an instrument flying trainer[^ref-3].

Pro Pilot USA was Sierra's attempt to widen that niche: rather than overhaul the simulator, it lowered the price and added beginner material in the hope of reaching "a new audience of general home PC users"[^ref-17]. At the time, Sierra described itself as a division of Havas Interactive[^ref-17].
