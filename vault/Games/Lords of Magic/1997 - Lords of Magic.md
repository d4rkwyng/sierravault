# Lords of Magic

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

Lords of Magic is a turn-based fantasy strategy game, intended to combine elements of Heroes of Might and Magic II and Lords of the Realm II, in which turn-based map play switches to real-time battles[^ref-1]. Developed by Impressions Games and published by Sierra On-Line in 1997, the game is set in the fantasy world of Urak, a once-peaceful land now fractured by chaos and threatened by the evil sorcerer Balkoth[^ref-2]. It blends turn-based adventure and exploration with real-time combat[^ref-3].

Lords of Magic allows players to choose from eight different faiths - Life, Death, Order, Chaos, Fire, Water, Earth, and Air - each with unique characteristics, units, and magical abilities[^ref-4]. The main objective is to defeat Balkoth, Lord of Death, by any means necessary while managing resources, exploring the world, and engaging in both diplomacy and warfare[^ref-1]. The Legends of Urak expansion was later bundled with the original game as Lords of Magic: Special Edition, released on September 30, 1998[^ref-1][^ref-26].

## Story Summary

The game opens with the narrative: "These are black days for Urak. Evil envelops the land and the people cry out for a savior."[^ref-8] For a thousand years, peace reigned on the pastoral lands of Urak, but forces of darkness led by the evil sorcerer Balkoth unleashed a nightmare of war and terrorism that destroyed the people and laid waste to the land[^ref-2]. Players take control of the leader of one of Urak's faiths and set out to defeat Balkoth[^ref-11].

The game features multiple storylines, with the Special Edition adding five new storylines based on legendary characters, including a hidden quest based on the Siegfried story that can be accessed by clicking the center of the quest selection room[^ref-12]. Each faith offers a different perspective and approach to the overarching conflict, with unique units, magical abilities, and strategic advantages.

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The scrolling map of Urak can be viewed at three zoom levels (a detailed view, a regional view, and a "World View"), selected with magnifying-glass buttons on the interface panel[^ref-8]. The game is controlled primarily through mouse input and combines turn-based strategic gameplay with real-time tactical combat[^ref-14]. Players can switch between different views to manage cities, armies, and diplomatic relations.

### Structure and Progression

The game follows the 4X strategy tradition (Explore, Exploit, Expand, Exterminate), where players begin by selecting one of eight faiths and then work to build their empire[^ref-15]. Lords can advance up to level 12 (champions up to level 10), with three character classes available: Warrior, Thief, and Mage[^ref-18].

Players must manage resources, recruit armies, explore dungeons, and engage in both diplomacy and warfare. The game features over 160 different spells and over 90 artifacts that can be discovered and used strategically[^ref-1]. Combat strengths vary between faiths, with some excelling at ranged combat while others favor melee or magical approaches[^ref-18].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The core gameplay revolves around strategic resource management and tactical combat. Each faith has distinct advantages and disadvantages - for example, the Life faith features "extremely powerful archers, and great magic skills" but lacks "strong armor," while the Water faith has significant economic advantages that allow them to "have their capital and all related buildings on level 3 by turn 50 while AT THE SAME TIME fielding 2 full size armies"[^ref-18][^ref-19].

The game includes single-player campaigns and multiplayer over the Internet, LAN, modem, or null-modem cable[^ref-7]; the manual allows up to four players over a LAN[^ref-8], while a 1997 Computer Games Strategy Plus review listed 2-8 players[^ref-40]. Lords serve as the most important units, as their death typically ends the game unless backup lords have been established[^ref-18]. Champions and lords can be equipped with treasures found in dungeons or taken from other factions[^ref-1]. Each faith's unit roster creates different tactical approaches: Chaos magic is based on chance and does extraordinary damage but can hurt the caster's own army[^ref-18], while the Water faith can leverage economic advantages for sustained military campaigns[^ref-19].

Artifacts grant faith- and class-specific bonuses such as extra attack, armor, or mana, and some can cast a spell once per day[^ref-16]. Managing the recruitment and upkeep of troops while balancing spell research and artifact hunting creates a complex economic puzzle central to long-term strategy.

### Modern Assessment

On Steam, 92% of 534 user reviews of the Special Edition are positive[^ref-10]. As of October 2026, GOG.com users rate it 4.2/5 from 117 reviews[^ref-29], and Metacritic users score the Special Edition 8.2/10 from 15 ratings[^ref-17] and the original 7.9/10 from 7 ratings[^ref-41]. GameRankings aggregated a 76.50% score for the original game[^ref-27], and MobyGames lists a 72% critic average from 27 ratings[^ref-7].

One modern review noted that "Lords of Magic could have been bigger" and praised its combination of "simplified elements from many gaming genres"[^ref-28]. However, technical issues persist, with some users reporting that "GOG made a bit of a mistake with this one. They applied the 3.01 beta patch instead of the stable patch, which causes extremely powerful units to spawn like mad early in the game even on easy mode"[^ref-29].

## Development

### Origins

Lords of Magic was developed by Impressions Games, a studio that "specialized in historical strategy games, and is most well known for its City Building Series"[^ref-30]. The game represented a departure from their typical historical focus into fantasy strategy gaming. Creative Director Chris Beatrice stated that "Lords of Magic Special Edition is the game we always wanted to make," describing the team's design philosophy[^ref-31].

### Production

The development team included Creative Director Chris Beatrice, designers Jeff Fiske, Gregor Koomey and Steve Serafino, Lead Programmer Mike Gingerich, and a total of 59 credited individuals across 62 roles[^ref-32]. The game featured extensive voice acting, with notable performers including Fran Brill, Ralph Byers, Keith Charles, and Thom Christopher[^ref-8]. The music was composed by Keith Zizza[^ref-13], with Ed Saltzman handling sound and video[^ref-8][^ref-32]; one reviewer wrote that "Keith Zizza did a great job with the music, there is an apropos theme for each of the individual elements. Each track is nicely orchestrated and epic sounding"[^ref-33].

The game was localized for German audiences with a full German voice cast including Peter Engel, Petra Espeloer, and others[^ref-32]. GameSpot found the release unstable[^ref-22], and Sierra issued a series of patches for the original game, up to version 2.0a[^ref-20].

### Technical Achievements

Lords of Magic was built on a custom engine[^ref-38]. The game required Windows 95, a Pentium 75 processor, 16MB RAM, Super VGA graphics, and a double-speed CD-ROM drive at minimum, with recommended specifications of Pentium 166 and 32MB RAM[^ref-21][^ref-34]. It runs only at 640x480[^ref-34]; on modern systems, Wikipedia advises setting the desktop to 800x600, with 1360x768 the highest known playable resolution[^ref-1].

The game's data files used the .mpq format and could be modified using tools like Ladik's MPQ Editor, enabling community modding[^ref-39]. Installation options ranged from 135MB for lower-end systems to 390MB for a full installation[^ref-8].

## Legacy

Lords of Magic has maintained a dedicated fan community with active modding support decades after its release. The game spawned several community modifications, including the GSZero mod that "adjustment has been made to make all AI lords mages to allow better artifact rewards in level 11 dungeons along with many other changes including balancing unit strengths"[^ref-35].

GameSpot's review said the game "falls far short of its potential" given its promise of combining the gameplay of Heroes of Might & Magic II with the style of Lords of the Realm II[^ref-22]. Despite its flaws, the game remains available through digital distribution on Steam and GOG.com[^ref-10][^ref-29].

The Special Edition, released in 1998, attempted to address many of the original game's shortcomings. GameSpot's review of it said it "corrects a number of flaws and adds several new features but somehow ends up being only a tiny bit more appealing than its predecessor," and named Heroes of Might & Magic II and Warlords III as "simply better options for a fantasy-strategy fix"[^ref-36].
