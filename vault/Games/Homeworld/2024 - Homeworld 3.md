# Homeworld 3

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 19, 2026</small>

## Overview

Homeworld 3 is a real-time strategy game developed by Blackbird Interactive and published by Gearbox Publishing, released on May 13, 2024 for Windows PC.[^ref-1] The game represents the long-awaited continuation of the legendary space-faring RTS franchise, arriving more than two decades after Homeworld 2's 2003 release.[^ref-17] Blackbird CEO Rob Cunningham called it "the realization of the vision of what we set out to create in the Homeworld series," with modern technology allowing "large-scale dynamic space combat packed with emotional sci-fi storytelling."[^ref-3]

The game is set 100 years after the events of Homeworld 2,[^ref-11] when a mysterious, destructive Anomaly has begun shutting down the hyperspace gates that connected the galaxy.[^ref-4] Players command the Hiigaran Mothership Khar-Kushan, whose navigator Imogen S'Jet must find out what is causing the Anomaly,[^ref-5] while the legendary Karan S'Jet, her mentor, has disappeared and "passed into myth and religious idolatry."[^ref-3][^ref-7]

Blackbird Interactive bills the game as "Tactical, beautiful, and wholly unique," with fleet combat in "dazzling, fully 3D space."[^ref-6] Its main addition to the series is cover-based tactics around terrain such as colossal ancient megaliths, asteroid fields and derelict wreckage.[^ref-26] CGMagazine wrote that it "balances incredibly technical gameplay with streamlined mechanics that is easy to get into, but difficult to master."[^ref-7]

## Story Summary

Homeworld 3 is set in 9745 GSY, 100 years after the events of Homeworld 2.[^ref-72] It follows the "Age of S'Jet," a golden age that began after Karan S'Jet unlocked an ancient hyperspace gate network.[^ref-10] The galaxy has enjoyed "an age of abundance thanks to the Hyperspace Gate Network," but the gates are now "catastrophically failing."[^ref-3] A mysterious, destructive Anomaly is shutting down the gates that connected the galaxy and its peoples.[^ref-4]

The narrative centers on Imogen S'Jet, a scientist and the protégé of Karan S'Jet,[^ref-11] described by CGMagazine as "Karan's grandaughter and mentee."[^ref-7] Karan and the previous Mothership, the Khar Sajuuk, "mysteriously disappeared into something known as The Anomaly,"[^ref-7] and Karan has since passed into myth and religious idolatry.[^ref-3] Imogen, navigator of the new Mothership Khar-Kushan,[^ref-5] must become Fleet Command and piece together the mystery behind Karan's disappearance and the Anomaly.[^ref-11]

Isaac Paktu is the story's other key character, and the Incarnate faction is the main threat to the Hiigaran fleet.[^ref-12] The story unfolds across 13 missions.[^ref-13] The developers deliberately kept players in the dark about the enemy early in the game, with lead writer Martin Cirulis explaining: "We didn't want to front-load it so that players would've been like, 'Oh yeah, we're going there to defeat that person because that faction does this.' Nope, you are flying by the seat of your pants just like our heroes are."[^ref-69]

PCGamesN's reviewer "mostly enjoyed how the dramatic cutscenes seamlessly transition into gameplay," but the story became one of the main player complaints.[^ref-16]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Homeworld 3 maintains the series' signature fully 3D space combat, allowing players to command their fleets across all axes of movement in vast, open battlespaces.[^ref-17] The development team worked extensively to make the controls more accessible than previous entries, with Blackbird founder Rob Cunningham noting: "It's way easier to play Homeworld 3 than it was to play Homeworld 1."[^ref-17] The game offers multiple control schemes including traditional mouse-based camera control and a new WASD camera option for players who prefer that style.[^ref-18]

The interface builds upon the classic Homeworld control scheme while introducing modern quality-of-life improvements.[^ref-21] Players can issue commands to individual ships or grouped squadrons, with the ability to assign formations, attack patterns, and movement orders through the command interface. However, some critics found unit responsiveness inconsistent: Eurogamer wrote that when you ask a ship to back away from certain death, "they'll either ignore the call or respond so lazily that they'll lose almost all of their hitpoints in the process."[^ref-20]

### Structure and Progression

The single-player campaign consists of 13 story missions that take approximately 6-8 hours to complete on standard difficulty.[^ref-13] A key series hallmark returns: fleet persistence between missions, meaning players carry their surviving ships and accumulated resources from one mission to the next.[^ref-21] This creates meaningful consequences for losses and encourages careful resource management throughout the campaign.

The campaign missions include:

1. **Hiigara** – Take command of the Khar-Kushan Mothership[^ref-13]
2. **Facility 315** – Retrieve and install a Mothership Production Facility module[^ref-13]
3. **Kesura Minor** – Activate the ancient Hyperspace Gate and jump to the Anomaly[^ref-13]
4. **Kala Terminus** – Repair and protect the Mothership after a disastrous Hyperspace jump[^ref-13]
5. **Trinity Gate** – Activate the Trinity Gate[^ref-13]
6. **The Lighthouse** – Navigate a dense asteroid field, salvaging an Incarnate Carrier[^ref-13]
7. **Naraka Gate** – Defend the Mothership from Incarnate ships while it repairs[^ref-13]
8. **Storm Strike** – Trigger two crippling explosions on the Warsage Citadel[^ref-13]
9. **Warsage Citadel** – Destroy the docked Incarnate War Fleet and take on the Warsage[^ref-13]
10. **Buran Ice Shelf** – Search for a lost Mothership using the Ice Shelf to hide your position[^ref-13]
11. **Chamber of Storms** – Fend off waves of enemies while retrieving the lost Mothership[^ref-13]
12. **Noctuua-27** – Survive the battle in Incarnate territory[^ref-13]
13. **The Lament** – Destroy the ancient weapon before it can be used against the galaxy[^ref-13]

Beyond the campaign, the game offers several additional modes. War Games is a roguelike mode, played solo or with up to two friends, built from three-mission "mini campaigns" in which players unlock upgrades and artifacts.[^ref-23] Online multiplayer supports up to six players in versus and co-op modes,[^ref-51] and six Skirmish maps were available at launch.[^ref-24]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The most significant gameplay innovation in Homeworld 3 is the introduction of terrain-based combat through massive megalithic structures scattered throughout the game's environments.[^ref-25] Rory McGuire, then Blackbird's Chief Creative Officer, described the change as moving space combat from "playing in a pool where you could go up and down and go side to side, but there was nothing in the pool" to something "more like a coral reef."[^ref-18]

The terrain system introduces cover-based tactics, with players using megaliths, asteroid fields and derelict wreckage as cover or to hide their movements from enemies.[^ref-26] "Fully simulated ballistics make line of sight and cover critical strategic considerations,"[^ref-3] and McGuire noted that a tracking missile will hit the cover if a ship ducks behind it.[^ref-18]

Resource collection remains central to gameplay, with players gathering Resource Units (RUs) from two sources: fixed resource "veins" and wreckages.[^ref-21] The game has no upkeep costs for units, encouraging players to build large fleets, though population caps limit maximum fleet size unless players capture enemy vessels.[^ref-21]

The unit counter system has been deliberately softened compared to previous entries. As Game Director Lance Mueller explained: "Homeworld 1 and 2 were known for very hard counters for units. You'd need an assault frigate or flak frigate to take out fighter units. For Homeworld 3, we're trying to soften that as much as possible."[^ref-17] The Hiigaran fleet features 16 combat ship types specializing in anti-capital ship warfare, while the Incarnate faction offers 15 ships focused on destroying smaller craft quickly.[^ref-27]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Critics were generally favourable: Metacritic rates the game "Generally Favorable" at 75/100.[^ref-36] Players were far less positive, and PCGamesN summed up the launch as "solid critic reviews but very mixed player ones."[^ref-16]

IGN awarded the game 8/10, with reviewer Dan Stapleton noting that "Homeworld 3's single-player campaign brings the series' epic space battles in for intense, close-quarters combat."[^ref-29] Shacknews gave a highly positive 9/10, with TJ Denzer concluding that "Homeworld 3 plays incredible from top to bottom."[^ref-29] Revisiting the game for the November 2024 War Games DLC, Denzer wrote that after the update "Strike craft spend more time fighting and less time buzzing around uselessly or getting mauled."[^ref-70]

PC Gamer's Fraser Brown scored the game 77/100, writing: "Homeworld 3 takes some big swings, but while it's a very good RTS, it never quite comes together in the same way as its predecessors."[^ref-31] CGMagazine gave 8/10, praising how the game "balances incredibly technical gameplay with streamlined mechanics that is easy to get into, but difficult to master."[^ref-7]

GamesRadar+ offered a more tempered assessment, with Rick Lane noting: "Homeworld's operatic space combat is as engrossing as ever in its third outing, although a weaker story and a slightly odd cover system dim the shine of its star."[^ref-32] Digital Trends was more critical, with Jason Rodriguez scoring it 3/5 and calling it "a disappointment that was 20 years in the making."[^ref-33]

Inverse provided one of the most enthusiastic reviews, with Hayes Madsen scoring 9/10 and declaring: "Homeworld has never been better... It's genuinely hard for me to think of any more ways Homeworld 3 could have honored the legacy of the franchise."[^ref-34] IGN Italia offered a contrasting view with a 6.5/10 ("Sufficiente"): Andrea Giongiani called the plot *dozzinale* (run-of-the-mill) and the game a disappointment next to its predecessors.[^ref-23]

### Modern Assessment

Critic and player scores diverged sharply.[^ref-36]

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **Metacritic (Critic):** 75/100 (41 reviews, Generally Favorable)[^ref-36]
- **Metacritic (User):** 3.1/10 (86 ratings, Generally Unfavorable)[^ref-36]
- **OpenCritic:** 77 Top Critic Average (33 critic reviews), 70% of critics recommend[^ref-29]
- **Steam:** Mixed, 41% positive of 3,175 English reviews (October 2026);[^ref-71] 38% of over 4,500 reviews in November 2024[^ref-38]

According to PCGamesN, the game was criticized for its story and for mechanics "which felt overly streamlined compared to other games in the series."[^ref-16] Technical issues at launch, including what Digital Trends called "egregious pathfinding problems and combat AI woes" and co-op disconnections, further dampened reception.[^ref-33]

In November 2024, when Blackbird announced that Update 1.3 would be the game's "last major content update," PCGamesN reported an average concurrent Steam player base of less than 50.[^ref-38]

## Development

### Origins

The genesis of Homeworld 3 stretches back to the original development of Homeworld 2 in the early 2000s. As Blackbird founder Rob Cunningham put it: "In essence, Homeworld 3 was our original dream for Homeworld 2." He called the vision "utterly impossible" with the technology of the time: "We couldn't do massive environments with these megaliths, so we had to wait 20 years."[^ref-17]

Blackbird Interactive was founded by former Relic Entertainment staff who had worked on the original Homeworld games. MobyGames gives 2010 as the founding year, and Cunningham told Eurogamer "When I started Blackbird it was 2010";[^ref-41][^ref-42] the Homeworld fan wiki instead dates it to 2007 and says the founders left because THQ was no longer interested in more ambitious strategy games.[^ref-39] The studio's first project was Hardware, a free-to-play strategy game inspired by Homeworld.[^ref-41]

When THQ went bankrupt in 2013, Gearbox Software emerged as the highest bidder for the Homeworld franchise, acquiring the IP with stated intentions to "preserve and assemble the purest form of the original acclaimed and beloved games."[^ref-40] A deal was subsequently signed at PAX between Gearbox and Blackbird Interactive to transform Hardware into the next official Homeworld game.[^ref-41] This became the prequel Homeworld: Deserts of Kharak, released in January 2016.[^ref-41]

According to Cunningham, the "spark of Homeworld 3" was "seeded somewhere around 2017. After Deserts of Kharak."[^ref-42] The project was officially announced in August 2019 alongside a crowdfunding campaign on Fig.[^ref-43]

### Production

The crowdfunding campaign set a symbolic $1 goal, intended primarily as a community engagement tool rather than funding necessity. Gearbox clarified: "We want it to be clear that the game is on a solid foundation, funding-wise."[^ref-44] The campaign raised over $1.5 million from more than 8,400 backers.[^ref-45]

In June 2021 the investment portion of the Fig campaign was cancelled following Embracer Group's acquisition of Gearbox earlier that year, with Fig CEO Justin Bailey confirming "no money ever changed hands" regarding the investment component.[^ref-45] Randy Pitchford, Gearbox studio boss, described the acquisition as "like a rocket booster to our ambitions."[^ref-45]

In August 2022 Cunningham described the Homeworld 3 team as "40-odd" people within a studio of "330-something";[^ref-42] the full MobyGames credits list 1,115 people (1,088 professional roles).[^ref-8] Blackbird moved to a four-day work week at the beginning of 2022, midway through development.[^ref-54] Chief Creative Officer Rory McGuire told Game Rant that August: "It's been amazing. It's been really amazing... numbers-wise, it's almost identical to where we were with the five-day work week, except everyone is more refreshed."[^ref-68]

The game underwent multiple delays from its original Q4 2022 target.[^ref-3] In June 2022 it moved to the first half of 2023; the statement promised to deliver quality "in a healthy and sustainable way for all those involved." PC Gamer read this as avoiding crunch, while noting that "the word 'crunch' isn't used anywhere in the message."[^ref-77][^ref-47] In May 2023 the game slipped to February 2024.[^ref-78] It was later set for March 8, 2024, then on February 7, 2024 delayed to May 13 after feedback from the public War Games demo and outside playtesters.[^ref-48]

### Studio and Publisher Changes

The final stretch of development coincided with upheaval at both companies. In August 2023 Blackbird confirmed it had laid off 41 employees after several unannounced projects were cancelled.[^ref-73] A second round of layoffs followed on February 14, 2024, a week after the final delay.[^ref-74] On March 27, 2024, Take-Two Interactive announced a definitive agreement to acquire Gearbox from Embracer Group in a deal valued at $460 million, with Homeworld among the intellectual property included.[^ref-75]

### Technical Achievements

Homeworld 3 was built on Unreal Engine 4 (engine build 4.27.2).[^ref-9] The game features real-time ship damage, dynamic ship destruction, and situationally responsive audio that reacts to the battlefield state.[^ref-11]

The terrain system is the game's biggest change. Rob Cunningham told Eurogamer: "When we made Homeworld 1, the big deal to us at the time [...] was this thing was in 3D and it was in empty space."[^ref-42] Recalling those limits, he later told PC Gamer: "The problem was the space: there was just so much space. We had some asteroids, but that was it."[^ref-17]

Gearbox's announcement said "fully simulated ballistics make line of sight and cover critical strategic considerations."[^ref-3] Game Director Lance Mueller noted that "Terrain creates a soft counter for the hard counters in the game, too."[^ref-49]

Concept designer Brennan Massicotte, discussing ship design, explained the art team's approach: "The Mothership is a great example because it retains so much of the core idea, but feels like a flying knife chiseled out of stealth angles."[^ref-50] Art director Karl Gryc, recalling the Deserts of Kharak vehicle work whose shape language "had a significant influence on Homeworld 3," said: "We put a lot of effort into ensuring everything felt true to scale while also being aesthetically pleasing."[^ref-50]

### Technical Issues

Reviewers reported several technical problems at launch:

- "Egregious pathfinding problems and combat AI woes"[^ref-33]
- Ships ignoring granular orders, such as waypoints, and flying into enemy fire[^ref-64]
- Co-op War Games sessions disconnecting mid-match, forfeiting rewards and XP[^ref-33]
- Maps with "a bright hazy glare" that made resources hard to see[^ref-33]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- The game is dedicated to the memory of Alex Gomersall (1981-2022)[^ref-8]
- Following a fan request, the Mothership Khar-Kushan can rotate 90 degrees on its axis to switch between vertical ("banana") and horizontal ("croissant") orientations[^ref-67]
- Mission 6, The Lighthouse, is an asteroid-field mission that Lance Mueller called "an homage to Homeworld 1's asteroid mission, with a little bit of a twist"[^ref-54][^ref-13]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Third-party revenue calculators estimate the Year One Pass DLC at roughly 25,600 units sold on Steam.[^ref-56]

The game launched at $59.99 for the Standard Edition, with premium editions ranging up to $174.99 for the Collector's Edition, which included physical ship models, a lithograph, and a keychain.[^ref-57] Early access for Fleet Command and Collector's Edition pre-orders began May 10, 2024, three days before the general release.[^ref-24]

### Awards

- **Best Technology/Innovation** at the Canadian Game Awards 2025[^ref-65]
- Finalist, **Strategy** category, TIGA Games Industry Awards 2024[^ref-66]
- Named **#8 on PC Gaming Show's Most Wanted list** prior to release[^ref-58]

### Collections and DLC

Post-launch content followed an initially ambitious Year One Roadmap that was subsequently condensed:[^ref-31]

- **Historical & Technical Manual** (Free DLC, May 2024) – Lore document covering Hiigaran military history and ship specifications[^ref-59]
- **Kalan Raiders Fleet Pack** (August 15, 2024) – Two new War Games fleets[^ref-60][^ref-79]
- **Somtaaw Fleet Pack** (November 2024) – Classic faction returns[^ref-61]
- **Taiidan Fleet Pack** (November 2024) – Classic faction returns[^ref-61]

On September 20, 2024, Blackbird said it would combine all remaining paid DLC and free content drops into one November delivery, bringing forward two packs originally scheduled for 2025; in total, "three (3) free updates and two (2) paid DLCs worth of content."[^ref-76] Update 1.3 arrived on November 21, 2024, adding Hyperspace and Hyperspace Inhibitor abilities to Skirmish, 45 new War Games artifacts, 9 new challenges, a new Reinforce ability, and an overhaul of armor and weapon classes.[^ref-53][^ref-61]

### Fan Projects

The game supports modding through mod.io, with a custom in-game browser for downloading player-made Skirmish maps. Blackbird's custom Unreal editor for modders was not ready at launch; the studio said in May 2024 that it was still finalizing it for public release.[^ref-24]

### Critical Perspective

Homeworld 3 arrived 21 years after Homeworld 2.[^ref-34] Reviewers singled out its looks, with Geek Sleep Rinse Repeat calling it "one of the best-looking games I've played this year, no doubt about it."[^ref-63] Player reception was harsher, centring on the story and on mechanics that felt "overly streamlined" next to earlier entries.[^ref-16]

Blackbird ended active development with Update 1.3, about six months after launch.[^ref-38] At launch, lead producer Iain Myers-Smith had said: "We're on the cusp of a potential RTS Renaissance."[^ref-54] Back in 2022, Rory McGuire had described making the sequel as "a bit of a dream. Not quite a dream come true, but a dream brought to life."[^ref-68]
