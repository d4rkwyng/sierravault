# Cannonball Blitz

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 27, 2026</small>

## Overview

Cannonball Blitz is a 1982 action platformer developed by Olaf Lubeck and published by On-Line Systems, the company that would later become Sierra On-Line.[^ref-1] John Romero called it "the best Donkey Kong clone on the Apple II when it debuted" and "the closest Donkey Kong clone (other than Atarisoft's) you'll ever see on the Apple II."[^ref-2] The game gives Donkey Kong a Revolutionary War theme, replacing the arcade original's barrel-rolling gorilla with a soldier stationed atop Cannonball Castle, raining cannonballs down upon the player.[^ref-2][^ref-3]

The game was released during Sierra's prolific period of creating arcade-style action games, following its earlier clones Crossfire (a Targ clone) and Jawbreaker (a Pac-Man clone).[^ref-2] Players control a rebel soldier attempting to scale Nutcracker Hill to reach and destroy Cannonball Castle, facing rough terrain, enemy troops, and the titular constant rain of cannonballs.[^ref-4] The game featured three distinct screens that mirrored the structure of Donkey Kong while introducing unique mechanics like balloon rides to reach higher platforms.[^ref-2]

Cannonball Blitz achieved some notoriety in the Apple hacking community as being difficult to crack.[^ref-3] The game ran on any Apple II with 48K of memory,[^ref-1] and was ported to the VIC-20[^ref-21][^ref-22] and TI-99/4A.[^ref-3][^ref-5]

## Story Summary

Cannonball Castle, fortress of the enemy redcoats, sits high atop Nutcracker Hill.[^ref-4] The player assumes the role of a rebel soldier whose mission is to climb Nutcracker Hill and destroy the castle. The journey is fraught with danger, as the castle's defenders have set numerous traps and obstacles designed solely for the elimination of would-be attackers.[^ref-4]

The narrative framework gives the Donkey Kong formula a Revolutionary War theme.[^ref-2][^ref-3] Rather than rescuing a kidnapped woman, the player's ultimate objective on the first level is to capture a flag at the top of the screen.[^ref-6] A soldier stands in place of the large ape, manning cannons that send a continuous barrage of cannonballs rolling down toward the player.[^ref-6]

The game's promotional materials emphasized the dire odds facing the player: "Rough terrain and enemy troops are bent on your destruction, and a constant rain of cannonballs could cause a fatally large headache. Many men before you have tried and failed, so it's all up to you..."[^ref-4] Upon successfully completing all three levels, players are treated to a small celebration scene before the game restarts at the first level with increased difficulty.[^ref-3]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Cannonball Blitz can be played with either a joystick or the keyboard; at startup the game asks which the player wants to use, and one MobyGames reviewer found it "much easier with joystick."[^ref-2] John Romero, who played with the keyboard, wrote that "The only problems with the game were its keyboard control, but that was typical of the Apple II."[^ref-2] The single-player game required precise timing to navigate obstacles and jump over or avoid the rolling cannonballs descending from the castle above.[^ref-5]

### Structure and Progression

The game features three distinct screens that players must complete sequentially, similar to the original Donkey Kong's multi-screen structure.[^ref-2] After completing all three levels, players view a brief celebration animation before the game cycles back to the first level with modified timing to increase difficulty.[^ref-3]

- **Level 1:** Features seesaws instead of traditional ladders, requiring players to use timing and momentum to ascend. The objective is to reach a flag at the top of the screen, accomplished by grabbing onto a balloon that carries the player to the goal platform.[^ref-2][^ref-6]

- **Level 2:** The player starts on the lowest of six floors connected by ladders and must run over all twelve rivets to bring the structure down, while cannons pursue the player up and down ladders; hammers on two floors can be used to smash the cannons.[^ref-24]

- **Level 3:** Described as "particularly challenging" and by one MobyGames reviewer as "just impossible," this final screen pushes player skills to their limits.[^ref-3][^ref-2]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The goal of each level is to reach the flag at the top while evading the oncoming cannonballs.[^ref-7] Players can also catch balloons that occasionally fly by and ride them up to higher platforms; *Computer Gaming World* advised ignoring them except at the top of the first screen, where one is needed to reach the flag.[^ref-24] One MobyGames player review singled out "the innovation of the balloon" as a highlight.[^ref-2]

The game's difficulty was noted as exceptional even among Sierra's catalog: "Out of all Sierra's games, CannonBall Blitz is the most difficult."[^ref-2]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Cannonball Blitz received positive attention from gaming publications of its era. Ron Brinkmann of *Computer Gaming World* gave the game an enthusiastic endorsement, writing: "In the final analysis, Cannonball Blitz is a game every bit as challenging and fun as the arcade original. It will give you hours (days, months, years) of enjoyment."[^ref-3][^ref-24] The same review listed the Apple II disk at $34.95.[^ref-24]

*Ahoy!* magazine reviewed the VIC-20 version in January 1984, with E. C. Meade memorably declaring: "Cannonball Blitz, make no mistake about it, is Donkey Kong in dress blues. Not a bloody thing new here. However, you're going to find it a barrel of fun."[^ref-3][^ref-25] Meade found the animation "lovely to behold—if drab," called the sound effects "unbelievable," praised the freedom to "play any level you want when you want," and concluded that "The Vic-20 cartridge is a very good version of a fine game."[^ref-25]

### Modern Assessment

The game has received retrospective analysis from notable industry figures. John Romero, who would go on to co-create DOOM and Quake, wrote a short player review of Cannonball Blitz on MobyGames in 2005, praising it as "the best Donkey Kong clone on the Apple II when it debuted."[^ref-2] He noted that "the sounds were charming and the animation was decent" for its time.[^ref-2]

However, not all modern assessments have been as favorable. One MobyGames player review, by ClarkBHM in 2004, found the game fell short of its potential: "A good idea for a game in theory, but CannonBall Blitz fell much too far short of the mark."[^ref-2]

## Development

### Origins

Cannonball Blitz emerged from On-Line Systems' strategy of creating home computer versions of popular arcade games.[^ref-2] Designer Olaf Lubeck had previously written Gobbler, a Pac-Man clone that On-Line pulled after Atari claimed infringement, and ported John Harris's Jawbreaker to the Apple II.[^ref-23] After On-Line's legal fight with Atari over those games ended in a settlement, the company "had no hesitation publishing" Lubeck's Donkey Kong clone, on the view that game ideas and mechanics could not be copyrighted.[^ref-23]

Lubeck wrote Cannonball Blitz in his spare time while working at Los Alamos National Laboratory.[^ref-23] *Computer Gaming World*'s 1982 review gave the publisher's address as Coarsegold, California.[^ref-24]

### Production

The game was written in machine language.[^ref-4] Sources disagree on the year: Sierra Chest lists a first release of 1981 and an Internet Archive physical-media item is dated 1981, while others list the commercial release as 1982.[^ref-4][^ref-26][^ref-1]

**Development Credits:**[^ref-5]
- **Written by:** Olaf Lubeck
- **Cover Art:** Michael Carroll

### Technical Achievements

The game reproduced the three-screen structure of Donkey Kong while implementing unique mechanical twists appropriate for home computer hardware.[^ref-2] The balloon mechanic, allowing players to catch and ride balloons to higher platforms, was called an innovation by one MobyGames reviewer.[^ref-2][^ref-24]

After the game loads, an animation shows a soldier shooting a cannonball at the castle, which is destroyed to reveal the author.[^ref-2] *Ahoy!*'s 1984 review of the VIC-20 cartridge called the animation "lovely to behold—if drab" and the sound effects "unbelievable",[^ref-25] and John Romero later recalled of the Apple II original that "the sounds were charming and the animation was decent."[^ref-2]

### Technical Issues

The Apple II version's keyboard controls were cited as a source of gameplay friction, a common limitation shared by many games on the platform during this era.[^ref-2] The third level's extreme difficulty was mentioned by multiple reviewers, though this may have been intentional design rather than a technical issue.[^ref-2][^ref-3]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

Cannonball Blitz achieved some notoriety in the Apple hacking community as being difficult to crack.[^ref-3] The game contained a hidden message on track 17, sector D of the disk that taunted would-be crackers:

- **Copy Protection Message:** Accounts of the wording differ. The cracking tutorial *Krakowicz's Kracking Korner* quotes "Olaf Lubeck's challenge in track 17, sector D" as "YOU'LL NEVER CRACK IT",[^ref-27][^ref-3] while The Cutting Room Floor, citing 4am, gives "YOU'LL NEVER BREAK IT (TRY IT ON ANOTHER MACHINE)".[^ref-15]

**Additional Trivia:**
- The game was cracked and preserved by "4am" as crack number 735.[^ref-12]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

No sales figures for Cannonball Blitz have been found. The game was ported to the VIC-20 (1983) and TI-99/4A (1984).[^ref-5] It has a community "Dreamlist" entry on GOG.com, where users request re-releases; it is not sold there.[^ref-7]

### Collections

Cannonball Blitz appears on several multi-game Apple II disk images in the Internet Archive's software library. These disks pair it with other publishers' games and are not compilations released by On-Line Systems:
- Disk with Choplifter and Zenith[^ref-17]
- Disk with Sneakers, Asteroids, and Centipede[^ref-19]
- Disk with Night Crawler, Snack Attack, Sn0ggle, and Tranquility Base[^ref-13]

### Related Publications

**Original Documentation:**
- The game's retail package included back cover promotional copy describing the Revolutionary War setting and gameplay challenges.[^ref-4]
- The promotional text described the game as "an exciting new arcade game that will have you climbing the walls (the walls of Cannonball Castle) over and over again."[^ref-4]

### Critical Perspective

Cannonball Blitz is, in Wikipedia's words, "an historic military spin on Donkey Kong": rolling cannonballs replace barrels, a soldier stands in for the large ape, and the first level's objective is a flag rather than a kidnapped woman.[^ref-3]

On-Line Systems had published action games from its start, but the company "was first and foremost known for its hugely successful Hi-Res Adventure series."[^ref-23] Alongside those adventures it released many arcade games based on coin-op originals, including Crossfire and Jawbreaker before Cannonball Blitz.[^ref-2] John Romero called Cannonball Blitz "the best Donkey Kong clone on the Apple II when it debuted" and "the closest Donkey Kong clone (other than Atarisoft's)" on the platform,[^ref-2] while *Ahoy!* found the VIC-20 version had "Not a bloody thing new here" but was "a barrel of fun."[^ref-25]
