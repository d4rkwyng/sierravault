# WallWar

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

WallWar is an action game developed by Peter Oliphant and published by Sierra On-Line for the Atari 8-bit computers in 1983[^ref-1][^ref-9]. Originally titled "Force Fields" during development[^ref-3], it was released under Sierra's short-lived SierraVision label[^ref-3][^ref-9][^ref-22]. A Sierra software catalog of the period grouped WallWar with the company's other arcade-style titles under a SIERRAVISION heading, separate from its SIERRAVENTURE adventure games[^ref-26].

Two joystick-controlled "Microbots" each defend a plasma field behind a moving, multi-layered wall of colored bricks, and the first player to destroy the opponent's plasma wins[^ref-24][^ref-25]. Retro365 described the game as "visually impressive with a lot of things going on at once", noting that it borrowed elements from different games, such as the kinetic brick wall in the middle[^ref-3]. One player can face the computer, or two players can play head to head[^ref-1][^ref-24][^ref-25].

Period reviews were split. The Atari Computer Enthusiasts newsletter called it "an innovative game"[^ref-24], while *The Book of Atari Software 1984* gave it an overall rating of D despite a B+ for graphics[^ref-25]. WallWar "sold only a very limited number of copies"[^ref-3].

## Story Summary

WallWar has no story beyond its premise. Sierra's catalog described it as "Two futuristic robots engage in their favorite game. Or is it a game?"[^ref-26]

Each player controls a "Microbot" protecting a "plasma field". The two sides are separated by a five-layer light barrier that must be penetrated[^ref-24].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

MobyGames lists joystick and keyboard as input devices[^ref-1]. Period sources list the game as requiring joysticks[^ref-24][^ref-26]. A demonstration game shows how play works before you start[^ref-24].

The game accommodates one or two players[^ref-1][^ref-26]: "you may play the Atari, or another human opponent"[^ref-24].

### Structure and Progression

The playfield is divided by a moving, multi-layered wall of colored bricks, with each player's plasma container behind their Microbot[^ref-25]. Retro365 summarized the objective as breaking through the center wall and destroying the opponent's force field[^ref-3]:

- **Offensive Goal:** Shoot through the wall of bricks so that shots reach the opposing Microbot or its plasma[^ref-25]
- **Defensive Goal:** Protect your plasma field by putting your Microbot in the way of incoming shots or by using "laser" beams to destroy the incoming missiles[^ref-24]
- **Victory Condition:** "The first to destroy his opponent's plasma supply wins"[^ref-25]

### Puzzles and Mechanics

WallWar has no puzzles. A player cannot "kill" the opposing Microbot by shooting it; a hit only slows it down and damages it temporarily[^ref-24]. Hits on vulnerable spots, especially the side blinkers that steer the laser or the gunport, can disable a Microbot for a while[^ref-25]. Bullets can be fired fast and straight, or slowly and steered[^ref-25].

The ACE reviewer called it "not your usual 'shoot 'em up', in spite of the fact the game involves almost constant shooting"[^ref-24]. *The Book of Atari Software 1984* found that "the player needs to get past the boredom of knocking out a nearly endless supply of intervening bricks before any of his bullets strike a target"[^ref-25].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Jim Bumpas reviewed WallWar in the April 1983 *Atari Computer Enthusiasts* (ACE) newsletter. He called it "an innovative game" and wrote that "the graphics are done in very fine resolution, and the use of color is very pleasing. The sounds are appropriate"[^ref-24]. He singled out the demonstration mode and "the indirect nature of the violence inflicted upon one's opponent" as innovations[^ref-24].

*The Book of Atari Software 1984* (p. 117) gave WallWar an overall rating of D and a B+ for graphics. Its other grades included Controllability C, Documentation D+, Holds Interest D- and Value for Money D[^ref-25]. The review concluded: "It is a pointless abstract game with exceptionally beautiful and colorful graphics ... It is, unfortunately, one of those non-entity games with nice graphics"[^ref-25].

### Modern Assessment

Retro365's 2021 retrospective by Ernst Krogtoft called the game "visually impressive with a lot of things going on at once"[^ref-3]. It also noted that "Neither WallWar nor Mr. Cool became commercial success stories"[^ref-3].

## Development

### Origins

By 1982, Peter Oliphant was a programmer at Mattel and wanted to move into personal computers. During a one-week vacation he programmed a small Atari 8-bit prototype arcade game called "Force Fields"[^ref-3]. Mattel had no interest in supporting a competitor's hardware, so Oliphant kept refining the game during evenings and nights. By early autumn 1982 it was ready to show to publishers[^ref-3].

(Retro365's mention of MOS Technology's 6502 refers to the handheld prototypes Oliphant programmed at Mattel, not to WallWar specifically[^ref-3].)

### Production

Oliphant took a day off and went to Oakhurst to show the game to Ken Williams of Sierra On-Line[^ref-3]. Williams liked it and saw potential. Oliphant thought he needed only a month to finish it, but Williams gave him two months and offered $16,000, "three times the amount he had hoped for"[^ref-3]. Oliphant quit Mattel to go independent[^ref-3].

Mattel had rejected the game, but it still regarded it as company property. Oliphant was subpoenaed at his going-away party to give the game up. With help from a lawyer, he resolved the dispute[^ref-3]. He then completed the game and added a splash screen at Williams' request, and Sierra released it as WallWar[^ref-3].

Both period reviewers praised WallWar's visuals. ACE wrote that "the graphics are done in very fine resolution"[^ref-24], and *The Book of Atari Software 1984* said it had "exceptionally beautiful and colorful graphics"[^ref-25]. The game was written in machine language[^ref-9][^ref-25].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

WallWar was a commercial disappointment and "sold only a very limited number of copies"[^ref-3]. Ernst Krogtoft noted that "Neither WallWar nor Mr. Cool became commercial success stories"[^ref-3], referring to the two games Peter Oliphant made for Sierra On-Line. Retro365 adds that after the North American video game crash in late 1983 "everybody moved on", Oliphant included[^ref-3].

### Collections

None of the sources consulted record WallWar in a Sierra compilation. Today the game is available mainly through preservation archives[^ref-4][^ref-22].

### Fan Projects

No significant fan remake or modification projects for WallWar have been documented.

### Related Publications

None of the sources consulted record a hint book or strategy guide for WallWar.

**Primary Documentation Sources:**
- **Retro365:** Lists its sources as a Matt Chat interview with Peter Oliphant, Wikipedia, and Steven Levy's *Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution*[^ref-3]

### Critical Perspective

WallWar was one of Sierra's early arcade-style releases. The company's catalog sold these under the SIERRAVISION heading, separate from its SIERRAVENTURE adventures[^ref-26]. Critics were divided. ACE saw innovation in WallWar's indirect combat[^ref-24], but *The Book of Atari Software 1984* judged its beautiful graphics wasted on a dull game[^ref-25].

The development story behind WallWar is perhaps better known than the game itself. Oliphant moonlighted on it while at Mattel, was subpoenaed by his employer over it, and then saw it sell very few copies[^ref-3].
