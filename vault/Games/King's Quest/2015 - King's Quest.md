# King's Quest

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview
King's Quest is a 2015 episodic adventure game developed by The Odd Gentlemen and published by [[Activision]] under the revived Sierra Entertainment brand for Windows, PlayStation 3, PlayStation 4, Xbox 360, and Xbox One[^ref-1][^ref-3][^ref-13].

Released in five chapters between July 2015 and October 2016, the game reimagines the classic King's Quest franchise, featuring an elderly King Graham narrating his youthful adventures to his granddaughter Gwendolyn[^ref-1][^ref-3].

The game is told as a flashback, with an elderly Graham recounting his adventures to his granddaughter[^ref-3], a framing Common Sense Media likened to *The Princess Bride*[^ref-15].

IGN called it "a beautiful experiment in resurrecting the conventions of gaming's past and dressing them up in the lush graphics available today" and awarded it 8/10[^ref-9].

The game received a 4.5/5 (90/100) rating from Adventure Gamers, who called it "an instant classic" and recommended that "wise, brave, and compassionate adventurers alike should definitely consider having a crack at it"[^ref-6].

## Story Summary
The narrative of King's Quest (2015) unfolds as a series of stories told by an elderly King Graham to his granddaughter Gwendolyn, who listens from his bedside in the kingdom of Daventry[^ref-3]. When Graham dies in the game, he often rewinds the story a bit or remarks it was not what he did[^ref-3]. His narration is also heard during regular gameplay, especially for descriptions of the environment[^ref-3].

The game offers entirely new stories and characters, but they often reference the earlier games[^ref-3]. The new stories are not told in chronological order and are interspersed with sections in the present that revolve around Gwendolyn[^ref-3]. The protagonist is Graham, the same protagonist who first appeared in the original King's Quest in 1984[^ref-3].

The game consists of five chapters[^ref-3]:
- **Chapter 1: A Knight to Remember** - A young Graham arrives in Daventry and enters a grand tournament to become a Knight of Daventry
- **Chapter 2: Rubble Without a Cause** - Newly crowned King Graham is kidnapped by goblins along with Daventry's townsfolk and must escape and save the kingdom[^ref-1]
- **Chapter 3: Once Upon a Climb** - Graham climbs a tower and finds two princesses, Vee and Neese, trapped by the witch Hagatha; the story of how he met Valanice reimagines King's Quest II[^ref-1]
- **Chapter 4: Snow Place Like Home** - After Alexander escapes Manannan, Graham takes the family on vacation, only to find the resort turned into a frozen palace of traps by an icy queen[^ref-1][^ref-11]
- **Chapter 5: The Good Knight** - Near the end of his life, Graham tells Gwendolyn of his final confrontation with Manannan[^ref-1]

The Complete Collection includes an exclusive epilogue that shifts perspective to Gwendolyn, who applies the lessons learned from her grandfather's stories during her first independent adventure[^ref-2][^ref-11].

## Gameplay

The game environment is shown through different perspectives for each scene with scrolling parts, but the camera cannot be controlled[^ref-3]. Visually it has a cel-shaded graphical style[^ref-3]. The player can move Graham freely in the 3D environment[^ref-3].

### Interface and Controls
- When Graham is close to an interesting element, this is signified through one or multiple icons in the bottom right corner of the screen[^ref-3]
- The player can choose to examine, interact, or pick up objects[^ref-3]
- Collected items are stored in an inventory[^ref-3]
- In many locations it is possible to switch to a first-person perspective to examine the environment[^ref-3]

### Structure and Progression
- The game largely focuses on the story and puzzle-solving, but there is also a large amount of exploration and action sequences with escapes and fighting[^ref-3]
- Graham often needs to run and he can also push or manipulate objects in various ways[^ref-3]
- These sections are sometimes shown from a first-person perspective[^ref-3]
- There are many cut-scenes, on-rails sequences, and some Quick Time Events[^ref-3]

### Puzzles and Mechanics
- The game incorporates choices and consequences in various ways and they are carried across episodes[^ref-3]
- There are many branching dialogue options that lead to different outcomes[^ref-3]
- Puzzles often have multiple approaches without a fixed solution[^ref-3]
- Generally there are three types of choices to make or paths to follow: bravery, wisdom, or compassion, though these are never mentioned explicitly[^ref-3]
- This affects the story and especially the way Gwendolyn will act in the present as her grandfather's stories are the inspiration for her acts[^ref-3]
- These choice elements hearken back to the original King's Quest games, such as the multiple ways Graham could deal with the dragon in the first game[^ref-3]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews
Adventure Gamers awarded the game 4.5/5 (90/100), stating: "A revival of a classic adventure series, the episodic King's Quest is itself an instant classic, giving each new installment its own unique focus. Wise, brave, and compassionate adventurers alike should definitely consider having a crack at it"[^ref-6]. Common Sense Media found the framing "nothing short of charming", noting that when Graham dies, the storytelling "dismisses it as product of an old man's faulty memory"[^ref-15].

IGN's Leif Johnson awarded Chapter 1 an 8/10, praising the game as "full of humor, heart, and beauty" and calling it "an admirable revival of classic adventure games"[^ref-9]. He noted that "there's hardly an environment in the whole story that doesn't evoke some awe"[^ref-9]. ZTGD gave Chapter 1 a 9.5/10 "Excellent" rating, calling it "the surprise hit of 2015 so far" with "hilarious writing with a stellar VO cast"[^ref-10]. Not every critic agreed. Rock Paper Shotgun's John Walker panned Chapter 1, writing "I am struggling to remember a game with a worse first hour" and criticising its "astonishingly dreadful design decisions" and "monstrously slow pace"[^ref-12].

GameFAQs users gave the game a rating of "Great" based on 26 ratings, with an average completion time of 30 hours for the full series[^ref-4]. To honor the originals without copying their look, The Odd Gentlemen hand-painted the game's art, drawing on references from the 1980s games[^ref-19]. IGN wrote that "there's hardly an environment in the whole story that doesn't evoke some awe"[^ref-9], and Common Sense Media praised its "storybook-worthy graphics"[^ref-15].

Digitally Downloaded's review of the complete series praised the narrative structure, stating "Creative Director Matt Corba [sic] should be given some sort of award for his work with this game" and concluding that "King's Quest does everything right"[^ref-11]. The reviewer credited Maggie Elizabeth Jones's performance as Gwendolyn in Chapter Five's final scenes with bringing them to tears[^ref-11].

Common Sense Media praised the game as a "funny, surprising adventure with beautiful graphics," recommending it for ages 10+ and noting the "Princess Bride-style" storytelling approach where elderly King Graham relates tales to his granddaughter[^ref-15]. The review highlighted the "incredible vocal cast" and praised the game's "fun alternation among exploration, action, and puzzle solving"[^ref-15].

The Complete Collection earned recognition within the gaming industry, receiving a nomination for Adventure Game of the Year at the 20th Annual D.I.C.E. Awards from the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences in 2017[^ref-1][^ref-21]. Chapters 1 and 3 posted the highest Metacritic scores among the chapters Metacritic tracked on all three platforms[^ref-1].

**Chapter 5: The Good Knight** received generally positive reviews with Destructoid's Chris Carter giving it 8.5/10, while PlayStation Lifestyle praised its exploration of "deep subjects of mortality, life accomplishments, and what we leave behind" and awarded it 8/10[^ref-1].

## Development

### Origins
The 2015 King's Quest reboot originated in August 2014 when Activision announced the revival of the Sierra Entertainment label and selected independent studio The Odd Gentlemen to develop a new entry in the classic adventure series[^ref-1]. Its first trailer debuted at The Game Awards 2014 on December 5, 2014[^ref-20]. Creative Director Matt Korba led the project, envisioning a reimagining of King Graham's story structured around an elderly Graham recounting his adventures to granddaughter Gwendolyn[^ref-4].

### Production
The game was developed as a five-part episodic adventure series, serving as both a reboot and re-imagining of the classic King's Quest franchise[^ref-3]. The development team chose to include the framing device of the grandfather-granddaughter storytelling to provide greater emotional depth and explore themes of legacy, heroism, and mortality[^ref-3]. The script for the first chapter alone was 640 pages long, including branching paths, Easter eggs, narration, object use and interaction, and dialogue trees[^ref-1]. Each chapter of the series was longer and more complex than some similar episodic series such as those made by Telltale Games[^ref-1].

According to Creative Director Matt Korba, the game's controls focused on "one-button context," resulting in a game without complicated interfaces or controls[^ref-1][^ref-18]. The original games are considered part of the canon of the new series, with each chapter taking place between those games; however, previous games may be reinterpreted in completely new ways[^ref-1][^ref-18]. All of Graham's choices are heroic; as The Odd Gentlemen's Lindsey Rostal put it, "We didn't want it to be 'good Graham' or 'bad Graham.' Graham is always good."[^ref-18]

The voice cast features veteran actor Christopher Lloyd (Doc Brown from Back to the Future) as the elderly King Graham, with Josh Keaton (Shiro from Voltron) voicing young Graham[^ref-9][^ref-11][^ref-1]. Wallace Shawn (Rex from Toy Story, Vizzini from The Princess Bride) voices the antagonist Manannan, bringing what reviewers described as "a quick-witted but twisted feel"[^ref-11]. Maggie Elizabeth Jones delivers a standout performance as Gwendolyn, with her "enthusiasm and childlike innocence" praised by critics[^ref-11]. Additional voice talent includes Tom Kenny (SpongeBob SquarePants), Loretta Devine, Kevin Michael Richardson, and Zelda Williams (daughter of Robin Williams)[^ref-12][^ref-15][^ref-1].

### Technical Achievements
- Built on Unreal Engine 3[^ref-2], with hand-painted textures[^ref-19]
- Multi-platform release across Windows, PlayStation 3, PlayStation 4, Xbox 360, and Xbox One[^ref-1]
- Choice-consequence mechanics tracking player decisions across virtue categories (bravery, wisdom, compassion)[^ref-3]
- Branching dialogue system affecting narrative outcomes across all five episodes[^ref-3]
- First-person perspective mode for detailed environmental examination[^ref-3]
- Quick Time Events integrated into action sequences[^ref-3]
- Released: July 28, 2015 (Chapter 1), with subsequent chapters through October 2016[^ref-4]

## Legacy

King's Quest (2015) was the first new entry to actually ship since 1998's King's Quest: Mask of Eternity, after several reboot attempts that never passed the announcement or concept stage[^ref-1].

The game blends traditional adventure-game mechanics with choice-driven storytelling through its grandfather-granddaughter framing device[^ref-1]. Asked about King's Quest VIII: Mask of Eternity, creative director Matt Korba said, "King's Quest VIII exists… We're not doing a lot with Connor – we might have a cameo… but we're focused on the core family."[^ref-18]

The series remains available through digital distribution on Steam[^ref-17].
