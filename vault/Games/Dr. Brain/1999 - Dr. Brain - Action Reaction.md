# Dr. Brain Action Reaction

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

Dr. Brain: Action Reaction is a first-person 3D puzzle game for children, combining action and puzzle play[^ref-23]. Knowledge Adventure developed it and released it for Windows on August 19, 1999[^ref-11][^ref-32]. It is the third installment of the Dr. Brain series by Knowledge Adventure, based on the series by Sierra[^ref-7]. The game runs on Unreal Engine 1[^ref-7][^ref-12].

The player is captured along with Dr. Brain by S.P.O.R.E. (Sinister People Organized Really Efficiently) and taken to a secret underwater base, from which the object of the game is to escape[^ref-7]. The game has 45 levels[^ref-3][^ref-7]. It is also known as EnigManía and Enigme Mania[^ref-23], and in Germany, France and Spain it was published by Sierra On-Line as a "Big Fun Software" release[^ref-41].

## Story Summary

The player and Dr. Brain are captured by S.P.O.R.E. (Sinister People Organized Really Efficiently)[^ref-7]. As the product description puts it, "You're one of the world's most brilliant scientists and have been captured by Sinister People Organized Really Efficiently"[^ref-3]. The player is taken to S.P.O.R.E.'s underwater base, escapes a jail cell with Dr. Brain's help, and solves puzzles that cause havoc in the base until finally escaping[^ref-39].

Using the three laws of physics, switch flipping, rocket turrets, and the "helping hand" to knock out guards, the player and Dr. Brain must foil S.P.O.R.E.'s plan of world domination and capture the evil Dr. Craven[^ref-7]. The player must use quick footwork and fast thinking to avoid obstacles along the way[^ref-7].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Dr. Brain: Action Reaction is a first-person puzzle game[^ref-30] with a direct-control interface[^ref-23]. PCGamingWiki lists single-player and multiplayer modes and classes it as action, FPS, platform and puzzle[^ref-32]. Players move with the keyboard and mouse together, which Review Corner said "may take a little getting used to at the outset"[^ref-44]. StrategyWiki's walkthrough warns of "fiddly flight controls" in the ring-flying level[^ref-40].

The tutorial teaches basic movement, and StrategyWiki notes: "If you are in a hurry and know basic first person controls, feel free to climb the stack of boxes in the corner of the jail cell and skip to the end of the tutorial"[^ref-38]. At aspect ratios wider than 4:3 the engine crops the field of view, and a community fix exists for it[^ref-6].

### Structure and Progression

The game has 45 levels[^ref-7]. Players begin by escaping a jail cell, then face switch flipping, rocket turrets and the "helping hand"[^ref-7]. In the tutorial, players avoid rockets fired by a turret and pass a robot that "always turns to the right when it hits a wall"[^ref-38]. Each level is harder than the last, and Dr. Brain gives advice and instructions on every level[^ref-44]. PC Gaming World noted that "if you have trouble with a particular problem you can skip the level and move on"[^ref-42].

Later levels include gelevator mazes and a ring-flying section[^ref-40]. A Reddit user reported that, because the game runs on Unreal, the console command "open creditlevel" loads a developer level that "contains slightly disturbing imagery"[^ref-34].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Puzzles draw on the laws of physics, motion and geometry, and players fire ball cannons, fly through field simulators and manipulate electromagnetic fields[^ref-44]. The game includes attractors, which bend the path of certain balls or pull the player when the attractor vest is switched on[^ref-30]. Robots follow a fixed route, change direction when they hit a wall, and can be steered into useful positions by moving blocks into their path[^ref-23].

Level 10 introduces portal doors and a small portal maze[^ref-40]. A Reddit user posting about the game wrote that it "had FULLY FUNCTIONAL portals! They worked in exactly the same way as the ones in Portal - you can see out of them (with correct perspective), and they transfer momentum", and noted it came out six years before Narbacular Drop and eight years before Portal[^ref-34].

### Educational Content

Both SuperKids and Review Corner list it for ages 10 and up[^ref-43][^ref-44]. Review Corner listed the skills it covers as "physics, logic, critical thinking, problem solving, mechanics, geometry, spatial awareness, reflexes"[^ref-44]. SuperKids wrote that its puzzles "require kids to develop and use critical thinking and problem solving skills"[^ref-43]. The game does not display a score during play[^ref-23].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

MobyGames lists four critic reviews averaging 82%; it has no player score[^ref-42].

SuperKids gave it 5 out of 5, rating Educational Value 3.5, Kid Appeal 5.0 and Ease of Use 4.0. Its bottom line: "This 3D adventure requires coordination, quick thinking and patience. Along with Rome, it isn't done in a day. But it is, without a doubt, a fun ride!"[^ref-43]

Review Corner scored it 8.7 out of 10, calling it "at once challenging and fun"[^ref-44]. Its reviewers praised the graphics and said "Puzzle lovers will be motivated to work through the levels"[^ref-44].

PC Gaming World (UK, December 1999) gave it 4 out of 5: "Although a thinking game for kids, Dr Brain is fun too, and the first-person action gameplay will make them feel like they're playing a more adult game"[^ref-42]. The German magazine PC Player (January 2000) gave it 60%[^ref-42].

### Modern Assessment

GameFAQs users rate it "Great" (14 ratings)[^ref-11]. A Reddit post in r/Portal drew attention to its portal levels[^ref-34], and StrategyWiki's walkthrough opens Level 10 with: "Thought that Portal was the first first person puzzle game with portals? Think again"[^ref-40]. On modern Windows, the unofficial OldUnreal Multimedia Patch adds support for current Windows versions, plus bug fixes and extra video and audio renderers[^ref-32].

## Development

### Origins

Dr. Brain: Action Reaction is the third Dr. Brain game by Knowledge Adventure, based on the series Sierra created[^ref-7]. Marcus Smith and Stephen Park are credited as level designers; Smith is also credited as writer[^ref-45]. Albert H. M. Reinhardt produced it, Ari Schindler was executive producer, and Thomas M. Fiola was lead programmer[^ref-45].

### Production

Character voices came from Carol Bach-y-Rita, Grey DeLisle-Griffin, Michael Gough and Neil Ross[^ref-45]. Giorgio Bertuccelli and Michael Skloff wrote and produced the music, which Wikipedia describes as "a collage of soft rock, techno, and pop"[^ref-7]. Epic Games is credited for the engine[^ref-41], and the game uses Unreal Engine 1[^ref-7].

The game ships with Unreal's editor package but no editor executable; an OldUnreal moderator found that copying Unreal 224's UnrealEd.exe works[^ref-30]. Up to 16 players could play online for free through AdventureLink[^ref-44].

### Technical Details

Review Corner gives the minimum requirements as Windows 95/98, a Pentium 166, 32 MB of RAM, a 4X CD-ROM drive and 100 MB of disk space, with the game optimized for a Pentium III 500 MHz and 128 MB of RAM[^ref-44]. PCGamingWiki instead lists an Intel Pentium II 266 MHz as the minimum CPU[^ref-32]. The game was sold on CD-ROM[^ref-13].

## Legacy

It was not the last Dr. Brain release: see [[2011 - JumpStart Advanced 3rd-5th Grade - Adventures of Dr. Brain]]. An OldUnreal moderator recommended it to players "into games like Quantum Conundrum and Portal"[^ref-30].

JumpStart Games and Jordan Freeman Group re-released the game digitally on October 7, 2023[^ref-13][^ref-41].

## Downloads

**Download / Preservation**

The game is still sold: the 2023 digital re-release is available DRM-free on the ZOOM Platform[^ref-32].
