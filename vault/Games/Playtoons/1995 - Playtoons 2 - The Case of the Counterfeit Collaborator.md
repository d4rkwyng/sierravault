# Playtoons 2: The Case of the Counterfeit Collaborator

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 29, 2026</small>

## Overview

Playtoons 2: The Case of the Counterfeit Collaborator is an interactive storybook and creativity game developed by [[Coktel Vision]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] in 1995[^ref-1][^ref-22][^ref-18]. It was also released as "Playtoons Nr. 2: Rummelsdorf Hoch Zwei" in Germany and "Playtoons n°2: Micmac à Champignac" in France[^ref-2]. It is the second entry in the Playtoons series of children's creativity software[^ref-3].

The game pairs an interactive comic book with a construction kit in which children make their own animated stories[^ref-4]. Sierra Chest describes the story as effectively the same as the Spirou cartoon episode "Micmac à Champignac" (1993), using the same story, voice actors, art and animations, with interactive screens added[^ref-22]. That episode belongs to the first season (1993) of the *Spirou* animated series[^ref-23].

As with the rest of the series, if several Playtoons games are installed on the same system, the assets from all of them become available in the editor, "essentially combining themselves into one game"[^ref-4].

## Story Summary

Spirou gets a call in the middle of the night from his friend, the inventor Count of Champignac, who mentions an urgent mission and a grave threat before the line goes dead after a piercing yell. Spirou and his pet squirrel Spip pick up Fantasio, who has a bad head cold and suspects another prank, and drive through the night to Champignac[^ref-22].

At the castle, the Count explains that fifteen years earlier he and his colleagues swore to devote part of their work to relieving world hunger, and agreed to meet secretly at Champignac to reveal their results, which is today. The day before, Interpol warned him that an impostor, a spy for a large international food corporation, may slip in among the scientists. He has called on Spirou and Fantasio to unmask the impostor[^ref-22][^ref-1].

The Count places the plans of the inventions in a wall safe as his colleagues arrive. That night footsteps are heard in the attic, but no intruder is found. The next morning the safe is open and empty[^ref-22].

The child can read along and listen to narration[^ref-22], and each panel starts with narrated text[^ref-2]. Sierra Chest's series description says: "The child reads, listens and becomes drawn into a script that will make them tremble with excitement or burst out laughing"[^ref-22]. Across the series, players can play the story straight through or jump to any page, and some pages lead to unlisted pages[^ref-3].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

Playtoons 2 uses a point-and-select interface over fixed, flip-screen panels[^ref-1]. Sierra Chest compares the series to "a traditional comic strip or cartoon for children", where the player can set off animations from each screen[^ref-22].

### Structure and Progression

The game has two parts[^ref-22]:

- **Interactive story:** the Spirou and Fantasio story adapted from the cartoon, with added interactive screens
- **Construction game:** a kit, shared across the series, for building your own animated stories

### Puzzles and Mechanics

Players can create up to 20 scenes, choosing their sceneries, objects and characters and having the characters interact with the objects[^ref-22][^ref-11]. A built-in camera function lets players make their own animated film with voice-overs[^ref-22].

Wikipedia describes the series as focused on teaching children school topics in a digital format meant to be more enthralling for kids[^ref-3].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

MobyGames records one critic review. The German magazine *Fun Online* (January 1996) gave the Windows version a 2 on a numeric scale where the lowest score is best, which MobyGames normalises to 83%[^ref-24]. The review notes that every character, room and object from the story is available in the comic construction kit[^ref-24].

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 83%, based on one review (*Fun Online*)[^ref-24]

## Development

### Origins

Coktel Vision was a French developer that Sierra On-Line acquired on October 29, 1993,[^ref-27] after which Sierra published Coktel's games in the United States[^ref-20]. After Sierra was bought by Havas Interactive, Coktel moved to developing only educational games[^ref-12][^ref-20].

The game is licensed from the Spirou animated series. Its credits read "Based on the original animated film SPIROU © Dupuis - TF1 - Ciné Groupe, 1993", and Dupuis's Léon Perahia (audiovisual direction and development) and Patrick Pinchart (multimedia development) are credited alongside Coktel's team[^ref-8].

### Production

The game runs on Coktel Vision's Gob engine[^ref-5].

**Development Credits (Windows 16-bit version):**[^ref-8]
- **Project Lead:** Valérie Courtial
- **Art Director:** Jeff Rey
- **Interactive Script:** SIERRA Edutainment
- **Graphics:** Jean-Christophe Charter, Jacques Dimier, Joseph Kluytmans, Kaki Chapoullié, Pascal Pautrot
- **Music & Sound:** Studio Big wheels, Charles Callet
- **Programming Lead:** Arnaud Delrue
- **Voices (German version):** Brigitta Scholtz, Peter Semmler

### Technical Achievements

Assets are shared across titles: installing more Playtoons games (and Creator Kit add-ons) enlarges the range of sceneries, objects and characters available in the construction game[^ref-4][^ref-22].

Sierra Chest notes that Playtoons 3: The Secret of the Castle was the first Playtoons title to use construction kit backgrounds, items, characters and animations in its story panels[^ref-11].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Sierra On-Line published the game in the United States (1996). Coktel Vision published it in France, and in Germany it was released by Sierra Coktel Deutschland GmbH[^ref-13]. No sales figures have been found.

### Collections

MobyGames' releases page lists only standalone releases, with no compilation[^ref-13].

### Fan Projects

The Internet Archive hosts a disc image of the French release[^ref-7]. ScummVM can detect the game but does not list it as officially supported (see Technical Issues)[^ref-25][^ref-26].

### Related Publications

- **Creator Kit add-ons:** Sierra Chest says the construction game grows with the "Creator Kit add-ons" you install[^ref-22]. The "Monsters" construction kit add-on is tied to Playtoons 1: Uncle Archibald, not this game[^ref-11]
- **Animated Film Tools:** A built-in camera function allows creation of animated films with voice-overs[^ref-22]

### Critical Perspective

The one contemporary review on record, from *Fun Online*, focused on the construction kit, noting that players get the full fun of making their own comics alongside the story[^ref-24].
