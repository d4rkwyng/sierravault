# IndyCar Racing

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 13, 2026</small>

## Overview

IndyCar Racing is a racing simulation video game developed by [[Papyrus Design Group]] and released in 1993 for MS-DOS.[^ref-1] It was Papyrus's follow-up to [[1989 - Indianapolis 500 - The Simulation|Indianapolis 500: The Simulation]] (1989).[^ref-1] Reviewing it in February 1994, *Computer Gaming World* called it "the only current driving sim where 180 mph feels like 180 mph,"[^ref-24] and the magazine's June 1994 awards issue named it the Premier Simulation of the Year, calling it "the motor sports aficionado's dream come true."[^ref-25] The game was intended as a realistic simulation of CART IndyCar Racing, featuring contemporary drivers, chassis, and engines from the 1993 racing season.[^ref-3]

With an official license from CART, IndyCar Racing offered eight real track names and designs with authentic sponsors and logos, putting players behind the wheel against real IndyCar drivers on circuits including Michigan International, Nazareth PA, Laguna Seca, Portland International, Milwaukee, Loudon NH, Toronto, and Long Beach.[^ref-1][^ref-4] The game arrived "hot on the heels of Geoff Crammond's excellent Formula One Grand Prix," establishing Papyrus as one of the leading developers in sim racing—a legacy that continues to this day.[^ref-5][^ref-6]

The game paired a detailed physics model with real-time, perspective-correct texture mapping.[^ref-7] Recalling why he began making racing simulations in the first place, creator David Kaemmer told GameSpot, "I wanted to make a program that would show someone, as much as possible, what it was like to drive a racecar."[^ref-7]

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

IndyCar Racing simulates realistic car handling, with breakaway parts when cars make contact.[^ref-1] The game supports various input devices including keyboard and racing/steering wheel peripherals.[^ref-1] Players can choose from multiple TV-type replay views to review their races.[^ref-10] The game's texture-mapped 3D graphics run best on the fastest PCs of the era, with Papyrus recommending local bus video or PCI video for optimal speed.[^ref-11]

### Structure and Progression

Players can engage in single races or a full championship season mode, experiencing the complete IndyCar racing experience.[^ref-3] The game features a comprehensive garage and setup routine that allows detailed car customization, simulating the real decisions that professional racing teams must make.[^ref-10]

- **Single Race:** Quick race on any available track
- **Championship Season:** Full season competition against AI opponents
- **Practice Mode:** Free practice sessions for learning tracks and setups
- **Multiplayer:** Two-player racing via modem or null-modem connection[^ref-2]

### Cars and Customization

IndyCar Racing features a pure open-wheel simulation based on American races, with an extensive car setup system that mirrors real-world racing engineering.[^ref-10] Players can adjust numerous aspects of their car's performance, including tire pressure, wing angles, and other mechanical settings to optimize performance for each unique circuit.

### Tracks

The base game included eight circuits from the IndyCar circuit:[^ref-4]

1. **Michigan International Speedway**
2. **Nazareth Speedway (PA)**
3. **Laguna Seca Raceway**
4. **Portland International Raceway**
5. **Milwaukee Mile**
6. **New Hampshire Motor Speedway (Loudon NH)**
7. **Exhibition Place (Toronto)**
8. **Long Beach Grand Prix**

The IndyCar Circuits expansion pack later added seven more tracks, for 15 in all. The Indianapolis Motor Speedway, missing from the initial release, came in a separate add-on once the licensing was secured, which brought the total to 16.[^ref-1][^ref-7]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

IndyCar Racing was well received on release. In *Computer Gaming World*'s February 1994 review, Gordon Goble called it "the only current driving sim where 180 mph feels like 180 mph" and concluded that it "is the purest driving game ever let loose on the gaming public."[^ref-24] The magazine's June 1994 Premier Awards named it Simulation of the Year, calling it "the motor sports aficionado's dream come true."[^ref-25] *Electronic Entertainment* made it a finalist for its 1993 Best Game award and wrote that it "is so realistic, A.J. Foyt could use it to practice."[^ref-2]

- **Hyper Magazine:** 92% (February 1994)[^ref-2]
- **Electronic Entertainment:** 9/10 (March 1994)[^ref-2]
- **Computer Gaming World:** Premier Simulation of the Year (June 1994)[^ref-25]
- **PC Gamer US:** #20 in "The Best Games of All Time" (August 1994)[^ref-2]
- **PC Gamer UK:** #9 in "Top 50 PC Games of All Time" (April 1994)[^ref-2]

The game also won Best Sports Program at the 1994 Codie Awards, presented by the Software Publishers Association (SPA).[^ref-12]

### Modern Assessment

Modern players still praise its physics model. One GOG.com user wrote: "Unbelievable physics model - even by today's standards. Car damage is very accurately portrayed and you have to be a very skilled driver to navigate a lap, let alone a whole race."[^ref-13] The Cutting Room Floor describes it as the title that "cemented Papyrus' status as one of the leading developers in simracing."[^ref-6]

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** Moby Score 8.1; critic average 87% from 19 ratings (as of October 2026)[^ref-1][^ref-14]
- **MobyGames Player Rating:** 3.8/5 from 23 ratings (as of October 2026)[^ref-14]

## Development

### Origins

IndyCar Racing was Papyrus's second racing title, developed four years after the release of Indianapolis 500: The Simulation in 1989.[^ref-10] David Kaemmer and Omar Khudari formed Papyrus as a company that, in GameSpot's words, "operated primarily as a contract programming services company," while Kaemmer's own goal was "to build the first truly authentic PC racing simulation."[^ref-7] In a July 1993 *Computer Gaming World* interview (#108), conducted before IndyCar Racing shipped, Kaemmer said they "did form Papyrus with the intention of developing games, although Indy wasn't in our plans at that point."[^ref-15]

The original Indianapolis 500 was made on a budget of about $90,000, and Kaemmer said Amiga sales were what "helped get us to the break-even point."[^ref-7] In the 1993 interview he credited Indy 500's realism to "a very complex and accurate physics model" that "includes hundreds of variables that influence the car's handling," and said of the new game that "the physics model is actually superior to Indy's."[^ref-15] Between the two games, Papyrus did contract programming and computer consulting work while it assembled the money and staff for IndyCar Racing.[^ref-7]

### Production

Development was led by David Kaemmer. The team secured an official license, ensuring authentic track names, designs, sponsors, and logos.[^ref-1] However, not all drivers could be included—notable absences included Nigel Mansell and Al Unser Jr., while Mario Andretti and Danny Sullivan appeared in generic liveries.[^ref-2][^ref-5]

**Development Credits:**[^ref-26]
- **Original Concept / Lead Programming:** David Kaemmer
- **Programming:** Eugene Lin, John Wheeler
- **Graphics / Artwork:** Adam Levesque, Doug McCartney, Warren Sze
- **Music:** Joe McDermott, George Alistair Sanger
- **Sound:** Rob Wallace
- **Producer:** Dan Scherlis
- **Voice:** Paul Page

Adam Levesque, who later co-founded Blue Fang Games, designed and produced all of the game's 3D models. He later said he "didn't have any strong background in racing" when he joined Papyrus.[^ref-17]

### Technical Achievements

IndyCar Racing rendered real-time, perspective-correct texture-mapped 3D graphics at 320×200, a resolution GameSpot later called "blocky" even for the time.[^ref-7] Retro blogger Lilura1 has argued that it was "practically matching coinop racers from Sega and Namco."[^ref-8]

Kaemmer recalled that the team had not planned to texture the road, for frame-rate reasons, but added it after seeing a demo of Geoff Crammond's World Circuit (1992).[^ref-1][^ref-7] The game also featured a replay system capable of storing around one hour of footage.[^ref-2]

### Technical Issues

The game manual documented several known issues:[^ref-11]

- Memory conflicts with resident software could cause problems
- Game crashes when hitting walls or during Paul Page speech with certain sound card issues
- Cannot use IRQ above 7
- Gravis UltraSound not directly supported (must use SBOS compatibility mode)
- Insufficient Memory error appears on some tracks
- Frame rate issues on slower systems
- No sound after booting with startup disk

### Easter Eggs and Trivia

- **Missing Drivers:** The game notably lacked Nigel Mansell, while Mario Andretti and Danny Sullivan appeared with generic liveries rather than their real-world sponsors.[^ref-2][^ref-5]
- **Mario Andretti's Livery:** In earlier versions/patches, Mario Andretti had a different, orange livery that was later changed.[^ref-6]
- **Paul Page Announcement:** Real-life IndyCar announcer Paul Page provided voice work for the game, introducing it with: "I am Paul Page. From Papyrus, this is IndyCar Racing."[^ref-6][^ref-26]
- **Version 1.05 Changes:** Changed fictional driver names to make them less obvious representations of real drivers not officially licensed.[^ref-6]

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

IndyCar Racing shipped over 400,000 units to retailers and was a top-five seller for 1994.[^ref-2][^ref-17] GameSpot put worldwide sales at roughly 300,000 units, compared with 200,000 for the original Indianapolis 500, and called it "a definite step up from 1989's maiden effort and a real feather in the cap," though not "the earth-shattering total for which Papyrus had wishfully longed."[^ref-7]

Sierra On-Line acquired Papyrus in late 1995.[^ref-20] GameSpot's retrospective noted that "to say that the last decade and a half of computer driving would have suffered had Papyrus not been at the wheel is a serious understatement."[^ref-21]

### Awards

- **SPA Codie Awards 1994:** Best Sports Program[^ref-2][^ref-12]
- **Computer Gaming World 1994:** Premier Simulation of the Year[^ref-2][^ref-25]
- **Electronic Entertainment 1994:** Editors' Choice Awards – Best Game (finalist / Honorable Mention)[^ref-1][^ref-2]
- **PC Gamer US 1994:** #20 best computer game ever[^ref-2]
- **PC Gamer UK 1994:** #9 best computer game of all time[^ref-2]

### Expansion Packs

Two expansion packs were released for IndyCar Racing:[^ref-22]

- **Indianapolis Motor Speedway Expansion Pack** (1994) – Added the iconic Indianapolis oval track[^ref-1]
- **IndyCar Circuits** (1994) – Additional tracks from the CART circuit[^ref-1]

IndyCar Circuits took the track count from 8 to 15, and the Indianapolis pack added the Speedway that was absent from the original release, for 16 in all.[^ref-1][^ref-5]

### Platform Availability

The game has been preserved and is available through various abandonware and archive sites. The Internet Archive hosts installation packages that have been tested to run using DOSBox on modern Windows operating systems including Windows 11.[^ref-22]

### Critical Perspective

Kaemmer later told GameSpot that IndyCar Racing "was the natural follow-on to Indy 500" and that it "sold pretty well, even overseas," but that its successor NASCAR Racing "sold several times as many units."[^ref-7]

Papyrus went on to create NASCAR Racing (1994), which sold over 1,000,000 copies, and eventually Grand Prix Legends (1998), which "is still considered to be one of the finest racing games of all time."[^ref-21] After Vivendi Universal shut the studio down in May 2004, David Kaemmer recovered the rights to Papyrus's racing simulation technology.[^ref-21] With John W. Henry he formed a company that acquired the NASCAR Racing 2003 Season code, which Kaemmer developed into iRacing, released in 2008.[^ref-23]
