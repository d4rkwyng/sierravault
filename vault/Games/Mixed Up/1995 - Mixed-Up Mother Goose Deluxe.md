# Mixed-Up Mother Goose Deluxe

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Mixed-Up Mother Goose Deluxe is a 1995 remake of Roberta Williams' children's adventure Mixed-Up Mother Goose, developed and published by Sierra On-Line for Windows and Macintosh[^ref-1]. MobyGames describes it as "another updated version" of the game with "a new set of graphics" and "a redone soundtrack that has all of the nursery rhymes played in different musical styles"[^ref-1]. Sierra pitched it at children aged 3 to 6, teaching "logic, organization and memory skills"[^ref-4]. Sierra's own *InterAction* magazine listed it as "Ages 3 to 6" at $39.95 for Windows and Macintosh CD-ROM[^ref-27], and a 1996 MacAddict order-card listing also gives ages 3-6[^ref-30].

Sierra Chest counts it as the fourth installment of the game and describes it as "updated for multimedia computers" with high-resolution graphics and digitized music and voice[^ref-4]. The version before it was the 1991 VGA/Multimedia CD remake[^ref-12]. The box also included a music CD of the game's songs, which Sierra's advertising called "a free bonus audio CD of the 18 songs in the game"[^ref-4][^ref-25].

## Story Summary

The player's chosen character starts out tucked up in bed, and after a short dream sequence arrives outside the house of Mother Goose, who laments that her rhymes are mixed up because the nursery rhyme characters have lost the items they need[^ref-11]. "Little Bo Peep has lost her sheep; the cat's lost his fiddle; there's lots of trouble in Mother Goose Land"[^ref-8]. The child reunites 18 animated characters with their lost items, and each character then performs its song with animation and text so the child can sing along[^ref-4][^ref-7].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The child picks a boy or girl character and enters a name, then starts in a bedroom filled with objects to click on; the adventure begins when the child clicks the book[^ref-1]. Movement is point-and-click, and the game saves automatically on quitting[^ref-11]. There is no on-screen text for the spoken dialogue, but when a character asks for an item, a picture of it is shown[^ref-11].

### Structure and Progression

There are 18 mini-quests, such as finding Jack and Jill's pail or Bo Peep's sheep[^ref-11]. Items are picked up when the character walks near them, and there is only one inventory slot: picking up a new item drops the one already held on that screen[^ref-11]. An egg carton at the bottom left of the screen fills with golden eggs as quests are completed[^ref-11]. A world map can be used for navigation and to hear completed rhymes again, and Mother Goose can tell the player where each object is[^ref-1]. Sierra's *InterAction* preview says the player selects one of 12 player characters, who are "hipper" than in the original and "physically and culturally diverse"[^ref-27].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

The items, and at least one of the nursery rhyme characters, are placed randomly, so they are not in the same place each time a game is started[^ref-11]. Metzomagic's reviewer noted that children need to remember where the characters and objects are, and where they have dropped items[^ref-11].

## Development

### Origins

Sierra had remade Mixed-Up Mother Goose twice before Deluxe: an SCI version in 1990, and a VGA/Multimedia CD version in 1991 in which the rhymes were given voices and sung[^ref-12]. MobyGames describes that 1991 version as featuring "VGA graphics, complete digitized speech along with improved animations"[^ref-26]. Wikipedia calls Deluxe the final remake, with "further improved SVGA graphics and a bonus audio CD, as well as the introduction of different melodies and styles of music"[^ref-12]. A MobyGames user review compares Deluxe with the earlier "CD/Disk version" and calls it "similar to the last version released, with most of the assets from that game reused here"[^ref-1].

### Production

MobyGames credits 37 people (32 professional roles and 5 thanks)[^ref-16]. Roberta Williams is credited as designer, Wendy Albee as producer, Marcia Bales as lead artist, and Ellen Ratajak, Robert W. Lindsley and Oliver Brelsford as lead programmers[^ref-16]. Guy Whitmore composed the music[^ref-16][^ref-7]; *InterAction* credits him with 18 all-new songs, each in a different musical style, and says an audio CD of the new songs, with an instrumental-only version of each and printed lyrics, was included[^ref-27]. Rhonda Matson is credited as Spanish expert[^ref-16].

The bonus soundtrack CD, composed by Guy Whitmore, is catalogued on VGMdb with a release date of June 1, 1995, as a Sierra On-Line enclosure marked "Not for Sale (U.S.)" and "Comes with the game"[^ref-31]. The Sierra Help Pages describe it as the extra audio CD that came with the game, with music that can be played separately and instrumental versions that a child can sing along with (karaoke)[^ref-32]. Both sources list the 18 songs followed by instrumental versions of 18 of them[^ref-31][^ref-32].

The nursery rhymes are set in many popular music styles, "from Reggae to Blues to Country and Western," according to Metzomagic[^ref-11]. Sierra's own advertisement told readers: "Imagine 'Little Jack Horner' done in reggae"[^ref-25].

Most of the game's resources stay on the CD. The Windows executable must be launched with the path to its RESOURCE.CFG file, or it fails with a "Title not found" error[^ref-18]. In user comments on MyAbandonware, one player reports running the Windows 3.x version under DOSBox, which boots the Windows 3.1 included in the download, and another used winevdm but found the game stuck at 640x480; a third reports that the Mac download would not open on OS X 10.10.5, with a message that it "may be damaged or incomplete"[^ref-3].

## Legacy

Sierra's own *InterAction* magazine (Spring 1995) said the original Mixed-Up Mother Goose had sold over 500,000 copies and "won numerous awards", naming the Software Publishers Association's Best Early Education Program and the "Parents' Choice Award: Best Classic Program"; these are Sierra's claims about the original game, not an independent record[^ref-27]. The Amazon product listing for Deluxe says it is the "Winner of the Parent's Choice Classic Software Award" and lists the "Children's Software Award for Preschoolers - Parenting Magazine"[^ref-19]. Sierra's December 1995 advertisement for Deluxe in *PC Gamer* did not name individual awards, saying only that the list of awards Mixed-Up Mother Goose had won "could fill up the rest of this page", and claimed the game had "introduced more than 500,000 little ones to the computer"[^ref-25].

According to the archive.org uploader, Coktel Vision released the game in Europe with English, Latin American Spanish, French and German options[^ref-9]. The archive.org uploader's description says the French and German options are "fully dubbed, even the songs"[^ref-9], and the ISO's file list shows RESOURCE.AUD files in the disc root and in its French, German and Spanish folders[^ref-9]. The localized titles were *Ballades au pays de Mère l'Oie* (French), *Gänseliesel Musikalisches Suchspiel* (German) and *Baladas en el País de la Madre Ganso* (Spanish)[^ref-1]. The game was also included in *King's Quest: Collection Series* (1997)[^ref-1], which Sierra's Fall 1997 *InterAction* described as all seven King's Quest adventures plus three more Roberta Williams games, Deluxe among them (#73120, DOS/Win CD, $39.95)[^ref-28].

ScummVM supports the Windows version (game ID `sci:mothergoosehires`) at "Excellent" level; the Macintosh version is listed as unsupported[^ref-22].
