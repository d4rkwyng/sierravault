# Mixed-Up Mother Goose

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

Mixed-Up Mother Goose (SCI Remake) is the first of three remakes Sierra On-Line made of Roberta Williams' 1987 children's adventure *Mixed-Up Mother Goose*[^ref-7]. Wikipedia calls it "Mixed-Up Mother Goose Enhanced" and says it "was remade using the SCI0 system, which meant improved graphics"[^ref-7]. MobyGames describes it as an updated version that "utilizes Sierra's SCI game interpreter and features enhanced graphics and sound," while "the gameplay remains the same as the previous version"[^ref-19].

Sierra announced the remake in its Summer 1990 *Sierra News Magazine* as one of two classics "rewritten for SCI", with "new, improved higher resolution graphics and better animation"[^ref-20]. The Fall 1990 issue told readers to look for "Mother Goose (SCI) in 16-color (EGA) versions, with major sound card support, in the fall of 1990"[^ref-21], and *Computer Gaming World* logged "Mixed-Up Mother Goose 'Enhanced Version' (Sierra) IBM" among conversions received in its November 1990 issue[^ref-23]. MobyGames dates the DOS and Amiga releases to 1991[^ref-19].

It is a different game from both the 1987 AGI original and the CD-ROM version. Sierra's Fall 1990 magazine described the CD as "an even more modern version" with "rich 256-color graphics" and "actual digitized actors' voices"[^ref-21], and *Computer Gaming World* reported that the CD came "with both an English and Japanese voicetrack on the same CD"[^ref-31]. MobyGames keeps the VGA/CD game as a separate entry[^ref-17]; ScummVM's engine data also separates the SCI0 "mothergoose" game from the VGA "mothergoose256"[^ref-29].

## Story Summary

The premise is unchanged from the original: "all of the nursery rhymes in Mother Goose Land have become mixed up!"[^ref-19] The player finds the missing items for each nursery-rhyme character. MobyGames gives two examples: "Little Bo Peep is looking for her sheep and Jack Be Nimble lost his candle stick"[^ref-19]. When an item is returned, "the complete nursery rhyme will play with the lyrics appearing on screen"[^ref-19].

## Gameplay

The manual pitches the game at "children ages 4 and up", with "over 44 delightful screens". "Eighteen of Mother Goose's most beloved rhymes" are included, and the child must find "the 20 missing characters and objects"[^ref-26]. The player types in the child's name, then chooses "the on-screen kid who looks like your child"[^ref-26]. *Compute!*'s review gives the same count: Mary's lamb, Jack and Jill's pail "and 16 other rhymes"[^ref-25]. (Sierra's Fall 1991 *InterAction* catalog says "sixteen mixed-up rhymes"[^ref-27]; the manual and *Compute!* both give eighteen.)

Old King Cole's rhyme "is missing three different items which must be returned to him in the correct order"[^ref-26]. The CD version cut this, and its manual counts "18 missing characters and objects"[^ref-30][^ref-7].

"The Inventory Window can hold only one object at a time"; picking up a new object drops the old one[^ref-26]. MobyGames adds that "each time the game is played objects will be in a new location"[^ref-19][^ref-5]. The kid moves by mouse, joystick or the keyboard, and Sierra packed a map and a poster in the box[^ref-26].

Adventure Gamers' database entry calls it "a children's adventure game so simple that once you start the game, even a young child can take over"[^ref-3].

## Reception

| Source | Score | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| *Compute!* (June 1991) | No score | Positive review by Eddie Huffman[^ref-25] |
| MobyGames players | 4.2/5 | 7 ratings, 2 reviews[^ref-19] |

A 2014 MobyGames player review says: "This remake was created with the company's newer SCI0 engine and it is impressive." It concludes: "It has the same game mechanics, but improves on their quality"[^ref-19].

*Compute!* reviewed this version in June 1991. The review's spec box lists an IBM PC with 512K RAM, support for the "Roland MT-32, Ad Lib, Game Blaster, Sound Blaster", and a $39.95 price, which matches the SCI release[^ref-25][^ref-20]. Eddie Huffman wrote that "combining inspired graphics and ease of play unlikely to frustrate even a four-year-old, it draws a child in". He concluded: "For educational value in a pleasing package, this is one wild goose chase worth completing"[^ref-25]. A mail-order ad in *Computer Gaming World* (April 1991) listed it as "Mother Goose Enhanced"[^ref-24]. The Compute! quote on Wikipedia belongs to the original release; see [[1987 - Mixed-Up Mother Goose]].

## Development

Sierra CEO Ken Williams wanted the old favorites brought forward for a new generation. "As classic as these games were," he said, "we felt there was room for improvement." Sierra's artists "redesigned and redrew every background graphic", every animation loop was redone, and composers added new stereo soundtracks[^ref-21]. Sierra's Summer 1990 announcement listed optional music support for "Adlib, Roland MT-32 and LAPC-1", GameBlaster and SoundBlaster cards, and Tandy three-voice sound. It required 512K, recommended an 8MHz CPU, and warned that "CGA support is in black and white only." The suggested retail price was $39.95[^ref-20]. Owners of the original could upgrade for $15 by sending disk #1 or the front cover of the documentation[^ref-20][^ref-21]. Runners-up in Sierra's "Draw Mother Goose Contest" each won "a new SCI version of Mixed-Up Mother Goose"[^ref-21].

MobyGames credits this version (DOS) as follows[^ref-19]:
- Game designer: Roberta Williams
- Executive producer: Ken Williams
- Producer: Josh Mandel
- Lead programmer: Juan Carlos Escobar
- Art designer: Gerald Moore
- Composer: James William Elliot
- "Mother Goose" theme: Amenda Lombardo
- Music arrangements and sound effects: Ken Allen
- Documentation: Marti McKenna

The manual's own credits use the short forms Jerry Moore and Carlos Escobar, spell the composer "James William Eliot", and add additional artist Cheryl Loyd and programmers Scott Murphy, Chris Smith and Chris Hoyt[^ref-26].

### Atari ST upgrade

*Sierra News Magazine* (Spring 1991) announced two titles from the "Classic's Reillustrated" collection for the Atari ST: King's Quest I and Mixed-Up Mother Goose. ST owners could upgrade by sending "$15 for Mixed-Up Mother Goose" with disk #1 or the front cover of the documentation[^ref-22]. The SCI manual includes Atari ST and Macintosh install steps as well as MS-DOS and Amiga[^ref-26], but no ST or Macintosh release of this version is confirmed here. Wikipedia adds that "a retail boxed version was not sold in stores"[^ref-7]; no period source confirms that.

### Platforms

MobyGames also lists Apple II and Apple IIgs releases[^ref-19], but no period source supports an Apple version of this SCI game. In 1989 Ken Williams said "the SCI development language... is just too big and too complex for the relatively slow CPU of the Apple IIGS machine", and Sierra stopped SCI development for that computer[^ref-28]. Neither the manual nor ScummVM's data lists an Apple version[^ref-26][^ref-29]. Sierra's Fall 1991 *InterAction* listed the SCI version beside the separate Multimedia CD: "Recently redesigned and redrawn, Mixed-Up Mother Goose (SCI) features better, more enjoyable art and a lively, fun soundtrack"[^ref-27].

## Legacy

This version was followed by the 1991 VGA/CD remake and by *Mixed-Up Mother Goose Deluxe* (1995)[^ref-7]. The *Roberta Williams Anthology* includes the 1987 original and the 1991 multimedia CD remake[^ref-7].
