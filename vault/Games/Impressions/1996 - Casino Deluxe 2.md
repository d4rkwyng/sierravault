# Casino Deluxe 2

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 23, 2026</small>

## Overview

Casino Deluxe 2 is a gambling simulation game for Windows, developed by [[Impressions Games]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] in autumn 1996[^ref-1][^ref-2]. Sierra previewed it as "the upcoming Casino Deluxe II" in the Summer 1996 issue of its InterAction magazine[^ref-20], mail-order retailers were listing it at $25 by the November 1996 issue of Computer Games Strategy Plus[^ref-24], and Sierra's website offered the Windows CD at $29.95 as "available now" in December 1996[^ref-18]. Sierra Chest dates its release to October 22, 1996[^ref-1]. Its box copy invites players to face the "intensity, allure and passion of authentic casino gambling," under the tagline "Beat the odds...to a pulp!"[^ref-2]. The sequel to [[1995 - Casino De Luxe|Casino De Luxe]] (1995), it advertises 3-D SVGA graphics and eighteen casino games[^ref-2][^ref-19].

The game lets players gamble without risking real money[^ref-4]. Its headline feature is on-screen professional advice, described on the box as "like having a casino expert right by your side"[^ref-2]. A Gambling Academy offers lessons on the games and their histories[^ref-4].

Casino Deluxe 2 ran on Windows 3.1 and Windows 95[^ref-5]. Sierra's catalogue said the CD "Includes a Blackjack Internet version for head-to-head play and a free laptop version"[^ref-21]. Sierra listed it in The Game Catalogue 1996-1997[^ref-6].

## Story Summary

Casino Deluxe 2 has no narrative; it is a gambling simulation set in a 3D casino environment[^ref-4]. Players enter a lobby and take an elevator to the floor of the game they want to play, each floor accompanied by casino ambience such as the murmur of gamblers and the sound of slot machines[^ref-9].

The dealers talk to the player, and advice can be requested during play[^ref-4]. Tips and strategies appear as on-screen text messages, which the product description says can be used "anytime you play games of chance - even if they're for real"[^ref-4].

The Gambling Academy offers audio and visual instruction on how to play games such as red dog poker and craps, along with the history behind them[^ref-9].

## Gameplay

### Interface and Controls

The game is controlled with mouse and keyboard[^ref-15], and the box promises a "Player-Friendly Interface - so even beginners can start winning right away"[^ref-2]. MobyGames classifies its perspectives as first-person and top-down[^ref-2]. Players move between games via an elevator that plays muzak, with different games on different floors[^ref-9].

Advice is available any time, "even during play," and the box calls it a "secret weapon"[^ref-2]. It appears as on-screen text messages when the player asks for help[^ref-4].

### Structure and Progression

The game offers eighteen casino games[^ref-2]. Sierra's 1996 product page lists them as Pai Gow Poker, Red Dog Poker, 5 Card Stud, 5 Card Draw, "7 Card Draw", Craps, Roulette, Blackjack, six slot machines and four video poker machines[^ref-18]; a reseller's listing names the individual machines[^ref-11]:

- **Slot Machines:** Six themed machines: Lucky Sevens, Home Run, Space Race, Wild West, Famous Faces, and Cornucopia[^ref-11]
- **Video Poker:** Four variants: 10's or Better, Deuces Wild, Jacks or Better, and Joker Poker[^ref-11]
- **Table Poker:** 5 Card Draw, 5 Card Stud, and 7 Card Stud[^ref-11] (Sierra's page calls the last "7 Card Draw"[^ref-18])
- **Table Games:** Blackjack, Craps, Pai Gow Poker, Red Dog[^ref-11], and Roulette[^ref-18]

Players can move between games and floors freely; there is no linear progression[^ref-9].

### Puzzles and Mechanics

As a gambling simulation, Casino Deluxe 2 has no traditional puzzles[^ref-4]. The Gambling Academy teaches the rules of each game, with tips and game histories[^ref-9]. Sierra's 1.01 patch notes show the game tracks the player's cash and chips across saved games, and that version 1.0 let players split, double down or raise without sufficient funds[^ref-13].

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews

Casino Deluxe 2 received little press coverage. MobyGames lists two critic reviews: All Game Guide, scored at 70%, and an unscored review from GamePower[^ref-17]. The text of those reviews could not be checked for this page.

In Italy, PC Game Parade's January 1997 news column described it as a gambling simulator with six slot machines and four video poker machines, adding that "you have to like the genre" and that it should already be in shops[^ref-25].

Reviews of the first game are covered on [[1995 - Casino De Luxe]].

### Modern Assessment

Old-Games.com's write-up calls it "a nice little game for players who enjoy gambling but don't want to use real money" and a "useful tool if you want to hit some real casinos," concluding: "Overall, this game is a must for anyone who wants a casino on their computer."[^ref-9]

**Aggregate Scores:**
- **MobyGames:** 70% critic average (1 rating)[^ref-17]
- **Metacritic:** No critic or user reviews available[^ref-14]

## Development

### Origins

Casino Deluxe 2 was developed by [[Impressions Games]] and published by [[Sierra On-Line]][^ref-1][^ref-2]. It follows [[1995 - Casino De Luxe|Casino De Luxe]] in the Casino Deluxe series[^ref-1][^ref-2]. No individual designer is credited in the sources consulted.

Sierra's Summer 1996 preview promised "eight gaming categories with 18 unique games" and tutorials covering "everything from doubling down and splitting pairs to roulette wagering systems, and even the detailed ins-and-outs of draw poker"[^ref-20].

### Production

The box lists 3-D SVGA graphics, "movie quality music" and dealer voices as features[^ref-2][^ref-19]. The CD-ROM included a free laptop version[^ref-5][^ref-19]. Its internet play was limited to Blackjack: Sierra's catalogue described "a Blackjack Internet version for head-to-head play"[^ref-21]. A modern reseller's compatibility listing notes that internet play is not supported[^ref-11].

### Technical Specifications

**CD-ROM Version:**[^ref-15]
- **Colors:** 256 colors[^ref-9]
- **Media:** CD-ROM[^ref-8]
- **Hardware Requirements:** VESA compatible display, Mouse[^ref-15]
- **CD Image Size:** About 159 MB (159,330,304 bytes)[^ref-8][^ref-12]
- **Control:** Mouse, Keyboard[^ref-15]

### Technical Issues

The initial release (version 1.0) contained several bugs that were addressed in patch 1.01[^ref-13]:

- Dealer incorrectly saying "Coming Out" in Craps when not a coming out roll[^ref-13]
- Incorrect dealer calls in Craps[^ref-13]
- Blackjack allowing Split/Double Down without sufficient funds[^ref-13]
- Red Dog allowing Raise without sufficient funds[^ref-13]
- Video Poker graphical issues and payout problems[^ref-13]
- Problems with cash totals over $40,000[^ref-13]

A note from Sierra Help Pages warned: "Version 1.0 saved games WILL NOT work with version 1.01 if you have experienced any of the money errors!"[^ref-13]

The 256-color palette causes images to "wash out" momentarily when the game switches between modes, according to Old-Games.com's write-up[^ref-9]. Internet play is not supported in the reseller's modern compatibility release[^ref-11].

## Legacy

### Sales and Commercial Impact

Casino Deluxe 2 appeared in Sierra's The Game Catalogue 1996-1997[^ref-6]. No sales figures are available.

### Collections

Casino Deluxe 2 was also included in the compilation Grand Casino Deluxe[^ref-1]. In late 1997 Sierra offered it in a free-bonus Sports 3-Pack (Windows CD, #W07354) with YOU DON'T KNOW JACK Sports and 3D Ultra Pinball, promising "blackjack, roulette, slots and more!"[^ref-22]

The game has been preserved through multiple Internet Archive uploads[^ref-8][^ref-12].
