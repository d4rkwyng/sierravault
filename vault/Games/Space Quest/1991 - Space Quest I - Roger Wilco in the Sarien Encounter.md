# Space Quest I: Roger Wilco in the Sarien Encounter

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview
Space Quest I: Roger Wilco in the Sarien Encounter is a 1991 VGA remake of the original 1986 Space Quest: The Sarien Encounter, developed and published by [[Sierra On-Line]] for MS-DOS, Amiga, and Macintosh[^ref-2][^ref-4].

Designed by [[Mark Crowe]] and [[Scott Murphy]], the remake features enhanced 256-color VGA graphics, Sound Blaster support, digitized sounds, and a point-and-click icon interface replacing the original text parser[^ref-3][^ref-4][^ref-5].

The VGA graphics were deliberately drawn in a 1950s B-movie style[^ref-2][^ref-5].

Released on August 20, 1991, some puzzles were changed or replaced with new solutions to accommodate the new interface, and it became possible to skip the arcade sequences[^ref-4][^ref-5].

According to the fan site SpaceQuest.net, the remake was "marketed very aggressively", but like Sierra's other VGA remakes of the period it "flopped with a bang"[^ref-37].

## Story Summary
The remake retells the story of the original 1986 release[^ref-1]. In the Earnon galaxy, Roger Wilco works as a janitor on the research ship Arcada; he wakes from a nap in his broom closet to discover that the Sariens have boarded and taken over the ship[^ref-1]. They have made off with the Star Generator, an experimental device that would be catastrophic in their hands[^ref-1].

Taking a keycard from a dead crewman, Roger escapes in a pod just before the Arcada blows itself up[^ref-1]. He crash-lands on Kerona, a parched desert world, and destroys a spider-droid the Sariens have sent after him[^ref-1]. In nearby caves, a mysterious figure promises help if Roger slays a monster called Orat[^ref-1].

With Orat dealt with, the figure gives Roger a skimmer so he can reach the settlement of Ulence Flats and find a ship[^ref-1]. There he wins money on a cantina slot machine, buys a ship and a navigation droid to fly it, and overhears where the Sarien ship Deltaur is located[^ref-1].

Aboard the Deltaur, Roger disguises himself as a Sarien, sets the Star Generator to self-destruct, and gets clear just before the ship explodes[^ref-1]. The grateful people of Xenon reward him with the Golden Mop[^ref-1].

## Gameplay

The remake uses Sierra's SCI game engine with a point-and-click interface and icons along the top of the screen, replacing the AGI engine's text parser[^ref-4]. The new version features enhanced graphics (256-color VGA) and Sound Blaster support[^ref-4].

### Interface and Controls
- Point-and-click interface with icons along the top of the screen[^ref-4][^ref-5]
- No longer requires typing commands; players can interact using mouse-driven icons[^ref-2]
- The text parser is completely replaced by the icon interface[^ref-4]
- Mouse support for movement and interaction[^ref-2]
- Introduces "Smell" and "Taste" icons alongside standard Walk, Use, and Talk—these have no practical use but provoke humorous responses[^ref-30]

### Structure and Progression
- The game takes place across the same locations as the original: the Arcada spaceship, the desert planet Kerona, the town of Ulence Flats, and the Sarien ship Deltaur[^ref-1]
- It is possible to skip the arcade sequences[^ref-4]
- Several additional scenes and more elaborate text descriptions enhance the humorous effect[^ref-4]

### Puzzles and Mechanics
- Some puzzles have been slightly changed to accommodate the new interface[^ref-4]
- As a form of copy protection, coordinates in the VGA version while in the escape pod and the rocket purchased at Tiny's Used Spaceships are only found in the manual[^ref-5][^ref-18]
- The code for retrieving the cartridge aboard the Arcada can only be found in the manual[^ref-18]
- Maximum score is 201 points in VGA (vs 202 in EGA)[^ref-19]
- **VGA-exclusive actions:** Get widget, get plant, use plant on grate monster, leave Deltaur airlock, get ID card—these replaced several EGA-only actions like lifting the platform and kicking the dead body[^ref-19]
- **VGA-exclusive items:** A magnet on the Arcada's Star Generator base and extra cash hidden in bar dust are unique to the VGA version; the magnet can be used on the Slots-o-Death machine to win consistently[^ref-17]

## Reception

### Contemporary Reviews
Dragon Magazine (January 1992) praised the redesigned VGA version, noting the upgraded 256-color graphics, improved point-and-click interface, better music, and digitized speech, concluding: "The game plays far better than before and is highly enjoyable, richly deserving its fame"[^ref-35]. Writing for VideoGames & Computer Entertainment, Arnie Katz noted that the game "benefited from a recent face-lift," adding: "The graphics are much better, and the point-and-click interface makes play easier than the parser-driven original"[^ref-32].

SpaceQuest.net's history of the game says that, like Sierra's other VGA remakes of the time (Leisure Suit Larry I, King's Quest I, Police Quest I), it was "marketed very aggressively, but to no avail as all of these remakes flopped with a bang"[^ref-37]; no sales figures for the remake have been published.

### Modern Assessment
Retro Freak Reviews recommended the remake over the original, stating that the humor, art, animation, and music were improved[^ref-33]. The game holds a MobyScore of 7.4/10 with 70% positive critic reviews and ratings from 67 players[^ref-4]. On GOG, the Space Quest 1+2+3 collection has a 4.3/5 rating[^ref-7]. My Abandonware users rate it 4.49/5 from 45 votes[^ref-2]. HowLongToBeat reports the main story takes approximately 3 hours to complete[^ref-8].

- **MobyGames:** 7.4/10 MobyScore, 70% critics (67 player ratings)[^ref-4]
- **GOG:** 4.3/5 (collection)[^ref-7]
- **Steam:** Very Positive (collection; 85% positive as of October 2026)[^ref-9]

## Development

### Origins
Around 1990-1991, Sierra began releasing VGA remakes of their mid-1980s hits using the new SCI gaming system[^ref-37]. This offered them a chance to try out the new engine, and among these releases were Leisure Suit Larry I, King's Quest I, Police Quest I, and Space Quest I[^ref-37].

Space Quest I was given an overhaul when it was remade using Sierra's SCI language, which allowed the game to upgrade from its original EGA graphics to VGA[^ref-5]. The VGA graphics were drawn deliberately in a 1950s B-movie style[^ref-2][^ref-5]. Scott Murphy later admitted he had little say in the art direction: "The VGA art designer wanted an old fashioned space movie look. It sounded good in theory but wasn't executed as was advertised"[^ref-16]. Much of the artwork was outsourced to Korea—Murphy jokingly blamed "The Simpsons" for this industry trend[^ref-16]. In addition to the new graphics, it featured digitized sounds and an icon interface[^ref-5].

According to SpaceQuest.net's Troels Pleimert, the remake "was created entirely without The Two Guys' input"[^ref-39].

Both are nonetheless credited as Game Designers in the final product[^ref-31].

The credits list Bill Davis as Creative Director, Douglas Herring and Scott Murphy as Directors, and Herring also as Art Designer[^ref-31][^ref-1].

Scott Murphy reportedly gave management a list of things he disliked, chiefly the retro art style, and was told, "we can't change that this far along in development"[^ref-39].

Roger Wilco's hair color was also changed from brown to blond in this version[^ref-37].

An early concept for the original game would have let players choose a male or female character, reportedly dropped because every animation frame would have been needed for both and storage space was limited[^ref-40].

Several of the puzzles from the original game were changed or replaced with new solutions[^ref-5]. Fans are sharply divided between which version of the game they prefer: many enjoy the rich graphical layout of the 1991 remake, while others appreciate the historical purity of the original[^ref-5].

The game was released in both VGA and a rare EGA version based on the VGA version, and a 32-color Amiga version[^ref-5]. Ken Allen composed the music for the remake[^ref-4].

### Technical Achievements
- Used Sierra's SCI engine with 256-color VGA graphics[^ref-3][^ref-4]
- Point-and-click icon interface replacing the text parser[^ref-4][^ref-5]
- Sound Blaster support and digitized sounds[^ref-4]
- 1950s B-movie art style for all graphics[^ref-2][^ref-5]
- Additional scenes and more elaborate text descriptions[^ref-4]
- Platform releases: August 20, 1991 MS-DOS, 1991 Amiga, 1992 Macintosh[^ref-4][^ref-5]
- **System requirements:** 286 CPU, 640KB RAM, DOS 5.0, 6MB hard drive, VGA/MCGA graphics; sound support for AdLib, Sound Blaster, Roland MT-32, Pro Audio Spectrum, Game Blaster, and PC Speaker; General MIDI support via patch[^ref-20][^ref-28]
- **Original media:** Five 5.25" or four 3.5" floppy disks; later Kixx re-release on CD-ROM[^ref-28]
- **Localization:** A Spanish/English bilingual version was also released[^ref-12]
- Fully supported in ScummVM since version 1.2.0; runs on SCI engine at 320x200 resolution with 256 colors[^ref-21]
- The Amiga version was a 32-color conversion[^ref-5]
- **Speed bugs on modern systems:** The acid rain sequence in Kerona cave, desert dehydration timer, and Spider Droid arrival all run too fast; official Sierra patches fix memory and SoundBlaster initialization issues, while NewRisingSun patches address timing bugs[^ref-12][^ref-20]

### Easter Eggs and Trivia
- **King Graham cameo:** In the VGA ending sequence, King Graham from King's Quest appears to award Roger the Golden Mop—a crossover between Sierra's flagship franchises[^ref-17]
- **ZZ Top legal issue:** The game originally featured a band resembling ZZ Top, but legal concerns from the band's lawyers forced Sierra to change it—later versions replaced the ZZ Top sprites with a generic alien band (two small aliens and an alien drummer); removing all `*.v56` files restores the original sprites[^ref-17][^ref-20]
- **Latex Babe easter egg:** Using the TONGUE icon on the purple spiral above the doorway after obtaining the Sarien uniform triggers a glitchy Latex Babe animation[^ref-22]
- **"Don't Press" button:** Pressing the button in the escape pod transports Roger to Nottingham Castle from Conquests of the Longbow (fatal). In the 1986 original the same button dropped him at Daventry from King's Quest I, complete with moat alligators and Ken and Roberta Williams overheard inside; the remake removed the King's Quest references[^ref-17][^ref-30]
- **Instant replay death:** One VGA death scene features the Two Guys from Andromeda popping up to announce an instant replay of Roger's particularly stupid moment[^ref-30]
- **Sci-fi references:** New to the remake, the music in Kerona's hologram area opens like the theme from 2001: A Space Odyssey[^ref-38]
- **Cameos:** Robbie the Robot from "Forbidden Planet" works as the store droid; a Star Trek Warbird appears when selecting wrong coordinates; the time pod from Space Quest IV appears when leaving Ulence Flats; Blues Brothers and Madonna appear in the bar[^ref-17]
- **Hitchhiker's Guide references:** The triple-breasted hooker from Eroticon VI sits at the Ulence Flats bar; the arms dealer on the Sarien ship sounds like Marvin the Paranoid Android[^ref-29][^ref-30]
- **Hayao Miyazaki reference:** The robot shop features "Hay-A-O," a creature resembling characters from Laputa: Castle in the Sky, named after director Hayao Miyazaki[^ref-30]
- **Droids-B-Us parodies:** The store features Dalek robots (Doctor Who), Maximillian (The Black Hole), Huey (Silent Running), and the Lost in Space babysitter droid; fan sources say the Two Guys won the Toys-R-Us lawsuit over the original game's "Droids-R-Us", though the store was renamed Droids-B-Us, and the remake added a Toys-R-Us-style giraffe logo[^ref-5][^ref-38][^ref-30]
- **Removed crossover:** In the 1986 original, a Sarien guard (programmed in by Ken Williams) chatted about King's Quest II and III; the remake dropped the King's Quest dialogue references[^ref-38][^ref-4]
- **Dev trivia:** Kerona was named after Corona beer, the developers' preferred drink in 1986[^ref-38]; manual credits Scott Murphy as director despite him not working on the VGA remake[^ref-23]
- **Packaging:** The remake was sold in 16-color and 256-color packages, and was also offered via a mail-order deal, some copies shipping in original-1986 boxes[^ref-4]

## Legacy

Although the remake received positive reviews, fan histories describe it, like Sierra's other VGA remakes of the era, as a commercial flop[^ref-37][^ref-17]. According to Sierra On-Line's SEC filing, combined sales of the Space Quest series surpassed 1.2 million units by the end of March 1996[^ref-34].

In 1992, Adventure Comics (a division of Malibu Graphics) published a three-issue comic series titled "The Adventures of Roger Wilco" based on the original game's plot, with issues released in February, April, and May 1992[^ref-36]. The remake followed Space Quest IV (March 1991), adopting that game's "Roger Wilco in/and the…" naming convention; a time pod from Space Quest IV even appears when leaving Ulence Flats[^ref-1].
