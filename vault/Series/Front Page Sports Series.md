# Front Page Sports Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

Front Page Sports was Sierra On-Line's line of sports simulations from 1992 to the end of the decade, developed mostly by [[Dynamix]]. Sierra's 1996 annual report described it as a series of "numerous titles that simulate football, baseball, golf, bass fishing, flying and auto racing", many using "actual player statistical data", and said the company had "sold more than 500,000 copies in this series".[^ref-12] The vault's game pages credit most entries to Dynamix, *Golf* and its add-on courses to Headgate Studios, and *Football Pro '98* and *NFL Football Pro '99* to Synergistic Software.

The football games, the series' core, were known for their statistics (more than 300 categories), a play editor for designing custom plays, and career leagues in which players aged and retired.[^ref-4] The first game had no NFL or players' association licence, so its teams and players were fictional; the 1993 *Football Pro* gained an NFLPA licence for real player names, and *Football Pro '95* was officially licensed by the NFL.[^ref-4][^ref-21][^ref-22]

The football line ended when *NFL Football Pro '99*, released in December 1998, was recalled on January 21, 1999; its successor, *NFL Football Pro 2000*, was then cancelled.[^ref-18][^ref-32][^ref-4]

## Subseries

### Football (1992–1998)

The flagship subseries ran to seven versions, from the 1992 original to *NFL Football Pro '99*.[^ref-4] Front Page Sports Football offered:[^ref-4]

- **Licensing**: the 1992 original had no NFL or players' association licence, so its teams and players were fictional. The 1993 *Football Pro* added real player names through an NFLPA licence,[^ref-21] and from *Football Pro '95* onward the games were officially licensed by the NFL, with real NFL teams and players.[^ref-22][^ref-30]
- **Play editor**: users could design and save custom offensive and defensive plays.
- **Career leagues** with player aging, drafts and retirements.
- **Statistics**: more than 300 statistical categories, with box scores and league leaders.

By *Football Pro '98*, Wikipedia notes, the series' on-field gameplay had failed to keep pace with EA's *Madden NFL*, which "for many had become a superior overall game primarily due to the gameplay aspects", although it still did not match Front Page Sports' statistical engine.[^ref-4] *NFL Football Pro '99* was rebuilt in 3D to compete with Madden, but it was "rushed to release to meet the 1998 holiday rush in a completely unplayable state", and Sierra recalled it on January 21, 1999.[^ref-4][^ref-18][^ref-32]

### Baseball (1994–1997)

Three entries: *Baseball '94* (DOS), *Baseball Pro '96* (June 30, 1996) and *Baseball Pro '98* (May 31, 1997). Next Generation praised *Baseball '94* for letting players control every aspect of a game to recreate any game in baseball history, and the series remains popular as a simulation engine for online leagues.[^ref-6]

### Golf (1997)

*Front Page Sports: Golf*, developed by Headgate Studios, was "Sierra's answer to Links".[^ref-7][^ref-27] Gamecenter's Gordon Goble rated its graphics slightly below *Jack Nicklaus 4* and the *Links LS* games.[^ref-27] Three add-on course disks followed: Coeur d'Alene, Black Diamond Ranch and Pasatiempo.[^ref-43][^ref-44][^ref-45]

### Skiing (1997–1999)

*Ski Racing* (1997) simulated four World Cup events on six courses based on real ones.[^ref-25] Its follow-up was released in North America as *Sierra Sports Skiing 99*, in stores by November 10, 1998;[^ref-24] *Ski Racing 99* is that game's 1999 German edition, *DSF Ski '99: Extreme Edition*.[^ref-8]

### Fishing (1996–1997)

*Front Page Sports: Trophy Bass 2* (1996), the sequel to Sierra's *Trophy Bass*, was released under the Front Page Sports name, and Sierra's InterAction magazine announced it with *Golf* as the two new FPS additions for Christmas 1996.[^ref-19][^ref-26] *Trophy Rivers* (1997) followed with river fishing for salmon, trout and steelhead, using spinning gear, bait-casting tackle or a fly rod, with bait, lures or flies; InterAction singled out its fly-fishing.[^ref-31][^ref-9]

## Commercial Performance

Sierra's annual report for the fiscal year ended March 31, 1996 said the company had "sold more than 500,000 copies" in the Front Page Sports series across all its sports.[^ref-12]

## Later History

After the *Football Pro '99* recall, Sierra cancelled *NFL Football Pro 2000*.[^ref-4] The brand later returned: in October 2009 the French studio Cyanide announced a closed beta of a new online version of Front Page Sports Football,[^ref-4] and Cyanide released *Front Page Sports Football* on Steam on October 2, 2014, developed by Cyanide Studio and published by Cyanide Studio and Nacon.[^ref-29]

## People

Dynamix was founded in Eugene, Oregon.[^ref-36] Wikipedia's Dynamix article describes the series as "designed by Pat Cook and Allen McPheeters".[^ref-36]

- **[[Patrick Cook]]** (Pat Cook) was the designer of *Front Page Sports: Football*; Computer Gaming World's 1994 award text called *Football Pro* "to date, designer Pat Cook's masterpiece".[^ref-33] Sierra's InterAction magazine (Holiday 1996) still called him "FPS Designer Pat Cook".[^ref-35]
- **[[Allen Mcpheeters]]** (L. Allen McPheeters) is named with Cook as a designer of the series by the same Wikipedia source.[^ref-36] His own vault page covers his lead design of *Baseball '94* and his later work at Synergistic Software.
- **Vance Cook** was the designer and director of *Golf* at Headgate Studios; he had previously worked on *Links 386 Pro*.[^ref-27]
- **[[Scott Youngblood]]** is credited with direction and design on *Football Pro '97* and as designer of *Ski Racing* (1997); InterAction named him with Cook as one of the "Front Page Sports Developers".[^ref-37][^ref-38][^ref-35]
- **[[Doug Johnson]]** is credited with additional design on *Baseball '94* (alongside Youngblood) and on *Football Pro '97*.[^ref-39][^ref-37]

## Critical Reception

The series' honours were concentrated on the football line, all from Computer Gaming World. In its June 1994 awards the magazine named *Front Page Sports Football Pro* its Premier Sports Game of the Year, saying that "Since its inception, Front Page Sports Football has been the leading football game among our readership".[^ref-33] In March 1996 the magazine inducted *Football Pro* into its Hall of Fame, naming project leader Patrick Cook.[^ref-34] In its May 1995 Premier Awards the magazine honoured *Football Pro '95* for a third year in a row: "This year's championship ring will be worn by FPS: Football '95, for an unprecedented third year in a row."[^ref-46] The magazine's November 1996 fifteenth-anniversary ranking placed *FPS Football 1995* at No. 11, crediting the series with "winning an unprecedented three CGW Premier awards".[^ref-47]

## Legacy

The football games set the template the later entries followed: a play editor, deep statistics and multi-season leagues in which players aged and retired. The brand was revived later: Cyanide announced an online version in 2009 and released *Front Page Sports Football* on Steam in 2014 (see Later History above).[^ref-4][^ref-29] The baseball games remained in use as a simulation engine for online leagues.[^ref-6]

## Playing Today

No GOG or Steam listing as of November 11, 2026 for the original Dynamix and Synergistic titles. The only series-branded title on sale is Cyanide's unrelated 2014 *Front Page Sports Football* on Steam.[^ref-29] The originals were DOS and early Windows games.
