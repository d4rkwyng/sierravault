# King's Quest Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

The King's Quest series is one of the most influential franchises in adventure game history. MobyGames calls it "Sierra's longest-running and largest adventure game series",[^ref-1] and Wikipedia credits it as "primarily responsible for building the reputation of Sierra".[^ref-2] Created by [[Roberta Williams]] in 1984, the series introduced players to the fairy tale kingdom of Daventry and its royal family across eight mainline entries, a 1990 SCI remake of the first game, and a 2015 episodic re-imagining.[^ref-2] By 1994 the series had sold 2.5 million copies, which Wikipedia describes as "the bestselling computer game series at that time"; Sierra reported more than 3.8 million by March 1996, and by 1997 the total had reached 7 million.[^ref-2]

The original King's Quest was commissioned by IBM as a showcase adventure for its PCjr computer, and it let players walk an animated Sir Graham around a full-colour scene, behind and in front of objects, while still typing commands.[^ref-3][^ref-7] Wikipedia calls it "the first in the genre enabling players to, in third-person perspective, move their character behind and in front of on-screen objects",[^ref-2] MobyGames says the first game "is widely regarded as the progenitor of third-person perspective adventures",[^ref-1] and IEEE Spectrum describes it as "the first animated 3D adventure game".[^ref-11] In 2020 King's Quest was inducted into the World Video Game Hall of Fame at The Strong, which says it "set the standard for the graphical adventure game genre".[^ref-10]

## Themes and Setting

### The Kingdom of Daventry

Daventry serves as the central setting for the series, a fairy tale kingdom where magic is real and classic folklore comes to life. The world draws heavily from European fairy tales, Greek mythology, and classic literature, creating a tapestry of familiar stories woven into original adventures.

Key locations across the series include:
- **Daventry** - The home kingdom, threatened in various games
- **Kolyma** - Setting of KQ2, home of Count Dracula
- **Llewdor** - The land of the wizard Manannan in KQ3
- **Tamir** - A fairy-haunted land in KQ4
- **Serenia** - Desert and forest realms in KQ5
- **The Land of the Green Isles** - An archipelago in KQ6
- **Eldritch** - A troll-infested realm in KQ7
- **Daventry to the Realm of the Sun** - Connor's quest across seven worlds in KQ8[^ref-22]

### The Royal Family

The series follows multiple generations of Daventry's royal family:

- **King Graham** - The protagonist of KQ1, KQ2, KQ5, and the 2015 reboot. Started as Sir Graham, a knight who won the crown.
- **Queen Valanice** - Graham's wife, rescued in KQ2, playable in KQ7
- **Prince Alexander** - Twin son, kidnapped as a baby, protagonist of KQ3 and KQ6
- **Princess Rosella** - Twin daughter, protagonist of KQ4 and co-protagonist of KQ7
- **Connor** - An unrelated knight protagonist in KQ8

## Technical Evolution

The King's Quest series showcased Sierra's technological advancement across three generations of game engines:

### AGI Era (1984-1988)
The Adventure Game Interpreter powered KQ1-KQ3 and the AGI release of KQ4, and Ars Technica notes it remained "the backbone of Sierra's adventure games until it was superseded in 1988":[^ref-7]
- 160x200 resolution with 16 colors
- Text parser interface ("look tree", "get sword")
- Animated sprites over static backgrounds
- Real-time movement with arrow keys

### SCI Era (1988-1994)
The Sierra Creative Interpreter debuted in 1988 with King's Quest IV, which shipped in both AGI and SCI versions.[^ref-15][^ref-7] The engine moved through several generations within the series:[^ref-15]
- **SCI0 (1988)** - 320x200, 16-colour EGA, still with a text parser (KQ4 SCI and the 1990 KQ1 remake)
- **SCI1 (1990)** - 256-colour VGA and an icon-driven point-and-click interface, beginning with KQ5
- **SCI1.1 / SCI32** - KQ6, then KQ7's high-resolution SVGA graphics[^ref-16]
- KQ4 was among the earliest PC games to support sound cards[^ref-2]

### 3D Era (1998-2016)
- KQ8 introduced real-time 3D graphics and action elements; its engine was built from an earlier version of Dynamix's 3Space[^ref-14]
- The 2015 re-imagining by The Odd Gentlemen was built on Unreal Engine 3 and released in five chapters plus an epilogue[^ref-13]

## Critical Reception

| Game | Notable Reception |
|------|-------------------|
| KQ1 | Defined the graphic adventure genre; World Video Game Hall of Fame, 2020[^ref-7][^ref-10] |
| KQ4 | First in the series, and among the first games, to feature a female protagonist[^ref-4][^ref-2] |
| KQ5 | Certified Platinum by the SPA (250,000 audited units) by February 1993; 300,000–400,000 US sales by November 2000[^ref-20][^ref-5] |
| KQ6 | "Generally considered the best title in the series"; about 400,000 copies in its first week[^ref-6] |
| KQ7 | High-resolution art "reminiscent of Disney and Don Bluth animated films"[^ref-16] |
| KQ8 | Action-oriented departure that "underperformed commercially and critically"[^ref-2] |
| KQ 2015 | Mostly positive chapter reviews (Chapter I: Metacritic 82/100 on PC), with Chapters II and IV mixed[^ref-13] |

## Legacy

### Industry Impact
- Ars Technica calls the first game "the game that defined the genre"[^ref-7]
- Let players move an animated character behind and in front of objects in a third-person view[^ref-2][^ref-3]
- KQ4's Rosella was among the first female protagonists in computer games[^ref-2][^ref-4]
- The Strong cites the series as setting "the standard for the graphical adventure game genre"[^ref-10]

### Fan Community
The series maintains an active fan community decades after the original games:
- **AGDI** created fan remakes of KQ1-3[^ref-8]
- **The Silver Lining** - A fan-made continuation released under a non-commercial licence from Activision, on condition that it be made clear it is "not an official sequel"[^ref-19][^ref-9]
- **King's Quest Omnipedia** - Comprehensive fan wiki[^ref-1]

### Merchandise and Adaptations
- *The King's Quest Companion* (Peter Spear) contains novelizations of the games, and the KQ7 Players Guide novelizes that game[^ref-2]
- Three original novels were published by Boulevard Books[^ref-2]
- Hint books and strategy guides
- The 2015 reboot introduced the series to new audiences
