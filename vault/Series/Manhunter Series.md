# Manhunter Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Manhunter series is a two-game post-apocalyptic science-fiction adventure series developed by **Evryware** (Dave Murry, Barry Murry, and Dee Dee Murry) and published by Sierra On-Line in 1988 and 1989.[^ref-1][^ref-11] MobyGames describes it as a "series of first-person adventure games, set in a world where alien 'Orbs' rule mankind", in which the player is a Manhunter employed by the Orbs to investigate the human resistance but eventually rebels against them.[^ref-2] The Murrys have said that "the tone of Manhunter was darker than other Sierra titles."[^ref-8] Sources disagree on when Dee Dee Murry joined the team: Evryware's team page says she has been lead artist "since 1984,"[^ref-21] the 1992 manual for *The Ancient Art of War in the Skies* says she "joined the team in 1987,"[^ref-22] and the 1997 interview places it around the time *Manhunter: New York* was released ("At this point, our sister, Dee Dee joined the team").[^ref-8] See [[Evryware]] for the conflict.

Barry Murry is credited as composer of both games.[^ref-1][^ref-6] *Manhunter: New York* appeared on DOS, Amiga, Atari ST, Apple II, Apple IIGS and Macintosh;[^ref-3] *Manhunter 2: San Francisco* on DOS, Amiga, Atari ST and Macintosh.[^ref-17]

The series is unusual within the Sierra catalog for three reasons:

1. **Not Sierra-developed** — Evryware was an outside developer. Sierra "gave us the engine and told us that we could do whatever we wanted with it. They didn't even see the game until it was almost done," the Murrys recalled.[^ref-8] 
2. **A silenced world, seen in first person** — Under the Orbs, "speaking is a capital offense", and the nameless Manhunter investigates through a first-person view and an overhead tracking map.[^ref-3] Unlike other AGI games, it used no text parser.[^ref-1]
3. **A planned trilogy** — Posters throughout the series tease a third installment, *Manhunter 3: London*,[^ref-16] which was never made. See [[CXL - Manhunter 3]] for the cancelled-entry documentation.

## Key Characters

- **The Manhunter** — the unnamed player character, a human tracker employed by the Orbs in both games; the sequel has the player assume the identity of a local Manhunter killed in the crash.[^ref-1][^ref-6]
- **The Orbs** — the alien rulers, who look like giant floating eyeballs.[^ref-1]
- **Phil (Phil Cook)** — the antagonist, a killer who murders members of the human resistance; he escapes at the end of the first game, is pursued to San Francisco in the second, and flees again in an Orb ship toward London.[^ref-1][^ref-6]

## Manhunter: New York (1988)

The game opens with the New York skyline under attack in 2002; the main game is set in 2004, when humans wear robes, are forbidden to speak and carry implanted tracking devices.[^ref-1] Each chapter, the Orbs assign the Manhunter a crime or target to investigate. The "MAD" computer "displays the logs of all civilian movement in an area in an overhead map view"; the player follows the people who met the victim, then switches to a first-person view at New York landmarks to find clues and report the suspect's name.[^ref-3]

**Design features:**
- **No text parser** — Manhunter: New York used a first-person perspective and a rudimentary point-and-click interface instead of a parser.[^ref-1] The Murrys called it the "first at Sierra to not use the annoying parser."[^ref-8]
- **Manual** — The game shipped with an "Official" guide "by The Order of the Orb Alliance" that introduces the Manhunter Assignment Device (MAD).[^ref-18]
- **Real NYC landmarks** — The player investigates Times Square, Grand Central, Coney Island, Central Park and the Museum of Natural History,[^ref-9] and Central Park is "now a minefield".[^ref-5]
- **Action sequences** — Adventure Classic Gaming found the arcade sequences "annoying to play" and said they "bear little ties to the plot".[^ref-4]

**Reception:** Computer Entertainer (October 1988) called it "quite unlike any the company has ever released before" and "a fascinating and very sophisticated role-playing game that is quite out of the ordinary," rating it "Recommended."[^ref-20] Computer Gaming World's Scorpia wrote: "In spite of the arcade sequences, this is a clever game that requires you to really look at the screen and remember what you see."[^ref-5] CGW nominated it for Adventure Game of the Year, an award won by Interplay's Neuromancer.[^ref-13] Adventure Classic Gaming later called its puzzles "sickeningly linear".[^ref-4] The Murrys said both Manhunter games "sold very well (more than 100,000 of each)."[^ref-8]

## Manhunter 2: San Francisco (1989)

The sequel picks up "immediately where Manhunter: New York left off": the player crash-lands a stolen ship in San Francisco while chasing the serial killer Phil, assumes the identity of a local Manhunter, and works for the local human resistance.[^ref-17][^ref-6]

**Design evolution:**
- **Bay Area landmarks** — The Golden Gate Bridge, Coit Tower, Alcatraz and the TransAmerica Pyramid "all receive alien makeovers".[^ref-17]
- **Fewer arcade sequences** — Adventure Classic Gaming notes "there are less number of them in this sequel".[^ref-7] Scorpia called "the introduction of variable difficulty levels for the arcade sequences" the game's "one big change".[^ref-13]
- **Cliffhanger ending** — Phil Cook escapes in an Orb ship bound for London with the player clinging to its hull; no third game followed.[^ref-6]

**Reception:** Contemporary and later sources diverge. In Computer Gaming World, Scorpia called it "Somewhat on the gruesome side, and therefore not for everyone, but otherwise an excellent followup to the previous game; a definite 'must have' for Manhunter fans."[^ref-13] Adventure Classic Gaming rated it 3 of 5, saying its arcade sequences "unduly detract from the adventure element" and its puzzles were "still much too simple"; *Computer and Video Games* gave it 61%, criticizing the mix of adventure and arcade elements.[^ref-7][^ref-6]

## Manhunter 3 (Cancelled)

A third entry, *Manhunter 3: London*, was teased by posters in the games.[^ref-16] It was never made: "The association between Evryware and Sierra ended in 1989 and the two companies never partnered up again," and since no deal was struck to move the game into production, the Larry Laffer fan shrine judges it "probable that it never went beyond the conceptual stage."[^ref-12] See [[CXL - Manhunter 3]] for the cancelled-entry page.

## Series Design Identity

The Murrys set out to make Manhunter look "as different from the standard Sierra adventure title as we could":[^ref-8]

1. **No "Quest" in the title** — "The first thing we decided to do was to not use the word 'Quest' in the title!"[^ref-8]
2. **Cinematic presentation** — "unusual camera angles, extreme close-ups, first person views, long pans, aerial views, picture in a picture"[^ref-8]
3. **Tracker-device gameplay loop** — Track suspects on the MAD map, investigate locations, report the suspect's name to the Orbs[^ref-3]
4. **Suppressed-society premise** — "you are in a suppressed society and … in a position to do something about it"; aliens were chosen "because it would be obvious that they could not be defeated directly"[^ref-8]
5. **Real-location settings** — "Using real locations (New York and San Francisco) added realism to the game."[^ref-8]

## Critical Reception

Both games drew positive notice from the period press and more qualified verdicts later, with the arcade sequences the recurring complaint.

## Legacy

Neither game is sold digitally; both appear only as fan-request entries on the GOG Dreamlist.[^ref-10][^ref-19] Evryware's 1997 interview says its authors did not own the Manhunter rights ("We own the rights to Ancient Art of War but not to Manhunter"),[^ref-8] and Wikipedia says the rights are now held by Activision following its 2008 acquisition of Sierra's intellectual property.[^ref-6] That Activision claim rests on Wikipedia alone; no primary source was found.
