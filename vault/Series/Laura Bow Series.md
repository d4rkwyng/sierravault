# Laura Bow Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Laura Bow Mysteries are Sierra On-Line's graphical adventure games "in the mystery idiom", featuring the sleuthing of Laura Bow.[^ref-1] The short-lived series was created by [[Roberta Williams]], and its first game drew on elements of her first program, Mystery House (1980).[^ref-7][^ref-3] It spans two games: The Colonel's Bequest (1989), with design credited to Jacqueline Austin and Williams as writer and co-director, and The Dagger of Amon Ra (1992), designed and directed by [[Bruce Balfour]], with Bill Davis as creative director and Williams as creative consultant.[^ref-7][^ref-13][^ref-15] Set in the 1920s, they follow Laura Bow, a Tulane University student and aspiring journalist, through murder mysteries.[^ref-7][^ref-13] The Colonel's Bequest put observation and deduction ahead of traditional puzzle-solving, which Williams said was optional.[^ref-9] The Dagger of Amon Ra's puzzles were mostly "of the typical variety of inventory and environment interaction".[^ref-13]

The Colonel's Bequest stood out among Sierra adventures because story events happen whether or not Laura is there to see them. Its clock is not real-time: it advances in fifteen-minute steps when certain triggers are met.[^ref-3][^ref-4] Lemon Amiga likened the game to "an early computer version of the classic book 'And then there were none'".[^ref-4]

## Creators

### Roberta Williams and The Colonel's Bequest
Williams wrote The Colonel's Bequest and co-directed it with Chris Iden; Jacqueline Austin, a screenplay and novel writer, was credited as its creative designer.[^ref-7][^ref-15] As Williams explained in a 1990 START Magazine interview: "Colonel's Bequest is very much an interactive story. One of the directions Ken and I see Sierra going is in interactive entertainment - stories."[^ref-5]

Influences visible in the game include:
- **Agatha Christie** - Laura is stranded on the island "surrounded by suspects and potential victims in a classical Agatha Christie manner"[^ref-7]
- **Mystery House** - the "mansion murder" concept of Williams' first program[^ref-7]
- **The 1920s** - the game is set in 1925[^ref-7]
- **Classic horror films** - a shower scene that references *Psycho*[^ref-7][^ref-3]

The approach was deliberately experimental: "There is no real goal - you're an innocent thrust into a situation where suddenly all these people are dying and things are happening."[^ref-5]

### Bruce Balfour and The Dagger of Amon Ra
Hardcore Gaming 101 notes that "despite Roberta Williams' name on the box, she had little to do with The Dagger of Amon Ra beyond providing the concept and lending her character"; it was directed and designed by Bruce Balfour.[^ref-14] Wikipedia lists Balfour as designer, director and producer, Bill Davis as creative director, Josh Mandel as writer, and Williams as creative consultant.[^ref-13]

## Characters

### Laura Bow
- **Background:** Tulane University student, daughter of a detective, and aspiring journalist (1925); by 1926 a Tulane graduate working for a New York newspaper[^ref-7][^ref-13]
- **Role:** Amateur sleuth
- **Skills:** Investigation, observation, note-taking

### Colonel Henri Dijon
The Colonel's Bequest centers on this reclusive, childless plantation owner, whose gathering of his relatives for a reading of his will sets off the murders.[^ref-7]

### Supporting Cast
Both games feature casts of suspects, victims and red herrings with interconnected relationships, secrets and motivations.

## World and Settings

### Colonel Dijon's Plantation (The Colonel's Bequest)
The first game takes place on Colonel Dijon's decaying sugar plantation on an island in the middle of a Louisiana swamp, over a single night divided into eight acts.[^ref-7][^ref-3]

Besides the mansion, the fenced grounds include "the chapel, the barn, Celie's granny flat, the small gardens, the cemetery, the boat shed, and the gazebo area".[^ref-20]

### Leyendecker Museum (The Dagger of Amon Ra)
The sequel moves to New York in 1926, where Laura investigates the theft of the Dagger of Amon Ra from the Leyendecker Museum's Egyptian exhibit and the murders that follow. The museum is named after the artist J. C. Leyendecker, whose work set the game's visual style.[^ref-13]

## Innovative Design

### Trigger-Based Time
In The Colonel's Bequest, story events happen whether Laura is present or not. There are eight acts, one for each hour, and each hour is broken into fifteen-minute quarters; the clock "does not operate in real time" but advances when certain triggers are met, usually when Laura enters certain rooms or talks to certain people.[^ref-3][^ref-4] Josh Mandel, who beta tested the game, explained: "It gave the game a better sense of realism, that you could be in one place and miss something that was going on in another place."[^ref-6]

This meant players could:
- Miss crucial conversations by being elsewhere
- Piece together events from incomplete information
- See different parts of the story on replay

### Sleuth-O-Meter Scoring
Roberta Williams told *Computer Gaming World* (issue #68): "There is no scoring system in the game. Players gauge their progress in the wrap-up by means of a notebook which rates them on their detective skills."[^ref-9] At the end, Laura's notebook is reviewed and the "Sleuth-O-Meter" rates the player from "Barely Conscious" to "Super Sleuth", based on the events witnessed and items found.[^ref-10] Hardcore Gaming 101 adds that, as in other Sierra games, points are awarded for certain tasks, but they are kept hidden from the player until the very end.[^ref-3]

In the same interview she said: "We hope this product will help bridge the gap and take gamers away from the cushion of needing a score to see how well they do."[^ref-9]

### Minimal Traditional Puzzles
Williams told *Computer Gaming World* that "you don't need to solve a single puzzle in order to complete the game".[^ref-9] Success instead depends on:
- Eavesdropping on conversations
- Examining crime scenes
- Noting character movements
- Connecting relationship dynamics

## Technical Evolution

### The Colonel's Bequest (SCI0)
- 320x200, 16-colour EGA graphics[^ref-7]
- Text parser interface
- Trigger-based clock and event system[^ref-3]
- No voice acting: the game supports music and sound effects on a range of sound cards but "does not yet handle sampled audio"[^ref-18]
- Music by [[Ken Allen]][^ref-7]

### The Dagger of Amon Ra (SCI1.1)
- 256-colour (8-bit) VGA graphics[^ref-13]
- Point-and-click interface[^ref-13]
- Voice acting in the 1993 CD-ROM version only; the 1992 floppy release has none[^ref-13]

## Critical Reception

| Game | Notable Reception |
|------|-------------------|
| The Colonel's Bequest | Génération 4 95% and Joystick 92% (1990), though Amiga Format gave the Amiga version 60%;[^ref-11] Adventure Gamers retrospective 4 stars[^ref-2] |
| The Dagger of Amon Ra | PC Gamer says it "brought new layers of complexity to adventure game storytelling"[^ref-6] |

In *Games International*, Theo Clarke called The Colonel's Bequest "solid genre stuff" and said it "has a charm all of its own."[^ref-7] Adventure Gamers praised its "original sort of game, high replayability".[^ref-2] PC Gamer's 2020 retrospective called the two games "genuinely groundbreaking".[^ref-6]

## Legacy

### Influence
The Colonel's Bequest inspired Julia Minamata's *The Crimson Diamond*, which Ars Technica calls a "not-at-all-subtle spiritual successor".[^ref-16] *Computer Gaming World*'s November 1996 15th-anniversary issue ranked Laura Bow #13 among its 15 most memorable game heroes, saying she "captured the flavor of the flapper era".[^ref-19][^ref-1]

### Revival Efforts
- **Laura Bow and the Mechanical Codex** - Steam lists this NineZyme game as the "third installment of the Laura Bow franchise", with release "To be announced".[^ref-8]
- In August 2024 NineZyme reported a meeting with Microsoft and Ken and Roberta Williams, but told Adventure Game Hotspot that "Laura Bow is still mostly on hold, for the time being, until something more concrete is decided on the licensing."[^ref-12]

### Why Only Two Games?
The series ended after two entries. No source gives a reason; Williams was not the lead designer of the second game.[^ref-14]
