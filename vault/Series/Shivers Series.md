# Shivers Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 11, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Shivers series is Sierra's two-game puzzle-horror line: [[1995 - Shivers|Shivers]] (1995) and [[1997 - Shivers Two - Harvest of Souls|Shivers Two: Harvest of Souls]] (1997).[^ref-21] Both are first-person adventures with a slideshow-style presentation:[^ref-3][^ref-6] the player explores a deserted location (a haunted museum in the original, an abandoned Southwestern town in the sequel), solving puzzles while uncovering a supernatural backstory.[^ref-1][^ref-2]

MobyGames compares the first game to *Myst*,[^ref-3] and Adventure Gamers called it a "horror-themed Myst clone".[^ref-4] [[Roberta Williams]] served as creative consultant and co-writer on the original *Shivers* while she was working on [[Phantasmagoria Series|Phantasmagoria]].[^ref-1] Marcia Bales is credited as designer of both games and Willie Eide as lead game developer in both manuals;[^ref-33][^ref-34] Wikipedia's infoboxes list both as designers.[^ref-1][^ref-2]

**Releases:**
- **Shivers, Windows:** September 30, 1995 per MobyGames (Windows 16-bit); Wikipedia gives November 1995.[^ref-3][^ref-1]
- **Shivers, Macintosh:** an Online Gaming Review news item dated May 30, 1996 notes that "Shivers was released as well" for Mac owners; MobyGames lists a 1996 Macintosh release.[^ref-28][^ref-3]
- **Shivers Two, Windows:** Sierra's own press list records "Shivers II: Harvest of Souls is Released. (14-April-1997)".[^ref-27]
- **1998 compilations:** MobyGames groups two compilations with the series. *Best of Sierra Nr. 8* (the September/October 1998 issue) contains *Shivers* and *The Dagger of Amon Ra*;[^ref-29] *Le Meilleur de Sierra II* (1998) contains seven Sierra titles including *Shivers Two*.[^ref-30] They are collections of existing games, not new entries, which is why the series counts two games.[^ref-21]

## Themes and Setting

Both games confine the player to one location, a haunted museum in the first and an abandoned Southwestern town in the second.[^ref-1][^ref-2] The first game's Ixupi are evil spirits that drain a visitor's life force and can be imprisoned in matching containers;[^ref-25] the sequel moves to kachina, baho and kiva imagery.[^ref-2]

### Key Characters

- **Professor Windlenot** — the archaeologist who built the museum; in the first game his ghost explains how to imprison the Ixupi.[^ref-25]
- **The Ixupi** — the spirits that must be captured, each tied to a material such as sand, cloth or wax.[^ref-1]
- **Trip Cyclone** — the rock band whose members are turned into petroglyphs in the sequel.[^ref-2][^ref-6]
- **Darkcloud** — the kachina-masked villain of *Shivers Two*.[^ref-2]

## Technical Evolution

The original ran on Sierra's SCI32 engine (listed as SCI2.1 by PCGamingWiki), and ScummVM supports it.[^ref-15][^ref-8] The sequel kept SCI resources but moved its game logic into native code in DLL files, which is why ScummVM's SCI engine does not run it.[^ref-22][^ref-26] See [[Engine History]] for the wider SCI story.

## Shivers (1995)

The player is a teenager who has accepted a dare to spend the night in Professor Windlenot's Museum of the Strange and Unusual.[^ref-4] The museum's "Ixupi" spirits, trapped long ago by the Zapana, a fictional South American people, are each associated with a material or force such as sand, cloth, wax or electricity, and each must be captured in its matching vessel. They were released in 1980 by two local students who broke into the museum, and the game's ending reveals the fate of Windlenot and the two teens.[^ref-1]

**Design features:**
- **First-person slideshow exploration** — Similar to *Myst*, but with a life meter that the Ixupi can drain.[^ref-3]
- **Vessel-matching puzzles** — Each vessel is marked with a Zapana pictograph representing a material, helping the player match it to an Ixupi.[^ref-1]
- **Live-action cutscenes** — Actors were filmed in front of a blue screen and superimposed on the digital backgrounds with Ultimatte.[^ref-1]
- **Painted backgrounds** — Some 2,500 scanned watercolours, touched up in Photoshop and 3D Studio.[^ref-1]
- **Closed captions** — Dialogue was captioned at the suggestion of lead game developer Willie Eide, who came from a deaf family.[^ref-1]

**Reception:** Period notices were mixed. *PC Gamer* (March 1996) scored it 84% and summed it up as: "Puzzle fans who enjoyed Myst have a new one to add to their collection," while listing among its lows: "Doesn't deliver any real horror."[^ref-25] A February 1996 review by Hermann Peterscheck called it "more spooky than scary--more Scooby-Doo than Psycho".[^ref-31] *MacUser* (December 1996) was cool on it: "you're better off leaving Shivers out in the cold."[^ref-32] Adventure Classic Gaming rated it 3 of 5 and noted its "cult status",[^ref-23] and Adventure Gamers gave it 3.5 stars out of 5, calling it "worth the trip to the bargain bin".[^ref-4] It was re-released on GOG.com on June 21, 2018.[^ref-17][^ref-5]

## Shivers Two: Harvest of Souls (1997)

The player checks into a motel in Cyclone, Arizona, invited by friends in the rock band Trip Cyclone, who are in town filming a music video. The friends have been turned into petroglyphs by a kachina-masked villain called Darkcloud, and the player must find twelve bahos (prayer sticks) hidden around the abandoned town and return them to a kiva in Devil's Mouth Canyon to free them. A series of rock music videos gives clues to the puzzles.[^ref-2][^ref-6]

**Design changes:**
- **Integrated music videos** — Rock music videos double as puzzle clues.[^ref-2]
- **Southwestern setting** — Kachina masks, bahos and a kiva, a distinct cultural setting from the first game's museum.[^ref-2]
- **Freer movement** — The player can rotate 360 degrees in place and carry a full inventory, where the first game allowed only one item.[^ref-6]
- **Open town** — After the opening, "the player is free to explore the town, which is now completely abandoned."[^ref-2]

**Reception:** Mixed: GameSpot scored it 8.7/10, calling the plot "hauntingly intelligent";[^ref-7] AllGame gave 4/5, Adventure Gamers 3/5, and *Next Generation* 2/5.[^ref-2][^ref-20] Adventure Classic Gaming also gave it 3 of 5, observing that Sierra "has clearly listened to the many criticisms by gamers on Shivers during the design of this sequel".[^ref-24] It was re-released on GOG.com on May 29, 2020.[^ref-18][^ref-16]

## Series Design Identity

What unifies the two games:

1. **First-person exploration** with a slideshow-style interface.[^ref-3][^ref-6]
2. **Indigenous-American themes** — The first game's Ixupi were trapped by a fictional South American people; the sequel draws on Southwestern kachina and kiva imagery.[^ref-1][^ref-2]
3. **Music by Guy Whitmore** for both games.[^ref-1][^ref-2]
4. **Single-setting exploration** — One museum in the first game, one abandoned town in the second.[^ref-1][^ref-2]

## Critical Reception

Contemporary critics split on both games, usually praising atmosphere and puzzles while faulting the lack of real horror or the backtracking.[^ref-25][^ref-31][^ref-23][^ref-24]

## Legacy

Wikipedia describes *Shivers* as Sierra On-Line's first first-person adventure game, and the first first-person adventure by Sierra to feature a nameless and unseen protagonist.[^ref-1] Adventure Classic Gaming credits it with a "cult status" that made it "one of the sleeper hits in 1995".[^ref-23]
