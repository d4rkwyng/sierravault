# Quest for Glory Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Quest for Glory series (originally titled "Hero's Quest" until Milton Bradley's trademark on an electronic version of the board game HeroQuest forced Sierra to change the name)[^ref-1] is a series of "role-playing/adventure hybrids" originally developed and published by Sierra.[^ref-2] Computer Gaming World named the first game, *Hero's Quest*, its 1990 Adventure Game of the Year: "In combining the adventure game and role-playing genres, this introductory level game has an interesting story, fascinating encounters and intriguing puzzles."[^ref-9] Created by husband-and-wife team [[Corey Cole]] and [[Lori Ann Cole]],[^ref-12] the series spans five mainline games and one VGA remake, each set in a world drawn from a different culture: medieval Germany, the Middle East, Africa, Eastern Europe and ancient Greece.[^ref-2]

A distinctive feature is character import: players can carry their hero from one game to the next, "including the skills and wealth they had acquired".[^ref-1] Not every critic sees it as an unmixed blessing: The Digital Antiquarian's Jimmy Maher suspects the later games "may actually be more satisfying to play if you don't import your old character".[^ref-3]

## The Coles' Vision

[[Corey Cole]] and [[Lori Ann Cole]] co-designed the first four games; Lori was the sole designer of Quest for Glory V, on which Corey was a programmer.[^ref-12] The series combines Sierra-style adventure gameplay with role-playing:[^ref-2]

- **Character classes** - Fighter, Magic-User and Thief at first, with the Paladin added in QFG2[^ref-2]
- **Stats improve through use** - "climbing a tree eventually increases the skill value in climb, running increases vitality"[^ref-1]
- **Multiple solutions** - "Many tasks and puzzles in the games can be solved in different ways, depending on the class the player has chosen"[^ref-2]

## Character System

### Classes
- **Fighter, Magic-User, Thief** - The three original classes; the character usually joins a guild, which offers class-specific plots[^ref-2]
- **Paladin** - In QFG2 the hero can become a paladin during the game by following a strict moral code;[^ref-15] from QFG3 on, an imported hero can play as a Paladin[^ref-27]

### Stats
Each class has special abilities plus a shared set of attributes developed by performing tasks; class skills include parry for fighters and paladins, lock picking and sneaking for thieves, and spellcasting for magic users.[^ref-1] Health and stamina derive from strength, agility and vitality.[^ref-1]

### Character Import
Players can import their hero into later games, keeping "the skills and wealth they had acquired";[^ref-1] a hero initiated as a Paladin keeps that status and the paladin abilities when exported.[^ref-1] In Shadows of Darkness, the imported hero loses all his belongings before the game begins.[^ref-1]

## World and Settings

Each Quest for Glory draws on a different culture and mythology: Germanic/fairy tale, Middle Eastern/Arabian Nights, Egyptian/African, Slavic folklore, and Greco-Mediterranean.[^ref-1]

### Spielburg Valley (QFG1)
A town threatened by brigands, with forests and mountains full of ogres, goblins and the witch Baba Yaga.[^ref-14]

### Shapeir (QFG2)
A Middle East-like desert town troubled by elemental spirits.[^ref-15]

### Tarna (QFG3)
An Africa-like world where the hero, travelling with the paladin Rakeesh, must prevent a war between the human Simbani tribe and the Leopardmen.[^ref-17] Corey Cole has described Tarna as "the Gloriana analogue to Kenya in East Africa", with a Simbani language based on Swahili.[^ref-3]

### Mordavia (QFG4)
A setting "influenced by Eastern Europe, particularly its Slavic areal", reached through dark swamps and forest to a town in the grip of "a foreboding atmosphere".[^ref-18]

### Silmaria (QFG5)
A Greece-like country where the hero competes in the Rites of Rulership while an unknown assassin kills contestants.[^ref-19]

## Legacy

### Spiritual Successors
The Coles continued their work after Sierra:
- **Hero-U: Rogue to Redemption** (2018) - Kickstarter-funded by 6,093 backers who pledged $409,150 in 2012;[^ref-5] developed by Transolar Games[^ref-23]
- **Summer Daze: Tilly's Tale** (2023) - A visual novel about Tilly, "a mischief-making rogue at Hero-U",[^ref-6] funded by the 2019 *Summer Daze at Hero-U* Kickstarter (1,639 backers, $106,155)[^ref-13]

### Fan Community
- **Quest for Glory Omnipedia** - Fan wiki[^ref-7]
- **AGD Interactive** - Released a VGA fan remake of QFG2 using the Adventure Game Studio engine on August 24, 2008[^ref-1][^ref-8]
