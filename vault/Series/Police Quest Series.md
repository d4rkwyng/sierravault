# Police Quest Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Police Quest series is Sierra On-Line's police procedural adventure series. MobyGames describes the first three games as following Sonny Bonds, "a police officer in the fictional city of Lytton, California", designed under the supervision of former Highway Patrol officer Jim Walls.[^ref-1] The first game was created in 1987 and was reported to have been used as a training tool for police officers.[^ref-2]

Police Quest asks players to follow real police procedure. In the first game, "you'll also be late for the morning briefing, which will end the game abruptly" if you go it alone, and there is "a manual of department regulations you must virtually memorize in order to keep the game in motion for long."[^ref-12] One modern reviewer notes that "the game ends prematurely if you forget to check the tyres of your car before going out on patrol."[^ref-13]

## Jim Walls' Background

[[Jim Walls]] graduated from the California Highway Patrol academy in December 1971. In January 1984 he was involved in a shootout during a traffic stop, and in 1985 he was placed on administrative leave; he retired in 1986, which Wikipedia describes as "after 15 years of service".[^ref-5] (His own count differs; see [[Jim Walls]].) While on leave in 1985, his then-wife Donna introduced him to Ken Williams.[^ref-5]

According to Wikipedia, Williams asked Walls to condense his CHP experiences into a short story, then to expand it; the story developed into the plot of the first Police Quest.[^ref-5]

## The Lytton Police Department

The fictional Lytton Police Department is the setting for the Jim Walls trilogy.[^ref-1]

### Key Characters
- **Sonny Bonds** - Player protagonist; begins as a patrol officer and works up to detective in the first game,[^ref-14] has joined Homicide by PQ2,[^ref-15] and is a sergeant in PQ3[^ref-16]
- **Jessie Bains, "The Death Angel"** - Drug lord arrested in PQ1; he escapes and seeks revenge on Sonny in PQ2[^ref-14][^ref-15]
- **Marie** - Sonny proposes to her in PQ2; by PQ3 she is his wife, and she falls into a coma after an attack by the Kindred cult[^ref-15][^ref-16]

### Authentic Elements
Sierra's InterAction magazine listed the procedures PQ1 covers: "how to handle felony and routine traffic stops, intoxicated drivers, handcuffing, felony arrests, physical confrontation and the use of deadly force, radio transmissions, and more." Players also had to note the right Penal Code or Vehicle Code violation number when writing up a suspect.[^ref-6]

## The Gates Transition

[[Jim Walls]] left Sierra after Police Quest III.[^ref-10] Police Quest: Open Season (1993) carried former Los Angeles Police Chief [[Daryl Gates|Daryl F. Gates]]'s name, but he was credited as author; [[Tammy Dargan]] is credited as designer, writer, producer and director.[^ref-9] VICE describes the game as "chiefly written and designed by Tammy Dargan", with Gates giving her script notes.[^ref-10]

### Changes in Open Season
- New protagonist, LAPD homicide detective John Carey, replacing Sonny Bonds[^ref-17]
- Real Los Angeles setting instead of fictional Lytton,[^ref-17] grounded in "the gritty, violent Los Angeles that Gates knew"[^ref-10]
- Digitized photorealistic visuals[^ref-17]
- Procedure stayed central: MobyGames says it "focuses on realistic police procedures even more than the previous games"[^ref-17]
- Real-world controversy: Gates's hiring followed the 1991 Rodney King beating, and the Los Angeles Urban League's John Mack criticised Sierra for it; inside Sierra some staff called the game "Rodney King's Quest"[^ref-10]

## Technical Evolution

### AGI Era (1987)
- Text parser interface, with overhead-view driving between locations[^ref-14]
- Procedure manual (the "department regulations") needed to progress[^ref-12]

### SCI Era (1988-1993)
- PQ2 (SCI0): mouse movement plus a text parser; the driving sequences were dropped[^ref-15]
- PQ3: icon-based interface; modified driving sequences return[^ref-16]
- PQ1 VGA (1992): redrawn in 256-colour VGA with an icon interface and simplified driving[^ref-18]
- Open Season (1993): digitized photorealistic images[^ref-17]

## Legacy

### Training Applications
The series' best-known legacy is its reported use in law enforcement training.[^ref-2] Sierra's own InterAction magazine (Winter 1992), promoting the VGA re-release, said the game had been "used as a training tool by law enforcement agencies across the country" and "has proven to be a practical, effective training tool officers enjoy using. It safely demonstrates to rookies the consequences of failing to observe proper procedures".[^ref-6] Wikipedia credits the article to Rich DeBaun; the scanned issue shows no byline on it.[^ref-2][^ref-6]

### Industry Impact
- Sierra continued the brand as the SWAT "police simulator" line after Open Season[^ref-1][^ref-7]
