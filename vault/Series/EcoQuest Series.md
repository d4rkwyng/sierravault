# EcoQuest Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

The EcoQuest series is Sierra's two-game environmental adventure line for young players: [[1991 - EcoQuest - The Search for Cetus|EcoQuest: The Search for Cetus]] (1991) and [[1993 - EcoQuest - Lost Secret of the Rainforest|EcoQuest 2: Lost Secret of the Rainforest]] (1993).[^ref-1][^ref-2][^ref-16] Both star Adam, the son of an ecologist, who befriends the animals living in the places he visits.[^ref-17] The first game is set in the ocean; in the second, Adam and his father go to the Amazon.[^ref-17]

[[Gano Haine]] pitched EcoQuest. Once her idea was accepted, Sierra assigned [[Jane Jensen]] to work with her as co-designer, and Jimmy Maher calls the result "the first computer game in history that was the product of an all-female design team".[^ref-17] Haine alone directed the sequel.[^ref-17][^ref-19]

## EcoQuest: The Search for Cetus (1991)

Haine drew the idea from a beach she had seen covered with litter and sewage, so the first game was set in the ocean.[^ref-17] It was one of Jensen's first two Sierra projects, alongside *Police Quest III*.[^ref-12][^ref-3] Adam is helping to rehabilitate a dolphin his father has rescued when it begins to talk, and the two set out to find Cetus, the sperm whale king of the undersea kingdom of Eluria.[^ref-4] The game confronts players with human activities such as dumping litter, releasing helium balloons and abandoning fishing gear.[^ref-4] Haine and Jensen worked with the Marine Mammal Center of Sausalito, California, to get the science right.[^ref-17]

The game added a recycling symbol to Sierra's command icons, giving bonus points for removing litter from the sea.[^ref-4]

## EcoQuest 2: Lost Secret of the Rainforest (1993)

Jensen did not work on the sequel, "leaving the entirety of the game in the hands of Gano Haine"; she had finished the first game and moved on to *King's Quest VI*.[^ref-17][^ref-20] In *Lost Secret of the Rainforest*, Adam and his father go to the Amazon, where they confront "the bureaucrats, poachers, and clear-cutters" that threaten it.[^ref-17] Adam, now able to speak with animals as a matter of course, searches for "a remedy for the Grove People" and a way to save the rainforest.[^ref-16][^ref-7] A new "Ecorder" device lets him learn about the things he finds.[^ref-7]

The game's Grove People are an invented tribe, which Polygon calls "the fictional Grove People"; it reports they were "supposedly based on the Quechua people, most of whom are from the Andean highlands", and Haine described the game as "an imaginary story, and an ecologically driven story".[^ref-21]

**Reception:** Lisa Young of *Compute!* wrote that "young users may find some of the problems frustrating; however, the thrill of solving a difficult problem is rewarding and may encourage them to continue".[^ref-8]

## Availability

Neither game has an official digital re-release. Both are listed only on GOG's Dreamlist, where users vote for games they want sold.[^ref-22][^ref-23]
