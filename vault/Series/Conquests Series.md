# Conquests Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 9, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Conquests series is a two-game historical-and-mythological adventure line designed by **[[Christy Marx]]** for Sierra On-Line: [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot: The Search for the Grail]] (1990) and [[1991 - Conquests of the Longbow - The Legend of Robin Hood|Conquests of the Longbow: The Legend of Robin Hood]] (1991).[^ref-1][^ref-2] Both games adapt British legendry — Arthurian myth for *Camelot*, the Robin Hood tradition for *Longbow* — into Sierra adventure-game form (SCI0 for *Camelot*, SCI1 for *Longbow*), aiming, as Hardcore Gaming 101 puts it, at "more faithful, scholarly retellings of these stories" than the mash-up of "medieval lore, mythology and fairy tales" in Sierra's own *King's Quest*.[^ref-12][^ref-9] The artwork for both was provided by Marx's husband, Peter Ledger.[^ref-12]

Marx came to games from a career writing television animation, with credits including *G.I. Joe*, *Spider-Man and His Amazing Friends*, and *Jem and the Holograms* (which she developed and head-wrote).[^ref-3] *Longbow* in particular is built around branching outcomes: how the player performs over its days determines which of four endings Robin receives.[^ref-8]

The series produced exactly two games. Marx left Sierra after *Longbow* to return to writing for non-interactive media,[^ref-15] and the franchise was not continued by other designers. She later said that, "Had I remained at Sierra, the direction I was thinking about for a third game was Conquests of Charlemagne."[^ref-4]

## Conquests of Camelot: The Search for the Grail (1990)

Christy Marx's first major Sierra design. The player is **King Arthur**, who must lead a personal quest to find his missing knights (Galahad, Lancelot, Gawain) and ultimately retrieve the Holy Grail. The quest crosses England and then follows Galahad's trail to Gaza and Jerusalem, ending at the ancient Temple of Aphrodite, where the goddess directs Arthur to the Grail.[^ref-5]

**Design features:**
- **Action sequences** — including a joust with the Black Knight and a fight with a pack of boars.[^ref-12]
- **Manual-based riddles** — the illustrated manual, *Liber Ex Doctrina*, covers the evolution of the Arthurian and Grail myths and Greek and Roman mythology, and some of that information is needed to answer riddles in the game.[^ref-5]
- **Three-part scoring** — Skill, Wisdom and Soul points, the last awarded for good deeds that help others.[^ref-5]
- **Medieval-style music** — a soundtrack of "authentic-sounding medieval music" composed by Mark Seibert.[^ref-5]
- **High research density** — Marx "extensively researched both the mythology and the history of early Britain";[^ref-6] the manual's bibliography lists works by Charles Squire, Sir James George Frazer, Sir James Knowles, Barbara G. Walker, John Matthews & Bob Stewart and John M. Allegro, and recommends Mary Stewart's Arthurian trilogy.[^ref-16]

**Reception:** Generally positive. *Games International* gave it 9/10 and *Dragon* 4½/5, and *VideoGames & Computer Entertainment* named it "Best Computer Adventure Game of 1990" (per Marx's own credits page).[^ref-1][^ref-3] *Computer Gaming World*'s Scorpia was cooler: she found the EGA graphics inferior to earlier Sierra games and the puzzles too easy, calling it "a game more for the beginning adventure gamer, rather than the experienced one."[^ref-7]

## Conquests of the Longbow: The Legend of Robin Hood (1991)

Marx's second Conquests entry and the more widely celebrated of the two. The player is Robin Hood, leading the Sherwood Forest outlaws during King Richard's absence on Crusade. Gameplay is divided into days, each with tasks that must be completed before advancing; leaving vital tasks undone, along with the ransom money raised and the number of outlaws still alive, determines which of four endings the player receives.[^ref-8]

**Design features:**
- **Day-by-day structure** — the story unfolds over a series of in-game days, each closing with a cutscene of Robin and his men discussing events.[^ref-8]
- **Four endings** — ranging from Robin being hanged to being granted a noble title and Maid Marian's hand.[^ref-8]
- **Folklore beyond Hollywood** — the cast includes the Green Man, a powerful forest spirit whose trust Robin must win, and Maid Marian as a pagan forest priestess.[^ref-8]
- **Disguise and infiltration** — much of the game is spent "finding new disguises" and "infiltrating the towns, castles and abbeys for vital information";[^ref-9] disguises include a beggar's rags, a Fen Monk's and a monk of St. Mary's robes, and a yeoman's clothes.[^ref-17]
- **Nine Men's Morris** — the only mandatory minigame, an ancient strategy board game.[^ref-9]

**Reception:** *Dragon* gave it 5 out of 5 stars in March 1992.[^ref-2] *Computer Gaming World* did not score it, but in its November 1992 Game of the Year awards nominated it as one of four Adventure Game of the Year finalists, praising "its attention to historical detail (including the music) and number of puzzles"; the award went to *Monkey Island 2*.[^ref-10] It was Sierra's biggest seller in spring 1992,[^ref-15] and Adventure Gamers later ranked it the 42nd-best adventure game of all time (2011).[^ref-2]

## Series Design Identity

What unifies the two games:

1. **Designer-as-historian approach** — Marx's research-heavy methodology distinguished the series from Sierra's more comedic or pulp adventures.[^ref-12]
2. **Rewarding conduct** — *Camelot* awards "Soul" points for helping others, and *Longbow*'s ending depends on how well the player performs.[^ref-5][^ref-8]
3. **Period-flavoured music** — both scores were singled out for their medieval atmosphere.[^ref-5][^ref-10]
4. **Legendry-faithful storytelling** — drawing on folklore and scholarship rather than Hollywood adaptations.[^ref-12]

## Legacy

The Conquests series occupies a distinctive niche in Sierra's catalog — Marx's research-driven approach distinguishes it from the broader adventure-game humor and fantasy tradition.[^ref-12]

Both games are available on GOG.com individually.[^ref-13][^ref-14]

Christy Marx left Sierra after *Longbow* and returned to writing for non-interactive media.[^ref-15]

No Conquests revival has been announced; the IP sits with Activision Blizzard / Microsoft.
