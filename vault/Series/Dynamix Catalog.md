# Dynamix Catalog

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Dynamix catalog collects the games made by **Dynamix, Inc.**, the developer that [[Jeff Tunnell]] and [[Damon Slye]] founded in Eugene, Oregon, in 1984.[^ref-1][^ref-2] Sierra On-Line bought the studio in 1990 and closed it on August 14, 2001.[^ref-1][^ref-2] MobyGames lists 112 games released under the Dynamix name between 1985 and 2009, a count that includes later compilations such as the 2009 *Red Baron Pack*.[^ref-2]

Before Sierra, Dynamix made games for Penguin Software, Electronic Arts, Activision and other publishers.[^ref-27][^ref-1] Under Sierra it became, in Retro Invaders' words, "one of their largest development teams".[^ref-27] Its work there covered flight simulation (*Red Baron*, the Aces games), adventure (*Rise of the Dragon*, *Heart of China*, *The Adventures of Willy Beamish*, *Betrayal at Krondor*), puzzles (*The Incredible Machine*), sports (Front Page Sports) and mech combat (Metaltech, Earthsiege, Starsiege), which led to the *Tribes* online shooters.[^ref-1][^ref-28]

Because Dynamix made so many unrelated games, this page indexes its output as a **catalog** rather than a single series. Years and credits below follow each game's SierraVault page. For the studio's full history, see [[Dynamix]].

## Studio History

### Founding and pre-Sierra (1984–1990)

Tunnell and Slye first called the company Software Entertainment Company and renamed it Dynamix in 1984; Kevin Ryan and Richard Hicks then joined as co-owner partners.[^ref-2] Dynamix decided to publish its own games, but the 1989 Activision affiliated-label releases did not make enough money to keep the company going.[^ref-2] On March 27, 1990, the owners agreed to sell to Sierra On-Line for $1.5 million.[^ref-18] MobyGames gives August 1990 for the sale and says Sierra bought the company for its know-how in simulations, genres in which Sierra was weak.[^ref-2]

### The Sierra years (1990–1996)

After the buyout the studio grew from around 30 people to more than 100 by 1993.[^ref-2] Tunnell left after *Willy Beamish* to run Jeff Tunnell Productions and returned to Dynamix in 1995.[^ref-2][^ref-7] Slye left after *Aces Over Europe*, "in 1993 or 1994", by his own account.[^ref-28]

### CUC, Havas and closure (1996–2001)

Shortly after Tunnell's return, Ken Williams sold Sierra to CUC International in 1996.[^ref-2] CUC merged with HFS to form Cendant in December 1997, and on November 20, 1998, Cendant announced the sale of its consumer software division, Sierra included, to Havas; Havas was renamed Vivendi Universal Publishing in 2000.[^ref-23] A Sierra restructuring in September 1999 "resulted in the end of Dynamix as a brand and a separate business entity".[^ref-2] Sierra closed the studio on August 14, 2001; GameSpot reported that the closure "includes lay-offs for 97 Dynamix employees".[^ref-22][^ref-1]

### Legacy

Several Dynamix veterans, Tunnell among them, stayed in Eugene and founded GarageGames, which licensed the Tribes 2 engine from Sierra and turned it into the Torque Game Engine.[^ref-1] Wikipedia lists GarageGames' founders as Jeff Tunnell, Tim Gift, Rick Overman and Mark Frohnmayer, and dates its founding to 2000.[^ref-8]

## Critical Reception

- *Red Baron* (1990) won Computer Gaming World's 1991 Simulation of the Year award and was named to the magazine's Hall of Fame two years later.[^ref-19]
- *Betrayal at Krondor* (1993) won Computer Gaming World's Role-Playing Game of the Year award in June 1994.[^ref-21]
- *Starsiege: Tribes* (1998) won "Best Online Game of the Year" at IGN's Best of 1998 Awards.[^ref-4]
