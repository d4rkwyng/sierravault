# Hoyle Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

*Hoyle's Official Book of Games* (later *Hoyle Classic*, *Hoyle Card Games* or simply the Hoyle series) is a series of computer games released from 1989 to 2016, initially developed and published by Sierra On-Line. It focuses on playing cards, but has also included board games, puzzles, dice and dominoes.[^ref-1] The series is named after the 18th-century card expert Edmond Hoyle, author of *A Short Treatise on the Game of Whist* (1742),[^ref-2] and some titles also used trademarks and designs from the Hoyle brand of playing cards under licence from Brown & Bigelow.[^ref-1]

The original concept was submitted to Ken Williams by [[Warren Schwader]], On-Line Systems' first hired programmer, who had returned to Sierra in March 1988 to work with its new SCI language.[^ref-1][^ref-3] Schwader designed and programmed Volume 1 (1989) and Volume 2 (1990), led programming on Volume 3 (1991), and designed *Hoyle Classic Card Games* (1993), which Sierra Chest and Wikipedia number as Hoyle 4.[^ref-1][^ref-4][^ref-7] Early games had volume numbers and included humorous computer opponents drawn from other Sierra series.[^ref-1]

By the mid-1990s new versions were published yearly, with some variants for particular types of games, and the series spawned the *Hoyle Casino* spin-off in 1996.[^ref-1][^ref-17] In December 2004 VU Games assigned the Hoyle games licence to Encore, which published the series from 2005 to 2016.[^ref-18][^ref-1]

## Founding Innovations (1989–1993)

1. **Sierra-character opponents.** Volume 1 offered six card games (Crazy Eights, Old Maid, Hearts, Gin Rummy, Cribbage and Klondike) against 18 computer opponents, including characters from other Sierra games (Colonel Henri Dijon, Officer Sonny Bonds, Larry Laffer, King Graham, Roger Wilco and Princess Rosella) and Sierra employees such as Schwader himself.[^ref-6] *Computer Gaming World* found the humour of "interacting with the familiar Sierra characters" the best part of the game for most people.[^ref-7]
2. **Rule variants.** Volume 2 (1990) collected 28 solitaire games.[^ref-8]
3. **AI characterization.** SCI had been built for Sierra's adventure games, and implementing card games and AI for the characters in it "proved challenging".[^ref-1]

Sierra reported in its Fall 1990 newsletter that Volume 1 had sold more than 250,000 copies worldwide.[^ref-8]

## Commercial History

After the 1996 sale of [[Sierra On-Line]] (see [[Corporate Lineage]]) the series continued under the Sierra label, with yearly releases.[^ref-1] On December 21, 2004, VU Games announced the assignment of the Hoyle games licence to [[Encore Software]], effective December 23, 2004.[^ref-18] Encore (MobyGames: "Encore, Inc.", also known as Encore Software, Inc.)[^ref-11] published Hoyle titles from 2005; the *Hoyle Casino* line continued through *Hoyle Official Casino Games Collection* in 2016, which Steam lists under Encore with an October 14, 2016 release.[^ref-17][^ref-13]

## Critical Reception

*Computer Gaming World*'s January 1990 review of Volume 1 (issue #67, p. 48, Michael S. Lasky) was mixed to negative. It enjoyed the Sierra-character table talk but found the game slow ("incredible plodding speed"), noted that its Cribbage scoring departed from Hoyle's rules, and said the other games "are really children's games and offer no great excitement". It recommended Software Toolworks' Cribbage King/Gin King to serious Cribbage and Gin players.[^ref-7]

## Legacy

Hoyle ran for 27 years, from Volume 1 in 1989 to the 2016 *Hoyle Official Casino Games Collection*, the last two decades under first Sierra's later owners and then Encore.[^ref-1][^ref-18] Encore later continued with its own *Encore Classic Games* series from 2020.[^ref-1]

For more on the founding era, see the [[Warren Schwader]] designer page; for the engine, see [[Sierra Creative Interpreter]].
