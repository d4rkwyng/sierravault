# 3D Ultra Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 16, 2026</small>

## Overview

The 3D Ultra series is Sierra's casual-game line of pinball, mini-golf, model-train, radio-control racing and pool games. MobyGames groups the "Games by Sierra On-Line/Dynamix bearing the 3D-Ultra label", 23 items from 1995 to 2002 counting compilations and re-releases.[^ref-1] SierraVault has pages for 16 games from 1995 to 2010, plus the 1999 *3-D Ultra Pinball: Power!* compilation. The game pages credit the 1995–2000 games to [[Dynamix]] and the 2006–2010 mini-golf revival to Wanako Games.[^ref-18]

[[Jeff Tunnell]] created the 3-D Ultra Pinball series at Jeff Tunnell Productions, the studio he ran apart from Dynamix before returning to it in 1995.[^ref-2][^ref-11] MobyGames says JTP's last titles, *3-D Ultra Pinball* and *The Incredible Machine 3.0*, came out in 1995 "before Dynamix took over later that year".[^ref-11] Jimmy Maher writes that *Trophy Bass* and *3-D Ultra Pinball* "became the two best-selling games Dynamix would ever release".[^ref-6] The pinball series sold more than half a million copies by 1998.[^ref-16]

## Subseries

### 3-D Ultra Pinball (1995–2000)

Five pinball games: *3-D Ultra Pinball* (1995), *Creep Night* (1996), *The Lost Continent* (1997), *NASCAR Pinball* (1998) and *Thrillride* (2000), which Wikipedia calls "the final title of the series".[^ref-16] *Power!* (1999) was "a compilation" of the first game, *Creep Night* and *Lost Continent*, with *The Incredible Machine* and a demo as a bonus.[^ref-16] *NASCAR Pinball*, the fourth game in the pinball series, has a NASCAR theme and multimedia information about four NASCAR tracks; in Europe it was titled *3-D Ultra Pinball: Turbo Racing*.[^ref-3]

### 3D Ultra MiniGolf (1997–2010)

*3-D Ultra Minigolf*, designed by Dynamix and published by Sierra, was released in May 1997.[^ref-18] The later *Adventures* games were developed by Wanako Games.[^ref-18] *3D Ultra Minigolf Adventures* came out for Windows on December 20, 2006 and for Xbox 360 on April 18, 2007; the Xbox 360 version sold 11,049 units by January 2011 and 34,413 by the end of 2011.[^ref-4] Its sequel, *3D Ultra Minigolf Adventures 2* (2010), was released by Konami Digital Entertainment.[^ref-18]

### 3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers (1999–2000)

*Radio Control Racers* (1999) and *Radio Control Racers Deluxe: Traxxas Edition* (2000).[^ref-1]

### 3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown (1999–2000)

*3D Ultra Lionel Traintown* (1999) is a railroading game under the Sierra Attractions casual brand, "licensed by Lionel, LLC"; a *Deluxe* edition followed in 2000.[^ref-13]

### 3-D Ultra Cool Pool / Maximum Pool (1999–2000)

*Maximum Pool* (2000), developed by Dynamix and published under Sierra's Sierra Sports label for Windows and Dreamcast, is "the tenth game in the 3D Ultra series, and the successor to the 1999 game Cool Pool".[^ref-17]

## Studio Lineage

Tunnell left Dynamix to start Jeff Tunnell Productions and returned to Dynamix in 1995.[^ref-2] MobyGames dates JTP's founding to 1992 and describes it as a one-man developer.[^ref-11] MobyGames groups the 1995–2002 releases as Sierra On-Line/Dynamix titles.[^ref-1] Tunnell co-founded GarageGames in Eugene in 2000.[^ref-5] Wanako Games developed the three *Adventures* games.[^ref-18][^ref-4]
