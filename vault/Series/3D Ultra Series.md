# 3D Ultra Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

The 3D Ultra series is Sierra's casual-game line of pinball, mini-golf, model-train, radio-control racing and pool games. MobyGames groups the "Games by Sierra On-Line/Dynamix bearing the 3D-Ultra label", 23 items from 1995 to 2002 counting compilations and re-releases.[^ref-1] SierraVault has pages for 16 games from 1995 to 2010, plus the 1999 *3-D Ultra Pinball: Power!* compilation. The game pages credit the 1995–2000 games to [[Dynamix]], and several of them (*MiniGolf*, *Power!*, *Traxxas Edition*, *Cool Pool* and *Maximum Pool*) also credit or note [[Jeff Tunnell Productions]]. They credit the 2006–2010 mini-golf revival to Wanako Games.[^ref-18]

[[Jeff Tunnell]] created the 3-D Ultra Pinball series at Jeff Tunnell Productions, the studio he ran apart from Dynamix before returning to it in 1995.[^ref-2][^ref-11] MobyGames says JTP's last titles, *3-D Ultra Pinball* and *The Incredible Machine 3.0*, came out in 1995 "before Dynamix took over later that year".[^ref-11] Jimmy Maher writes that *Trophy Bass* and *3-D Ultra Pinball* "became the two best-selling games Dynamix would ever release".[^ref-6] The pinball series sold more than half a million copies by 1998.[^ref-16]

## Subseries

### 3-D Ultra Pinball (1995–2000)

Five pinball games: *3-D Ultra Pinball* (1995), *Creep Night* (1996), *The Lost Continent* (1997), *NASCAR Pinball* (1998) and *Thrillride* (2000), which Wikipedia calls "the final title of the series"; its Game Boy Color version was developed by Left Field Productions.[^ref-16] *Power!* (1999) was "a compilation" of the first game, *Creep Night* and *Lost Continent*, with *The Incredible Machine* and a demo as a bonus.[^ref-16] *NASCAR Pinball*, the fourth game in the pinball series, has a NASCAR theme and multimedia information about four NASCAR tracks; in Europe it was titled *3-D Ultra Pinball: Turbo Racing*.[^ref-3]

### 3D Ultra MiniGolf (1997–2010)

*3-D Ultra Minigolf*, designed by Dynamix and published by Sierra, was released in May 1997.[^ref-18] *3-D Ultra Minigolf Deluxe* followed from Dynamix in July 1998, adding nine new holes to the original eighteen.[^ref-18] Dynamix's May 1998 announcement called it "the first computer miniature golf game to offer a multiplayer option over LAN, modem or the Internet".[^ref-21] The later *Adventures* games were developed by Wanako Games.[^ref-18] *3D Ultra Minigolf Adventures* came out for Windows on December 20, 2006 and for Xbox 360 on April 18, 2007; the Xbox 360 version sold 11,049 units by January 2011 and 34,413 by the end of 2011.[^ref-4] Its sequel, *3D Ultra Minigolf Adventures 2* (2010), was released by Konami Digital Entertainment.[^ref-18]

### 3-D Ultra Radio Control Racers (1999–2000)

*Radio Control Racers* (1999) and *Radio Control Racers Deluxe: Traxxas Edition* (2000).[^ref-1]

### 3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown (1999–2000)

*3D Ultra Lionel Traintown* (1999) is a railroading game under the Sierra Attractions casual brand, "licensed by Lionel, LLC"; a *Deluxe* edition followed in 2000.[^ref-13]

### 3-D Ultra Cool Pool / Maximum Pool (1999–2000)

*Maximum Pool* (2000), developed by Dynamix and published under Sierra's Sierra Sports label for Windows and Dreamcast, is "the tenth game in the 3D Ultra series, and the successor to the 1999 game Cool Pool".[^ref-17] MobyGames also lists a Macintosh release on September 14, 2000, the same day as Windows.[^ref-15]

## Studio Lineage

Tunnell left Dynamix to start Jeff Tunnell Productions and returned to Dynamix in 1995.[^ref-2] MobyGames dates JTP's founding to 1992 and describes it as a one-man developer.[^ref-11] MobyGames groups the 1995–2002 releases as Sierra On-Line/Dynamix titles.[^ref-1] Tunnell co-founded GarageGames in Eugene in 2000.[^ref-5] Wanako Games developed the three *Adventures* games.[^ref-18][^ref-4]

## Critical Reception

Reception was uneven across the line. Contemporary reviews of the first pinball game were warm, the 1997 mini-golf game was panned, the Lionel train game drew a high online score and an industry award, and the Wanako-developed revival was received as mediocre. These notes summarise the series' game pages and carry over their sources.

| Game | Notable Reception |
|------|-------------------|
| 3-D Ultra Pinball (1995) | Arinn Dembo, in *Computer Gaming World*'s "Blazing Paddles" pinball survey (issue 138, January 1996), called the three-in-one table formation "something I've never seen before" and judged the game "recommended more for newcomers than old pros, but for those who want a pinball trainer, this game is the best I've seen"; she also found the shots and combinations "extremely easy to make".[^ref-23] |
| 3-D Ultra MiniGolf (1997) | GameSpot's Jeff Sengstack scored it 4.3 ("poor") and wrote of its "unrealized potential".[^ref-24] |
| 3-D Ultra Lionel TrainTown (1999) | Gamezilla's Christine and Mike Messersmith gave it 89 in a review posted March 27, 2000.[^ref-25] It won Family Entertainment Title of the Year at the 3rd Annual Interactive Achievement Awards; the Academy's own PC list marks it as the winner,[^ref-26] and Wikipedia records the same win.[^ref-13] |
| 3D Ultra MiniGolf Adventures (2006) | IGN scored the Xbox 360 version 7.5/10, calling its holes "a 10-year-old course designer's dream" while noting that "the single-player options are very thin".[^ref-27] GameSpot's Aaron Thomas gave it 5.5/10 ("Mediocre"), calling it "an unremarkable game that fails to capture the fun of real miniature golf".[^ref-28] Eurogamer's Dan Whitehead scored it 5/10 and called it "a curiously inert affair".[^ref-29] Wikipedia lists Metacritic aggregates of 66/100 (Xbox 360) and 35/100 (PC).[^ref-4] |
| 3D Ultra MiniGolf Adventures 2 (2010) | Metacritic showed no Metascore ("tbd") with four critic reviews and a user score of 6.1 from seven ratings.[^ref-31] |

## Legacy

The pinball games were the line's commercial peak: Wikipedia reports that the series sold more than half a million copies by 1998,[^ref-16] and Jimmy Maher writes that *Trophy Bass* and *3-D Ultra Pinball* "became the two best-selling games Dynamix would ever release".[^ref-6]

The 2006 *Adventures* game had a long gestation. The Sierra Chest records that it was originally created in 2000 as a sequel to *3D Ultra Minigolf Deluxe*, titled *Mini Golf Maniacs* (Windows and PlayStation 2); when the Dynamix game division in charge of it was shut down in 2001 it went unreleased for five years, until Wanako completed its development in 2006.[^ref-30] The Xbox 360 version of *Adventures* sold 11,049 units by January 2011.[^ref-4]

## Playing Today

### Purchase Links

- [Steam: 3D Ultra Minigolf Adventures](https://store.steampowered.com/app/10140/) — $9.99, checked November 11, 2026[^ref-22]

The Steam page is published by Activision and developed by Wanako Games, and describes 36 holes on three themed courses. It shows an original release date of December 20, 2006, while Steam's store API gives a Steam release date of July 23, 2009. Steam's listing is titled *3D Ultra Minigolf Adventures*, without "Deluxe"; the Sierra Chest describes the *Deluxe* edition as combining those 36 holes with the 18-hole *Lost Island* expansion for 54 holes.[^ref-22][^ref-30]

A query of GOG's catalog on November 11, 2026 for "3D Ultra" returned no 3D Ultra title, so no GOG listing was found for any series entry. No other series title was checked on Steam beyond this listing.
