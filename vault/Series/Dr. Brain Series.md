# Dr. Brain Series

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Dr. Brain series is an educational-puzzle franchise of 8 titles released between 1991 and 2011, built around a series of puzzles the player solves to aid characters named Dr. Brain, who changed from title to title (the first two Sierra games star the elderly absent-minded professor Dr. Thaddeus Egghead Brain).[^ref-1] The series was founded by **[[Corey Cole]]** (one half of the husband-wife team behind [[Quest for Glory Series|Quest for Glory]]) in 1991 with [[1991 - Castle of Dr. Brain|Castle of Dr. Brain]].[^ref-17][^ref-11] Sierra's 1996 annual report said the series "has been among the most successful of its kind at presenting children with pure problem-solving skills" and that the company had "sold more than 350,000 copies in this series".[^ref-18]

CUC International completed its purchase of Sierra in July 1996.[^ref-19] It announced a deal to buy [[Knowledge Adventure]] in November 1996 and completed it on January 31, 1997; Knowledge Adventure became part of the CUC Software division alongside Davidson, Blizzard Entertainment and Sierra On-Line, a sister company to Sierra rather than part of it.[^ref-20][^ref-21] Knowledge Adventure went on to make the later Dr. Brain games, and [[2011 - JumpStart Advanced 3rd-5th Grade - Adventures of Dr. Brain|2011's JumpStart Advanced]] entry is the most recent franchise appearance.[^ref-10]

## Castle of Dr. Brain (1991)

Corey Cole designed it.[^ref-17] The player has answered Dr. Brain's advertisement for a laboratory assistant, and the test is to get through his puzzle-filled castle.[^ref-4] Cole grouped the puzzles by subject into rooms: first three math problems, then three puzzles dealing with time, and later a word-puzzle room, a robotics-and-computers room and an astronomy room, "24 multifarious puzzles in all".[^ref-11] Some are variations on old favourites such as Mastermind, Hangman, and Knights and Knaves, and one requires the player to solve a cryptogram.[^ref-11][^ref-4]

Cole implemented three difficulty levels, which the player can change at any time.[^ref-11] Jimmy Maher writes that the game "became by far the most commercially successful of all the Discovery Series"; Cole estimates it sold over 100,000 copies at full price and another 150,000 as a budget re-release.[^ref-11]

## The Island of Dr. Brain (1992)

The sequel, designed by Patrick Bridgemon, sends the player, now Dr. Brain's assistant, to his private island to retrieve a battery, solving puzzles on the way to the island's centre.[^ref-11] Maher notes that it takes in foreign languages, musical notation, literature and the visual arts, and that its "lowlight is definitely the supremely tedious" Tower of Hanoi puzzle.[^ref-11]

## The Lost Mind of Dr. Brain (1995)

Developed by [[Bright Star Technology]], which took over the series after the second game, beginning with *Lost Mind*.[^ref-1][^ref-11] An experiment has mixed up the minds of Dr. Brain and his lab rat Rathbone, and the player solves puzzles to put him right.[^ref-7] It won the Software Publishers Association (SPA) award for Best Home Learning Game for Adolescents; Wikipedia dates the award to 1995.[^ref-7][^ref-23]

## The Time Warp of Dr. Brain (1996)

Dr. Brain is trapped in the "space time discontinuum", and the player saves him by completing puzzles at novice, expert or genius difficulty.[^ref-8] Its ten puzzle worlds, set at points along a timeline from prehistory to the far future, are Primordial Soup, Spelunking, Lizards and Eggs, Beaver Dam, Caveman Rock, Monkey See Monkey Do, Alchemy, Gridlock, Spaceshop and Brain Waves.[^ref-29][^ref-30] Who made *Time Warp* is less settled: Wikipedia's series article and Jimmy Maher place the later games with Bright Star, while Wikipedia's *Time Warp* article credits Sierra On-Line.[^ref-1][^ref-11][^ref-28]

## Knowledge Adventure-era entries (1998-2011)

CUC bought Sierra in July 1996, and its purchase of Knowledge Adventure, announced in November 1996, was completed on January 31, 1997.[^ref-19][^ref-21][^ref-20] With both companies inside CUC Software, the Dr. Brain franchise passed from Sierra to Knowledge Adventure. Its first two games, *Puzzle Madness* (October 1998) and *IQ Adventure* (1998), carried the "Dr. Brain Thinking Games" title.[^ref-22][^ref-9]

*Action Reaction* (1999) was the final dedicated-franchise entry. Dr. Brain was later carried under Knowledge Adventure's *JumpStart* brand, which hosted the *Adventures of Dr. Brain* game manual on its site.[^ref-10]

## Legacy

In its annual report for the fiscal year ended March 31, 1996, Sierra said it had "sold more than 350,000 copies in this series".[^ref-18] *Castle of Dr. Brain* was the best seller of Sierra's Discovery Series.[^ref-11]

No Dr. Brain game has been released since the 2011 JumpStart entry.
