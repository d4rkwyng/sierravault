# Bibliography

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

This is the canonical list of research sources the SierraVault archive draws on. It exists for two reasons: to give editors a single place to look when they're trying to verify a fact, and to make the archive's citation patterns auditable. Entries are grouped by tier, and within each tier they're sorted by approximate importance to the Sierra archive specifically.

For URL-by-URL specifics on a given game page, look at that page's References section. This page is the upstream index — what kinds of sources to trust and where to start.

---

## Tier 1 — Primary / Official sources

These are first-party publishers, employees, and contemporary primary documents.

### Books and memoirs

Ken Williams's memoir is a primary source; Kent, Levy and Donovan are secondary histories, listed here for convenience.


- **Ken Williams, *Not All Fairy Tales Have Happy Endings: The Rise and Fall of Sierra On-Line*** (Lulu.com, July 24, 2020; ISBN 978-1-71672-736-8). Self-published memoir by Sierra's co-founder covering founding through the CUC sale. The single most important primary source for the company's business history.[^ref-kw-book]
- **Steven L. Kent, *The Ultimate History of Video Games*** (Three Rivers Press, 2001; first published by Prima Publishing, 2001; ISBN 0-7615-3643-4). Industry-wide survey; Sierra appears only in passing (no Roberta Williams or Al Lowe interviews).[^ref-kent]
- **Steven Levy, *Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution*** (Doubleday, 1984). Documents early On-Line Systems and the broader Apple II hobbyist context.[^ref-levy]
- **Tristan Donovan, *Replay: The History of Video Games*** (Yellow Ant, 2010). Industry survey with adventure-game-genre coverage.[^ref-donovan]

### Official company pages

- **[guysfromandromeda.com](https://www.guysfromandromeda.com)** — Mark Crowe and Scott Murphy's official site; primary source on Space Quest and their post-Sierra projects, including SpaceVenture.[^ref-gfa]
- **[allowe.com](https://www.allowe.com)** — Al Lowe's personal site, including Sierra history pages, Leisure Suit Larry inside stories and design material, music downloads, and a list of past interviews.[^ref-allowe]
- **[Phoenix Online Studios news](https://www.postudios.com/company/)** — Source on *The Silver Lining* fan project and the studio's other games.[^ref-postudios]
- **[Pinkerton Road](http://web.archive.org/web/20190531105728/http://pinkertonroad.com:80/)** — Jane Jensen and Robert Holmes's studio site; primary on Gabriel Knight 20th Anniversary and Moebius.[^ref-pinkerton]
- **[AGD Interactive](https://www.agdinteractive.com)** — AGDI's hub for its King's Quest and Quest for Glory II remakes and its original game *Mage's Initiation: Reign of the Elements*.[^ref-agdi]
- **[Hero-U](https://www.hero-u.com)** — Lori and Corey Cole's studio site, primary on Hero-U and Summer Daze.[^ref-herou]

### Contemporary press archives

- **[Computer Gaming World Museum](https://www.cgwmuseum.org)** — Scanned issues of CGW from 1981–2006; the single best contemporary review source for 1980s–1990s Sierra titles.[^ref-cgwmuseum]
- **[Sierra Newsletter / InterAction Magazine PDFs at MoCagh.org](http://www.mocagh.org/sierra/)** — Scanned Sierra in-house newsletters, a partial run (about a dozen issues of Sierra News and InterAction); primary source for first-party announcements 1988–1996. The live site may refuse automated requests; a [Wayback copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20231111110630/https://www.mocagh.org/sierra/) lists the files.[^ref-mocagh]
- **[Archive.org Computer & Video Games magazine collection](https://archive.org/details/cvg-magazine)** — Scanned UK CVG, issues 1–126 (November 1981 – May 1992) plus yearbooks; covers PAL releases and EU reception.[^ref-cvgarchive]

### Press releases and corporate filings

- **[SEC EDGAR — Sierra On-Line filings](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0000724991)** — Sierra On Line Inc (CIK 0000724991): 10-K, 10-Q, S-3 and other filings dated 1994–1997, through the CUC acquisition. EDGAR holds no filings before 1994.[^ref-sec]
- **Activision Blizzard and Cendant 10-K filings; Vivendi 20-F filings** — Post-acquisition Sierra is a line item rather than a standalone entity, but the parent filings document brand strategy.[^ref-parentsec]

## Tier 2 — Curated databases

These are systematic, well-maintained third-party catalogs of game metadata.

- **[MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com)** — Most comprehensive game-credits database. Sierra's company page (`/company/17/sierra-entertainment-inc/`) was credited on 634 games from 1980 to 2017 as of a June 16, 2026 Wayback snapshot. The website now walls automated fetches, so SierraVault reads MobyGames data through the MobyGames API. Cite for: credits, platforms, release dates, screenshots, contemporary box-art.[^ref-mobygames]
- **[The Sierra Chest (sierrachest.com)](https://www.sierrachest.com)** — Fan-maintained Sierra-specific catalog with deeper coverage than MobyGames on box art, music, demos, manuals, version history. Cite for: collector edition contents, box art, asset preservation.[^ref-sierrachest]
- **[PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com)** — Compatibility, fixes, technical specifications, modern-system installation guides. Cite for: technical specifications, modern-system compatibility issues.[^ref-pcgw]
- **[IGDB](https://www.igdb.com)** — Game metadata, ratings, release history. Less authoritative than MobyGames for credits but useful for cross-reference.[^ref-igdb]
- **[GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com)** — User-contributed FAQs, walkthroughs, hint maps. Cite for: gameplay specifics, walkthrough quotes.[^ref-gamefaqs]
- **[StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org)** — Walkthroughs and strategy guides; complements GameFAQs.[^ref-strategywiki]
- **[Internet Archive — DOS games](https://archive.org/details/softwarelibrary_msdos_games)** — Browser-playable preservation of many original Sierra DOS releases. Cite for: confirming a specific version's behavior or existence.[^ref-iadosgames]

## Tier 3 — Long-form journalism and historical analysis

The most important secondary sources — extended retrospectives, oral histories, and academic-grade analyses.

- **[The Digital Antiquarian (filfre.net)](https://www.filfre.net)** — Jimmy Maher's gold-standard long-form history of the adventure-game era. Multi-part Sierra coverage including the Williams' founding, AGI development, every flagship series. Cite for: business history, design context, era atmosphere.[^ref-filfre]
- **[Hardcore Gaming 101](http://www.hardcoregaming101.net)** — Encyclopedic series articles on Sierra franchises (King's Quest, Space Quest, Quest for Glory, etc.). Cite for: gameplay analysis, version comparison, modern reception. (Site in maintenance as of October 2026; use [Wayback copies](http://web.archive.org/web/20240102000204/http://www.hardcoregaming101.net/).)[^ref-hg101]
- **Adventure Gamers** — Genre-specialist review site with a large archive of Sierra adventure reviews. (Note: the site was sold in 2025 and its content wiped; cite only Wayback snapshots dated on or before June 10, 2025.)[^ref-advgamers]
- **[Adventure Game Hotspot](https://adventuregamehotspot.com)** — Founded in 2022 by former Adventure Gamers editor Jack Allin. Hosts new reviews, interviews, retrospectives.[^ref-aghotspot]
- **[Sierra Gamers (sierragamers.com)](https://www.sierragamers.com)** — Site maintained by Ken Williams, with designer profiles, game pages, photos and an InterAction magazine archive.[^ref-sierragamers]
- **[SpaceQuest.net](https://spacequest.net)** — Comprehensive fan site for Space Quest including detailed easter eggs, cameos, plot inconsistencies, and the cancelled Space Quest 7 documentation. Subpages are organized per game, for example [`/sq1/eastereggs/`](https://spacequest.net/sq1/eastereggs/), [`/sq1/funfacts/`](https://spacequest.net/sq1/funfacts/), [`/sq1/cameos/`](https://spacequest.net/sq1/cameos/), [`/sq1/plotinconsis/`](https://spacequest.net/sq1/plotinconsis/) and [`/sq3/spoofref/`](https://spacequest.net/sq3/spoofref/); the cancelled Space Quest 7 material is at [`/sq7/`](https://spacequest.net/sq7/).[^ref-spacequestnet]
- **[Halcyon Days interviews (dadgum.com/halcyon/)](https://dadgum.com/halcyon/)** — James Hague's collection of interviews with classic computer and video game programmers, released in March 1997; the Warren Schwader interview is a key source on Sierra's first-hire era.[^ref-halcyon]
- **[Polygon](https://www.polygon.com), [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com), [Rock Paper Shotgun](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com), [IGN](https://www.ign.com)** — Mainstream games journalism; cite for: contemporary reviews, retrospective analyses, corporate-news coverage.

### Academic / Museum sources

- **[The Strong National Museum of Play — Sierra collection](https://www.museumofplay.org)** — Inducted King's Quest into the World Video Game Hall of Fame in 2020 (the only Sierra-published inductee to date; Half-Life was a finalist in 2018 and 2019).[^ref-strong] Its Brian Sutton-Smith Library and Archives of Play holds two Sierra archives: [Ken and Roberta Williams' Sierra On-Line Collection, 1979–1999](https://www.museumofplay.org/finding-aid-to-ken-and-roberta-williams-sierra-on-line-collection_062119/)[^ref-strong-kw] and the [Laine Nooney Sierra On-Line Collection, 1983–1993, 2015](https://www.museumofplay.org/finding-aid-to-the-laine-nooney-sierra-on-line-collection_restrictions-added_052316/).[^ref-strong-nooney]
- **[GDC Vault — Sierra talks](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024910/Beneath-the-Flesh-of-Walls)** — Laine Nooney's GDC 2018 talk *Beneath the Flesh of Walls: The Making of Sierra's 'Mystery House'*. (GDC Vault was redirecting to a maintenance page in October 2026; [Wayback copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20240112152808/https://gdcvault.com/play/1024910/Beneath-the-Flesh-of-Walls).)[^ref-gdc]
- **[[Sierra Documentary - Legends of Adventure]]** — The vault's page on the in-production Sierra documentary.
- **[MIT Lemelson — Roberta Williams](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/roberta-williams)** — Inventor-profile entry.[^ref-mitlemelson]
- **[IEEE Spectrum — Meet Roberta Williams](https://spectrum.ieee.org/meet-roberta-williams-the-queen-of-graphic-adventure-video-games)** — Joanna Goodrich's 2020 profile of Roberta Williams.[^ref-ieee]

## Tier 4 — Fan wikis and community knowledge bases

Useful for breadth and trivia, but cross-check facts against tier-1/2 sources before citing as primary authority.

- **[King's Quest Omnipedia](https://kingsquest.fandom.com)** — Detailed plot, character, location lore for the King's Quest universe.[^ref-kqomnipedia]
- **[Space Quest Omnipedia](https://spacequest.fandom.com)** — Space Quest lore and trivia.[^ref-sqwiki]
- **[Quest for Glory Omnipedia](https://questforglory.fandom.com)** — QFG lore and walkthroughs.[^ref-qfgwiki]
- **[Gabriel Knight Omnipedia](https://gabrielknight.fandom.com)** — Gabriel Knight series and Schattenjäger Order lore.[^ref-gkwiki]
- **[ScummVM Wiki](https://wiki.scummvm.org)** — Engine-level documentation for AGI, SCI, and SCI32 support, plus per-title compatibility notes.[^ref-scummvm]
- **[Sierra Help Pages (sierrahelp.com / wiki.sierrahelp.com)](https://wiki.sierrahelp.com)** — Modern-system compatibility patches, Mac/DOS instructions, fan-translation projects, the QFG2 VGA Sci Companion patches.[^ref-sierrahelp]
- **[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org)** — Useful for breadth and basic facts; always supplement with at least one tier-2/3 source for any non-trivial claim.

### Technology references

- **[SCI Companion](http://scicompanion.com)** — Modern SCI authoring tool used by fan-game projects.[^ref-scicompanion]
- **[Adventure Game Studio](https://www.adventuregamestudio.co.uk)** — Engine used by many fan/spiritual-successor Sierra-style games.[^ref-ags]
- **[VOGONS](https://www.vogons.org)** — Vintage Operating Game Online Network; community forum for DOSBox, hardware-accurate emulation, and abandonware compatibility.[^ref-vogons]

## Tier 5 — Abandonware / preservation archives

Use these to confirm a title's existence or to source screenshots, but never as the sole citation for a factual claim.

- **[MyAbandonware](https://www.myabandonware.com)** — Game preservation hub; entries include cover art, screenshots, technical notes.[^ref-myabandonware]
- **[AbandonwareDOS](https://www.abandonwaredos.com)** — DOS-era specialist; useful for screenshots and box art.[^ref-abandonwaredos]
- **[Home of the Underdogs (homeoftheunderdogs.net)](http://www.homeoftheunderdogs.net)** — Cult-classic preservation site, sometimes the only source for obscure Sierra-era titles.[^ref-hotu]
- **[ClassicReload](https://classicreload.com)** — Browser-playable preservation; cite for existence/playability confirmation.[^ref-classicreload]
- **[Internet Archive Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org)** — Universal fallback for any URL that has since gone dark. The vault's dead-URL worklist tracks Wayback availability.[^ref-wayback]

## Tier 6 — Commerce / availability

For confirming current digital availability, pricing, and release dates of re-releases.

- **[GOG.com](https://www.gog.com)** — Most of the modernized Sierra catalog (King's Quest 1–8, Space Quest collections, Police Quest, Quest for Glory, Gabriel Knight, etc.) lives here. **Important:** Never trust a GOG link in a vault citation without first verifying the game is actually available at that URL by opening the store page.[^ref-gog]
- **[Steam](https://store.steampowered.com)** — Subset of Sierra catalog plus most post-2014 re-releases and indie/alumni titles.[^ref-steam]
- **[Activision Blizzard / Microsoft store listings](https://www.activision.com)** — Official current-publisher listings.[^ref-activision]

## Sources to avoid (or use with extreme care)

- **AI-generated content sites** (Grokipedia, etc.) — Never use or cite, not even as a starting point.
- **Discord, Twitter, and YouTube comments** — Not citable; if a relevant claim originates there, find a permanent source that documents it.
- **emuparadise.me** — Site removed all ROM-related content; many older citations are now 404s. Replace with archive.org or remove.
- **Pure game-download sites** (oldgamesdownload, abandonwaregames.net, anygame, oldpcgaming) — Never link or cite as a source.
- **Warez and AI-text download sites** (filecr, bestdosgames) — Never a source; text copied from them is suspect.
- **xtcabandonware** — Carries casino and poker ads; do not cite.
- **New Game Network (newgamenetwork.com)** — Now serves casino pages; use Wayback copies only.
- **oldgamesfinder** — Domain lapsed and is now a parked ad page; remove on sight.
- **"Is this .exe safe?" file-info sites** (windowsbulletin and similar) — Scareware; remove on sight.
- **Download portals with gambling listings** (fileplanet.download.it, *.informer.com / Software Informer) — Remove on sight.
- **Jay is Games (live site)** — Review pages now carry gambling spam; use a pre-2020 Wayback copy, or cite as text only.
- **ffshrine** — Old threads redirect to a gambling portal; remove.
- **retro-replay** — Storefront blurbs that invent details; weak, never the sole support.
- **Internet Archive items tagged [cr]** (cracked copies) — Do not cite.
- **Encyclopedia mirrors** (Alchetron, HandWiki, Kiddle, wiki copies) — Never count as a second source.

## Verification checklist for adding a new citation

Before adding a citation to a vault page:

1. **Does the URL resolve?** Open it in a browser or `WebFetch` to confirm.
2. **Does the cited content actually support the claim?** Read the source, don't just trust the link text.
3. **Is the source authoritative for this fact?** A MobyGames credits page is good for credits; a fan blog's claim about a designer's intent is not, unless that blog is quoting a primary interview.
4. **Are you duplicating an existing citation?** Check the page's existing reference list first.
5. **Save a Wayback snapshot** if you're citing a contemporary source that might rot — visit `https://web.archive.org/save/<url>` before linking.
