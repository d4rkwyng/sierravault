# Corporate Lineage

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

The Sierra On-Line brand has changed hands repeatedly over four-plus decades, passing through a consumer-services rollup, a French media group and finally into the catalog of one of the world's largest video-game publishers. This page traces the ownership chain in chronological order, with the dollar values and dates that sources state, so that any game page's `sierra_lineage` field can be cross-referenced against a single timeline.[^ref-1]

The lineage breaks into seven eras:

1. **On-Line Systems** (1979–1982) — Founding, in the Williams' Simi Valley home, then Coarsegold and Oakhurst, CA.
2. **Sierra On-Line, independent** (1982–1996) — IPO era, flagship-series creation, acquired-studio growth.
3. **CUC International / Cendant** (1996–1998) — Acquisition by a consumer-services rollup that collapsed in an accounting scandal.
4. **Havas Interactive** (1998–2001) — Sale to Vivendi's French media subsidiary Havas.
5. **Vivendi Universal Games** (2001–2008) — Sierra Entertainment label inside Vivendi's games division.
6. **Activision Blizzard / Activision** (2008–2023) — Sierra divisions passed to Activision in the 2008 merger; the brand was briefly revived as an indie label in 2014.
7. **Microsoft Gaming** (2023–present) — Sierra IPs absorbed into Microsoft's catalog via the Activision Blizzard acquisition.

---

## Era 1 — On-Line Systems (1979–1982)

[[Ken Williams]] and [[Roberta Williams]] founded On-Line Systems in 1979 in Simi Valley, California[^ref-1][^ref-3] (MobyGames dates the On-Line Systems name from May 1980).[^ref-6] Ken had registered the company while planning a FORTRAN compiler for personal computers; he later recalled that the couple bought an Apple II for it.[^ref-3][^ref-39] Instead the company took off with Roberta's [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 1 - Mystery House|Mystery House]] in May 1980.[^ref-39] The family moved to Coarsegold in October 1980, and on December 1, 1980 the company opened its first office in nearby Oakhurst;[^ref-21] Sierra's own 1990 timeline instead dates the headquarters move from Coarsegold to Oakhurst to 1983.[^ref-39]

Early titles included [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 2 - The Wizard and the Princess|The Wizard and the Princess]], which sold more than sixty thousand copies,[^ref-6] as well as [[1981 - Threshold]] and [[1981 - Softporn Adventure]]. By 1983 the company had sales of $10 million, "making it one of the largest independent publishers of home-computer software",[^ref-21] and by early 1984 InfoWorld estimated it was the world's 12th-largest microcomputer software company.[^ref-1]

## Era 2 — Sierra On-Line, independent (1982–1996)

In 1982 On-Line Systems was renamed **[[Sierra On-Line|Sierra On-Line, Inc.]]**, after the Sierra Nevada mountains near Oakhurst.[^ref-1][^ref-6] The video-game crash hit Sierra hard: on one day in April 1984 the company shrank "from a company of 120 employees to one of 40".[^ref-8] IBM had come to Sierra in 1983 for software for its new PCjr and funded the development of the AGI engine and the first game written with it, [[1984 - King's Quest - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest]] (1984).[^ref-8]

Through the late 1980s and 1990s, Sierra established flagship adventure franchises (King's Quest, Space Quest, Police Quest, Leisure Suit Larry, Quest for Glory, Gabriel Knight, Laura Bow) and built a stable of acquired studios:

- **1990**: On March 27, 1990, Sierra announced its intent to acquire [[Dynamix|Dynamix]] of Eugene, Oregon, whose 3Space technology it had already licensed.[^ref-39][^ref-10]
- **1992**: Acquired [[Bright Star Technology]] (educational software).[^ref-11]
- **1993**: Acquired [[Coktel Vision]], a French developer and publisher of educational and entertainment software, on October 29, 1993.[^ref-12][^ref-16]
- **1993–1996**: Sierra sold its online service, [[Sierra Network Series|The Sierra Network]], in stages. On July 28, 1993 AT&T bought a 20% stake (General Atlantic Partners another 20%) and the service was renamed The ImagiNation Network; on November 15, 1994 AT&T bought all remaining shares from Sierra and General Atlantic for USD 40 million; on August 7, 1996 America Online bought the service from AT&T.[^ref-59]
- **1995**: Merged with The Pixellite Group, "a developer of personal printing software", on May 31, 1995.[^ref-16]
- **1995**: Merged with Inspiration, parent of [[Impressions Games]] (British strategy developer of the City Building and Lords of the Realm series), on June 20, 1995.[^ref-13][^ref-16]
- **1995**: Acquired Arion Software of Austin, Texas (September 1995) and Green Thumb Software, which, with Pixellite, took Sierra into the home-productivity market.[^ref-16][^ref-41]
- **1995**: Merged with [[Papyrus Design Group]], "developers of NASCAR Racing and Indy Car Racing", on November 30, 1995.[^ref-14][^ref-16]
- **1995**: Acquired SubLogic of Champaign, Illinois (November 1995), a 12-person maker of non-military flight simulators.[^ref-41]
- **1996**: Signed a merger agreement with Headgate, Inc. (later [[Headgate Studios]]) on April 12, 1996.[^ref-16]

Sierra went public in October 1988 at $9.00 a share, trading on NASDAQ as "Sier".[^ref-39] Its headquarters moved from California to Bellevue, Washington, in January 1994.[^ref-41] In the fiscal year ending March 31, 1996, Sierra's revenues were USD 158.2 million.[^ref-16]

## Era 3 — CUC International / Cendant (1996–1998)

On February 20, 1996, **CUC International** — a Connecticut-based consumer-services company best known for its Comp-U-Card discount-shopping membership business — announced that it would acquire Sierra On-Line in a transaction "valued at approximately $1.06 billion", based on CUC's share price.[^ref-19] The merger agreement was dated February 19, 1996, and the merger was completed on July 24, 1996.[^ref-18] CUC grouped Sierra with Davidson & Associates (owner of Blizzard Entertainment) and, from 1997, [[Knowledge Adventure|Knowledge Adventure]] and [[Berkeley Systems|Berkeley Systems]].[^ref-20][^ref-1] Studio changes in the CUC years:

- **1996**: Seattle's [[Synergistic Software]] (Conan, War in Middle Earth) joined Sierra later in 1996.[^ref-15][^ref-40] Sierra's fiscal-1996 annual report does not list it among the year's acquisitions,[^ref-16] and Sierra's *InterAction* magazine called it "the newest member of the Sierra family" in its Holiday 1996 issue.[^ref-64]
- **1997**: CUC completed its purchase of Knowledge Adventure, maker of the JumpStart series, on January 31, 1997, issuing 3,461,551 shares valued at USD 86.1 million.[^ref-60] Berkeley Systems followed in April 1997.[^ref-1]
- **1997**: On December 8, 1997, 7th Level announced an agreement in principle to sell its Cincinnati-based PyroTechnix games unit, with the rights to *[[1998 - Return to Krondor|Return to Krondor]]*, to Sierra for USD 1.2 million.[^ref-61]

Ken Williams stayed on after the sale but left CUC in November 1997; Roberta Williams remained with Sierra until [[1998 - King's Quest - Mask of Eternity|Mask of Eternity]] shipped on November 24, 1998,[^ref-1][^ref-58] and retired from Sierra in 1999.[^ref-21] Valve's [[1998 - Half-Life|Half-Life]] was published by Sierra Studios on November 19, 1998.[^ref-53]

In December 1997 CUC merged with HFS Incorporated to form **Cendant Corporation**. On April 15, 1998, Cendant announced that it had discovered accounting irregularities in certain former CUC business units;[^ref-57][^ref-58] for three years before the merger CUC had overstated its income by over USD 500 million.[^ref-20] Cendant later sold its consumer-software business.

## Era 4 — Havas Interactive (1998–2001)

On November 20, 1998, Cendant confirmed it would sell its consumer software unit — Sierra On-Line, Davidson & Associates and Knowledge Adventure — to the French media group **Havas** for USD 800 million, plus up to USD 200 million more under a contingency, giving "Havas and its parent Vivendi" control.[^ref-24] The sale to "Paris-based Havas SA, a subsidiary of Vivendi SA" closed on January 12, 1999.[^ref-57] The unit traded as **Havas Interactive** from 1998 to 2001.[^ref-27]

On February 22, 1999 ("Chainsaw Monday"), mass layoffs hit Oakhurst, where the original Sierra office was by then known as [[Yosemite Entertainment]]; about a third of the staff were offered relocation to Bellevue, and two of its projects were to resume there.[^ref-26][^ref-1] Relic's [[1999 - Homeworld|Homeworld]] was published by Sierra Studios on September 28, 1999.[^ref-33]

## Era 5 — Vivendi Universal Games / Sierra Entertainment label (2001–2008)

In 2001 Havas Interactive became Vivendi Universal Interactive Publishing and then **Vivendi Universal Games**, the name it kept until 2006, when it became Vivendi Games.[^ref-27] The company itself was renamed from Sierra On-Line, Inc. to **Sierra Entertainment, Inc.** around the turn of 2001–2002: MobyGames dates the change to December 2001,[^ref-6] and Sierra announced the new name on February 19, 2002.[^ref-1]

Sierra-label releases of this era included *[[2004 - Leisure Suit Larry - Magna Cum Laude|Leisure Suit Larry: Magna Cum Laude]]* (2004), published by Vivendi Universal Games and its subsidiary Sierra Entertainment,[^ref-56] and [[2005 - SWAT 4|SWAT 4]] (2005), developed by Irrational Games and published by Sierra Entertainment.[^ref-47]

In early 2004 Vivendi shut Sierra's last owned studios, Impressions and Papyrus, and by June 2004 it had closed Sierra's Bellevue office. Sierra was disestablished as a company on August 24, 2004, and continued as a division of Vivendi's games business.[^ref-1] VU Games assigned the Hoyle games license to [[Encore Software]]; effective December 23, 2004, Encore took over sales, distribution and marketing of the [[Hoyle Series|Hoyle]] products.[^ref-62]

In late 2005 the Sierra brand was relaunched from Los Angeles, and a new subsidiary, **Sierra Online**, was founded to focus on downloadable and online-only games.[^ref-1]

## Era 6 — Activision Blizzard / Activision (2008–2023)

**[[Activision]]** and **Vivendi Games** completed their merger to form **Activision Blizzard** on July 9, 2008, a day after Activision's stockholders approved it; Activision Publishing then included "the Sierra Entertainment, Sierra Online and Vivendi Games Mobile divisions".[^ref-32] The deal was valued at about **USD 18.9 billion**.[^ref-31]

Activision deemed most of the former Vivendi Games properties, including the Sierra Online division, "non-strategic". Several were sold — Codemasters bought the Leisure Suit Larry IP in February 2009 — and the Sierra Online division was closed.[^ref-1] Later events:

- **2013** — [[Gearbox Software]] bought the *Homeworld* rights at the THQ bankruptcy auction for **USD 1.35 million** (after which [[Blackbird Interactive]] continued the franchise).[^ref-33] *Homeworld* had not passed to Activision: THQ confirmed in 2007 that it had acquired the series rights from Relic and Sierra.[^ref-33]
- **2014** — Activision revived the Sierra brand as an indie-focused label, introduced at Gamescom in August 2014 with [[2015 - King's Quest|King's Quest (2015)]] (The Odd Gentlemen) and *Geometry Wars 3: Dimensions*;[^ref-34][^ref-35][^ref-52] *Shiftlings* followed.[^ref-36]
- **2016** — In July 2016 MCV reported, citing development sources, that the Sierra label no longer existed with one title still in production; Activision denied the label had closed.[^ref-36]

## Era 7 — Microsoft Gaming (2023–present)

On October 13, 2023, **Microsoft** completed its acquisition of Activision Blizzard, announced at **USD 68.7 billion** in January 2022, for a total purchase price of USD 75.4 billion, bringing Sierra's IP catalog under Microsoft Gaming.[^ref-31][^ref-37][^ref-65] At time of writing Microsoft has not announced plans for the dormant Sierra adventure-game IPs. [[2024 - Homeworld 3|Homeworld 3]] (developed by [[Blackbird Interactive]], published by Gearbox, May 13, 2024)[^ref-50] and *[[2024 - Tribes 3 - Rivals|Tribes 3: Rivals]]* (Prophecy Games, 2024)[^ref-51] were released by other companies.
