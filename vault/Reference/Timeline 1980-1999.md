# Timeline 1980–1999

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

This is the year-by-year chronicle of Sierra On-Line's independent era — from the May 1980 release of [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 1 - Mystery House|Mystery House]] (founded the company financially) through the February 1999 closure of the original Oakhurst studio under [[Corporate Lineage|Havas Interactive ownership]]. For the post-1999 corporate-and-IP arc, see [[Corporate Lineage]]; for the engine technical evolution, see [[Engine History]].

Ken Williams' 2020 memoir, *Not All Fairy Tales Have Happy Endings: The Rise and Fall of Sierra On-Line*, covers the same era from the founder's side.[^ref-1]

The narrative spine is releases, but each year's entry also notes corporate events, technical milestones, and personnel changes. Releases listed are flagship or first-of-kind titles; full year-by-year release lists are in the [[Release Timeline]]. Release years below follow the vault's individual game pages and the [[Release Timeline]].

---

## 1980

**Founding year.** [[Ken Williams]] and [[Roberta Williams]] found **On-Line Systems** in Simi Valley, California. Ken registered the company while planning a FORTRAN compiler for the Apple II, but that project was set aside for Roberta's [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 1 - Mystery House|Mystery House]] (May 1980), which Laine Nooney notes "is widely regarded as the first adventure game with graphics"; Smithsonian goes further, calling it "the first home computer game ever to include graphics as well as text".[^ref-2][^ref-10][^ref-43][^ref-3] In October 1980 the Williamses moved their family to Coarsegold, and on December 1, 1980 they opened the company's first office in nearby Oakhurst.[^ref-24]

**Major releases:** [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 1 - Mystery House|Mystery House]], [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 2 - The Wizard and the Princess|The Wizard and the Princess]], [[1980 - Hi-Res Adventure 0 - Mission Asteroid|Mission Asteroid]], [[1980 - Hi-Res Cribbage|Hi-Res Cribbage]], [[1980 - Hi-Res Football|Hi-Res Football]].

**Technical:** *Mystery House* runs on the Apple II with a text-parser interface; its pictures were stored using vector-graphics techniques Ken Williams developed, which Sierra still used when building *King's Quest*.[^ref-7]

## 1981

[[Warren Schwader]] is hired as On-Line Systems' first programmer.[^ref-4]

**Major releases:** [[1981 - Hi-Res Adventure 3 - Cranston Manor|Cranston Manor]], [[1981 - Hi-Res Adventure 4 - Ulysses and the Golden Fleece|Ulysses and the Golden Fleece]], [[1981 - Threshold|Threshold]] (Warren Schwader's Apple II arcade game; he said it "sold about 25,000 copies"), [[1981 - Softporn Adventure|Softporn Adventure]] (text-only adult title), [[1981 - Crossfire|Crossfire]], [[1981 - Jawbreaker|Jawbreaker]] (it won *Video* magazine's Arcade Award in April 1982), [[1981 - Pegasus II|Pegasus II]], [[1981 - Hi-Res Soccer|Hi-Res Soccer]].[^ref-4][^ref-10]

## 1982

**Rebrand:** On-Line Systems is renamed **Sierra On-Line, Inc.**, taking the name from the Sierra Nevada mountain range near Oakhurst.[^ref-5][^ref-6]

**Major releases:** [[1982 - Hi-Res Adventure 5 - Time Zone|Time Zone]] (a massive 6-disk adventure, ambitious for the Apple II), [[1981 - Frogger|Frogger]] (Sierra held the disk and tape rights to Sega's arcade game, while Parker Brothers made the cartridge versions; Sierra won the Softsel Hot List Best Seller Award for it in September 1982), [[1982 - Mouskattack|Mouskattack]], [[1982 - Pest Patrol|Pest Patrol]].[^ref-10][^ref-30]

## 1983

The video-game industry **crash of 1983** begins, devastating cartridge-based publishers and leaving Sierra with unsold cartridges and cassettes. Late in 1982 IBM had approached Sierra to develop software for its forthcoming home computer, the PCjr.[^ref-7][^ref-10] Sierra's own 1990 timeline dates its headquarters move from Coarsegold to Oakhurst to April 1983;[^ref-10] HistoryLink, which places the first Oakhurst office in December 1980, says the company "moved into its own building" in 1983.[^ref-24][^ref-39]

**Major releases:** [[1983 - Hi-Res Adventure 6 - The Dark Crystal|The Dark Crystal]], [[1983 - Troll's Tale|Troll's Tale]] (Al Lowe), [[1983 - Sammy Lightfoot|Sammy Lightfoot]] (Warren Schwader, who said of its page-flipping animation: "I'm not saying that I invented it originally, but I discovered it on my own"), [[1983 - Mine Shaft|Mine Shaft]], [[1983 - Learning with Leeper|Learning with Leeper]].[^ref-4][^ref-27]

## 1984

**Collapse and rescue.** On one day in April 1984, with unsold cartridges piling up after the crash, Sierra shrank "from a company of 120 employees to one of 40"; Al Lowe was among those let go and was rehired as an outside contractor.[^ref-27] John Williams' 1990 account in Sierra's own magazine puts it as "over 130 employees to less than 35 in a matter of weeks".[^ref-10] IBM, which had funded Sierra's work for the PCjr, published [[1984 - King's Quest - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest]] for the PCjr in May 1984, after a first public showing at the Winter CES that January.[^ref-9][^ref-27] Designed by [[Roberta Williams]] and built on the new [[Adventure Game Interpreter|AGI]] engine, it was the first Sierra game with a third-person on-screen protagonist, and the Strong Museum says it "set the standard for the graphical adventure game genre".[^ref-8][^ref-9]

**Major releases:** [[1984 - King's Quest - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest: Quest for the Crown]], [[1984 - Wizard of Id's WizMath|Wizard of Id's WizMath]], [[1984 - Wizard of Id's WizType|Wizard of Id's WizType]], [[1984 - Mickey's Space Adventure|Mickey's Space Adventure]], [[1984 - Gelfling Adventure|Gelfling Adventure]] (licensed from *The Dark Crystal*), [[1984 - Learning with FuzzyWOMP|Learning with FuzzyWOMP]], [[1984 - Donald Duck's Playground|Donald Duck's Playground]] (Al Lowe).

## 1985

A Tandy 1000 version of *King's Quest* is released on May 24, 1985.[^ref-42] With IBM's encouragement, Sierra publishes *King's Quest* for other computers; MobyGames dates the Apple II version to October 1984, the Amiga, Atari ST and Macintosh versions to December 1986, and the DOS release to 1987.[^ref-9][^ref-32] *King's Quest II* is released in April 1985.[^ref-10]

**Major releases:** [[1985 - King's Quest II - Romancing the Throne|King's Quest II: Romancing the Throne]] (Roberta Williams), [[1985 - Sierra Championship Boxing|Sierra Championship Boxing]], [[1985 - Stunt Flyer|Stunt Flyer]]. ([[1985 - Thexder|Thexder]] is Game Arts' 1985 Japanese release; Sierra's US Apple II and MS-DOS versions followed in 1987.)[^ref-31]

## 1986

**Major releases:** [[1986 - King's Quest III - To Heir Is Human|King's Quest III: To Heir Is Human]] (Roberta Williams; shipped November 1986), [[1986 - Space Quest - The Sarien Encounter|Space Quest: The Sarien Encounter]] (Mark Crowe & Scott Murphy), [[1986 - The Black Cauldron|The Black Cauldron]] (licensed from Disney).[^ref-10]

## 1987

Sierra's "Two Guys from Andromeda" ([[Mark Crowe]] and [[Scott Murphy]]) and [[Jim Walls]] cement the company's franchise model — multiple lead designers each owning a distinct series.

**Major releases:** [[1987 - Mixed-Up Mother Goose|Mixed-Up Mother Goose]] (Roberta Williams), [[1987 - Police Quest - In Pursuit of the Death Angel|Police Quest: In Pursuit of the Death Angel]] (Jim Walls), [[1987 - Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards|Leisure Suit Larry in the Land of the Lounge Lizards]] (Al Lowe; remake of *Softporn Adventure* with graphics), [[1987 - Space Quest II - Vohaul's Revenge|Space Quest II: Vohaul's Revenge]].

## 1988

**Sierra IPO** — In October 1988, a year after "Black Monday", Sierra goes public at $9.00 a share on NASDAQ under the symbol "Sier".[^ref-10] [[Sierra Creative Interpreter|SCI]], created by Jeff Stephenson, replaces AGI; its first game is *King's Quest IV*.[^ref-11] The first version, SCI0, still uses a text parser and runs at 320×200 in 16-color EGA.[^ref-16]

**Major releases:** [[1988 - King's Quest IV - The Perils of Rosella|King's Quest IV: The Perils of Rosella]] (Roberta Williams; released alongside an AGI edition for lower-end machines)[^ref-11], [[1988 - Police Quest II - The Vengeance|Police Quest II: The Vengeance]] (Jim Walls), [[1988 - Leisure Suit Larry Goes Looking for Love (in Several Wrong Places)|Leisure Suit Larry 2]], [[1988 - Manhunter - New York|Manhunter: New York]] (Evryware; Sierra-published), [[1988 - Silpheed|Silpheed]] (Game Arts; Sierra-localized), [[1988 - Gold Rush|Gold Rush!]] (Doug and Ken MacNeill).

## 1989

The watershed year. SCI matures; Sierra ships flagship adventures across all its franchises. [[Hoyle Series|Hoyle]] launches as Sierra's casual-games arm.

**Major releases:** [[1989 - The Colonel's Bequest|The Colonel's Bequest]] (Roberta Williams; founds Laura Bow series), [[1989 - Space Quest III - The Pirates of Pestulon|Space Quest III: The Pirates of Pestulon]], [[1989 - Leisure Suit Larry III - Passionate Patti in Pursuit of the Pulsating Pectorals|Leisure Suit Larry III]], [[1989 - Quest for Glory I - So You Want to Be a Hero|Quest for Glory I: So You Want to Be a Hero]] (Lori & Corey Cole; founds the Quest for Glory RPG-adventure hybrid series), [[1989 - Manhunter - San Francisco|Manhunter: San Francisco]], [[1989 - Hoyle Official Book of Games - Volume 1|Hoyle Official Book of Games: Volume 1]] (Warren Schwader; founds the Hoyle franchise), [[1989 - A-10 Tank Killer|A-10 Tank Killer]] (published by [[Dynamix]] itself and distributed through Mediagenic, before Sierra bought the studio).[^ref-12]

## 1990

**Acquisition:** On March 27, 1990, Sierra announces its intent to acquire [[Dynamix]] of Eugene, Oregon, whose 3Space technology it had already licensed.[^ref-10][^ref-12]

**Major releases:** [[1990 - King's Quest V - Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder|King's Quest V: Absence Makes the Heart Go Yonder]] (Roberta Williams; SCI1's VGA debut, which combined 256-color VGA with an icon-driven point-and-click interface in place of the text parser)[^ref-16], [[1990 - Conquests of Camelot - The Search for the Grail|Conquests of Camelot]] (Christy Marx), [[1990 - Quest for Glory II - Trial by Fire|Quest for Glory II: Trial by Fire]], [[1990 - Roberta Williams' King's Quest I - Quest for the Crown|King's Quest I SCI remake]], [[1990 - Mixed-Up Mother Goose (SCI Remake)|Mixed-Up Mother Goose SCI]], [[1990 - Stellar 7|Stellar 7]] (Dynamix), [[1990 - Hoyle Official Book of Games - Volume 2|Hoyle Volume 2]].

## 1991

*King's Quest V* ships on CD-ROM in August 1991 with voice acting, "the first of Sierra's full-fledged adventure games to do so"; Ken Williams coined the term "talkies" for such re-releases.[^ref-28] Sierra announces The Sierra Network, an online service for its games, on May 6, 1991, and after months of testing it officially begins operating in California in early June 1991;[^ref-34][^ref-37] it was renamed The ImagiNation Network after AT&T bought a 20 percent stake in July 1993, and AT&T took sole ownership on November 15, 1994.[^ref-6][^ref-34][^ref-35]

**Major releases:** [[1991 - Space Quest IV - Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers|Space Quest IV: Roger Wilco and the Time Rippers]], [[1991 - Police Quest III - The Kindred|Police Quest III: The Kindred]], [[1991 - Conquests of the Longbow - The Legend of Robin Hood|Conquests of the Longbow: The Legend of Robin Hood]], [[1991 - EcoQuest - The Search for Cetus|EcoQuest: The Search for Cetus]], [[1991 - Heart of China|Heart of China]] (Dynamix), [[1991 - The Adventures of Willy Beamish|The Adventures of Willy Beamish]] (Dynamix), [[1991 - Castle of Dr. Brain|Castle of Dr. Brain]] (Corey Cole; founds Dr. Brain series), [[1991 - Leisure Suit Larry 5 - Passionate Patti Does a Little Undercover Work|Leisure Suit Larry 5]] (Al Lowe).

## 1992

**Acquisition:** Sierra acquires [[Bright Star Technology]] (educational software).[^ref-13]

**Major releases:** [[1992 - King's Quest VI - Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow|King's Quest VI: Heir Today, Gone Tomorrow]] (Jane Jensen + Roberta Williams), [[1992 - The Dagger of Amon Ra|The Dagger of Amon Ra]] (Bruce Balfour, second Laura Bow), [[1992 - Quest for Glory III - Wages of War|Quest for Glory III: Wages of War]], [[1992 - Aces of the Pacific - WWII 1946|Aces of the Pacific: WWII: 1946]] (Dynamix).

## 1993

**Acquisition:** On October 29, 1993, Sierra acquires [[Coktel Vision]], a French developer and publisher of educational and entertainment software (Gobliiins, Lost in Time, Inca).[^ref-14][^ref-26] The SCI2 engine debuts, bringing 640×480 SVGA in 256 colors and 32-bit protected-mode code, with *Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers* among its first games.[^ref-16]

**Major releases:** [[1993 - Gabriel Knight - Sins of the Fathers|Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers]] (Jane Jensen; founds Gabriel Knight series), [[1993 - Police Quest - Open Season|Police Quest IV: Open Season]] (designed by Tammy Dargan, produced in cooperation with former LAPD chief Daryl F. Gates), [[1993 - Space Quest V - The Next Mutation|Space Quest V: The Next Mutation]] (Mark Crowe; developed at Dynamix), [[1993 - Freddy Pharkas - Frontier Pharmacist|Freddy Pharkas: Frontier Pharmacist]] (Al Lowe + Josh Mandel), [[1993 - Pepper's Adventures in Time|Pepper's Adventures in Time]] (a Discovery Series title from an original concept by Bill Davis), [[1993 - EcoQuest - Lost Secret of the Rainforest|EcoQuest 2: Lost Secret of the Rainforest]], [[1993 - Betrayal at Krondor|Betrayal at Krondor]] (Dynamix; based on Raymond E. Feist novels), [[1993 - Aces Over Europe|Aces Over Europe]] (Dynamix), [[1993 - Hoyle Classic Card Games|Hoyle Classic Card Games]], [[1983 - Early Math|Early Math]] (Bright Star).

## 1994

Between August 1993 and early 1994 Sierra moves its corporate offices from Oakhurst to Bellevue, Washington; game development and The Sierra Network stay in Oakhurst.[^ref-33][^ref-39][^ref-40][^ref-41]

**Major releases:** [[1994 - King's Quest VII - The Princeless Bride|King's Quest VII: The Princeless Bride]] (Roberta Williams + Lorelei Shannon), [[1994 - Outpost|Outpost]] (Bruce Balfour; colony-management sim), [[1994 - Lode Runner - The Legend Returns|Lode Runner: The Legend Returns]].

## 1995

**Acquisitions:** On June 20, 1995 Sierra merges with Inspiration, parent of [[Impressions Games]] (UK; Caesar, Pharaoh, Lords of the Realm), and on November 30, 1995 with [[Papyrus Design Group]], "developers of NASCAR Racing and Indy Car Racing".[^ref-17][^ref-18][^ref-26]

**Major releases:** [[1995 - Phantasmagoria|Phantasmagoria]] (Roberta Williams; seven discs of live-action video; refused classification in Australia; Sierra's bestselling computer game to that date)[^ref-29], [[1995 - The Beast Within - A Gabriel Knight Mystery|The Beast Within: A Gabriel Knight Mystery]] (Jane Jensen; full-motion video sequel to GK1), [[1995 - Shivers|Shivers]] (Marcia Bales), [[1995 - Torin's Passage|Torin's Passage]] (Al Lowe; outside-LSL family game), [[1995 - Police Quest - SWAT|Police Quest: SWAT]] (full-motion video police simulator; founds the SWAT spin-off series), [[1995 - Caesar II|Caesar II]] (Impressions), [[1995 - Lode Runner On-Line - The Mad Monks' Revenge|Lode Runner On-Line: The Mad Monks' Revenge]].

## 1996

**The pivot.** On February 20, 1996, [[Corporate Lineage|CUC International announces it will acquire Sierra]] in a stock transaction "valued at approximately $1.06 billion"; the merger agreement is dated February 19, and the merger closes on July 24, 1996.[^ref-19][^ref-20] [[Ken Williams]] stays on after the sale.[^ref-6] Sierra acquires Seattle's [[Synergistic Software]].[^ref-21]

**Major releases:** [[1996 - Lighthouse - The Dark Being|Lighthouse: The Dark Being]] (Jon Bock), [[1996 - Phantasmagoria - A Puzzle of Flesh|Phantasmagoria: A Puzzle of Flesh]] (Lorelei Shannon), [[1996 - MissionForce - Cyberstorm|MissionForce: Cyberstorm]] (Dynamix), [[1996 - Leisure Suit Larry 7 - Love for Sail|Leisure Suit Larry: Love for Sail!]] (Al Lowe).

## 1997

[[Ken Williams]] leaves CUC International in November 1997; he left his office at Sierra On-Line for the last time on November 1, 1997.[^ref-6][^ref-36]

**Major releases:** [[1997 - Shivers Two - Harvest of Souls|Shivers Two: Harvest of Souls]], [[1997 - Betrayal in Antara|Betrayal in Antara]], [[1997 - Diablo - Hellfire|Diablo: Hellfire]] (the official expansion for Blizzard's *Diablo*, developed by Sierra's Synergistic Software division and published by Sierra on November 24, 1997).[^ref-6]

## 1998

**Major releases:** [[1998 - King's Quest - Mask of Eternity|King's Quest: Mask of Eternity]] (Roberta Williams + Mark Seibert; 3D action-adventure; Roberta stayed with Sierra until it shipped on November 24, 1998),[^ref-36] [[1998 - Quest for Glory V - Dragon Fire|Quest for Glory V: Dragon Fire]] (designed by Lori Ann Cole, with Corey Cole on programming; final QFG entry), [[1998 - Half-Life|Half-Life]] (Valve; published by Sierra on November 19, 1998),[^ref-6] [[1998 - Caesar III|Caesar III]] (Impressions), [[1999 - Hoyle Casino|Hoyle Casino]], [[1997 - Driver's Education '98|Driver's Education '98]].

**Corporate:** On November 20, 1998, after the Cendant accounting scandal, Cendant confirms it will sell its consumer software unit to the French media group Havas for USD 800 million, plus up to USD 200 million more under a contingency.[^ref-22] The sale to Havas SA, a subsidiary of Vivendi, closes on January 12, 1999, placing Sierra under [[Corporate Lineage|Havas Interactive]] ownership.[^ref-38]

## 1999

**Closure year.** On February 22, 1999, the Oakhurst studio — by then known as [[Yosemite Entertainment]] — is shut down; about a third of its employees are offered relocation to the Bellevue office.[^ref-23] [[Roberta Williams]] retires from Sierra in 1999.[^ref-24]

**Major releases:** [[1999 - Homeworld|Homeworld]] ([[Relic Entertainment]]; published in September 1999; real-time space strategy),[^ref-6] [[1999 - SWAT 3 - Close Quarters Battle|SWAT 3: Close Quarters Battle]] (Sierra Northwest), [[1999 - Half-Life - Opposing Force|Half-Life: Opposing Force]] (Gearbox; Sierra-published), [[1999 - NASCAR Racing 3|NASCAR Racing 3]] (Papyrus), [[1999 - Starsiege|Starsiege]] (Dynamix).

The independent Sierra era ends with the studio closure. The brand continues under Havas/Vivendi/Activision/Microsoft ownership — see [[Corporate Lineage]] for the post-1999 narrative.

## Personnel arc summary

- **Founded the company** (1980): [[Ken Williams]], [[Roberta Williams]][^ref-10]
- **First hires** (1980–1981): [[Warren Schwader]], [[Jeff Stephenson]]
- **Tier-1 designers** during the 1984–1996 golden era: [[Roberta Williams]], [[Al Lowe]], [[Mark Crowe]], [[Scott Murphy]], [[Jane Jensen]], [[Lori Ann Cole]], [[Corey Cole]], [[Jim Walls]], [[Christy Marx]], [[Bruce Balfour]], [[Lorelei Shannon]]
- **Engine and SCI architecture**: [[Jeff Stephenson]] (created SCI in 1988), [[Bob Heitman]][^ref-11]
- **CUC-era departure**: [[Ken Williams]] (left CUC in November 1997)[^ref-6][^ref-36]
- **Into the Havas era**: [[Roberta Williams]] (through Mask of Eternity, shipped November 24, 1998; retired from Sierra in 1999)[^ref-36][^ref-24]
