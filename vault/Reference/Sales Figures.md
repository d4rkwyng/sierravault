# Sales Figures

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: November 12, 2026</small>

## Overview

This page collects commercial sales figures for Sierra games that can be traced to a source we have checked. Computer-game sales data is fragmentary: the figures below come from Sierra's own statements (its fiscal 1996 Form 10-K and its newsletters), Software Publishers Association award notices reported in the trade press, period and later press reports (one citing the PC Data retail tracker), and developer disclosures (Valve's figures for Half-Life). Every figure below carries its source and the date it applies to. Where a number appears in a magazine scan, its digits were checked against the page image.

For the corporate-financial context (Sierra On-Line's IPO, acquisition prices), see [[Corporate Lineage]]. For year-by-year release context, see [[Timeline 1980-1999]].

The two Phantasmagoria figures conflict: Roberta Williams's 2006 recollection of over a million copies in under a year is not borne out by Sierra's 10-K, filed July 1, 1996, which reported "More than 600,000 copies of this product have been sold."[^ref-cuc-acq]

Not used: a 2002 remark by Westwood's Louis Castle that "The best seller was King's Quest at 750,000" names no specific King's Quest game, so it is not assigned to any title here. The remark sits in the interview's Blade Runner (1997) discussion, beside Grim Fandango (about 200,000) and Monkey Island 3 (about 300,000), so "that year" points to roughly 1998-99 and it is not comparable to the US-only PC Data figures above.[^ref-kq8-sales]

On February 20, 1996, CUC International announced that it would acquire Sierra in a transaction "valued at approximately $1.06 billion", based on CUC's closing share price of $37.50 on February 16, 1996.[^ref-cuc-feb] The merger agreement was dated February 19, 1996, and the merger was completed on July 24, 1996.[^ref-cuc-price]

## Sales-data caveats

- **Self-reported Sierra figures** — Newsletter and magazine figures were Sierra's own marketing claims. Sierra's 10-K financial statements were audited, but the series and title unit counts in the fiscal 1996 10-K appear in its Item 1 business description, which is not part of the audited statements and gives no as-of date; they are the company's own statements in a legal filing.[^ref-cuc-acq]
- **SPA certifications** — The Software Publishers Association's Platinum award meant an independent audit had confirmed 250,000 units sold; CGW wrote in 1993 that "Most excellent titles only sell about 100,000 units and even an above average title may only sell about 50,000 units."[^ref-cgw103]
- **Modern digital re-release sales (GOG, Steam)** — no sourced figures are listed on this page.
- **Hoyle franchise sales** are particularly opaque. Encore Software took over sales, distribution and marketing of the Hoyle products from December 23, 2004.[^ref-encore]

Sierra's electronic filings on SEC EDGAR run from June 1994 to February 1997; earlier filings, including the 1988 IPO prospectus, are not there.[^ref-sec-sierra]
