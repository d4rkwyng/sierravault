# Cancelled Game Index

<small style="color: gray">Last updated: October 26, 2026</small>

## Overview

This is the curated index of every cancelled Sierra-extended-catalogue game documented in the vault. Each entry summarizes what the cited sources say about the title's status, its lead designer (where a source names one), when and why it ended, and whether anything is known to survive. For the full per-title page, follow the wiki link.

The vault uses the `CXL -` filename prefix for cancelled games and a lower citation threshold (5+ instead of 15+), as set out in the repository's inclusion criteria (`docs/INCLUSION_CRITERIA.md`). Pages of "in-development" status that haven't shipped use the `TBD -` prefix instead and are listed separately at the bottom of this page.

## Cancelled games

### [[CXL - King's Quest IX]]

- **Type:** Cancelled franchise sequel
- **Lead designer:** [[Roberta Williams]] wrote the original concept; later attempts were made without her.[^ref-28]
- **Cancellation:** Several official attempts never shipped: *King's Quest: Twins of Change* (1999–2002, Sierra's Seattle studio under Vivendi), a Silicon Knights project (2007) and a Telltale Games project (2011).[^ref-28] *King's Quest* (2015) by The Odd Gentlemen was a reimagining; its creative director Matt Korba said "it's not King's Quest IX or anything like that".[^ref-28]
- **Status today:** No playable build is known. A summary design document was given to the King's Quest Omnipedia with Ken and Roberta Williams' permission.[^ref-28]

### [[CXL - Space Quest VII - Return to Roman Numerals]]

- **Type:** Cancelled franchise sequel
- **Lead designers:** [[Scott Murphy]] and [[Leslie Balfour]][^ref-2][^ref-11]
- **Cancellation:** Started in early 1997;[^ref-2] put "on hold indefinitely" in December 1997;[^ref-11] a proposed restart in early 1999 quickly stalled, and the project died when Sierra closed its Oakhurst studio on February 22, 1999.[^ref-11][^ref-29] A separate Space Quest reboot contracted to Escape Factory was cancelled on December 19, 2002.[^ref-11]
- **Status today:** A short video teaser shipped on the August 1997 *Space Quest Collection*, and Rich Powell's concept art survives; documented at [SpaceQuest.net/sq7](https://spacequest.net/sq7/).[^ref-2][^ref-11]
- **Significance:** The Space Quest creators' later game, [[2022 - SpaceVenture|SpaceVenture]] (Guys from Andromeda, "Created by the minds behind the beloved Space Quest series"), entered Steam Early Access on April 1, 2025 and was fully released on December 23, 2025.[^ref-12]

### [[CXL - Leisure Suit Larry 8]]

- **Type:** Cancelled franchise sequel
- **Lead designer:** [[Al Lowe]]
- **Cancellation:** "At the end of 1998, while Sierra and I were negotiating a contract for Leisure Suit Larry 8: Lust in Space, Sierra cancelled production," Lowe writes.[^ref-13] Of the contract talks he says, "They didn't want to negotiate, so I left."[^ref-14]
- **Significance:** The next Larry game, *Leisure Suit Larry: Magna Cum Laude* (2004), was published by Vivendi Universal Games and its subsidiary Sierra Entertainment, without Lowe;[^ref-15] it was followed by *Box Office Bust* (2009) and, later, Replay Games' *Leisure Suit Larry: Reloaded*.[^ref-16]

### [[CXL - Manhunter 3]]

- **Type:** Cancelled franchise sequel
- **Lead designer:** Evryware (original Manhunter team)
- **Cancellation:** 1989. *Manhunter 2: San Francisco* ended on a cliffhanger that set up *Manhunter 3: London*, but "The association between Evryware and Sierra ended in 1989"; no production deal was ever struck.[^ref-17]
- **Status today:** No assets are known; the Manhunter Shrine says "virtually no details" were ever released.[^ref-17]

### [[CXL - Babylon 5 - Into the Fire]]

- **Type:** Cancelled licensed game
- **Cancellation:** 1999. IGN reported that "due to this reorganization" Sierra cancelled four titles, including *Babylon 5* and *Orcs: Revenge of the Ancients*, along with *Desert Fighters* and *Pro Pilot Paradise*.[^ref-18]
- **Status today:** A beta build dated September 1999 surfaced online in 2014.[^ref-19]

### [[CXL - Middle-earth Online]]

- **Type:** Cancelled MMO
- **Senior designer:** Janus Anderson[^ref-20]
- **Cancellation:** 1998–2001. Sierra began the game in 1998 for a planned 2000 release; it suffered layoffs and a move from California to Washington in 1999.[^ref-20] During Sierra's September 1999 reorganization a spokesperson said Sierra was "halting production on it now" to retool and restaff.[^ref-18] Vivendi Universal took over the license in 2001, and it passed to Turbine, "which took the IP and did its own thing".[^ref-20]

### [[CXL - SWAT - Urban Justice]]

- **Type:** Cancelled SWAT series entry
- **Cancellation:** Announced in February 2002 and made at Sierra's Bellevue, Washington office;[^ref-21][^ref-22] in April 2004 a Vivendi Universal spokesperson said work had been "discontinued" in favour of SWAT 4.[^ref-21]
- **Status today:** IGN published the team's developer diaries in 2002.[^ref-22]
- **Significance:** The series continued under Sierra: *SWAT 4* (2005) was developed by Irrational Games and published by Sierra Entertainment.[^ref-23]

### [[CXL - Front Page Sports - Football Pro '99]]

- **Type:** Released, then recalled; its successor was cancelled
- **History:** *NFL Football Pro '99* shipped to retailers in December 1998 and was recalled on January 21, 1999 for "errors in the program's code"; Sierra then planned *Football Pro 2000* for August 1999.[^ref-24] That 2000 version "was cancelled shortly after the 1999 version recall".[^ref-25]
- **Note:** This page is filed as cancelled although the '99 game was released; see its own page for details.

### [[CXL - Red Baron (2013 Remake)]]

- **Type:** Cancelled remake (modern era)
- **Lead designer:** [[Damon Slye]] (Mad Otter Games)[^ref-27]
- **Cancellation:** 2013. Mad Otter Games' Kickstarter for a new *Red Baron* closed with "Funding Unsuccessful": $40,493 pledged of a $250,000 goal from 1,118 backers.[^ref-26] In September 2015 Slye was looking to raise $50,000 for a prototype, "with a potential release in 2016"; no game followed.[^ref-27]
- **Significance:** An alumni crowdfunding attempt, not a Sierra or Activision project.

### [[CXL - Precinct (2013)]]

- **Type:** Cancelled spiritual successor
- **Lead designer:** [[Jim Walls]][^ref-10]
- **Cancellation:** 2013. The Kickstarter for *Precinct*, "from the creators of 'Police Quest'", was cancelled with $85,756.56 pledged against a $500,000 goal.[^ref-10]
- **Significance:** Example of an alumnus revival attempt that didn't reach commercial production.

## In-development (TBD - prefix)

These are NOT cancelled but unreleased. Listed for completeness; see each page for current status.

### [[TBD - Gabriel Knight 4 - Five Hearts]]

- **Lead designer:** [[Jane Jensen]]
- **Status:** Jensen released *Five Hearts* as a short story on November 19, 2024 (not a video game).[^ref-30] A full GK4 video game has not been formally announced.

### [[TBD - King's Quest IV Remake (Infamous Adventures)]]

- **Lead developer:** [[Infamous Adventures]]
- **Status:** Unconfirmed. Infamous Adventures announced on July 27, 2012 that it was no longer producing games;[^ref-33] see the project page for the little that is known since.

### [[TBD - Order of the Thorne - Fortress of Fire]]

- **Lead developer:** Infamous Quests
- **Status:** In development for years without a release date; planned follow-up to [[2015 - Order of the Thorne - The King's Challenge|Order of the Thorne: The King's Challenge]], which was released on January 26, 2016.[^ref-32]

### [[TBD - Quest for Infamy - Roehm to Ruin]]

- **Lead developer:** Infamous Quests
- **Status:** Announced in March 2015 as a prequel to [[2014 - Quest for Infamy]].[^ref-31]

## Patterns and observations

Read together, the Cancelled folder shows three recurring patterns:

1. **Ownership-change cancellations (1997–1999).** Space Quest VII was shelved in December 1997 and abandoned in 1999;[^ref-11] Leisure Suit Larry 8 died in contract talks at the end of 1998;[^ref-13] Babylon 5 and Middle-earth Online were hit by Sierra's September 1999 reorganization,[^ref-18] which followed the February 22, 1999 layoffs at Oakhurst.[^ref-29]
2. **Vivendi-era restarts that never shipped (1999–2004).** King's Quest: Twins of Change,[^ref-28] the Escape Factory Space Quest[^ref-11] and SWAT: Urban Justice[^ref-21] were all begun and then dropped while Sierra was owned by Vivendi.
3. **Alumni crowdfunding attempts (2013).** Damon Slye's Red Baron failed on Kickstarter[^ref-26] and Jim Walls's Precinct was cancelled by its creators.[^ref-10]

Manhunter 3 fits none of these: no deal to make it was ever struck.[^ref-17]
